# THE SETTLEMENT — HOW TO SAY IT

The end goal, out loud, for Network School and its founder. **The spoken lines are drafts to argue with, not a script.**

## ONE SENTENCE

Everyone here is building the outside layer, and it still gets filled with the humans we already have, me very much included — so the half I work on is who's around you all day, and the end goal is one place where that has no end date.

## THIRTY SECONDS (the hallway version)

In case we haven't met yet I'm Alex, nice to meet. I'm part of the Paradanta project — we run a school. Main centre near Sochi, one in Latvia, small houses in a few countries. I've got a room there, I do kitchen shifts.

And everything we run has an end date, twelve days, a month at the outside, and then everybody goes home, me included. My teacher puts it like, you put a cucumber in brine and it doesn't try to become a pickle, it just becomes one, because of what it's sitting in.

So the thing I want is the same conditions with no end date. And the test isn't whether people stay, it's whether they come out and build anything.

## TWO MINUTES (the full version)

In case we haven't met yet I'm Alex, nice to meet. I'm part of the Paradanta project, we do conscious human development, which mostly means we try to make people happier.

And it already exists, kind of. A teacher, Gennady, who's been at it decades. A main centre near Sochi, one in Latvia, small houses in a few countries. People in teams of eight to twelve, responsible for each other. I've got a room there, I do kitchen shifts.

But everything we run has an end date — twelve days, a month at the outside — and then everybody goes back to the same room and the same job, me included.

And I'm not arguing with what you're building here, the rules, the governance, none of it — I'd just say whatever we build, we the people who inhabit it end up deciding its fate. So there's a second layer nobody designs on purpose, which is who's around you all day and what they're doing at seven in the morning. That's conditions too, just the faster half.

And people try to fix that by wanting it more, and I'll be honest with you, I've been at that for years and I'm not great at it. Motivation is like weather, you can't schedule weather. My teacher puts it like — you put a cucumber in brine, it doesn't try to become a pickle, it just becomes one, because of what it's sitting in. Your will's still needed, it's not magic, the surroundings just do most of the lifting.

**[YOUR BEAT — one dry line, roughly here. Nobody writes it but you.]**

So the next thing isn't a better course, it's the same conditions with no end date. And the point isn't the place, it's that people come out and build things — science, art, another centre, or just living next to their family differently. Nobody believes that from a talk, somebody has to show it. So the test isn't whether people stay. If nothing comes out of it, it failed.

And to be honest we're not there. We've got the centres, we haven't got the place. I'm not asking you for anything, I just figure if you're building one too you should know what mine is for. And if you see where it breaks, tell me.

## THE LOGIC, STEP BY STEP

1. That place partly exists: teacher, two centres, small houses, teams of eight to twelve. **Defensible, load-bearing** — and it goes first, because the concrete half is the half that reads as real. Tie the list to blank 1.
2. Every format is a dose with an end date. **Defensible**; what survives the return is unmeasured, which is blank 3.
3. The outside layer gets inhabited by the humans who already exist. **Defensible** — his own written line.
4. A second layer, who's around you daily, is designed by nobody. **Defensible**, and it stops step 3 reading as a critique.
5. Effort is the usual lever there and doesn't hold. **Asserted**, himself as the evidence.
6. Surroundings are the reliable lever; will still required. **Asserted**, unmeasured — the cucumber carries it.
7. So the unit of work is a place, not a course. **Follows from 6.**
8. So the next build is the same conditions, no end date. **Does not fully follow.** It jumps from a month straight to forever and skips every duration in between. This is the hole; objection 2 is where it gets hit.
9. The output is people leaving and building things. **Asserted** — the only non-circular version, and the only falsifiable commitment in here.

## FOR THE FOUNDER SPECIFICALLY

He already believes step 6 — he built a residential community on it — so selling him the mechanism hands back his own thesis at half speed. Lead instead with the two things he has probably not been pitched: the metric is exit rather than retention, and the method is handed down with a living person responsible for it. On the difference, his cohort rotates — that much is in the record; that nobody here runs a handed-down method with assigned teams is his own guess, and one resident can falsify it out loud, so check it in the building first or say it as a question. Status there comes from teaching, not pitching, so the honest no-ask is the credible move — though a no-ask also files him under philosophy rather than project. Blank 5 is where that gets decided, not in the hallway.

## THE OBJECTIONS AND HIS ANSWERS

**1. "That's what this place already is. What have you got that we don't?"**
Yeah, mostly, and that's why I'm here. Two differences, neither flattering — you rotate, and the thing I want has no end date; and ours is a handed-down method with teams, not a set of practices you assemble yourself.

**2. "You go from a month straight to forever. Why not ninety days? A year? Come back every quarter?"**
Honestly, I don't know where the line is. I only know a month isn't it. If somebody wants to run the in-between versions first I'd take that, I'd learn more from it than from arguing about it.

**3. "Every intentional community has said this. The base rate is awful."**
You're right, I'm not arguing with the record. The structural thing is what counts as winning — for most of them staying inside is the win, for us it's people going out and building. If nothing comes out of it, it failed. That's the test I'd take.

**4. "Where's the output — and what's left six months after somebody goes home?"**
I don't have the output yet, and I don't have the six-month number either, which is fair to hold against me. The selection thing is fair too, you can't randomise anyone into a settlement. Nearest test is people who wanted to come and couldn't, against people who did. I haven't run it.

**5. "So what do you want — money, land, people? Can I come, and on what terms?"**
Nothing, right now. [ONLY IF TRUE — see blank 5.] I'm not recruiting. If you want to see it there are centres, you can come and look. *(The terms half — cost, jurisdiction, visas, what happens if you leave — is blank 4, and he will be asked it in this room.)*

## WHAT KILLS IT

- Opening on theory. The first concrete thing — the centres, the rota — arrives before any argument; everything in front of it is costing him.
- The ambition in its own language — a seed for a new era, latent potential, human evolution — the phrasing that starts a stranger checking.
- Naming the settlement before the cucumber, which makes it a brochure.
- Any phrasing where "no end date" comes out as "people don't leave." Same fact, opposite reading.
- Letting step 3 sound like a prediction that their city will fail.
- Dropping the join-in, after which it reads as a man explaining people to people.
- Anything that smells like recruitment; his own line is that then doors close. "The team's assembled" is that smell, not its opposite.
- Drifting into the practice, or saying "proof" where "show it" does the job.

## BLANKS ONLY HE CAN FILL

1. **Which centres are running today, and which he'll name.** A short true list beats a long half-aspirational one. Formats too: the residential doses are the 3–4 day, the twelve-day and the month — the two-week Fundamentals is the online one, and a resident who checks the site will find that.
2. **The teaching-years number, if he uses one.** His copy says more than 45, the site over 40. Pick one, or say "decades."
3. **What happens after a format ends** — how many are still doing anything six months on, and what of it went for him personally. A bad number argues for the settlement better than none. Nothing in the record says his own fades, so he cannot say it until he decides it's true.
4. **The state of the settlement:** land, country, jurisdiction, visas, headcount, what a resident pays, what happens if they leave, date — or none yet. "We haven't started" is fine; saying nothing makes it a daydream, and half this room's first question is whether they could legally set foot in it.
5. **The ask** — nothing, or something, decided before he opens his mouth. One concrete version worth weighing: a bounded cohort run where he is now, fixed length, fixed headcount, his method, with the measure agreed before it starts and re-read at six months. It costs the host a room and it produces the number in blank 3 from people he did not select, which is the hole in his own objection 4.
6. **One thing he has watched surroundings do to people** — a pattern across many, never one person's story.
7. **What he'd accept as disproof, and over what horizon, in his own number.** He has not named one, so nobody else should.
8. **The dry beat.** Left empty on purpose.
