# What we do and why — advanced version

Built as a chain of "why". Each part answers the question the previous part leaves open. The root (we live as if separate from the world) sits in the middle, as the last answer in the chain, and everything after it is the way out.

Spine: BRACED → TAKING → THE BORDER → THE TURN → WHAT WE DO → WHAT WE SEE → THE SEED

---

### BRACED

Remember learning to float. You lie back in the water and every muscle fights it. You kick, you lift your head, and you sink. Then one day you stop fighting, and the water holds you. It was holding you the whole time.

Most people live their whole life like that. Braced, against nothing. Every idea about how things should be, every fear, every old wound, standing between them and what's actually here. It started as protection: a child learns anger gets punished and tears get laughed at, so the body locks them down. Hold a lock long enough and it becomes who you think you are.

Here's the catch. Feeling is one channel, not two. The same lock that keeps the anger and the tears down keeps the joy down with them. You can't dam half a river. So a braced person doesn't just feel less pain. They feel less of everything. Try it: tense your whole body, hard, and then try to feel glad about anything. You can't. Nothing gets through.

### TAKING

With the channel closed we can't feel good from the inside. So we take it from the outside: food, screens, sex, drugs, status, approval. Each works for a while, because it briefly loosens the grip. And winning doesn't fix it. We can reach the peak of a career and still wake up not knowing what we're living for. Ask Jim Carrey.

The taking is run by the mind: the part that scans for the next thing to get, compares us to others, and judges what we already have. It wedges itself between us and life and holds us on a short leash: we don't see, we react. Success doesn't retire it. So the day we win, it turns on us.

### THE BORDER

So why does the mind do this? Because it thinks it's guarding something. Something small, alone, and easy to lose.

You've felt this. When something really hurts, the world shrinks to the size of your body. There's you, and there's everything else, and everything else is far away. Now imagine living at a low dose of that, every day, for years. That's what a braced body does to a person: it makes them feel small, alone, and surrounded. Not as a thought. As a fact in the body. And from that fact everything we've described follows. You defend, because you're alone. You take, because you're cut off from what you need. The root isn't greed and it isn't weakness. It's one belief the body holds: I stop at my skin, and everything past it is other.

A cell that lives by that belief inside a body has a name. It stops listening to the organism, takes what it wants, grows, and dies together with the host. Cancer. Multiply that cell by billions and you get the civilization we have. It takes attention from people, resources from nature, health from our bodies, and the future from our children. It's built by people who can't feel good without taking, because they feel cut off from the very thing they're taking from.

That's the whole diagnosis. Now the good part.

### THE TURN

The belief is false, and the body already knows it. Feeling good is our default state. We don't build it. We don't earn it. Release the tension and it arises on its own, the way water flows when you stop blocking the pipe. Children are the proof: they are happy for no reason at all.

And look at what happens when the tension goes. The edge goes soft. You lie back and the water holds you. You yawn because someone else did. You walk into a room and your body settles before anyone has said a word. Separation takes effort. Connection is what's there when the effort stops. That's why feeling good and feeling part of things arrive together: they're the same event.

And when a person no longer needs to take in order to feel alive, something flips. They start to give. They start to create. Not from scarcity. Not "I'll prove them wrong." But "I see what could exist and I want to build it." That's what a healthy cell does. It doesn't decide to support the body. It's part of the body, so giving is simply what its life looks like.

### WHAT WE DO

You can't argue a body out of a belief it holds as tension. You can only give it evidence. Enough evidence, often enough, that the guard it keeps posting can finally stand down. That's the whole method, and it has four tools.

- **Meditation, body and breath work.** Every day, together. Attention goes inside, and the edge you feel turns out to be made of tension. Watch it and it softens.
- **Giving.** Real work for real needs: cooking, building, teaching. Every hour spent on someone else is an hour the body spends not defending anything.
- **A purpose.** Something bigger than your own comfort. Frankl saw it in the camps: the ones who lasted had someone or something waiting for them outside. You can't carry a purpose like that and stay small.
- **Discipline.** A rhythm you keep whether you feel like it or not. Habit puts the armour back on every morning, so it has to come off every morning too.

Around all of it, a clean space, no alcohol, no substances, and people further along than we are. Because calm is contagious, the same way a yawn is. Your body cannot stay tense for long around people who have let go. In a group, the shared atmosphere does half the work for you. That's why depth is a team thing.

### WHAT WE SEE

We've done this for twenty years, with tens of thousands of people from all over the world. Two centers, Europe and Russia. 120 people live in them full time and run everything: courses, retreats, cooking, building. And we've watched the change thousands of times. It goes in order, and the order is a person coming out of hiding.

First a person starts to listen. For real. Attention leaves the self it was protecting, for the first time, and lands on someone else. Then giving appears: time, attention, help, offered without being asked and without keeping score. Then perception clears, like a dirty window being wiped. They see people and situations as they are, because a person who no longer feels under siege doesn't need to see threats. So the measure is not knowledge or years. It's two things: has giving appeared, and how clearly do they see. Both are the same measure: how much of the person is still on guard.

A man arrives with panic attacks and a bottle. Years later he's sober, runs a business, and his family trusts him. Their faces change. People notice it before they can name it. And the ones who go far enough all say the same thing, in different words: the line I drew between me and everything else was never there. Fear drew it. Nothing else did.

### THE SEED

So here is what we are actually doing. Not making people calmer, and not making them nicer. Building the conditions in which a person remembers what they are: not a sealed thing in a hostile world, but a part of one whole, the way a cell is part of a body. The Buddha said it twenty-five centuries ago: suffering comes from not knowing what we are. Every tradition has said it since. We just do it with a method, in a body, in a group, every day.

And now we're building a settlement. A place where people who've remembered this live, work and raise children together, and where the environment itself speeds up the remembering for whoever enters. It isn't a retreat from the world. For a person who no longer feels separate, there is no "outside" to retreat from. What they discover there goes straight back out, as a technology, a piece of art, a discovery, a school, or a new seed somewhere else.

A society of people who don't live as separate runs on a different fuel. It doesn't need to devour, for the same reason a healthy body doesn't eat itself. If that works at the scale of one town, it's a seed. Proof that another kind of society is possible, and then we can copy it.

That's what we do. We take tense, resistant people who feel alone in a hostile world, give their bodies the evidence they need to let go, and let them build the world they were never apart from.
