# What we do and why — 5-minute video, two rewrites of the advanced draft

Both rewritten from paradanta_advanced.md (1,419 words) down to ~720, keeping its chain: BRACED → TAKING → THE BORDER → THE TURN → WHAT WE DO → WHAT WE SEE → THE SEED. The root (I stop at my skin) stays in the middle. Rewritten, not cut: sentences carry two ideas where the long draft used two sentences.

- **Variant 1 — Fused.** The advanced draft's voice. Full chain, civilization included.

Both in the "we" form throughout; "you" only in the one live instruction (tense, then try to feel glad).
- **Variant 2 — Camera.** Said to a lens: shorter breaths, the tense test done live with the viewer.

## VARIANT 1 — FUSED

### BRACED

Remember learning to float. We lie back in the water and every muscle fights it. We kick, we lift our head, and we sink. Then one day we stop fighting, and the water holds us. It was holding us the whole time.

That's how most of us live: braced, against nothing. Somewhere back there, a child learned that anger gets punished and tears get laughed at, and the body locked them both down. But feeling is one channel. The lock that keeps our anger down keeps our joy down with it. Try it right now: tense everything, hard, and try to feel glad about something. Nothing gets through. Even worse, a braced person sees fewer options. It's like tension is a tax on how many doors we can see.

### TAKING

So if nothing good gets through from the inside, we go and get it from the outside. Food, screens, sex, drugs, status, approval. Each one works for about an hour. Even winning doesn't fix it. We reach the top and wake up not knowing what we're living for. Ask Jim Carrey. And the one running all these errands is the mind. It's always scanning for the next thing to get, and always judging what we already have. It stands between us and our life like a guard. And the day we win, it turns on us.

### THE BORDER

But why does it stand guard? Because it thinks it's protecting something small, alone and easy to lose. We all know that feeling. When something really hurts, the world shrinks to the size of our body. There's us, and there's everything else, and everything else is far away. A braced body keeps us in a low dose of that for years. We feel small, alone and surrounded, not as a thought, but as a fact in the body. And everything follows from that. We defend ourselves, because we're alone. We take, because we're cut off from what we need. It all comes down to one belief the body holds: I stop at my skin, and everything past it is other.

A cell that lives by that belief has a name. It stops listening to the body, takes what it wants, and dies with the host. Cancer. That's the whole diagnosis. Now the good part.

### THE TURN

That belief is wrong. Feeling good isn't something we build or earn. It's the default. Release the tension and it comes back on its own, like water when we stop pinching the hose. And something else goes away with the tension: the sense of being a separate thing in a hostile world. Nobody decides it. We notice it, the way we notice a headache is gone. Tension was producing it. And it's not like we become connected. We just stop feeling cut off. And a person who doesn't feel cut off doesn't need to take from anyone. That's the cell coming back to health. It doesn't decide to support the body. It's part of the body, so giving is simply what its life looks like.

### WHAT WE DO

None of this can be forced. Like sleep, it comes when the conditions are right. So the real question is how to build those conditions, and that's what our method is. It's simple. One half is letting go: meditation, and work with the body and the breath. The other half is taking an active part in life: real work, tasks that matter, for a goal bigger than our own comfort. Discipline, so that both actually happen. And all of it together with other people, never alone, because what we're undoing is the illusion of being separate, and nobody undoes that separately. Letting go and taking action, one after the other. That's the whole method.

### WHAT WE SEE

We've been doing this for twenty years. Tens of thousands of people have gone through it. Two centers, and 120 people who live in them full time. And the change is easy to notice. People start listening more and talking less. They begin to give: sharing, taking care of the people around them, and then of more than that. And naturally, they start to see the world the way it actually is. Fewer threats, more options. They feel and understand other people with less effort, and being with others gets easy. That's the measure. Not what someone knows, and not how many years they've practised. It's these practical, day-to-day changes that actually matter. And the ones who go far all say the same thing: the line between me and everything else was never there.

### THE SEED

So now we're building a settlement. A place where people who've remembered this live, work and raise children together, and where the environment itself speeds up the remembering. And it isn't a retreat from the world. Gandhi led the independence of India and still kept one day of silence every week. He did both at the same time, and so do we. Whatever people find in that place goes straight back out into the world, as a technology, a piece of art, a school, or a new seed somewhere else. A society of people who don't live as separate doesn't need to devour anything, for the same reason a healthy body doesn't eat itself. That's what we do. We take people who feel alone in a hostile world, we create the conditions for them to let go, and then we let them build the world they were never apart from.
