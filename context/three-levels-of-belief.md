# Three levels of belief

Context material for future work. It is not part of the essay, and no essay part was changed for it. The idea is Alex's, told in his own words on 2026-10-14 and formalized here with his examples and his meaning kept.

People hold beliefs on three levels. The levels differ in how easily a belief can be looked at, questioned and changed.

## Regular beliefs

The first level is the ideas we all carry around and think are right. An average healthy adult can look at these ideas critically and change them when the circumstances are right, and this is even more true of someone who has done some inner work. Which car brand is the best is a good example. So are Apple against Android and strong preferences in many other fields. Such opinions can sit very close to the heart, and still most people are willing to change them when they meet a good enough reason.

## Core beliefs

The second level is the beliefs a person has built their identity around. The same belief can sit on either level, depending on the person. Someone who defines themselves as a Porsche person, or as a lifelong supporter of a soccer club, will defend that to the end, while for someone else the same idea is a preference. Statements about oneself belong here too, such as "I'm the kind of person who gets angry."

The same goes for beliefs about the world. Racism can be a core belief for people who grew up in places where it is an integral part of the culture. Even a hint that a core belief might be untrue or irrelevant is met with strong negativity, because considering it would mean considering not being who they are. That is the whole idea of a core belief. In life or death situations people can change some of them. This usually means a huge overhaul of the person's life. It does happen, and it is rare, and it almost always takes a great event or a series of events.

## Beliefs held as facts

The third level is made of things a person does not experience as beliefs at all. They hold these as how things are, and they cannot picture them being discussed even in theory. Good examples are hard to find, because most of the ones that matter are easy to argue with. Gravity works well. When I let go of something, it falls to the floor, and nobody puts that up for debate. The sun rises in the morning is another. Most people agree about these, which is part of what makes them feel like facts.

Even here the line moves from person to person. That the earth is round is a fact for most people, and then it comes up for discussion, because some people claim it is flat. The ideas people take as facts are the ones they find extraordinarily difficult to let go of.

## Why this matters for awakening

The process of awakening and remembering who we are is the process of letting go of beliefs on all three levels. The levels also explain why it is so difficult.

With core beliefs we feel a lot of resistance to changing them, and we need help from somebody we trust.

With the third level there is no conscious resistance to point at. We cannot even make a case against the change, because we never treated it as something that could change. For this we usually need a guide we trust more than we trust ourselves, someone who can tell us that this is different from what we think. Our first reaction will be to ask how they can claim that. What makes the difference is that we believe them anyway, and make the effort to see a new reality beyond those ideas. In the case of gravity, the idea to try on would be that levitation exists.
