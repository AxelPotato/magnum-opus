Q&A · likely questions · one beat each

  • “Isn't this just Stoicism?” Yes, on the action leg and on presence. The addition: the letting-go leg trained in the body, daily. The Stoics wrote at night. This sits in the morning. Both.
  • “How is it different from therapy?” Not against it. Welwood's prescription is both. Therapy works the story. Stillness works the brace in the body. Inner agency is the act that follows. Many people do both.
  • “What's the evidence?” The two questions, answered today and in two weeks. The four observables. I won't quote studies for this method. The honest limit: the evidence is people, and you can meet them.
  • “What is the school? Is it a cult?” Name it plainly: the school, the teacher, twenty years, two centres, 120 people living it. Nothing to hide. The test for any school: do people leave more capable in the world, or less? Ask the ones who left.
  • “Can I do this alone with an app?” Yes, slowly, and you can fool yourself for years. The three dampers: a reference point, people further along, a guide and a schedule. An app gives the third, at best.
  • “Doesn't ambition need tension?” Biles. The drive got the medals; the tension took her out of the air in Tokyo. 95 × 2 = 190. The drive stays. The brace goes.
  • “Where is this? Why there?” Where the school grew up. Say the places flat. If that's a blocker for someone, it's a fair one.
  • “What actually happens on the retreat?” One paragraph: morning stillness, real work with real people, stillness again, for days. Everything else in the follow-up message.
  • “Where does ADHD fit?” Wide aperture, no steering wheel. Letting go widens what you see. Action is the wheel. Same chart.
  • “Isn't letting go just passive? Isn't that the bypassing you described?” That's the top-left. Letting go here is the apology, the call, the sentence. The most active thing you'll do this week.

  • “Can you send the slides?” Yes: the PDF. Say the group gets it.
