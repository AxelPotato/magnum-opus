# The life goal, said with the chart — for someone not spiritual

Uses only the talk's concepts: the two axes, the rooms, the door, the line, the far corner, the border. No school vocabulary.

## 30 seconds

You know the chart: release on one axis, action on the other, and the far corner where nothing feels impossible and there's nothing left to defend. Most people would call that corner the goal. I think it's the edge of the map. Because the whole map rests on one quiet assumption: that I'm a separate thing, and a separate thing has to protect itself and take what it needs. Every room on the chart is that thing getting better at its job. My goal is to find out, first-hand, that the assumption was never true. Not to believe it. To see it, the way you see that the room is lit. Once you see it, there's nothing left to protect, so nothing left to tense against, and nothing you stand apart from. That's what the word "enlightenment" pointed at, before it got covered in incense.

## Two minutes

Remember the fist. Tense everything and you turn into a small, hard object in a world of other objects. That feeling, I end here and everything past this point is other, is what sits under the whole chart. Tension is the body's version of it. Fear is the mind's version. Grabbing is what it makes us do.

Now remember what happens when the tension goes. You lie back and the water holds you. The edge softens. Everyone has felt that for a moment: athletes call it the zone, musicians call it the muse, most of us call it a gut feeling we actually followed. Everyone visits. Almost nobody lives there.

The far corner of the chart is where someone does live there. Fully active, fully released, present in whatever is in front of them. Gandhi. Marcus Aurelius. That is as good as a person can be while still being a person on the chart.

What I want is one step past that, and it isn't more of the same. It's the moment the small, defended self stops running the show. You stop believing you're part of everything and start seeing it, the way you see that a room is lit. The "illusions" people talk about are just the things fear drew: the border, the scarcity, the "I'll be happy when", the enemy. When the border goes, they go with it.

And you don't vanish. The people who lived closest to this acted more, not less, because nothing was in the way anymore. That's the reason I want it: to be useful, with nothing standing between me and whoever is in front of me.

## The three questions

- **"Isn't that just being a vegetable, feeling nothing?"** The opposite. Feeling is one channel; the border is what dulls it. Take the border away and everything comes through, including the hard things. Presence at full volume, not zero.
- **"Why would anyone want that?"** Ask what they'd do if nothing felt impossible and nothing needed defending. Then: that, all day, for other people. Not for the experience. The experience-seekers are the ones who plateau.
- **"How do you know it exists?"** I don't know it the way I know Paris exists. I know the direction, because every step toward it is measurable on this chart: options I see, how long a reaction lasts, when I last said sorry first. And I've met people further along, and a room settles when they walk in. Beyond that, I'd rather spend my life walking toward something I'm not sure of than sitting in a room I'm sure I hate.

## Words

Avoid: enlightenment (say once, then translate), awakening, consciousness, oneness, ego, the universe.
Use: border, fear, present, of use, nothing to defend.

The sentence that earns trust: **"I don't know if I'll get there. I know the direction, and I can show you my last step."**

# For someone who has NOT seen the talk

The two ideas get built in one breath each before the goal lands. No "the chart I showed".

## 30 seconds

People change along two lines. One is how much fear and tension you're carrying: braced, or let go. The other is how much you act in the world: things happen to you, or you make things happen. Almost everyone works on one of the two. The rare person who has both is what we mean by high agency: calm, active, present, nothing feels impossible. Most people would call that the finish line. I think it's the edge of the map. Because the whole map rests on one quiet assumption: that I'm a separate thing, and a separate thing has to protect itself and take what it needs. Everything on the map is that thing getting better at its job. My goal is to find out, first-hand, that the assumption was never true. Not to believe it. To see it, the way you see that the room is lit. Once you see it, there's nothing left to protect, so nothing left to tense against, and nothing you stand apart from. That's what the word "enlightenment" pointed at, before it got covered in incense.

## Two minutes

Make a fist. Hard. Now try to feel glad about anything. You can't. That's the first idea: tension narrows what you can feel and how many options you can see. Most people are braced like that all day, against nothing. It started as protection, and if you hold it long enough it starts to feel like your personality.

Second idea: agency. Some people, life happens to them. Others make things happen. Both are trainable, and they're independent of each other. You can be relaxed and passive: the beach type. You can be tense and hyperactive: the founder type. Almost nobody is released and active at the same time, because nobody teaches you to work on both. When you do meet someone like that, you know it. The room settles. Gandhi ran a country and still kept one day of silence every week.

Now the part that's mine. Notice what the fist did to you. You became a small, hard object in a world of other objects. I end here; everything past this point is other. That feeling is the root under both ideas. Tension is the body's version of it. Fear is the mind's version. Grabbing is what it makes us do. Release the tension and the edge softens: you lie back in the water and it holds you. Everyone has felt that for a moment, the zone, the muse, a gut feeling you actually followed. Everyone visits. Almost nobody lives there.

The best a person can be is to live there: fully active, fully released, present in whatever is in front of them. What I want is one step past that, and it isn't more of the same. It's the moment the small, defended self stops running the show. You stop believing you're part of everything and start seeing it, the way you see that a room is lit. The "illusions" people talk about are just the things fear drew: the border, the scarcity, the "I'll be happy when", the enemy. When the border goes, they go with it.

And you don't vanish. The people who lived closest to this acted more, not less, because nothing was in the way anymore. That's the reason I want it: to be useful, with nothing standing between me and whoever is in front of me.
