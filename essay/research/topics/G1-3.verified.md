## 1. The picture in brief

The three founders give Alex three different things and share one "no".

Guenon (1886 to 1951) gives the frame: one primordial Tradition, each religion with an outer and an inner face, the Infinite as Universal Possibility (so a whole must include manifestation), and a Self that was never individualised. He is also the school's hardest "no" on lifetimes. He held that the traditional doctrine is transmigration, a change of state, not the return of the same person, and wrote book-length attacks on Theosophy and Spiritism.

Coomaraswamy (1877 to 1947) gives the best checkable witness for the axiom: Shankara's line that the Lord is the only transmigrant, the pair Self and self, recollection, self-naughting. He read sources in their languages and did not collect quotations. He read Buddhism through Vedanta, which Theravada specialists reject.

Schuon (1907 to 1998) gives the argument for why forms differ and why a whole separates: the Absolute is also Infinite, the Infinite includes the appearance of limit, and that appearance is Maya. No sentence of his on rebirth or Theosophy has been read by anyone in this folder.

The two items flagged for settling stay unsettled by text; best-memory verdicts are in 2C. The school rejects the Western return of the same individual. All three require one tradition in practice, so none would endorse witnesses collected without lineage. Their rule that forms differ while the inner teaching agrees is Alex's correction of 2026-10-07.

## 4. How they compare: the method, concretely

Guenon starts from a principle and reads each tradition as saying it in other terms. Example: the cross as Universal Man, under four names. He tests terms by original language, regular lineage and metaphysical rather than psychological content, which is how the Theosophy book fails Blavatsky. Differences are adaptation to a people and an age, or decline.

Coomaraswamy compares sentences and symbols, not religions. He sets an Indian, a Greek and a German line side by side (smrti, anamnesis; two birds, two selves). He explains sameness by a common source and differences by audience and level, and he changed his mind on Buddhism between 1916 and 1943.

Schuon fixes a ladder first (Absolute, Infinite; Beyond-Being, Being, Existence), sorts each tradition into outer and inner, and compares like with like: Eckhart with Shankara and Ibn Arabi, never a catechism with the Gita. Differences are willed: each form is a window that must claim to be the only one. Example: the Shahada as discernment (nothing is real but the Real) then concentration on the Real, beside neti neti and identity with Atma [M].

The second generation start inside one form, state its doctrine in its own technical words, then name equivalents in a sentence or footnote.

Common risk: the inner layer is whatever agrees, so a disagreeing text can always be assigned to the outer layer. The comparer equates, not the traditions: Ein Sof with Brahman, Nirvana with the Absolute, Eckhart's uncreated spark with the Intellect (a proposition of that kind was condemned in 1329, in the bull In agro dominico of 27 March 1329 [M]).

## 5. What scholars say

Sedgwick, Against the Modern World (2004), is the standard history. He says Guenon made no pretence of Indological method and treated Hinduism as a repository of truth, not a changing body of belief [C]. Sylvain Levi rejected the 1921 thesis for ignoring history [C]. Legenhausen finds the comparisons tenuous and the selection prejudiced [C]. Meautis and Price call the Theosophy book selective [C]. Rahula objects to Coomaraswamy's anatta, and Conze compared the Pali attan with Samkhya's purusha, not the Upanishadic Atman [C]. Katz (1978) argues mystical experience is shaped by tradition; his target is the common-core thesis, not Schuon's metaphysical unity [M]. Insiders (Smith, Nasr, Oldmeadow, Shah-Kazemi) praise Schuon for showing how to stay loyal to one religion and understand others [M]. Allegations about Schuon's circle are in Sedgwick [M]. Fair summary: weak history of religions, coherent metaphysics, read for the metaphysics.
