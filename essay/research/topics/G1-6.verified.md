## 4. How they compare

The Traditionalists (Schuon, Guenon, Coomaraswamy): each religion has a necessarily different outer form and a shared inner core; differences come from audience and form, and outer claims are exclusive because a form must be. Schuon's Transcendent Unity of Religions (French 1948, English 1953 [M]) is the model, with pairs such as salvation (public) and deliverance (inner). The cost [U, checker K8]: the Church condemned some Eckhart propositions, among them the uncreated-something line, and the Traditionalists quote Eckhart often; which line a given Traditionalist relies on is not shown here.

The Hasidic and Sufi method is internal: one text read on two levels. Habad reads one creation from two perspectives; Ibn Arabi holds incomparability and similarity together; Ghazali sorts the veiled into classes. These are doctrines of perspective, not comparisons between religions.

Panikkar looks for homeomorphic equivalents and refuses a common essence; Griffiths and Abhishiktananda equate by structure; Merton compares experience first; Suzuki sets passages side by side. Differences are explained by language and culture, outer form against inner truth, the seeker's stage, fulfilment (the other path reaches what Christ reveals), or real doctrinal difference that experience may still cross.

## 5. What scholars say

Scholem read Hasidism as devekut (attachment), not union; Idel disputes this. On Habad, Elior's two-perspective reading is most cited. Ibn Taymiyya and the Salafi line reject the Akbarian reading; Chittick defends Ibn Arabi. The Mishkat's last section is disputed. McGinn places the mystics inside the Church's tradition while recording that it policed their language (Eriugena 1225, Porete 1310, Eckhart 1329). Katz (1978) argues that experience is shaped by tradition, so identity claims fail; Forman, Hick and Smith defend modest versions [M, not researched]. (Checker K9: "Smith" is not identified; if Huston Smith, the paper is "Is There a Perennial Philosophy?", JAAR 1987 [U]. Katz's essay is "Language, Epistemology, and Mysticism" in Mysticism and Philosophical Analysis, 1978 [M].)
