## 1. The picture in brief

China gives the best checked parallels in this slice, and they are Daoist and Confucian. Wang Yangming says nearly in so many words: one body, hidden by selfish desire, restored by removing the hiding, and when the hiding wins, kin kill kin. Zhuangzi 2, 3 and 6 give the one whole, the great awakening, death as transformation and sitting and forgetting. Early Daoism has no karma and no rebirth of a person. Daoist karma appears as inherited burden (Taiping jing) and spirit-audited retribution (Ganying pian), and rebirth arrives from Buddhism. S4's gap is real and has a history.

Iran splits. Zoroastrianism witnesses return (frashokereti) and a descent the souls chose (fravashi), but it has one life, no one whole and no letting go. The Gnostic stream is the closest match to all three clauses. Excerpta ex Theodoto 78.2 is a one-sentence version, the Gospel of Truth names forgetting and waking, and Corpus Hermeticum I tells a fall through love and a climb that sheds a fault at each of seven spheres, with an eighth beyond and the Father above it. (Checker, C11: the ninth is not in CH I.)

Sikh scripture gives the sharpest pairing for letting go (haumai against hukam), karma with grace (Japji 4) and stages (pauris 34 to 37). Jain texts give karma as matter and non-possession as attachment, and no one whole.

Primal and African sources give relational unity (Ubuntu, Lakota relatives), a destiny chosen before birth (ori, nkrabea) and rebirth into a lineage (atunwa, abiku, ogbanje, Tlingit, Inuit). They are not monism, and the comparer's filter is thick (Neihardt, Brown, Mbiti). Movement comparers are few: Traditionalists on Daoism and the Plains Indians, Theosophists on Gnostics and Zoroaster, Smith on primal religion, Corbin on Iran. I know none for Sikh, Jain, Yoruba or Akan material.

## 2. Findings

### China and East Asia

1. Zhuangzi 2, one with me and the great awakening. Claim: heaven and earth were born with me and the ten thousand things are one with me, and then the text doubts that "one" can be said (the one plus the saying of it makes two). Only after a great awakening do we learn this was a great dream, and the butterfly dream ends with the remark that there must be some distinction, called the transformation of things. Who: Zhuangzi (inner chapters, fourth century BCE), tr. Watson 1968, Graham 1981, Ziporyn 2009. Compared by: Smith (Tao at the Infinite level), Izutsu 1966 (structure only, beside Ibn Arabi), Guenon-line writers [M]. Scholars: Graham and Hansen read chapter 2 as perspectival scepticism, not a One. Tier [M]. Use: the draft's butterfly sentence is right; add the great awakening, and say the chapter doubts the One can be said. (Checker, C2: recall agrees; Chinese search handles are in the log; the draft already cites chapter 2.)

2. Zhuangzi 6 and 22, the four friends and the Great Clod. Claim: four friends join because each would take nothing as head, life as spine, death as rump, and knows life and death, being and loss as one body. One falls ill and twisted and admires the Creator-Transformer. The dying Lai says the Great Clod loads him with form, labours him with life and rests him with death, so what makes life good makes death good, and he tells his weeping family not to disturb the transformation. Chapter 22 says life is qi gathered and death qi dispersed, so the ten thousand things are one. Who: Watson, Graham. Tier [M]. Use: the best Daoist witness for letting go of death. Trap: the transformation recycles qi and no individual persists, so "rat's liver, insect's leg" is not transmigration, and the Daoist one is stuff, not consciousness.

3. Zhuangzi 4, 6 and 7, subtraction by stages. Claim: in 4 the heart-mind is fasted: unify the will, listen with qi, because the Way gathers in emptiness. In 6 Yan Hui forgets benevolence and righteousness, then ritual and music, then sits and forgets: drops limbs and body, dismisses perception and intellect, leaves form, casts off knowing, and joins the Great Thoroughfare. In 7 the perfect person uses the mind like a mirror and stores nothing. Compared by: Slingerland 2003, Effortless Action (the paradox of trying to be effortless). Tier [M]. Use: letting go as removal in stages fits Yoga Sutra 4.3 and Part one, and echoes minus four to zero.

4. Zhuangzi 3, fuel and fire, and Huiyuan. Claim: the end of chapter 3 says we can point to the fuel used up, while the fire is passed on and nobody knows where it ends. Huiyuan (334 to 416) used fire passing from fuel to fuel for the spirit passing from body to body (Hongming ji, fascicle 5 [U, pointer, C4]), and Fan Zhen (about 450 to about 515; treatise about 500) answered with a treatise on its perishing. Cited parallel: Nagasena's flame. Tier [M]. Use: the only classical Chinese flame image for continuity, in a Buddhist reading of a Daoist line; the Zhuangzi's own sense is probably the passing on of life or teaching.

5. Karma in Daoism: Taiping jing, Ganying pian, Lingbao. Claim: the Laozi and Zhuangzi have no karma. The Taiping jing (Han, about second century CE, a composite) teaches chengfu, inheritance and burden: faults pile up across generations, the living inherit them, and good conduct relieves them. The Ganying pian (twelfth century, ascribed to Laozi) says fortune and misfortune have no gate and people call them in, retribution follows like a shadow, spirits record deeds and subtract years, and descendants pay what is left. It also says to treat another's gain and loss as one's own. The Lingbao scriptures (fourth and fifth centuries) took rebirth and karma from Buddhism. Who: Hendrischke 1991, 2006; Bokenkamp 1997, 2007; Zurcher 1980; Brokaw 1991; Legge, SBE 40 (1891). Tier [M], locators [U]. Use: Daoist karma is inherited, collective and audited by officials, not oneness. The nearest to "done to another part of myself" is chengfu's shared ledger plus the Ganying golden rule.

6. Return in Laozi and inner alchemy. Claim: Laozi 16 returns to the root, which is stillness; 40 says return is the Way's movement; 42 says the Way gave birth to one, two, three and the ten thousand things. Neidan (Zhouyi cantong qi, second century, is the early text; the four-step formula is a later neidan codification, and Zhang Boduan's Wuzhen pian has a preface dated 1075 [C5]) reverses that sequence: refine essence into qi, qi into spirit, spirit into emptiness, emptiness back to the Way. Who: Robinet; Pregadio 2008; Wilhelm and Jung 1929. Compared by: Jung, Burckhardt 1960 (alchemy), Guenon. Tier [M]. Use: the best Chinese stage sequence ending in return. Trap: Jung reads it as psychology.

7. The Neo-Confucian one body. Claim: Cheng Hao (1032 to 1085, Yishu 2A) says the humane person forms one body with all things, and that medical books call numb limbs not humane. Zhang Zai (1020 to 1077, Western Inscription) calls heaven father, earth mother, all people siblings, but ends that in death he will be at peace, and teaches qi condensing and dispersing like ice in water, so no rebirth. Wang Yangming (1472 to 1529, Inquiry on the Great Learning, 1527) says the great person takes heaven, earth and things as one body from the humaneness of the mind, shown by the child at the well and by crying birds, broken plants and shattered tiles (alarm and compassion, cannot bear it, pity, regret or concern, in that order, as I recall [M, about 85 percent, C6]). The small person has the same mind but, hidden by selfish desire, harms things and even kills kin, and the one body is lost; the task is to remove the hiding. Compared by: Tu Weiming, Ivanhoe 1990, Ching 1976 (translation: Chan 1963); not the movements. Scholars: love is graded. Tier [M]. Use: the best non-Western witness for the fingers (numb limb, kin killing kin) and a veil lifted by removal.

8. Dogen. Claim: Genjokoan (1233, revised 1252): to study the Buddha way is to study the self, to study the self is to forget the self, to forget the self is to be verified by the ten thousand things, and body and mind of self and others drop off, leaving no trace of realisation. Bendowa rejects the Senni view that the body perishes and mind lasts. Who: tr. Tanahashi 1985. Compared by: Kyoto School (Nishitani, Abe) with Eckhart, not the movements. Tier [M], Bendowa locator [U] (question and answer section, about question 10, C7). Use: letting go as forgetting the self. Trap: Dogen denies an enduring soul, so he is no witness for a soul continuing between lives.

9. Shinran. Claim: Shinran (1173 to 1263; Tannisho, compiled by his disciple Yuien) says one cannot save oneself by calculation (hakarai) and must leave it to Amida's vow; if a good person is reborn in the Pure Land, how much more an evil one (Tannisho 3 [M]); this is naturalness (jinen). Tannisho 13: Shinran asks Yuien whether he would kill a thousand people, and says that with the right karmic conditions one might do anything, even kill a hundred or a thousand without wishing to [C8, corrected from "kill a hundred"]. After going (oso) the saved return (genso) to help others. Compared by: Barth and Otto (grace parallel) [M, titles U]. Tier [M]. Use: letting go as dropping one's own calculation, with karma and grace together as in Japji 4. His self-name, the foolish bald one, matches Alex joining himself into the mistake.

10. Wonhyo and Zongmi. Claim: Wonhyo (617 to 686) wrote on the Awakening of Faith and on reconciling doctrinal disputes (hwajaeng). One Mind has two gates, suchness and arising-and-ceasing; ignorance stirs waves on one sea; practice returns to the source of the One Mind and benefits beings. Zongmi (780 to 841, Inquiry into the Origin of Humanity) ranks Confucian, Daoist and Buddhist accounts of human origin and finds it in the One Mind. Critical Buddhism (Hakamaya, Matsumoto) calls the One Mind of the Awakening of Faith non-Buddhist, and the text is probably Chinese, about 550 [M]. Tier [M]. Use: the closest East Asian statement of the three clauses (P13 said so), and the return includes helping beings. Specialists dispute whether it is Buddhism.

### Iran and the Gnostic stream

11. Zoroastrianism: Yasna 30, Chinvat, frashokereti, fravashi. Claim: Yasna 30 (Gathas) has two primal spirits, twins, good and bad, and the wise choose between them. The Gathas mention a bridge of the separator, and Pahlavi texts have the soul meet its daena, a conscience-self, and cross a bridge that widens or narrows. At frashokereti ("making wonderful") a saviour raises the dead, all pass through molten metal, evil is undone and the world renewed. The Pahlavi Bundahishn says the fravashis were offered a choice, to stay unembodied or to enter the world, fight evil and be resurrected, and they chose to enter [M, locator unknown]. One life then judgement; Parsi Khshnoom teaches reincarnation [U]. Who: Boyce 1975 to 1991. Compared by: Plethon and Ficino (Zoroaster as oldest sage), Theosophists (Blavatsky; Besant 1897), Corbin; no Traditionalist file. Tier [M]. Use: a witness for return and a chosen descent, and a counterexample for one whole and letting go. Not for lives or karma.

12. Manichaeism. Claim: Mani (216 to about 274) taught two principles. Darkness swallowed part of Light (the Primal Man's armour); the Living Spirit calls and the Primal Man answers; the cosmos is a machine (sun and moon as ships) for freeing Light; the Elect refine light in their bodies, the Hearers feed them, and at the end the Light is gathered. Souls are Light, and harming plants harmed it (Augustine's jibe about weeping figs, Confessions 3.10.18). Transmigration for the unfinished is attested [U; checker's recall agrees, still U]. Mani presented his church as completing the messengers to India (Buddha), Persia (Zoroaster) and the West (Jesus), as al-Biruni reports [M]. Who: Kephalaia, Cologne Mani Codex, Turfan texts; BeDuhn 2000; Gardner and Lieu 2004. Tier [M]. Use: the earliest deliberate comparer and a fall-and-gathering myth. Differences: the fall is an attack, not purposeful play, and matter is evil.

13. Mandaeans. Claim: surviving Gnostics of Iraq and Iran. Texts: Ginza Rba, Qolasta, Book of John, Diwan Abatur. The soul is Light sent into a body to be tried, with a heavenly counterpart (dmuta); the rite of ascent (masiqta) takes it past watch-stations to the World of Light; baptism is in running water. Who: Drower 1937; Lupieri 2002; Buckley 2002. Compared by: Blavatsky (through the Codex Nazaraeus [U]), Mead 1924 (The Gnostic John the Baptizer). Tier [M]. Use: a living witness to descent and return of Light with a heavenly double. They hold their teaching exclusive, so they are no witness for universal oneness.

14. Valentinian texts, and Thomas. Claim: the Gospel of Truth (Nag Hammadi I and XII, probably Valentinian) says ignorance of the Father produced terror and Error, a fog; people live as in a nightmare and wake to find it was nothing. The Totality sought the one it came from and was inside him. Whoever has knowledge is called, hears, turns and ascends. Excerpta ex Theodoto 78.2 (the Valentinian source, Theodotus, preserved in Clement of Alexandria's notebook; not Clement's own view [C9]) says freedom is knowing who we were, what we became, where we were, where we were thrown, where we hasten, from what we are redeemed, what birth is and what rebirth is. Thomas 22 says make the two one; Thomas 70 says what you bring forth will save you; Thomas is not Valentinian [C9]. Who: tr. Layton 1987 (and Casey 1934, Sagnard 1948 for the Excerpta [U]). Compared by: Mead (the Valentinian fragments known before 1945), Jonas 1958, Pagels 1979. Tier [M], line numbers [U]. Use: Excerpta 78.2 is the best single sentence for the axiom's three clauses plus "birth". Trap: Valentinians sorted people into types, which the essay does not.

15. The heavenly twin cluster. Claim: in the Hymn of the Pearl (Acts of Thomas 108 to 113) the prince's robe of glory is his own image and speaks to him. Mani had a Twin (Syzygos); the Mandaeans have dmuta; Zoroastrians daena and fravashi; Suhrawardi the Perfect Nature and a tale of the soul's exile and return. Corbin gathers these as one theme (Spiritual Body and Celestial Earth, 1960). Wasserstrom (1999) calls Corbin's circle crypto-religious [U, wording, C10]. Tier [M]. Use: a bridge to "a soul that continues" with a heavenly Self, and to finding 25.

16. Corpus Hermeticum I (Poimandres). Claim: the Human, born in the Father's image, sees his form in Nature's water, loves it and is joined to her, so humans are mortal in body and immortal in the essential Human. After death the soul climbs through seven zones and gives back a fault at each (plotting, desire, arrogance, daring, greed, lies among them), enters the eighth with its own power and rises to the Father: the good end for those with knowledge is to be made god. Who: Copenhaver 1992 (I.12 to 26); Mead 1906 (public domain). Compared by: Ficino, Mead, Guenon (as Western initiation). Scholars: Festugiere (philosophical), Fowden 1986 (a religious way) [M]. Tier [M]. Use: the best parallel to the arrow: stages that shed faults, with an eighth beyond them and the Father above, like minus four to plus four and "birth". The ninth belongs to the Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth, not to CH I [C11]. The fall is by love, not punishment.

17. Corpus Hermeticum XIII, XI, VII. Claim: in XIII (Secret Sermon on the Mountain) Tat asks to be reborn; Hermes says the womb is wisdom in silence and the seed the true good. Twelve tormentors (ignorance, grief, desire and the rest) are driven out by ten powers, and Tat says he sees the universe and himself in Mind and is everywhere: heaven, earth, animals, plants, the womb, before and after birth. XI.20 says to know God one must become equal to God, grow immeasurably, leap out of every body and become eternity. Who: Copenhaver 1992. Tier [M], numbers [U]. Use: XI.20 is the nearest ancient text to Alex's zoom-out; XIII gives rebirth as a seeing in this life with a teacher, not a post-mortem birth.

### Sikh and Jain primary texts

18. Japji pauris 1 and 2: the wall, hukam, haumai. Claim: the Mul Mantar opens with Ik Onkar and ends with the Guru's grace (Guru Granth Sahib p. 1). Pauri 1 asks how to become true and how to break the wall of falsehood (kurr di pal), and answers: walk in the Command (hukam) written with you. Pauri 2 says all is within hukam and none beyond it, and one who understands hukam does not speak in ego (haumai). Who: Guru Nanak (1469 to 1539); tr. Macauliffe 1909, Shackle and Mandair 2005. Compared by: Wilson (World Scripture), Sant Mat teachers. Scholars: Mandair 2009 says "one God" for Ik Onkar imports colonial categories. Tier [M] (p. 1 sure; wording from memory). Use: the wall of falsehood is a good wall for Alex's walls; haumai against hukam is the Sikh name for the hidden separation, and the world itself is called true (Asa di Var, about p. 463 [U]).

19. Japji pauri 4, haumai, gurmukh and manmukh. Claim: pauri 4 (p. 2) says by karma we get the robe of the body, by grace the gate of liberation, and to know that the True One is all in all. Haumai is a chronic disease whose remedy lies within it (Asa di Var, about p. 466 [U]); Guru Amar Das says haumai and the Name cannot dwell together (about p. 560 [U]). The gurmukh faces the Word and lives in hukam; the manmukh faces the mind's own will and wanders in births. Liberation while alive (jivan mukti) is dying to haumai while living. Guru Arjan lists Ram, Khuda, Gosain and Allah as one God's names (Ramkali, about p. 885 [U]). Who: GGS; McLeod 1968. Tier [M]. Use: law and grace together (P16); gurmukh and manmukh map onto Part two's two views. (Checker, C13: recall agrees with all the [U] pages; they stay [U].)

20. Japji pauris 34 to 37: five realms. Claim: Dharam Khand (34) is the field of action, where ripe and unripe are judged; Gian Khand (35) counts winds, waters, fires, Indras, suns, moons and regions without end; Saram Khand (36) is where mind, intellect and understanding are shaped; Karam Khand (37) is the realm of grace, held by those filled with God, who neither die nor are deceived; Sach Khand (37) is where the Formless dwells, among continents, solar systems and universes (khand, mandal, varbhand) without end. Who: Guru Nanak; McLeod 1968. Tier [M], line wording [U]. Use: a stage scale in the tradition's own words, ending in a count of solar systems and universes that echoes the space-travel mapping. Sikh commentators read the realms as states; some Western readers as stages.

21. Jain texts: karma as matter, mutual support, non-possession. Claim: Tattvartha Sutra (Umasvati, 2nd to 5th century; both sects): right view, knowledge and conduct together are the path (1.1); souls support one another (5.21); bondage has five causes (wrong view, non-restraint, negligence, passions, activity); because of passions the soul takes in matter fit to be karma (chapter 8); eight kinds, of which four (knowledge-obscuring, perception-obscuring, deluding, obstructive) are the destructive ghatiya karmas that damage the soul's own qualities, and only the first veils knowledge [C14, corrected]; stop the influx and shed what is bound (chapters 6, 9, 10). Possession is attachment (7.17 in the Digambara numbering, 7.12 in the Svetambara [C14]); the Dasavaikalika 6 says the same (about verse 21 [U]). Who: tr. Tatia 1994. Compared by: neither movement. Tier [M], numbers [U]. Use: karma-as-matter is the opposite of "done to another part of myself"; the Jain link is that harm binds the harmer through the passions. Possession as attachment matches Part one's letting go.

22. Kundakunda and Haribhadra. Claim: Kundakunda (Samayasara) says that from the ultimate standpoint the soul is pure and never bound, which sounds like Advaita, while the conventional standpoint describes binding. Haribhadra (about eighth century; Shaddarshanasamuccaya) compared the systems and says he has no bias for Mahavira and no hostility to Kapila, and that a reasoned saying is to be accepted (Lokatattvanirnaya 1.38, as I recall [U, C15]; not the Shaddarshanasamuccaya). Tier [M]. Use: a Jain comparer who rules by reasoning, not loyalty. Souls stay many.

### Primal and African

23. Lakota, Black Elk, and the filter. Claim: mitakuye oyasin (all my relatives) closes Lakota prayer and names kinship with all beings. Black Elk Speaks (Neihardt 1932) shapes the interviews into a poem, and DeMallie's Sixth Grandfather (1984) prints the 1931 transcripts so the two can be compared; reports say Neihardt reworked the closing, which I recall as his own composition and not in the transcripts [U, C16]. The Sacred Pipe (Brown 1953) records the seven rites from Black Elk. Black Elk was a Catholic catechist (Steltenkamp 1993). Brown belonged to Schuon's circle, and Schuon wrote The Feathered Sun (1990) on the Plains Indians. Critics: Deloria (1973); the 1993 Lakota declaration against outsiders conducting ceremonies [M]. Tier [M]. Use: cite as relatives, not monism, and warn that Black Elk's famous lines come through two editors.

24. Ubuntu. Claim: the Nguni and Sotho proverb says a person is a person through other persons. Mbiti (1969) gave the English formula I am because we are, as I recall it, and Tutu (1999) says a person with ubuntu is diminished when others are. Menkiti (1984) says personhood is achieved in stages: a child is not yet a full person, and ancestors remain persons while remembered. Critics: Gyekye (moderate communitarianism), Metz 2007, Gade 2011 (the philosophical use is recent). Tier [M]. Use: the best non-Asian witness for "no separate me", and Menkiti is a stage scale.

25. Yoruba, Igbo and Akan: chosen destiny and return to the lineage. Claim: Yoruba tradition says each person chooses an ori (inner head, destiny) kneeling before the creator, and atunwa (coming again) returns ancestors into their lines (authors "Babatunde, Yetunde" unidentified, do not cite [U, C17]); abiku children are born to die. Igbo have ogbanje, bound by a token (iyi-uwa), and chi, a personal portion of the creator. Akan say the okra, a life-giving part of God, takes a destiny (nkrabea) before birth and returns to God. Who: Idowu 1962; Abimbola 1976; Achebe 1958; Gyekye 1987; Fortes 1959 (Tallensi prenatal destiny). Rebirth is within the lineage and tied to ancestors, not to moral progress. Tier [M], Akan rebirth [U]. Use: a pre-birth choice appears in Plato (Er) and in West Africa. Critic: Okot p'Bitek 1970 says Mbiti and Idowu Hellenised African gods.

26. Tlingit and Inuit rebirth. Claim: Tlingit say the dead are reborn into the same matrilineal clan, with birthmarks, dreams and names marking who has returned (Stevenson 1966; Mills and Slobodin 1994). Inuit give a newborn the dead person's name (atiq), which carries part of the person (Guemple 1965 [U]); Rasmussen 1929 records sila, air and breath that is also mind. Tier [M], sources [U]. Use: belief witnesses only; do not cite Stevenson as proof.

27. Aboriginal Dreaming. Claim: Stanner (a 1953 paper, printed 1956 in Australian Signpost [U, C18]; White Man Got No Dreaming, 1979) fixed "the Dreaming" and said it is everywhen; Strehlow disputed the older "Dreamtime" gloss. Spirit-children enter mothers at sites, tying each person to ancestral country (Spencer and Gillen 1899). Rose 1996: country is living kin. Eliade used it as primordial religion. Tier [M]. Use: the Dreaming is the real, so it is the wrong witness for "illusion", though it could witness a relational, sited whole.

## 4. How they compare

Matching terms (geyi). Third and fourth century Chinese Buddhists explained nirvana and emptiness with Daoist words (wu, Dao), and Dao'an later criticised the practice. Sengzhao carried Zhuangzi's line into Buddhist prose, rewording it as heaven and earth sharing one root with me and the ten thousand things being one body with me; the "same root" wording is Sengzhao's, not Zhuangzi's, and Chan texts quote it [C20b; Zhao lun and the Blue Cliff Record case 40 are pointers, U]. This is Alex's method, and it shows both use and cost.

Ranking (panjiao). Zongmi graded Confucian, Daoist and Buddhist accounts of human origin and put the One Mind on top. Schuon's outer and inner levels work the same way: equate at the top, rank below.

Reconciling (hwajaeng). Wonhyo argued that disputes come from partial views, each right from its position; the Ming slogan of three teachings in one (Lin Zhao'en) did the same [M].

Founder's formula. Mani said one message had come through Buddha, Zoroaster and Jesus and that he completed it; Chinese Manichaeans wrote it in Buddhist words.

Names against realisation. Guru Arjan lists Ram and Khuda and Allah and says the difference is knowing hukam. Haribhadra accepts what is reasoned, whoever says it.

Structure and symbol. Brown and Schuon compare symbols, Corbin figures (the twin), Izutsu structures, Eliade patterns.

Anthology by theme. Perry (A Treasury of Traditional Wisdom, 1971, a Perennialist anthology) and Wilson (World Scripture, 1991, made by an affiliate of the Unification movement [M]) set passages side by side by topic, and explain differences as outer form, local language or stage of the writer. [C20a: the colleague had "Smith (1991)"; Smith's 1991 book is a survey, and his theory book is Forgotten Truth, 1976.]

## 5. What scholars say

Africanists object that comparers fit African ideas into Christian or Greek frames (Okot p'Bitek 1970; Mudimbe 1988 on the "African gnosis" built by a colonial library) [M]. Dogon cosmology rests on Griaule's informant (van Beek 1991), and the Sirius claim is discredited [M].

Native studies scholars say "all my relations" names obligations among distinct beings (Deloria; Rose; Hallowell) and is not a monism [M]. Sikh scholars reject "Hindu-Muslim synthesis", and McLeod's method drew protest [M].

Gnosticism as a category is questioned (Williams 1996; King 2003), and Yates's Hermetic thesis was revised. Jonathan Z. Smith says similarity is the scholar's construction and each comparison needs a stated purpose and a stated difference [M, wording U].
