## 1. The picture in brief

Nobody has tested the sentence as written, because no lab manipulation is called "letting go of the tension that holds the sense of separation". What exists is five neighbouring bodies of work.

Meditation trials test a practice that overlaps with letting go. They find small prosocial effects, clearest against doing nothing, weaker against an active control, and, in the one meta-analysis that checked, close to nothing when the teacher was not also an author. Studies of mystical-type experience test the wall thinning for a few hours and find lasting self-reported and observer-reported change, including in prosocial attitudes, in small, selected, hard-to-blind samples. Self-other overlap research shows felt oneness predicts helping, mostly of close others, and the causal reading is disputed. Awe studies show a small-self induction can raise generosity, with uncertain replication, and acute shared stress can also raise giving, which blocks the lazy version "tension always reduces giving". Older helping research (hurry, personal distress, felt security) gives the cleanest support for the mechanism: less distress and more felt safety make people attend to someone else and help.

Verdict: a fair hypothesis with indirect support and two real qualifications. Effects are small and depend on who runs the study and on the control group. And some loosening (less guilt, an independent self-view) lowered reparation and giving in the lab. The teachers' order has no direct research counterpart; the nearest is Eisenberg's sequence from regulation to sympathy to helping. The essay's "it is small" about Cialdini is not something I can confirm: the dispute is about interpretation, not size.

### B. Mystical-type experience and lasting change

6. Pahnke 1966 and Doblin 1991.
Pahnke and Richards, "Implications of LSD and experimental mysticism", Journal of Religion and Health 5, 1966, from Pahnke's 1963 Harvard thesis (title as I recall it: Drugs and Mysticism: An Analysis of the Relationship between Psychedelic Drugs and the Mystical Consciousness) [M]. Griffiths, Richards, McCann and Jesse, "Psilocybin can occasion mystical-type experiences having substantial and sustained personal meaning and spiritual significance", Psychopharmacology 187, 2006 [M]. 36 hallucinogen-naive adults interested in spirituality, double-blind crossover, psilocybin against methylphenidate [M gist; N U]. ### D. The small self and generosity

15. Piff et al. 2015, with Bai 2017 and Yaden 2016.
Piff, Dietze, Feinberg, Stancato and Keltner, "Awe, the small self, and prosocial behavior", JPSP 108(6) [M]. Five studies [U]: dispositional awe predicted generosity in an economic game; a minute looking up at tall eucalyptus trees rather than a building led people to pick up more spilled pens; awe videos raised generosity and ethical choices; a feeling-small measure mediated [M gist]. Qualifier: a transient induction is not habitual tension, though "small self" is the mediator the essay's border needs. Use: pair with the arrow's space picture.

17. Older helping work: hurry, distress, felt security, mood.
Darley and Batson 1973, "From Jerusalem to Jericho", JPSP 27(1) [M]: about 40 seminary students sent across campus to give a talk; those told they were late helped a slumped man far less than those with time (about 10 percent against about 63, U), and the talk's topic, the Good Samaritan or not, made no reliable difference [M gist]. Fits: mild pressure removes helping, and being told what to value does not restore it ("without anyone telling them to be"). Criticism: small, one setting, situationist over-reading [U]. Batson, O'Quin, Fultz, Vanderplas and Isen 1983 (JPSP 45): people high in personal distress helped less when escape was easy; empathic people helped either way [M gist]. Eisenberg and Fabes 1990 (Motivation and Emotion 14): better emotion regulation goes with sympathy rather than personal distress, and with helping [M gist]. [Verification pass: tier lowered to [U] for the citation. As I recall, the 1990 paper reviews how empathy is conceptualised and measured and how it relates to prosocial behaviour; the regulation-to-sympathy model is better cited from Eisenberg, Fabes and colleagues 1994 (JPSP 66) or Eisenberg 2000 (Annual Review of Psychology 51).] Mikulincer, Shaver, Gillath and Nitzberg 2005, "Attachment, caregiving, and altruism: boosting attachment security increases compassion and helping", JPSP 89(5) [M]: priming felt security raised compassion and willingness to help, partly by lowering personal distress [M gist; N U]. ### E. Order of change, markers, comparers

18. The order: listening, giving, clearer perception.
I know no study that tracked this order. Nearest pieces. (a) Eisenberg's sequence, regulation then sympathy then helping [M; a concept, not a longitudinal meditator study]; it matches "attention leaves the self and lands on someone else". (b) The ReSource Project (Singer and colleagues, Leipzig), nine months in modules ordered presence, affect, perspective [M gist]; the order is designed, not observed. (c) Vago and Silbersweig 2012, the S-ART framework (self-awareness, self-regulation, self-transcendence) [M]; a theory. (d) The Theravada ten fetters: the stream-enterer drops self-view, doubt and ritual clinging; ill-will weakens at the second stage and goes at the third [M]. That is a tradition's order, self-view first, ill-will later, quotable as the tradition's claim and not as evidence. (e) Ego-development stage research (Loevinger, Cook-Greuter), with reports from a Transcendental Meditation group, so an allegiance caution [U]. For "clearer perception": Mascaro and colleagues 2013 (SCAN), compassion training raised empathic accuracy, small N [M gist]; Kang, Gray and Dovidio 2014, six weeks of loving-kindness lowered implicit bias against outgroups versus a discussion control [M gist]; Desbordes and colleagues 2012, training changed amygdala response to emotional pictures [M gist]. ## 4. How they compare

By instrument. Stace listed traits he found in mystics of several traditions. Pahnke made the list a questionnaire, Hood a scale, Griffiths' group the MEQ. A score measures how near a report is to Stace's list. Cross-cultural checks show the scale works across languages, not that the experiences are one.

By fruits. James judged states by what they did to a life; Pahnke's last category and Griffiths' friend-and-family ratings put numbers on it; Smith and Zaehner ask whether a drug experience is a lived mysticism.

By method. Wilber's three strands make the claim checkable by anyone who does the practice; what is compared is what the practice shows, not what the texts say.

By borrowed practice. Loving-kindness from metta, compassion courses from Tibetan mind training, MBSR from Zen and vipassana, stripped of doctrine and tested by function, which sidesteps whether the doctrines match.

Differences are explained four ways. Stace: same core, different interpretation. Zaehner: different experiences, different moral and theistic quality. Katz: concepts shape experience from the start, so no pure core. Scientists: dose, setting, expectation. Alex's "different words, same things" sits with Stace against Katz on the experience question, and nearer Schuon's form-versus-essence (outer forms differ, inner teaching agrees) on the question of the texts [my inference, U]. Katz's attack bites on claims about experiences; the essay's claim is about what the traditions say, which is a more modest claim, and the essay can say so. Other explanations of difference in the comparers' literature, as sibling P12 lists them [M]: form versus essence (Schuon); a filter (Hick); level of reality (Huxley, Smith, Wilber); distortion of an original teaching (Blavatsky); real and part of the experience (Katz); different ultimates enacted together (Ferrer).

## 5. What scholars say

Katz (1978), Proudfoot (1985), Sharf (1995) and Taves (2009) hold that concept shapes experience and the unity core is partly a modern Western construction; Forman (1990) replies on experience [M]. Hick (1989) is a pluralist who accepts that each tradition shapes experience and still argues for one Real; he is a neighbour of the perennial view and not a defender of a common core of experience [M; correction taken from sibling P08, same kind of evidence]. Psychology of religion treats the MEQ and Hood scales as reliable but Western-built [M]. Meditation research has its own correctives: Van Dam, Kreplin, and Sharf's "Is mindfulness Buddhist?" (2015) [M]. Psychedelic trials are criticised for functional unblinding, expectancy and researcher allegiance [M gist]. On helping, the field mostly accepts that empathy leads to helping and disputes why [M].

Best for what, for the job of "what you can check":
- The claim as an argument and not an anthology: Frithjof Schuon, The Transcendent Unity of Religions (French 1948, English 1953): outer forms differ, the inner centre agrees. This is the clearest statement of the distinction Alex was asked to respect, the public face of a tradition against its inner teaching. Note that it is 1948, three years after Huxley, though the school is older.
- The founder and the line-by-line comparers: Rene Guenon (Introduction generale a l'etude des doctrines hindoues, 1921; Man and His Becoming According to the Vedanta, 1925) and Ananda K. Coomaraswamy (Hinduism and Buddhism, 1943; Time and Eternity, 1947), the best source for checked parallels such as Eckhart beside the Upanishads.
- A fair one-volume guide and an advocate in one author: Huston Smith, The World's Religions (first The Religions of Man, 1958) for the traditions' public faces and Forgotten Truth (1976) for the shared map. His levels are levels of worlds and not stages of growth.
- Inner voices of Islam and Buddhism said from inside the school: Martin Lings, What Is Sufism? (1975); Titus Burckhardt, An Introduction to Sufi Doctrine; Marco Pallis, A Buddhist Spectrum (1980). Seyyed Hossein Nasr, Knowledge and the Sacred (1981), for the case against the modern worldview. Whitall Perry, A Treasury of Traditional Wisdom (1971), the school's own large anthology and counterpart to Huxley's.
- The scholar's version of "the mystics agree", and early comparers outside every school: W. T. Stace (1960), William James (1902), Evelyn Underhill, Mysticism (1911), Rudolf Otto, Mysticism East and West (German 1926, English 1932).
- The method for checking, closest to this slice: Ken Wilber, Eye to Eye (1983): injunction, apprehension, confirmation. Wilber is a synthesiser whom specialists criticise, so cite him for the method and as a map and not as a witness.
- Theosophy: Blavatsky, The Key to Theosophy (1889, the plain handbook), The Secret Doctrine (1888) and The Voice of the Silence (1889), with the first object of the Society as the comparative aim. Campbell (1980) and Godwin (1994), already in the essay's list, are the scholarly guides.
- The honest critics, to name once: Steven Katz (1978), Robert Forman (1990, the reply), Jorge Ferrer (2002), Richard King (1999), Mark Sedgwick, Against the Modern World (2004) on the Traditionalists, Wouter Hanegraaff on Western esotericism.

Cautions to carry:
- The movement is a family and not one doctrine. The Traditionalists (Guenon, Coomaraswamy, Schuon and the people after them) were hostile to Theosophy (Guenon wrote against it in 1921 and 1923) and reject the modern Western form of reincarnation. Alex's pairing of "the perennial philosophy and Theosophy as the two comparers" is therefore his own, and should be presented as his [M; siblings P09 and P10 report the same].
- Do not credit one author with another's view. Stace's "common core of experience" is an academic, experience-based claim; Schuon's "transcendent unity" is a metaphysical one that places the unity in the Intellect and its object, not in experience; Huxley's is an anthology arranged by theme; Wilber's adds stages. Katz's 1978 attack was aimed at the Stace-type claim [M].
- Serious allegations about the Schuon circle are reported in sibling P04. I could not check them and repeat nothing here; read P04 before Schuon is quoted at length.
- Huxley traps, in case he is used: his Foreword wrongly says Leibniz coined "philosophia perennis" (Steuco used it in 1540; Leibniz wrote it in a letter of 26 August 1714); the "doors of perception" line is Blake's, via Huxley's title; the "reducing valve" idea in The Doors of Perception is taken from C. D. Broad and ultimately Bergson, as Huxley says [M]. His three-part definition of the perennial philosophy should be checked against the printed Foreword before it is quoted.
- Use for the empirical record in this slice: when the essay says the traditions describe the same ideas in different words, pair that claim with Schuon's form-versus-essence idea and Stace's interpretation-versus-experience split, and say that the lab instruments assume Stace's list, so they cannot count as independent support for it (finding 9).
