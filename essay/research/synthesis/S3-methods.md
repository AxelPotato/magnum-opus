Scope, after Alex's correction. The perennial philosophy movement is much wider than Huxley. Huxley appears once, as one anthologist beside Perry, Stace, Wilson and others. Two comparing families run through the methods: the Traditionalists (Guénon from 1921, Coomaraswamy, Schuon from 1948, then Burckhardt, Lings, Pallis, Nasr, Perry, Smith) and the Theosophists (Blavatsky and after). ## 1. The thematic anthology

Who. Huxley, The Perennial Philosophy (1945), chapters by theme, not by tradition [M]. Perry, A Treasury of Traditional Wisdom (1971), far larger, from the Schuon circle, and sorted by level, which Huxley's is not [M, the sorting U]. Stace, The Teachings of the Mystics (1960); Wilson, World Scripture (1991); Novak (1994); Needleman (ed.), The Sword of Gnosis (1974); Eliade, From Primitives to Zen (1967) [all M]. On the Theosophical side, Walker (1888) and Head and Cranston (1961, 1977) on rebirth [M].

Example. Huxley's chapters are themes: That Art Thou, Charity, Mortification, Non-Attachment, Right Livelihood, Time and Eternity, Immortality and Survival [M for titles, U for numbers]. The most useful worked case for Part two is the files' own reconstruction of a row: heading "the other is oneself", passages Isha Upanishad 6 and 7, Gita 6.29, Leviticus 19:18, Matthew 22:39, Shantideva (Bodhicaryavatara 8), the hadith of the believers as one body [M for the texts, U for Shantideva's verse numbers]. What the row hides: Leviticus and Matthew ground love in a shared status under God, Shantideva in the lack of a fixed self and the ownerlessness of suffering, the Isha in identity. The draft already says most rest on reciprocity or empathy.

Strength. Fast and readable. It shows that wording can be set side by side across many traditions. Huxley chose people who report first hand, and not theologians [M, list U].

Weakness. The headings belong to the anthologist, each excerpt was picked for fit, context is cut, and the translation often carries the equation already [M]. Huxley read translations of his day. For Eckhart the usual source behind such anthologies is Pfeiffer's 1857 edition, which mixes authentic sermons with texts later judged not Eckhart's [M for the problem, U for which translation Huxley used]. Textual scholars add that an Eckhart line read alone sounds like Advaita, and read in its Dominican sermon belongs to a Christian theology of the soul [M]. Inside the movement, Schuon rejects the arithmetic of a common factor, since for him the unity lies above the forms and is not found by overlap [M]. Huxley's Foreword phrase is recalled as "Highest Common Factor in all preceding and subsequent theologies" [M, recalled]. His other move, that knowledge is a function of the knower's being, makes the claim hard to refute [M]. The objection is the files' own, not a named scholar's.

Borrow. Alex already does this in his table. He differs from Huxley as the files stress: Huxley subtracts to a common factor, Alex derives from an axiom and then shows examples [M]. Honest use: say the headings are his; take each passage from a named primary translation and not through an anthology; add the "what the row hides" line wherever the grounds differ; state his selection rule, which the draft already promises (mystics, closely read scripture or different vocabulary, and say which). Keep Huxley's three-clause definition as a paraphrase until the Foreword is open ("substantial to" in his wording, "underlying" in the draft) [M].

## 2. The parallel-passage method

Who. Coomaraswamy, Otto, and for the Traditionalists Shah-Kazemi. Guénon states the doctrine first in Sanskrit terms and reads the rest as saying it in other words [M].

Example. Coomaraswamy's paper "On the One and Only Transmigrant" (Journal of the American Oriental Society, Supplement 3, 1944, printed with "Recollection, Indian and Platonic") opens on Shankara, Brahma Sutra Bhashya 1.1.5, rendered "Verily, there is no other transmigrant but the Lord" [C for the wording, M for the locator]. What else the paper cites is [U]. His unit is one sentence or symbol followed through Sanskrit, Greek, Latin and German, with a short comment [M]. Otto, West-östliche Mystik (1926; English 1932), sets Eckhart beside Shankara term by term: Shankara : Brahman : Ishvara : Atman as Eckhart : Godhead : God : Soul [C for the formula]. Otto also records differences, such as a dynamic Godhead against a static Absolute [U].

Strength. A reader can open the line and check it. It matches Part two's habit of one consequence at a time. Otto is the model of a comparer who lists the differences.

Weakness. One voice for three thousand years, tilted to Advaita [M, recalled as the standard complaint]. His reading of Buddhism moves between 1916 and 1943, though the 1916 "transmigration of character" line may quote an older formula and not be his own [C, M]. Rahula named him and Horner as smuggling a self into the Buddha's teaching [C gist, which text U]. Theravada commentary reads brahma-bhuta as "become holy", not "become Brahma" [M]. His school refuses syncretism and would not accept Alex's witness method [M].

Borrow. Quote one line at a time from a named translation. Put the comparer in the sentence: "Otto sets Eckhart's Godhead beside Shankara's nirguna Brahman", not "they are the same". Compare inner voice with inner voice (Eckhart with Shankara and Ibn Arabi, as Shah-Kazemi does), never a catechism beside the Gita [M]. If Coomaraswamy answers "who carries over", state the price: the line says the individual is not what carries over [M].

## 3. The glossary equation of terms

Who. Dara Shikoh, Majma al-Bahrayn (c. 1655) and Sirr-i Akbar (1657) [C]. Blavatsky's Key (1889) and the Theosophical Glossary (1892, posthumous, ed. Mead); Purucker's Occult Glossary (1933) [M]. Schuon's grid [M]. Guénon on Universal Man [C]. Subba Row's "Aryan-Arhat" essay [M for the title]. Faivre names this practice "concordance" and calls it a frequent but secondary mark of Western esotericism [M].

Example, working. Dara set the five elements against the Hindu panch bhut, Brahma, Vishnu and Mahesh against the angels Jibril, Mika'il and Israfil, and called the Upanishads the "hidden book" (kitab al-maknun) of the Quran [C]. His jamal and jalal against three gunas is [U], and incomplete. Example, strained. Blavatsky read the Tibetan bde-ba-can as Sanskrit deva plus chan, "dwelling of the gods", and equated devachan with Sukhavati. The Tibetan renders Sukhavati, a place of practice reached by Amitabha's vow, not a rest between lives [M for direction, U for spellings]. Alaya as Universal Soul, Anima Mundi and Over-Soul is the same move, though alaya-vijnana is an individual stream carrying seeds [M].

Strength. It shows which words do the same job. Subba Row gave it an honest form: the count of principles differs between schemes because each cuts the same thing at different joints [M for the argument, U for details].

Weakness. The comparer picks the match, and the equation is made before the reader sees a quotation [M]. Dara's Vedanta came out in Sufi words, and Supriya Gandhi (2020) reads his translation choices as pushing Advaita-Sufi compatibility [C gist]. Specialists keep apart Ein Sof and Brahman (Scholem, Idel), emptiness and Brahman (Loy), buddhi and "spiritual soul" (Subba Row himself) [M]. The glossaries lean on nineteenth-century Orientalists and many etymologies are fanciful [U].

Borrow. Keep the table, as the draft's atman-and-Brahman, emptiness, karma-that-veils sentence does, and label each row as a comparer's equation. Panikkar's term for the honest version is homeomorphic equivalents, terms that play a similar role in different systems without being identical [M]. Loy's rule: give each sense of a shared word its own name [M]. Write "Schuon equates nirvana with the Absolute", keeping the essay's own line that emptiness means no fixed independent nature [M].

## 4. Symbols and myths

Who. Blavatsky (egg, serpent, lotus, tree, cross, "keys") [M gist, U particulars]. Guénon, The Symbolism of the Cross (1931), Universal Man [C summary]. Coomaraswamy, the Sun door and Figures of Speech or Figures of Thought (1946) [C for titles, M for content]. Campbell, The Hero with a Thousand Faces (1949); Jung's amplification; Eliade's morphology; Mead on the Poimandres [M].

Example. The veil of Isis. Blavatsky's title plays on the inscription at Sais. Plutarch (On Isis and Osiris, about section 9) puts it on a statue of Athena whom the Egyptians identify with Isis, and writes "robe" (peplos), not "veil". "Veil" comes later: Kant quotes it in a footnote in section 49 of the Critique of Judgment, and Proclus gives a longer form [M, locators U]. A symbol is edited as it travels. A second case: Coomaraswamy's Sun door, with Mundaka 1.2.11 (the pure go through the door of the sun), John 10:9 ("I am the door") and the clashing rocks [M for the idea, U for verse numbers].

Strength. A symbol carries an idea without a doctrine. Alex's machine is of this kind, and he says it is a picture.

Weakness. Nearly any story can be read through a key, so the method cannot be tested [M]. J. Z. Smith: similarity is made by the comparer, and a comparison is never a plain "A is like B" but has two things and a respect [M for the gist, U for page and words]. Campbell's "return" in the monomyth is a return to the ordinary world and not to the One. The match to the third clause lies in his Part II, the cosmogonic cycle [M].

Borrow. Use images as pictures and say whose image each is. Give the origin and check the wording ("robe", not "veil", if Plutarch is used). Keep Campbell's two scales apart. Use the Sun door as a witness for Alex's door only if he says that the chart's door is not the traditional exit from the cosmos [M].

## 5. The exoteric and esoteric split

Who. Guénon, Introduction générale (1921), with chapters on esoterism and exoterism [C from a listing]. Schuon, The Transcendent Unity of Religions (French 1948, English 1953), with the Sufi triad sharia, tariqa, haqiqa as template [M]. Blavatsky: an outer form for the many, an inner meaning for the few, the "Eye Doctrine" and "Heart Doctrine" [M]. Besant, Esoteric Christianity (1901) [M]. Smith, with The World's Religions as the public face and Forgotten Truth as the inner [M].

Example. Schuon on exclusivism: the outer layer must present itself as the one truth, because that is what makes a form work for a whole society, and the inner layer sees the outer as a symbol [M]. His pair salvation and deliverance: Paradise is the public face, union the inner [M]. The cost, recorded in the files: the Church condemned the Eckhart proposition about something uncreated in the soul, in the bull In agro dominico of 27 March 1329, after his death [M, article number U].

Strength. It gives a reason why popular teaching may not carry the idea. That is Alex's own rule of 2026-10-07 in source form.

Weakness. It cannot fail: a text that disagrees is assigned to the outer layer, and the esoteric is defined as whatever agrees with the metaphysics [M]. The files say the same of the Theosophical version, which adds priestly corruption and deliberate blinds [M]. Schuon's grid is Advaita and Sufi theory laid over other traditions, and Scholem and Idel do not use it [M].

Borrow. Credit Schuon and the Traditionalists for the rule, not only Huxley, and say it is a theory that some scholars reject [M]. Use it as a reading rule with a stop. For each quote name the layer (scripture, mystic, popular teaching) and say where the popular layer says something else. Do not rescue a disagreeing text by moving it outward. Write a specialist's objection as what the specialist adds, and not as a verdict that the tradition fits badly.

## 6. The primordial tradition, or comparison by descent

Who. Plethon (1438), Ficino's prisca theologia (Argumentum to the Pimander, 1463), Steuco, De perenni philosophia (1540) [C]. Guénon's primordial Tradition; Blavatsky's Wisdom-Religion; Smith, whose title phrase is Guénon's [M].

Example. Blavatsky's Introductory calls the Secret Doctrine the universally diffused religion of the ancient and prehistoric world, with proofs in "secret crypts of libraries" kept by the Occult Brotherhood [M, about 80 percent on the wording, not for printing]. Steuco: one principle of all things and one knowledge of it among all peoples [C, English from summaries]. Both explain agreement as inheritance and difference as loss of keys, literal reading or priestcraft.

Strength. It supplies a mechanism, and an old one.

Weakness. The historical claim failed. Casaubon redated the Hermetica in 1614 [C]. No one has found the Book of Dzyan or any Senzar [M]. Coleman (printed 1895) traced much of her text to printed books [M]. Most historical comparers had a home team: Ficino, Cusa and Steuco put Christianity on top, and Dara read Vedanta in Sufi words [C, M].

Borrow. Do not. Say in one sentence that he claims no original revelation and no lost source. His explanation of agreement is a fourth kind (common logic) beside revelation, transmission and common experience [M]. If he names the primordial claim, say that Smith's phrase is Guénon's. Add that he has no team.

## 7. Stage maps

Who. Underhill, Mysticism (1911), five stages; Teresa's seven mansions; Climacus's thirty steps; the Jain fourteen gunasthanas; Attar's valleys; Kukai's ten stages; Besant and Leadbeater's initiations; Smith's four levels; Wilber [M].

Example, working. The cleanest term equation in the files is dharma-megha, the tenth bhumi in Buddhism and the last samadhi in Yoga Sutra IV.29 [M]. Example, strained. Besant and Leadbeater map the four Buddhist stages (sotapanna, sakadagami, anagami, arhat) onto the first four of five initiations, with Christian labels (Birth, Baptism, Transfiguration, Crucifixion, Resurrection) [M, Hindu labels U]. In the Pali texts an arahant already is an asekha, so their split of the two is an addition [M]. Their "Birth" is the first initiation, not Alex's stage after plus four.

Strength. A shared shape of distance words (mansions, valleys, heavens). The draft's line that other traditions draw staged maps too is safe [M].

Weakness. Rungs run from four to a hundred. Sevens may come from the planets, but that fits Hermes, Mithras, Dante and not Teresa, the Jain fourteen or the Buddhist ten [M]. Katz would say maps may match the writers' vocabulary and Ferrer that one ladder assumes one summit [M]. Krishnamurti, raised as World Teacher, dissolved the Order at Ommen on 3 August 1929 and said truth is a pathless land [M for the date, words not for printing, whether "stage" is in the address U]. The strict Traditionalists read history as decline, and Smith's levels are worlds, not stages [M].

Borrow. Say the number of rungs varies, and map by function and not by count. Claim no one-for-one match with Underhill or Teresa. Do not write "no other scale is centred on a door": nine-step and threshold-centred scales exist, though none is numbered symmetrically about zero [M]. Say the arrow is for a person and makes no claim that the world improves, which answers the strict school [M].

## 8. Each religion as a perspective on one Reality

Who. Schuon (Absolute and Infinite; Beyond-Being, Being, Existence), Guénon (Universal Possibility), Smith (four levels), Hick (the Real in itself, met through personal and impersonal faces), Schmidt-Leukel [M].

Example. Schuon fixes the level before equating. Nirguna Brahman, Dhat and Eckhart's Gottheit sit at Beyond-Being; Ishvara, Allah as Lord and God the Father at Being [M, individual pairs U, and "Allah" is also used for the Essence]. The Shahada read as discernment (nothing real but the Real) then concentration on the Real is set beside neti neti then identity with Atma [M]. Why the forms differ: the Infinite includes every possibility, receptivity differs, and a form that stressed its own relativity would no longer save anyone [M, a reconstruction, not Schuon's numbered list].

Strength. It explains "sometimes different gods" without calling the gods the same. It also offers an argument for why a whole would separate: an infinite that excluded the finite would be limited [M].

Weakness. The grid is circular and tilted to Advaita and Sufism [M]. Buddhist specialists keep nirvana and the Absolute apart [M]. Scholem and Idel, and Orthodox and Catholic theologians, keep a line between Source and many that the comparers erase [M]. Plantinga and D'Costa say Hick's pluralism is itself a doctrine [M]. The Traditionalists rest the claim on doctrine and the Intellect, not experience, so Katz's attack mostly misses them [M].

Borrow. One sentence: "one comparer reads the personal God and the impersonal Absolute as two degrees of one thing, and Buddhists and many theologians would not accept the equation" [M]. Offer "the Infinite includes the finite" as an argument credited to the movement and labelled an argument, not a proof.

## 9. The claimed esoteric revelation

Who. The Theosophical Society (1875): the Masters; the Mahatma Letters (about 1880 to 1884, published 1923, Barker; Hao Chin 1993); the Book of Dzyan and its Stanzas; Leadbeater's clairvoyant reports; Bailey writing for "the Tibetan" [M].

Example. The three propositions of the Secret Doctrine Proem (one boundless Principle; universes that appear and vanish in cycles; the identity of all souls with the Over-Soul and a pilgrimage under karmic law) are given as what she was taught, not as logic [M, wording recalled, not for quotation]. "Over-Soul" is Emerson's word, and in her third proposition it is "an aspect of the Unknown Root", a softer step than Alex's axiom [M for the structure, U for the exact wording]. The authority rule: where a text and a Master disagree, the Master wins [M]. Her Preface calls the book a nosegay with only the string her own, but the line is Montaigne's (Essais III.12), which she quotes [M].

Strength. It answers why the traditions agree. It was an early, bold act of respect for Asian texts, and Gandhi's Autobiography says the Key sent him back to his own scriptures [M gist, chapter U].

Weakness. It cannot be checked. The SPR's Hodgson report (1885) judged her phenomena fraudulent, and Harrison, "J'Accuse", Journal of the SPR 53 (1986), pp. 286 to 310, challenged it in the SPR's own journal [M]. Whether the SPR ever withdrew the report is unchecked [U]. Müller wrote against "Esoteric Buddhism" in The Nineteenth Century (1893) [M, date details U]. The stock reply is the Buddha's "closed fist of a teacher" (acariyamutthi) in Digha Nikaya 16, and the paragraph number differs by translator [M]. Isis Unveiled (1877) did not teach reincarnation as the rule. The rule is in Sinnett's Esoteric Buddhism (1883), the Key (1889) and the Secret Doctrine [M]. That bears on the draft sentence naming Isis as a main route for karma and reincarnation, which is too wide for Isis.

Borrow. Not the method. Borrow the stance of gathering what lies scattered and saying what is one's own. Olcott's Fourteen Fundamental Buddhist Beliefs (accepted 1891 by authorities of several schools, signatories U, drafted earlier, 1884 U) is the one Theosophical comparison shown to the people compared [M, U]. The contrast sentence is ready: her reason for agreement is a lost original and hidden teachers, and his is an axiom and the reader's own check. Hammer names three strategies in this literature (appeal to tradition, to science, to experience), and Alex's method is none of them [M].

## 10. Comparison by experience

Who. James, The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902); Underhill (1911); Bucke (1901); Otto; Stace, Mysticism and Philosophy and The Teachings of the Mystics (both 1960). The foils are Zaehner (1957), Smart (1965), Katz (1978) and Forman (1990).

Example. Stace's two types: extrovertive (the unifying vision, all things One, seen through the senses) and introvertive (unitary consciousness emptied of content). Both share marks of objectivity, blessedness, holiness, paradoxicality and ineffability, and interpretation added afterwards explains the differences [M]. James gives four marks (ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, passivity) and the "eternal unanimity" passage, in Lectures XVI and XVII, exact lecture U [M]. His screen is "the filmiest of screens", not "the thinnest" [M, recalled]. His three conclusions: such states are authoritative for the one who has them, they give outsiders no duty to accept them, and they break the claim of the sense-bound mind to be the only kind [M].

Strength. James, Underhill and Bucke are public domain. James's second conclusion is the best authority for "not a proof", and his test by fruits supports "what you can check".

Weakness. Katz: "There are NO pure (i.e. unmediated) experiences", p. 26 of his essay [M, recalled, keep the parenthesis]. Proudfoot and Sharf call "experience" a modern category [M]. Zaehner: nature, monistic and theistic mysticism differ and cannot be merged [M]. Stace's core is called circular, since cases that do not fit are called not mystical [M]. Forman answers Katz with contentless events [M]. The strict Traditionalists say the criterion is doctrine and intellectual intuition, not experience [M].

Borrow. Say the claim is about texts and ideas, with "reports" and not "the same experience". A family of ideas survives Katz at the level of structure, which is all Alex claims [M]. Use James's limit on outsiders as his permission.

## More methods the files show

Dialogue and negotiation. Cusa's De pace fidei (1453), "una religio in rituum varietate" [C, word order U]; Akbar's Ibadat Khana (1575) [M]; the Parliament of 1893 [C]. The yield was peace and policy, rarely a finding of sameness [M].

Practice. Ramakrishna's lake, Gandhi's common prayer [M]. Schuon sets the Name side by side: japa, dhikr, the Jesus Prayer, the nembutsu [M]. Yoga Sutra 1.12 joins practice and letting go in one line [M]. Sameness is lived before it is argued, which suits Part three.

Structure without identity. Izutsu, Sufism and Taoism (1966); Sells (1994); Loy, Nonduality (1988) [M]. They set shapes side by side and say where they diverge.

Ranking. Zongmi, Kukai, Vivekananda and the Baha'i scheme put their own tradition on top; Hacker called Hindu tolerance inclusivism [M]. Gita 4.11, 7.21 and 9.23 are inclusivist, so do not write that the Gita says all religions are equal [M]. Every comparer has a top, and Alex's is his axiom.

Why the traditions differ is also the comparer's claim: rites differ (Cusa); words differ (Pico, Dara); stage of education (Lessing, Leibniz); corruption (Plethon, Steuco, Blavatsky); paths up one mountain (Ramakrishna, Coomaraswamy); outer and inner (Guénon, Schuon); levels (Smith); temperament (Huxley, Hodson); perspective (Schuon); filter (Hick) [C, M]. Alex needs none of them. He can say the same few ideas turn up in different vocabularies and decline to say why.

## Practical guide: wording equations that stay true to each tradition

1. Name the respect. Write "both say the sense of a separate self is built and can end", never "they teach the same thing" [M].
2. Claim texts and ideas, not feelings. Say "reports", "a family of ideas", "keep turning up". The draft's own wording is right [M].
3. Give the tradition's own term first, then the gloss, then its job. Maya means dependent, neither fully real nor simply unreal, and "illusion" translates it badly [M]. Blavatsky's own gloss makes maya what changes and ends, close to mithya [M].
4. Attribute every equation to its maker. "Schuon sets nirvana beside the Absolute and many Buddhist scholars keep them apart." "Blavatsky equated devachan with Sukhavati, and the Tibetan name renders Sukhavati" [M]. Say "the teaching later called wahdat al-wujud", since the phrase is not in Ibn Arabi's own works as far as the files know [M].
5. Match inner with inner at the same level, and say which layer each quote comes from. Never rescue a disagreeing text by moving it outward.
6. Say where the grounds differ under similar words. For the golden rule: reciprocity (Hillel, Analects 15.24, Matthew 7:12), analogy (Gita 6.32, Sutta Nipata 705), identity (Isha 6 and 7, Gita 13.28) [M]. Shantideva reaches compassion by the ownerlessness of suffering, and the Acharanga line reads as oneness only in one modern commentary, while Jacobi's translation is more careful [M].
7. Keep each tradition's own account of what continues. Buddhism: no fixed self and yet something continues (the lamp-flame in the Milindapanha). Advaita: the one Self is the only transmigrant, and the individual does not carry over. Judaism: gilgul, which Saadia rejected. Islam: growth after death without return (Mulla Sadra) and tanasukh mostly rejected. Christianity: one life, purification, then epektasis [M]. Say "rebirth" or "transmigration", since "reincarnation" carries Spiritist freight [M].
8. Use each tradition's own relation word. Christianity keeps participation and not identity. Chabad is "close to acosmism in the Tanya's reading", which is not "the world does not exist" [M, U]. In Judaism separation is a rupture to repair. Jainism agrees on the practice and objects to the One, since souls are many [M]. The Traditionalists reject Theosophical return, though Schuon's own word on Theosophy is unchecked [M, U].
9. Quote only from a named translation. Do not take lines from memory, an anthology or a quote site. Traps in the files: "Truth is one, the wise call it by many names" (Rigveda 1.164.46 is about names of gods, and Gandhi and Vivekananda quoted it); loose "Rumi" lines (use Nicholson or a named translator); Eckhart on "thank you" (unsourced [U]); "He who knows himself knows his Lord" and "I was a hidden treasure" (Sufi sayings, not accepted as hadith); "There is no religion higher than truth" (the Benares family motto, not Blavatsky's); "heresy of separateness" (unplaced [U]); "Over-Soul" (Emerson's); "doors of perception" (Blake's) [M, C].
10. Write an objection as what a specialist adds, and never as "this tradition fits badly". Never claim a tradition says what it does not. The draft's habit is the model.
11. Say once that every comparer had a home team and a top, and that Alex has no team and his top is his axiom [M].
12. Say once that the movement is plural. For a sentence in the draft: the perennial philosophy is a family of books and schools, not one doctrine (Guénon from 1921, Coomaraswamy, Schuon 1948, Perry's anthology 1971, Smith 1976); Huxley (1945) made the phrase familiar to English readers; the authors disagree with each other on rebirth, method and Theosophy; Alex uses them as witnesses and not as a team [M]. If he thinks Huxley is not the best, that is his view and should read as his.
