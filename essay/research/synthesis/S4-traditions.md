# S4 tradition by tradition: what the perennialists and the Theosophists cite, equate and say

Synthesis of P01 to P18 and T01 to T09, read against essay/part-2-spiritual.md and part-2-notes.md. Repository untouched.

## 0. How to read this

Status. No file in the research set was confirmed by a search in its verification pass, because the search budget was spent and every fetch was blocked. So nothing below is verified. "Best citation" means the best attested and most useful pointer, with its tier, and not a checked quotation. Tiers: [C] a colleague's first-pass search result that nobody re-saw (first-pass [V] tags are carried as [C]); [S] gist supported, wording from memory; [M] memory held with confidence; [U] memory held with doubt, not for the essay until checked. A claim built from several parts takes its weakest tier. No recalled wording is cleared for printing: open the text and name the translation. P15 and P17 have no checked copy, so where I lean on either alone I say so.

Cast. Traditionalists: Guenon (Introduction, 1921), Coomaraswamy, Schuon (French 1948, English 1953), then Burckhardt, Lings, Pallis, Schaya, Nasr, Perry (1971), Smith (1976) and Shah-Kazemi (2006) [M for titles and years unless a file marks them]. Theosophists: Blavatsky (Isis 1877, Secret Doctrine 1888, Key and Voice 1889, Glossary 1892 edited by Mead), Sinnett, Subba Row, Olcott, Judge, Besant, Leadbeater, Mead, Purucker. Huxley (1945) is one anthologist among these and appears only where he equates something. "Best" about any author is Alex's judgement, and no file ranks the books.

Habits that run through every tradition below. The Traditionalists fix a ladder first (Absolute, Being, Existence) and compare inner layer with inner layer: Eckhart beside Shankara and Ibn Arabi, never the catechism beside the Gita [M]. Unity lies above the forms, so outer forms may contradict. They require one regular tradition and initiation, which Alex's many witnesses do not [M]. The Theosophists claim one archaic Wisdom-Religion. For them agreement is descent, difference is corruption, literal reading and lost keys, and equations are tabulated (the Glossary, the seven principles) [M]. Every comparer also has a top: Dara reads Vedanta in Sufi words, Vivekananda puts Advaita highest, Kukai and Zongmi their own Buddhism, the Baha'i scheme the latest revelation [M, P13]. The two movements disliked each other. Guenon wrote Le Theosophisme (1921) and L'Erreur spirite (1923) against Theosophy [C], while sharing its idea of one primordial teaching [C, Smoley in Quest]. Coomaraswamy joined the Society about 1907 and left [C, year U]. Whether Schuon named Theosophy himself is unchecked [U].

## 1. Hinduism: Vedanta, Upanishads, Gita

Who cites it. Everybody. Guenon treats Hindu doctrine as the oldest and most complete and states each doctrine first in Sanskrit terms, then reads the other traditions as saying it in other words (Introduction 1921, Man and His Becoming 1925) [C for the books, M for the method]. Coomaraswamy builds on the Self and the self and on the one transmigrant [C]. Schuon uses Atma and Maya and the pair Absolute and Infinite (Logic and Transcendence 1970, Survey of Metaphysics and Esoterism 1986) [M]. Smith adds four levels of reality and of self [M]. The Theosophists use Parabrahm, Mulaprakriti, Maya, the Over-Soul (Emerson's word, taken over) and the Puranic cycles [M]. Subba Row, a Madras Advaitin, gave the Gita lectures (Krishna as Logos, Mulaprakriti as the veil of Parabrahman) [M, year U].

Texts and figures. Chandogya 6.8.7 (tat tvam asi), 6.2.1 to 3 (may I be many) and 6.9 to 10 (creatures merged in Being do not know it, then return). Brihadaranyaka 1.4.1 to 4 (the Self alone, afraid, wishing for a second, divides in two), 1.4.10, 3.7 and 4.4.3 to 5. Mundaka 2.1.1 and 3.1.1, Isha 6 and 7. Gita 2.22, 4.11, 6.29 to 32, 6.45, 13.28, 15.7, 18.66, and 13.16 (13.17 in some editions). Brahma Sutra 2.1.33, Gaudapada Karika 2.32, Yoga Sutras 1.12 and 4.3 [M all]. Rigveda 1.164.46, the oldest many-names line, is about the names of gods, and the slogan "truth is one, the wise call it by many names" is a loose English form [M].

Terms they equate. One: Brahman and Atman, nirguna and saguna Brahman (Schuon's Beyond-Being and Being), Parabrahm for the Theosophists [M]. Veil: maya, avidya. Blavatsky's Glossary defines Maya as whatever changes and ends, closer to the Advaita mithya (dependent) than to "unreal" [M]. Return: moksha and jivanmukti. Guenon reads it as realizing a Supreme Identity that was always so, with nothing returning, and Schuon agrees [M]. Lives: Guenon says transmigration means change of state, not return to the earthly state [C, locator unknown]. Coomaraswamy says the one Self is the only transmigrant. Theosophy says the reincarnating Ego returns and the personality does not, reading the Upanishadic "thread" as a thread-soul. In Brihadaranyaka 3.7 the thread (sutra) is the wind, and sutratman is later Vedanta [M]. Gita 7.7 (gems strung on a thread) is the better image [M]. Karma: action, and "as one acts so one becomes" (Brihadaranyaka 4.4.5) [M]. Guenon's line that karma "means action and nothing else" [C] overstates, since consequence is classical [M]. Letting go: Gita 2.47, 3.27, 18.66, and Yoga Sutras 1.12 (practice with dispassion) and 4.3 (remove the barrier, as a farmer opens a channel) [M].

Relation to the others. Guenon puts it first. Hindu inclusivism (Gita 4.11, 7.21, 9.23) lets other worship reach Krishna imperfectly, which is not "all religions are equal" [M, Hacker, P13]. Dara's Majma al-Bahrayn (about 1655) set Sufi and Upanishadic terms side by side, with the Upanishads as the hidden book of the Quran [C, P01].

Where the two movements differ. Rebirth: Theosophy teaches it as the rule, Guenon calls it a modern error, Coomaraswamy gives a third answer [C]. The seven principles of man are Theosophy's own count and no Hindu text has them. Subba Row dissented from Blavatsky and Sinnett on the number of principles and on whether Atma is a principle of a person [M that the quarrel existed, U on the points].

Best citation. Coomaraswamy's opening dictum, from Shankara: "Verily, there is no other transmigrant but the Lord" (Brahma Sutra Bhashya 1.1.5, in JAOS Supplement 3, 1944) [C, a catalogue and advaita-vision.org result; the locator comes from P03 and P05 and nobody re-saw it]. For why the whole divides, Brihadaranyaka 1.4.1 to 4 [M] is the closest text to "knowing needs a gap". For the axiom in Theosophy, the third proposition of the Secret Doctrine Proem: all souls identical with the Universal Over-Soul, itself an aspect of the Unknown Root, and an obligatory pilgrimage through the cycle of incarnation under cyclic and karmic law [M for the gist, U for the recalled wording, which is one memory repeated in several files].

Specialist objections. Advaita is one school among several, and "advaita" means not-two, which is not "one" [M]. Gita 6.41 to 45 already shows growth across lives, so the Theosophical ladder is not purely a Western import, though its evolutionary frame is [M, T06]. Most Indologists read the rebirth verses in the ordinary sense, against Guenon [M].

## 2. Buddhism: Theravada, Mahayana, Tibetan, Zen

Who cites it, Traditionalists. Schuon equates nirvana with the Absolute and sunyata with Beyond-Being, reads anatta as denying only the empirical ego, and gives Buddhism its own revelation and orthodoxy, with Pure Land set beside Christian grace (In the Tracks of Buddhism, English 1968) [M]. Coomaraswamy (Hinduism and Buddhism, 1943) argues that the Buddha works by elimination, as the Upanishads do, and denies the aggregates and not the Self [S]. His 1916 phrase "transmigration of character" may be quoted from Rhys Davids, and he later changed his view [M]. Guenon in 1921 called Buddhism a heterodox deviation [C], which I recall he read as a Kshatriya reaction against Hindu authority [M]. After Coomaraswamy, Pallis and Schuon pressed him he authorized amendments and in 1946 reviewed Hinduism and Buddhism as important [C]. Pallis, a practitioner, defends rebirth without a fixed self and the bardo (A Buddhist Spectrum, 1980) [M]. Huxley equates Dharmakaya and suchness with the Ground [M].

Who cites it, Theosophists. This is Blavatsky's Buddhism, and it is a different thing. Olcott's Buddhist Catechism (1881) and the Fourteen Propositions (accepted 1891, signatories U) are the one comparison shown to the people compared [M]. Sinnett's Esoteric Buddhism (1883) gave sevenfold man, kama-loca, devachan and the rounds [M]. The Key (1889) says the Society is not Buddhist and that no-self denies the personal soul and not the higher individuality [M]. The Voice of the Silence (1889) offers a Heart Doctrine against an Eye Doctrine, a "Book of the Golden Precepts" that nobody has found, the Two Paths, and Seven Portals, which are the six paramitas with viraga added [M]. She equates devachan with bde-ba-can, the Tibetan rendering of Sukhavati, which is a Pure Land to practise in and not a rest between lives [M]. Paranirvana reads Hindu pralaya into a Buddhist word, and her Nirmanakaya is an adept who stays, where the Buddhist nirmanakaya is a Buddha's visible emanation body [M].

Texts the comparers cite. Udana 6.4 (blind men and the elephant), whose original point is against clinging to views [M]. Udana 8.3 (the unborn, unmade, unconditioned). Samyutta 22.59 and 22.89 (not-self, the faint scent of "I am"). Samyutta 44.10 (the silence about a self) [U]. Anguttara 6.63 (intention is karma). Majjhima 22 (the raft), Majjhima 118 and the Sallatha Sutta. The Milindapanha lamp. The Awakening of Faith (One Mind, wind on water), the Heart Sutra. Shantideva: born into the family of the Buddhas (Bodhicaryavatara 3.25) and limbs and one body (8.91 in Padmakara), both numbering [U]. Dogen's Genjokoan, the ox-herding pictures, Mumonkan 2 (Hyakujo's fox). The Bardo Thodol, whose English title and framing come from Evans-Wentz (1927) and his Theosophical background [M, Lopez 2011].

Terms they equate. One: Dharmakaya, suchness, the Void, the Unconditioned, One Mind, Buddha-nature. Every one is the comparer's equation. Critical Buddhism calls Buddha-nature thought non-Buddhist, Nagarjuna denies any intrinsic nature including a One, and "not two" is not "one" [M, P12]. The notes also list SN 12.48, where the Buddha sets "everything is a oneness" among four extremes [U, notes only]. Veil: avidya, the wind that stirs the waves [M]. Return: original awakening, the bodhisattva who stays, the tenth ox picture [M]. Lives: rebirth with no fixed soul, the flame passed from lamp to lamp. Theravada rejected a state between lives and the Sarvastivada accepted one (Kathavatthu VIII.2 [U]). Karma: intention, and Samyutta 36.21 says not all suffering is karma [M]. Theosophy reads the skandhas as karmic germs [M]. Letting go is the strongest match with Part one: Samyutta 22.59, Majjhima 22 and 118, Dogen, and the Zen fox as the tradition's own warning against cold release [M].

Where the two movements differ. Both read anatta as denying only the small self. The Traditionalists then say the Buddha means the Self of Vedanta, while Theosophy says a higher individuality reincarnates [M]. Guenon's revision of his early verdict is the clearest case of a comparer correcting himself. Max Muller ("Esoteric Buddhism", Nineteenth Century, 1893 [M]) and Rhys Davids [U] criticized Blavatsky's Buddhism on its own ground, and the standard reply is DN 16, where the Buddha tells Ananda he has no closed fist of a teacher (acariyamutthi) [M]. Dharmapala, an Asian recruit, left Theosophy over its Masters and its mixing [M, date U].

Best citation. Against the secret-doctrine claim, DN 16, Mahaparinibbana Sutta, the closed fist [M, numbering differs by translator, Walshe 2.25 is U]. For the unconditioned, Udana 8.3 [M]. For the Traditionalist reading, Coomaraswamy's line that it is only by a process of elimination that the thought is reached that the Subject is No-thing [C, as printed in a mailing-list post quoting him, lower trust, source book U].

Specialist objections. Rahula says some "smuggle the idea of atta" into the Buddha's teaching and names Coomaraswamy and Horner [C, target text U]. Collins, Gombrich and the Pali mainstream read early Buddhism as denying any abiding self [M]. Theravada has no ground. Nattier argues the Heart Sutra Sanskrit is a back-translation from Xuanzang's Chinese of 649 [M]. Lopez and Prothero show Theosophical Buddhism as a Victorian composite [M]. Write "Schuon equates" and "she reads", never "Buddhism teaches".

## 3. Jainism

Who cites it. The files hold no Traditionalist book and no Theosophical text that uses Jainism as a witness. That is a finding. Those who do use it are anthologists, ethic-comparers and stage-map writers: Wilson's World Scripture [M], the 1993 Global Ethic endnote (Sutrakritanga I.11.33) [M], and Jaini (1979) on the gunasthanas [M]. The draft uses it for the veil, staged growth, non-harm and samayika.

Texts. Tattvartha Sutra 5.21 (parasparopagraho jivanam, souls serve one another), chapter 8 (bondage by karma), 10.5 to 8 (the freed soul rises to the edge of the cosmos like a flame or a gourd freed of clay; numbering differs by recension [U]). Acharanga 1.4.1 and 1.5.5, Sutrakritanga 1.11.33 [M, numbers U]. The fourteen gunasthanas, where a soul at stage 11 can fall back [M]. Anekantavada (Siddhasena, Haribhadra, Mallisena) [M].

Terms. Veil: karma is fine matter that obscures the soul's own knowledge, so the perfection was there all along [M]. One: none. Souls are many, eternal and uncreated, and Jain "maya" means deceit and not cosmic illusion [M]. Return: the siddha, still one of many. Lives: rebirth until the karma is shed. Karma: eight kinds of matter bound by the passions. Letting go: samayika, kayotsarga, the vow of non-possession, the verse "I forgive all beings, may all beings forgive me" [M].

Relation to the others, and the two movements. Anekanta gave the elephant an Indian philosophical frame [M]. Jains also hold that only their path liberates, and Shankara's commentary attacks the doctrine [M, P13]. Neither movement uses Jainism, so nothing separates them here. The one plain statement is that Jainism agrees on the practice and objects to the One [P15, unchecked, M at best]. The notes add that the Sutrakritanga refutes a single soul and that some souls are said never to be liberated [M].

Best citation. Tattvartha Sutra 5.21 [M]. The Acharanga line about the one you intend to strike has two renderings: Jacobi's is careful, and one modern commentary reads it as the oneness of killer and killed [M, notes and P17]. The draft already says both.

## 4. Judaism: Kabbalah and Hasidism

Who cites it. Schaya (L'homme et l'absolu selon la Kabbale, 1958 [U year]; English, The Universal Meaning of the Kabbalah, 1971) equates Ein Sof with the Absolute of other traditions, Adam Kadmon with Universal Man and Purusha, and Shekhinah with the Sufi sakina [M as the school's trope, U as Schaya's text]. Guenon calls Kabbalah genuine esoterism but wrote little on it [C]. Schuon gives Judaism little space [U]. The Theosophists equate Ain Soph with Parabrahm and the upper sephiroth with the Trimurti and the Trinity [M], through Knorr von Rosenroth, Franck and Eliphas Levi [M]. They cite gilgul as a witness for rebirth, and Purucker treats the Zohar as a form of the same tradition [U]. Blavatsky also treats Jehovah as a lower power, which breaks the rule against saying a tradition fits badly [M gist, T02].

Texts and figures. The Zohar line that the Torah, the Holy One and Israel are one (III 73a) [U wording]. Luria's three moves: tzimtzum, the breaking of the vessels, tikkun [M]. Adam ha-Rishon contains all souls, a different figure from Adam Kadmon [M]. Gilgul in the Bahir, the Zohar and Vital's Sha'ar ha-Gilgulim, with Saadia Gaon (933) against it [M]. Cordovero's panentheism [U formula]. Shneur Zalman's Tanya (1796), close to acosmism in its reading, which scholars debate [U]. The Maggid of Mezritch on ayin and bittul, and Nachman (Likutei Moharan II:48, the world a narrow bridge, have no fear at all) [M strongly, paraphrase]. Tamid 32a, Hillel at Shabbat 31a, and the rabbinic argument over Leviticus 19:18 and Genesis 5:1 (Sifra Kedoshim 4:12, Genesis Rabbah 24:7) [M, locators medium].

Terms they equate. One: Ein Sof, an equation Scholem and Idel resist because Ein Sof stands in a dynamic, even theurgic, relation to the sefirot [M]. Veil and separation: tzimtzum and the broken vessels. In Judaism this is a real rupture to repair, not an illusion [M, notes]. Return: teshuvah, tikkun, devekut. Scholem held devekut is attachment and Idel holds it can be union [M]. Lives: gilgul. Karma: measure for measure. Letting go: bittul, hishtavut, Tamid 32a.

Relation to the others, and the two movements. Schuon reads Kabbalah as the inner side of the Law [M]. The unity ethic is mostly about Jewish souls [notes]. Theosophy reads Kabbalah as part of its Wisdom-Religion and uses it for rebirth. The Traditionalists read it through the exoteric and esoteric grid, and whether Schaya treats gilgul is unknown [U].

Best citation. Luria's story, because it carries all three clauses of the axiom in one tradition's words (withdrawal for a world, shattering, repair) [M]. For the fingers, Akiva against Ben Azzai, the oldest version of the split between reciprocity and common descent, with Rabbi Tanhuma's one-line identity argument [M, locators medium]. Nachman's bridge [M strongly].

Specialist objections. Scholem and Idel treat Kabbalah as a historical development and do not use the grid [M]. Habad's own teaching distinguishes a lower and a higher unity and does not deny the world, so do not write "Chabad says the world does not exist" [M, P06].

## 5. Christianity: Eastern Fathers, Eckhart, the mystics

Who cites it. Coomaraswamy treats Eckhart as the clearest Western voice for the doctrine of the Self and pairs Mark 8:34 and John 12:25 with the Vedic sacrifice in "Self-Naughting" and "Atmayajna" (titles [C], contents M). P03 removed the line "the Upanishad of Europe" as unconfirmed. The formula Shankara : Brahman : Ishvara : Atman :: Eckhart : Godhead : God : Soul is Otto's (West-oestliche Mystik, 1926; English 1932), not Coomaraswamy's [C]. Schuon makes God and Godhead the Christian form of saguna and nirguna and reads the Trinity as symbols of Reality's structure [M, mapping U]. Shah-Kazemi sets Shankara, Ibn Arabi and Eckhart side by side [M]. Guenon held that the sacraments had sunk to the exoteric level. Schuon rejected that, and by one summary it was a central point of their split of 1948 to 1950 [C]. Huxley uses Eckhart as the Western hinge, in a line that may come through Pfeiffer's 1857 edition, which mixes authentic and doubtful sermons [M]. Theosophists: Besant's Esoteric Christianity (1901) reads the gospel life as five initiations and the Atonement as the Logos limiting himself, after Kingsford and Maitland's The Perfect Way (1881 or 1882 [U]) [M]. Mead's Fragments of a Faith Forgotten (1900) argues the Gnostics were the thinking half of early Christianity [M]. Blavatsky says Church doctrine was borrowed from older systems and that she is not against Christ [M]. Judge and Besant cite Origen, Matthew 11:14 with 17:10 to 13, and the Council of 553 [M].

Texts and figures. 1 Corinthians 15:28 and 12:12 to 27, Galatians 6:7 and 2:20, Ephesians 4:18, Philippians 2:5 to 8, Acts 9:4, John 17:21 and 3:3, Luke 15:17, Mark 8:34 [M]. Luke 17:21 can be "within you" or "among you", so it cannot carry the inner-Self reading alone [M]. Athanasius (De Incarnatione 54.3), Gregory of Nyssa's epektasis, Maximus (Ambigua 41), Evagrius on apatheia, Eriugena's Periphyseon, the Jesus Prayer [M]. Eckhart: the Godhead beyond God, "rid me of God" (Sermon 52), the eye sermon, the uncreated something, On Detachment [M, sermon numbers differ by Quint, Walshe and McGinn, so cite by opening words]. Theologia Germanica (Adam's fall as claiming "I, me, mine"), Boehme's Ungrund, John of the Cross, Teresa (Interior Castle, written 1577, printed 1588, seven mansions) [M].

Terms they equate. One: the Godhead (Gottheit), the ground of the soul, Eriugena's nature that neither creates nor is created. Veil: the Fall as self-will, ignorance in Ephesians 4:18. Return: apokatastasis, theosis, the birth of God in the soul, and after the birth the breakthrough into the Godhead (Sermon 52) [M]. Lives: here the tradition says no. It has purgatory, Origen's pre-existence and final restoration (Origen argued against transmigration), and Gregory's never-ending epektasis. Karma: Galatians 6:7 is accountability under God and has no rebirth in it, as the draft says. Letting go: kenosis, Gelassenheit and Abgeschiedenheit, nada, apatheia [M].

Relation to the others. The Church policed the strongest language: Eriugena (1225), Porete (1310), Eckhart (bull In agro dominico, 27 March 1329, after his death) [M, notes]. Fourth Lateran canon 2 (1215) says any likeness between Creator and creature hides a greater unlikeness [M]. Orthodoxy keeps participation and not identity (Palamas) [M]. The notes judge this tradition strongest on return, the one body and letting go, and weakest on a whole that experiences separation as its own self-experience.

Where the two movements differ. Guenon called Besant's version pseudo-Christianity or neo-Christianity [C]. The claim that the Council of 553 condemned reincarnation is Theosophical, and historians reject it: the anathemas concern pre-existence and the restoration of all [M]. Do not cite Jerome's Letter 130 as approval, since the anthologists seem to print a hostile passage as support [U].

Best citation. 1 Corinthians 15:28 [M]. Eckhart's saying that the eye with which he sees God is the eye with which God sees him is genuine [M], and the sermon number (12, "Qui audit me") is [U]. The better-attested condemned proposition is the uncreated something in the soul [M]. Quote Eckhart only from a critical edition, because of the Pfeiffer problem [M].

Specialist objections. McGinn and Turner read the Godhead as the Trinity's ground and union as by grace [M]. Flasch reads Eckhart inside Dominican and Neoplatonic theology [M]. Schuon's claim that the Intellect is uncreated is exactly the point the 1329 bull condemned [M].

## 6. Islam: Sufism, Ibn Arabi, Mulla Sadra

Who cites it. Guenon lived as a Muslim in Cairo from 1930 to 1951 and took the name Abd al-Wahid Yahya [C, initiation date U]. He finds Universal Man (al-Insan al-Kamil) under other names: Purusha, Adam Kadmon, the Chinese Wang [C]. Schuon wrote Understanding Islam (French 1961, English 1963), reads the Shahada as discernment followed by concentration, and uses sharia, tariqa and haqiqa as the template for every tradition [M]. Burckhardt (Introduction to Sufi Doctrine), Lings (The Book of Certainty, 1952), Nasr and Shah-Kazemi give the Sufi doctrine of the Essence, the Names and self-disclosure [M]. Dara Shikoh is an early systematic comparer [C]. The Theosophists use Islam little. The files show only Rumi's mineral-to-man lines in rebirth anthologies [U that Theosophists cite them] and the "shells" of kama-loka set against the spirit hypothesis [M].

Texts and figures. Quran 2:115, 2:156, 7:172, 57:3 to 4, 59:19, 89:27 to 30, 5:48, 17:7, 99:7 to 8, 4:1, 23:99 to 100. Hadith: voluntary devotion (Bukhari 6502), the believers as one body (Bukhari 6011, Muslim 2586), loving for the brother what one loves for oneself (Bukhari 13, Muslim 45) [M]. Two Sufi sayings are not accepted by hadith scholars: "I was a hidden treasure" (Ibn Arabi calls it sound by unveiling, not by transmission) and "he who knows himself knows his Lord" [M]. Ibn Arabi's "He and not He", the Names needing someone to be named to, creation renewed at each instant so that no self-disclosure repeats, and Tarjuman al-Ashwaq ode 11 [M]. Mulla Sadra's substantial motion and Four Journeys [M]. Rumi's grapes and Moses and the shepherd, Attar's thirty birds, fana and baqa [M].

Terms they equate. One: al-Haqq, the Essence (Dhat). The phrase wahdat al-wujud is a later label from Ibn Arabi's school and his critics, not clearly his own, so say "the teaching later called wahdat al-wujud" [M]. Veil: hijab, tajalli, ghafla (forgetting). Schuon links "He and not He" to maya, real and not it [M]. Return: fana, baqa, "to Him we return", 89:27 to 30. Lives: mainstream theology rejects tanasukh. Ibn Arabi's non-repetition and Mulla Sadra's growth after death keep the growth and drop the return, and the Druze accept rebirth [M]. Karma: 99:7 to 8 and 17:7, consequence with no rebirth. Letting go: islam as surrender, fana, dhikr as the Name practice beside japa, the Jesus Prayer and the nembutsu [M]. "Die before you die" is cited as a hadith but is not accepted [U].

Relation to the others. Schuon makes Islam the model of the outer and inner split [M]. Guenon reads Sufism as the heart of Islam [C]. Quran 5:48 and 49:13 are the verses pluralist readers use, and classical commentators read them inside the Abrahamic frame [M].

Where the two movements differ. The Traditionalists are largely Muslim and hold the mainstream Islamic position of one earthly life, barzakh and resurrection, so they reject Western reincarnation [M for the position, U for what each says in print]. Theosophy teaches rebirth as the rule. Mulla Sadra is the Muslim case of growth without return, which fits neither.

Best citation. Ibn Arabi, Tarjuman al-Ashwaq ode 11, "my heart has become capable of every form", in Nicholson's 1911 translation, which is public domain [M that it exists, U on verse numbers]. Ibn Arabi stayed within Islam, and the poem speaks of love and not of a metaphysical One [M]. Quran 2:115 and the one-body hadith are the other two [M].

Specialist objections. Classical commentators read the nearness verses as nearness of knowledge and power [notes]. Ibn Taymiyya and the Salafi line reject wahdat al-wujud, and Sirhindi argued for unity of witness only [M]. Zaehner argued that Bistami's fana was monistic and Indian-influenced and Junayd moved to theistic baqa, which Ernst contests [M, U]. The Quran refuses a return to this world (23:99 to 100).

## 7. Daoism, with the Chinese one-body line

Who cites it. Guenon treats Taoism as the inner side of Chinese tradition with Confucianism outside (Insights into Islamic Esoterism and Taoism, posthumous) [C]. Schuon sets the nameless Tao beside the Absolute [U as his pairing]. Smith puts the Tao at the Infinite level, and Huxley's chapter 2 equates it with Atman and the Ground [M]. Izutsu (Sufism and Taoism, 1966) compares structures without claiming identity [M]. No Theosophical treatment of Daoism turned up in the files.

Texts. Tao Te Ching 1, 16, 19, 25, 40, 42, 48. Zhuangzi 1 (the Peng against the cicada), 2 (the butterfly, and heaven and earth born with me), 4, 6 (sitting and forgetting), 7 (Hundun, who dies when Shu and Hu drill seven openings into him) [M]. For the Chinese one-body line, Zhang Zai's Western Inscription and Wang Yangming's Inquiry on the Great Learning (1527), with the child at the well from Mencius 2A6 [M].

Terms. One: the Tao, and "the Tao gave birth to One, One to Two" (42). Veil and separation: Hundun, differentiation as a cost. Return: "return to the root" (16). Letting go: wu-wei, decrease daily (48), forgetting. Slingerland (2003) names the paradox of trying to be effortless [M]. Lives and karma: nothing in the files [gap].

Relation to the others. The Chinese "three teachings are one" formulas (the Xici line, the Song "mind, body, world" saying of U source) were often rhetoric or joint worship, says Brook [M, P13].

Best citation. Zhuangzi 2 (heaven and earth born with me, the ten thousand things one with me) [M]. Wang Yangming and the child at the well is the best non-Western parallel to two fingers of one hand [M, P13], though Confucian specialists say one body means graded love, close people first [M]. Graham, Roth and Ames caution against reading the Tao as a metaphysical Ground [M].

## 8. Greek and Platonic tradition

Who cites it. Coomaraswamy sets anamnesis beside smrti ("Recollection, Indian and Platonic", JAOS Supplement 3, 1944 [C title, M content]) and the Katha chariot beside the Phaedrus chariot [M]. Schuon reads the Good beyond being (Republic 509b) as Beyond-Being, anamnesis as the Intellect remembering, and Plotinus's One as the Absolute [M]. The Renaissance comparers started here: Plethon's chain from Zoroaster to Plotinus, Ficino's prisca theologia, Pico's concordia, Steuco [C, P01]. The Theosophists claim descent through Ammonius Saccas and his Eclectic Theosophical School (the Key), a picture historians call later [M], and cite Plato for rebirth [M].

Texts. Republic VII (the cave, 514a to 520e, with the compelled return), 509b, and X (Myth of Er, 617e: blame lies with the chooser). Phaedo 64a and 67d to e (practice for dying). Phaedrus 246 to 249 (a thousand-year interval, return only after ten thousand years, three thousand for the lover of wisdom). Meno 81, Laws 903 to 905 (the whole caring for the whole) [M]. Plotinus: Enneads IV.9 (are all souls one), V.1.1 (audacity, tolma), I.6.9 (be a sculptor and cut away), VI.9.11 (the flight of the alone to the Alone) [M]. Proclus on remaining, procession and reversion. Pythagoras and the beaten dog (Xenophanes DK 21 B7), Empedocles DK 31 B117, Anaximander DK 12 B1, Marcus Aurelius 2.1, 8.34 and 11.8 (the severed limb) [M, Marcus numbers moderate].

Terms they equate. One: the One, the Good. Veil: the cave, Lethe, tolma. Return: the turning round of the soul (518c to d), epistrophe. Lives: the Myth of Er and Phaedrus. Plotinus accepted transmigration, and Porphyry and Iamblichus are reported to have rejected animal rebirth, a secondhand report [M]. Karma: Republic 617e, Laws 903, Anaximander. Letting go: the sculptor, practice for dying [M].

Relation to the others. Numenius, "what is Plato but Moses speaking Attic Greek", is quoted by Clement (Stromata I.22.150.4) [C], and should not be given to Pico [U]. Plotinus has three real hypostases, so he is not a monist in Shankara's sense [M].

Where the two movements differ. Theosophy claims the Ammonius lineage. The Traditionalists read Plato as an Eastern-style metaphysician and compare Plotinus with Shankara. Guenon's many states, with no state repeated, runs against a Phaedrus-style return [M, my inference].

Best citation. Republic 617e [M] and Enneads IV.9 [M]. For VI.9.11 name the translator, because the English varies [U on wording]. Marcus 8.34 for the fingers with the return built in, since one can grow back into the whole [M, number moderate].

## 9. Sikhism

Who cites it. The files show neither movement using Sikh scripture. The comparers who do are the reconcilers and anthologists: Wilson's World Scripture [M] and the stage-map writers (the five khands, P18) [M]. Sikh scholars reject "Hindu-Muslim synthesis" as the origin, since the revelation came to Nanak [M].

Texts. The Guru Granth Sahib: compiled by Guru Arjan in 1604, with later additions about 1705 to 1706 [M, later dates U]. An earlier file's "1678" is unsupported. It includes Kabir, Ravidas, Namdev and Baba Farid by design. Ik Onkar, hukam, nadar (the glance of grace). Japji pauri 4, closing line (the robe of flesh by karma, the gate of liberation by grace, p. 2) [M, reviewer recall]. Japji pauris 34 to 37 (the five khands) [M]. Kabir at p. 1349 (from one light the world arose) and Guru Arjan at p. 1299 (none is my enemy or stranger) [U pages]. "Na koi Hindu na Musalman" is a janamsakhi saying and not scripture [M].

Terms. One: Ik Onkar. Return: "light into light". Lives: rebirth through a long cycle, the human birth rare. Karma and grace together, the clearest scriptural form of consequence with a way out that is not payment [M]. Letting go: living by hukam. The pot and water image and the drop in the ocean are attributed to Kabir (Bijak), and the Tagore and Underhill Songs of Kabir (1915) came from a Bengali collection and is no evidence for early Kabir [M].

Best citation. Japji pauri 4, closing line [M]. The draft already uses it.

## 10. Zoroastrianism

Who cites it. The files show no Traditionalist treatment. The comparers are the Renaissance ones (Plethon's Zoroaster as oldest sage, Ficino, the Magian Oracles) [C] and Suhrawardi, who identified the wisdom of the Zoroastrian sages with Hermes, Pythagoras and Plato [C]. The Theosophists use it for symbol tables. Blavatsky sets seven Amshaspends beside seven Rishis and archangels, but the Amesha Spentas are six, or seven if Ahura Mazda is counted, so the seven is her count [M, T02, T08]. Besant's Four Great Religions (1897) matches Ahura Mazda and the holy immortals with the Hindu triple aspect [M, her count U].

Texts. The Dadistan-i-Dinik 94.5 appears in golden-rule lists [U]. No Zoroastrian witness for lives, karma or letting go appears in any file [gap], and nothing separates the two movements here.

Best citation. The Amesha Spentas count, as a caution against copying her equations [M]. Plethon's golden chain from Zoroaster to Plotinus is the earliest genealogy [C].

## 11. Egyptian and Hermetic

Who cites it. The Renaissance comparers (Ficino's Pimander, translated 1463, calls Hermes the first author of theology [C]), until Casaubon's redating in 1614 broke the genealogy [C, Yates's wording U]. The Theosophists lean on it hardest. Isis Unveiled (1877) calls the Hermetic philosophy the anciently universal Wisdom-Religion [M, wording U]. Blavatsky's first Masters were Egyptian in 1875 to 1878 before they were Himalayan [M, chronology U]. She equates the triads Osiris, Isis and Horus, the Trimurti and the upper sephiroth [M]. Mead's Thrice-Greatest Hermes (1906) belongs here [M]. The Traditionalists use it for the inner face of the Christian West, with Hermeticism and the Grail in Guenon [C], and Burckhardt's Alchemy (1960) reads the Hermetic, Arabic, Chinese and Indian arts as one symbolic science [M].

Texts. Corpus Hermeticum I (Poimandres): Man leans down, loves his reflection in Nature and is joined to her. After death the soul climbs through seven planetary spheres, giving back one fault at each, reaches the eighth and becomes God. Corpus Hermeticum XIII: rebirth, with Tat shedding twelve torments, receiving ten powers and seeing himself everywhere [M, paragraph numbers U]. The inscription at Sais: Plutarch (On Isis and Osiris 9) says "robe", and the later "veil" comes from Proclus and Kant [M]. Herodotus 2.123: the Egyptians said the soul passes through creatures over three thousand years, and Egyptologists find no Egyptian text with this cycle [M].

Terms. One: the Mind of Poimandres. Veil: Isis, Nature's embrace. Return: the climb through the spheres to the eighth. Lives: Herodotus, which Theosophists cite as the Egyptian "cycle of necessity", a phrase that is hers and not Herodotus's [M]. Letting go: dropping a fault at each sphere.

Relation to the others, and the two movements. Theosophy takes Egypt as origin and lineage. The Traditionalists take Hermeticism as one inner layer among others. Nock, Festugiere and Copenhaver read the Hermetica as Greek philosophical and religious writing of the early centuries CE, and Fowden stresses the Egyptian setting. None finds a secret ancient doctrine in the Theosophical sense [M]. Burkert shows the mystery cults were rites without systematic doctrine [M].

Best citation. Corpus Hermeticum I, the seven spheres and the eighth, paired with CH XIII on rebirth [M]. The draft's arrow and "birth" can point to it, but in Hermes the rebirth is a birth into seeing oneself everywhere, which may or may not be the school's stage after plus four. Mead's gloss is Theosophical and should be flagged as such [M, T08].

## 13. Flags for the Part two draft

These are tradition-specific points where the research bears on a sentence already in essay/part-2-spiritual.md. All are pointers to check.

1. Eckhart's eye line "raised against him in Cologne in 1326". Nobody could confirm it [U, P14], and the notes say Eckhart was not condemned for the eye saying. The better-attested condemned item is the uncreated something, in the 1329 bull [M].
2. Isis Unveiled and the Secret Doctrine as "one of the main routes" for karma and reincarnation. Isis rejected the Spiritist return and allowed rebirth only in exceptional cases. The Key, Esoteric Buddhism and the Secret Doctrine carry the doctrine, and Chajes (2019) covers the shift [C for the shift, M for the rest]. Edwin Arnold's The Light of Asia (1879) and the Orientalist translations were also routes [M].
3. Chabad. The draft says that from God's own point of view the world has no separate existence. Say "close to acosmism in the Tanya's reading", since scholars debate it [U, P14, P06].
4. Zohar III 73a, Mishnah Eduyot 2:10, the Quran 17:7 last clause and the Shantideva 8.91 numbering are unconfirmed in the notes. P18 adds that "born into the family of the Buddhas" is Bodhicaryavatara 3.25 in the numbering recalled [U].
5. The outer and inner split is Schuon's thesis in The Transcendent Unity of Religions [M], with Guenon's earlier distinction in the Introduction (1921) [C]. The draft's skeleton is Huxley's three-part definition. Say once that the Traditionalists would not accept Alex's method, that they and the Theosophists disliked each other, and that the authors disagree with each other on rebirth.
6. Do not cite Guenon as a witness for karma as something done to another part of oneself. His own line is that karma means action [C, overstated, P02].
7. The one-Self answer to "who carries over" is Coomaraswamy's [C], and Gaudapada's Karika 2.32 (no one bound, no one freed) is the plain Advaita version [M]. Both say the individual is not what carries over, which sits beside the saved-state picture and does not replace it.
