## 1. The one whole (the axiom)

Perennial authors. Guenon states it as logic. The Principle is the Infinite and Universal Possibility, nothing can lie outside it, so manifestation is included. The true Self was never individualized, so liberation is realizing an identity that was always so (Man and His Becoming, 1925; The Multiple States of the Being, 1932) [M for the books; the liberation-in-this-life paraphrase is C, from a publisher's description]. Schuon gives the clearest ladder: Absolute and Infinite, then Beyond-Being, Being and Existence, with Atma as the Absolute seen from inside a person [M; the detail of the Absolute and Infinite scheme U]. Coomaraswamy puts it as two selves: the one Self, the only knower, in all beings, and the contingent self (the two birds, Mundaka 3.1.1) [C for the birds, M for the rest]. Smith made it readable with four levels of reality and four of selfhood [M]. Burckhardt (Sufi), Schaya (Kabbalah) and Shah-Kazemi (Shankara, Ibn Arabi, Eckhart) run the same grid inside one tradition each [M, what each equates U]. Huxley's chapters 1 and 2 ("That Art Thou", "The Nature of the Ground") and the first clause of his Foreword carry the same idea [M].

Theosophists. The Proem of The Secret Doctrine (1888) gives three propositions: one boundless Principle beyond thought, universes that appear and vanish in cycles, and the identity of all souls with the Universal Over-Soul with an obligatory pilgrimage through incarnation under karmic law [M content, U wording]. "Over-Soul" is Emerson's word, 1841 [M]. Her Absolute has two aspects, Spirit and Matter, so it is not plain Advaita [M]. Subba Row gives the Indian form: Parabrahman, Mulaprakriti as its veil, the Logos as first manifest [M]. The Letters have the Masters deny a personal God and call Parabrahm "absolute immutable Law" [M gist, letter number U].

same | Islamic | Dhat; "He and not He"
same | Jewish | Ein Sof (Schuon's and Schaya's pairing, U as theirs)
same | Taoist | the nameless Tao, Tao Te Ching 1
same | Buddhist | Dharmakaya, emptiness (comparers' equation, specialists keep apart)
same | Greek | the One, the Good beyond being, Republic 509b
you are that | Hindu | tat tvam asi, Chandogya 6.8.7; Brihadaranyaka 1.4.10

Strongest quotations.
Emerson, "The Over-Soul" [C]: "We live in succession, in division, in parts, in particles. Meantime within man is the soul of the whole; the wise silence; the universal beauty, to which every part and particle is equally related, the eternal ONE". The punctuation before "the eternal ONE" is U. Public domain, the closest English sentence to the three clauses. Emerson is a shared precedent, not a member of either movement.
Huxley's Foreword [M, recalled; the repo notes say it was only checked through secondary quotations]: a metaphysic "that recognizes a divine Reality substantial to the world of things and lives and minds", a psychology that finds in the soul "something similar to, or even identical with, divine Reality". The draft's "underlying" is a fair paraphrase of "substantial to".
Blavatsky's third proposition [U wording, M gist; one memory repeated in several files]: "the fundamental identity of all Souls with the Universal Over-Soul, the latter being itself an aspect of the Unknown Root". Note the step: souls are identical with the Over-Soul, which is an aspect of the Principle, so it is softer than Alex's "one whole".

Where they split. Both start from one Principle beyond words and say the human core is not other than it. They split on authority. Blavatsky gives it as received teaching from hidden Masters and one archaic Wisdom-Religion [M]. Guenon and Schuon give it as metaphysics known by intellectual intuition inside one regular tradition, and Guenon wrote one book against Theosophy as a counterfeit (Le Theosophisme, 1921) and one against Spiritism and reincarnationism (L'Erreur spirite, 1923) [C], while holding that a central tradition underlies all religions [C, Smoley, Quest 2010]. Specialists object to both: "advaita" is "not two", which is not "one" (Loy) [M]; Buddhist emptiness is no One; Scholem and Idel do not read Ein Sof as Nirguna Brahman [M].

For Alex. His stance (an assumption, tested for coherence) is the honest one. Say once that Blavatsky's first and third propositions state the same shape, offered as received, and that Guenon and Schuon argue it as logic. Do not import her proposition two. Keep the softer step in mind if he quotes her. Never call Theosophy and the Traditionalists one family.

## 2. The veil and the illusion of separateness

Perennial authors. Schuon is the best source for "the illusion that feels real". Maya is real in its order and unreal if taken as separate, neither being nor non-being, with a veiling side and a revealing side, which he ties to the Sufi "He and not He". Illusion is taking the part for the whole [M; his Buddhist and Eckhart pairings U]. Guenon blames the age: individualism, "the negation of any principle higher than individuality", and the cycle of the Kali Yuga, whose length of more than six thousand years is his own figure, not the Puranic 432,000 [C for the line, its lead-in U]. Coomaraswamy reads maya as the One measured out into forms, his own etymology from ma, and sets forgetting (avidya) against recollection (anamnesis, smrti) [M]. Smith: the ego is identification with the lower levels [M]. James's "filmiest of screens" and Huxley's brain as a "reducing valve" (The Doors of Perception, 1954; from Broad and Bergson) are the English pictures nearest to Alex's walls [M]. Wilber: a boundary that is drawn can be undrawn [M].

Theosophists. The Glossary (1892, posthumous, edited by Mead): Maya is illusion; only what is changeless is real, so whatever changes is Maya [M, near-verbatim]. That sits nearer mithya, "dependent", than "unreal". The personality is the mask (persona), the individuality what lasts [M; the persona etymology in the Key U]. The Voice of the Silence (1889): mind is the slayer of the real [M]. Besant (The Self and Its Sheaths, 1895; A Study in Consciousness, 1904): the sense of separateness comes from the sheath [M titles, U content]. Bailey: three veils, maya, glamour, illusion [U]. Subba Row: a veil made of the One itself [M]. The phrase "heresy of separateness" is credited to Blavatsky online and nobody has placed it [U]; do not credit it.

Two kinds of cause: error of knowing (rope and snake, Shankara) and clutch of will (knot of the heart, Mundaka 2.2.8; tangle, Samyutta 1.23) [M, P15]. Two story families: fall (Genesis 3, Phaedrus, Hymn of the Pearl) and play or overflow (lila, Plotinus, tzimtzum) [M].

Strongest quotations.

Coomaraswamy, Satan essay [C, first-pass V]: "we dare not deny Satan until we have denied ourselves, as everyone must who would follow Him who said and did nothing 'of himself'".

Where they split. The same word names different things. Theosophy's Ego is the lasting individuality and its personality is the passing mask, so Alex's ego is their personality [M]. Guenon separates Self (Soi) from Ego (Moi) and individuality from personality in his own way [S, P02]. He criticised the Theosophical reading of the Vedantic sheaths (the 1925 book, U). The seven principles are no Hindu scheme and buddhi is discriminating intellect, not a "spiritual soul" [M]. Advaita scholars say "illusion" translates maya badly [M].

For Alex. His definition (a wrong reading of something real, a wall real inside the system) has three named supports: Schuon's degrees of reality, Coomaraswamy's maya, the Glossary's changeless against changing. Credit Schuon in the machine paragraph. Add the will side that fits his BE axis: the traditions' own images (knot, tangle, thread) are about holding. Do not borrow the Voice's verbs, slay and kill. His verb is release.

## 3. Why the whole separates itself

Perennial authors. Two kinds of answer appear, and they should not be merged. The logic kind is Guenon's and Schuon's: the Infinite cannot exclude any possibility, so apparent distance from itself is included. Maya is then play, neither error nor fall, and the Good tends to give itself [M]. Smith repeats it: an Infinite that could not include limits would be limited by that very fact [M, wording U]. The motive kind is older: Brihadaranyaka 1.4.1 to 4, where the Self alone is afraid, takes no delight, wishes for a second and divides in two [M]; Ibn Arabi's Real wishing to see the Names in a comprehensive being, a mirror (Fusus, Adam chapter) [M]; lila (Brahma Sutra 2.1.33, the reply to the objection in 2.1.32), which Coomaraswamy sets beside Eckhart's "without why" and Heraclitus fragment 52 [M]. Wilber (the Atman project), Grof and Watts give modern versions of the motive kind [M]. Huxley does not argue the question.

Theosophists. Blavatsky answers with a motive: the spark must pass through every form and win individuality by its own effort, and the pivotal doctrine admits no privileges, only what the Ego wins over many lives [M; wording U]. The Key restates it as unconscious divine purity becoming conscious divinity through experience [M; that it is in the Key U]. The Letters and Sinnett give the rounds as a V: descent into denser matter, a nadir on the fourth globe, and an ascent carrying self-consciousness back up [M]. Besant reads the Atonement as the Logos limiting himself in form [M]. Mead gives the Valentinian Sophia, who falls wanting to know the Father, and the Poimandres Man, who loves his reflection in Nature [M].

Terms side by side:

why many | Jewish | tzimtzum, the broken vessels, tikkun (Luria, sixteenth century)
why many | Islamic | the hidden treasure that loved to be known (a saying, not an accepted hadith; Ibn Arabi holds it by unveiling)
why many | Neoplatonic | overflow of the One; the soul's audacity (tolma), Enneads V.1.1
why many | Buddhist | unawareness stirs a thought (Awakening of Faith)
why many | Taoist | Hundun drilled with seven holes, Zhuangzi 7
why many | none | Gaudapada: the question belongs to appearance

Strongest quotations. None above [M]. The hidden-treasure Arabic is recalled in P06 [M] and the status "not an accepted hadith" is [M]. Blavatsky's "no privileges or special gifts" is [M, recalled, not for printing].

Where they split. The Traditionalists give logic with no motive, so for Schuon the Self was never lost. Theosophy gives a motive: consciousness is earned by passing through separation. Judaism treats the gap as a real wound to repair, not an illusion [M]. Genesis 3 and Philippians 2 are weak witnesses for a whole separating itself (kenosis concerns the Son, not creation) [M].

For Alex. His line, that a whole wanting to know itself needs a gap, is the motive kind and he should name it as one of two. Best additions: Brihadaranyaka 1.4.1 to 4 (check Olivelle) and the Ibn Arabi mirror, then one sentence of the Schuon and Smith logic. Blavatsky's effort answer ties neatly to Part one's agency. Say plainly that no witness proves it.

## 4. Lives, and the soul between lives

Perennial authors. This is where the movement splits. Guenon rejects reincarnation in the Western sense as a nineteenth century notion from Kardec and the Theosophists. The doctrine is transmigration, which is change of state, and no state is run through twice [C quotes below]. Coomaraswamy keeps transmigration and changes the subject: the one Self is the only transmigrant [C]. In 1916 he wrote of "the transmigration of character" (an older formula of the English Pali school, so check for quotation marks) [C, U on whose]. Schuon followed Guenon on Western reincarnationism and reads rebirth as change of state or a psychic residue [U, the item most worth checking in the books]. Nasr, Lings and Burckhardt hold the Islamic line of one earthly life, barzakh and resurrection [M, what each says in print U]. Pallis, a Buddhist, keeps rebirth without a soul [U]. Huxley's chapter "Immortality and Survival" (number U) separates survival from immortality [M, U on content]. Aurobindo and Wilber give staged rebirth outside the Traditionalist school [M]. Jung separates five senses of "rebirth" [M].

Theosophists. By the 1880s the individuality (the Ego) returns and the personality does not. After death come kama-loka and the long dream of devachan, and we forget because memory belongs to the personality's brain [M]. Isis Unveiled (1877) rejected the Spiritist return and allowed rebirth only in exceptional cases, which Chajes (2019) documents as a change by 1888 [C]. Besant, Judge and Sinnett carried it to readers. Theosophists reject return into animals [M].

rebirth, no fixed soul | Buddhist | the flame passed between lamps (Milindapanha); Samyutta 15 on beginningless samsara
return to repair | Jewish | gilgul (Bahir, Zohar, Luria)
forgetting between lives | Greek | Lethe, Myth of Er (Republic 10); Orphic Memory
in-between | Tibetan, Islamic, Christian | bardo, barzakh, purgatory

one life | Christian, Islamic | resurrection and judgment; Origen taught pre-existence, not reincarnation

Strongest quotations.
Guenon [C, locators unknown]: "transmigration essentially means change of state"; "the idea of reincarnation, like that of evolution, is a very modern idea; it appears to have materialized around 1830 or 1848 in certain French socialist circles" ("materialized" is odd, check the translation).
Coomaraswamy [C, from Shankara, BS Bhashya 1.1.5, locator S]: "Verily, there is no other transmigrant but the Lord."
Lessing, Education of the Human Race [C]: "Why should I not come back as often as I am capable of acquiring fresh knowledge, fresh expertness?" Paragraph numbers U.

Where they split. Sharpest in the whole matrix. Theosophy teaches return of an individuality. Guenon calls that a modern error [C]. Indologists read the Upanishads and Pali texts the other way, as rebirth in the ordinary sense [M]. The Council of 553 did not ban reincarnation; the anathemas concern pre-existence and the restoration of all, so do not repeat the story that anthologies and Theosophists tell [M, repo notes].

For Alex. The draft's ground is right: the axiom needs only that development continues. Say in one sentence that the Traditionalist founders (name Guenon) reject return and keep survival and states, and that the step to lifetimes comes from the axiom. Offer Coomaraswamy's one transmigrant as the Advaita answer to "who carries over", with the price: the individual does not carry over. Present the saved-state picture and the one-transmigrant picture as two labelled answers. Use "rebirth" or "transmigration" before "reincarnation". Do not cite Schuon for lifetimes [U]. Gita 6.45 and 7.19 support "the run is longer than one life" [M]. Fix the draft sentence that Isis Unveiled and The Secret Doctrine were main routes to karma and rebirth: the Key (1889), Sinnett (1883) and the later books were, and Arnold (1879) and Kardec (1857) came earlier [M, C-repo for Kardec].

## 5. Karma and the ethic built on unity

Perennial authors. Guenon is a caution, not a witness. He says "karma" means action and has never meant causality [C, overstated; Anguttara 6.63, "it is intention I call kamma", and Brihadaranyaka 4.4.5 are the counters, M]. Coomaraswamy treats karma as a law of action and consequence, not fate, with the Self in all as the ground of ethics [M, wording unverified; passage on the neighbour U]. Schuon makes the ethic ontological: the neighbour is the Self, the golden rule is the corollary of "that thou art", and humility keeps knowledge from turning into pride [M gist, citations U]. His own karma text is weak [U]. Huxley's "Charity" chapter makes the same move as an anthology [M, passages U]. Precedents who state the step plainly: Schopenhauer (tat tvam asi as the basis of compassion, section 22 U; "eternal justice", World as Will vol. 1 section 63, where tormentor and tormented are one will, M), then Deussen, Vivekananda and Radhakrishnan [M]. Smith: no passage found [U].

Theosophists. Karma is the law of harmony, with no judge. In recalled near-verbatim wording the one decree of Karma is absolute harmony, so it is we who reward or punish ourselves [M, vol. 1 near p. 643 U]. Karma-Nemesis, collective karma, and brotherhood as a fact in nature (the first Object) tie it to unity [M, locator U]. The Key's image is a bough that springs back [M]. Lords of Karma are a later Adyar personification, though Blavatsky already has recording Lipika [U].

The ladder. reciprocity | Hillel, Shabbat 31a; Analects 15.24; Matthew 7:12; Bukhari 13 and Muslim 45; Mahabharata 13.113.8 (number medium). analogy | Sutta Nipata 705; Gita 6.32; Mencius's child at the well. identity | Isha 6 and 7; Gita 6.29 and 13.28 ("does not harm the self by the self", the nearest classical text to the fingers); 1 Corinthians 12:26; Matthew 25:40 (grounds unity in Christ's identification with the needy; some scholars read "the least of these" as disciples); Ben Azzai on Genesis 5:1 and Tanhuma against Akiva; Wang Yangming; Marcus 8.34. Shantideva's limbs reach compassion by "no owner of suffering", not identity [M].

Strongest quotations. Blavatsky above [M, not for printing]. Isha 6 [M paraphrase]. Anguttara 6.63 [M]. No verified line exists.

Where they split. Guenon says Theosophy gave karma a moral-retribution sense [C]. Buddhists say nobody administers it; theistic Hinduism gives God a role; Advaita puts karma in appearance [M]. Christian and Jewish texts ground oneness in Christ, the Church or common descent, not metaphysical identity [M]. Levinas and Buber object that a merged self has no one to answer to [M].

## 6. Return, union, deliverance

Perennial authors. Guenon and Schuon: nothing returns because the Self never left, and deliverance is recognition [M]. Schuon splits exoteric salvation from esoteric deliverance and sets moksha, fana, theosis and nirvana as one event, a comparer's equation that Buddhists in particular do not accept [M, Buddhist and Taoist pairs U]. Coomaraswamy: return out of time (Time and Eternity, 1947), through recollection [M, U on detail]. Burckhardt and Lings: fana then baqa, passing away then abiding, with return into action [M]. Underhill and Teresa: union is active, the seventh mansion ends in the birth of good works, her own phrase in chapter 4 [M, wording not cleared]. Zaehner separates union of love from union of identity [M]. Stace: extrovertive and introvertive unity [M]. Huxley's chapter 13 (Salvation, Deliverance, Enlightenment) [M, U on number].

Theosophists. The pilgrimage returns with consciousness gained, the many back to the One [M]. The Letters keep nirvana apart from paranirvana at the end of a manvantara [M]. The Voice sets private liberation against the Great Renunciation, where the adept who could enter nirvana stays as a Nirmanakaya [M]. Subba Row: the individual finds itself one with the Logos and the Absolute [M]. Bailey adds a choice among seven Paths after the fifth initiation [M, labels U].

## 8. Stages of growth, and what lies beyond the last stage

Perennial authors. There is no single perennial stage map. The strict school describes degrees of Reality and cyclic decline, and rejects evolution as a world process [M]. Smith's four levels are worlds, not stages. Nasr and Schuon give the five divine presences and sharia, tariqa, haqiqa [M, which list Schuon uses U]. Coomaraswamy: the sun door out of the cosmos (Janua Coeli; year U) [M]. Lings: the three certainties [M]. Underhill's five stages and Bucke's three levels are nearer a growth picture [M]. Huxley has no stage map [U]. Beyond the last rung the files find four answers, each an interpretive grouping: rest (Jain siddha), no end (Gregory's epektasis, Ibn Arabi on one reading), return into action (bodhisattva, tenth ox picture, Teresa's seventh mansion), and no ladder at all (Gaudapada 2.32, sudden Zen) [M, readings flagged]. Birth images: John 3:3, the dvija, Eckhart's birth followed by the breakthrough (Sermon 52) [M].

Theosophists. The Voice's Three Halls and Seven Portals [M]. Besant and Leadbeater: probation, discipleship, then five initiations, with Buddhist, Hindu and Christian labels, an edit of the four Buddhist stages [M, Hindu names U]. Bailey: nine initiations and a choice of seven Paths [M, labels five to nine U]. In Bailey and in Besant and Leadbeater, "Birth" is the first initiation [M]. Blavatsky's own pilgrimage ends at the Dhyani level, and what lies above is called unknowable [M].

Where they split. The strict school reads history as decline and rejects evolution, so a growth arrow is a Theosophical, Wilber and Underhill shape [M]. Theosophy cut "arhat" and "asekha" into two stages, which the Pali does not [M]. Katz says stage maps may track vocabulary [M]. Repeated sevens may come from the seven planets and not a shared inner fact [M, colleague's reasoning].

For Alex. The draft's "other traditions draw staged maps too" is safe. Do not write that no other scale is nine steps centred on a door: nine-step scales and threshold-centred scales exist, and only the zero-centred numbering is unknown to the files [M]. Add that the arrow is a personal scale and makes no claim that the world improves, which defuses the Traditionalist objection [M]. For "birth" add that Eckhart himself puts a breakthrough after the birth, so the draft's "a lasting state" needs a clause [M]. Never write that Theosophy "calls it birth".

## 9. The cosmic scale: planes, worlds, hierarchies

Perennial authors. The strict school reads the ladder of the soul and the ladder of the cosmos as one ladder: distance words stand for degrees of reality, not kilometres [M]. Smith: four nested levels. Nasr: Islamic cosmology and the five presences [M, U on Schuon's list]. Guenon: states of being and the cross, a vertical axis of degrees crossing a horizontal axis of range [M]; his cycles of the Kali Yuga describe the decline of an age and run opposite to a personal ascent [C for the cycles, the contrast is P02's reading]. Burckhardt reads Ibn Arabi's planetary heavens as stages [U year]. Wilber's Great Nest: each level transcends and includes the one below [M]. Huxley: none.

Theosophists. The Great Breath and manvantara and pralaya at every scale, with Puranic numbers (the arithmetic holds, T05) [M]. Chains of globes, rounds, seven planes [M]. Dhyan-Chohans between the Absolute and humanity [M]. Besant and Leadbeater add Planetary and Solar Logoi; Bailey the Heavenly Man and cosmic planes, the strongest self-similar ladder, whose likeness to Alex's small square inside a 1,000 by 1,000 map is structural only [M]. Root races are what scholars find hardest to defend. Leave them out.

Terms side by side:
worlds as steps | Hindu | seven lokas
worlds as steps | Sufi | five presences (Hahut to Nasut)
worlds as steps | Kabbalah | four worlds, Atzilut to Asiyah
worlds counted | Buddhist | thousandfold systems, 1,000 and 1,000,000 and 1,000,000,000 worlds (AN 3.80 U); a count of worlds, not of potential
galaxy rung | Gurdjieff | "All Suns" as the Milky Way
speed picture | Dante | speed rises toward God, Paradiso 28 (line numbers U)
escape picture | Jain | the freed soul rises like a flame to the edge of the cosmos (Tattvartha Sutra 10.5 to 8, U)
a universe as one breath | Hindu, Theosophical | Day and Night of Brahma

Strongest quotations. None verified. The Proem's opening picture, "from which all proceeds, whither all returns" [U].

Where they split. Traditionalist hierarchies are static degrees; Theosophical ones are evolutionary planes. Guenon attacked the Theosophical constitution of man (the sheaths point U). No Theosophical text maps a stage to speed, and Theosophy puts Earth's Logos above the Master where Alex's arrow leaves Earth at plus one [M].

For Alex. The old maps were already scale maps, so plus two to plus four is that idea in modern units. Dante and the Jain flame are the closest old pictures of "gravity is the pull of the one you started with". Present the Buddhist thousandfold figure as an echo, not a source. The multiverse hint has older precedents in time (the Great Breath) and in space (numberless Brahmas) [M]. If Theosophy is named as a stage-mapper, add that Blavatsky's own work differs from the layers of Besant, Leadbeater and Bailey.

## What cuts across all nine

The two movements do not agree. Say it once, with names: Guenon (and, by inference, Coomaraswamy and Schuon [U]) called Theosophy a counterfeit and rejected its rebirth. They share only the idea of one tradition behind the religions. Huxley, by contrast, was friendly to Krishnamurti [M] and is no party to the quarrel. Keep the vocabularies apart: Masters, root races and Wisdom-Religion are Theosophical; exoteric and esoteric, primordial tradition and Beyond-Being are Traditionalist. "Primordial tradition" is Guenon's phrase, not Smith's.

How each compares. Blavatsky: genealogy, one archaic source, difference as corruption, authority from Masters, so the method cannot lose [M]. Guenon and Schuon: begin from a principle, fix the level, compare esoteric with esoteric, explain difference as willed form [M]. Coomaraswamy: philology and symbol, line by line [M]. Huxley: subtract to a "Highest Common Factor in all preceding and subsequent theologies" [M, recalled]. Alex derives from one axiom and shows fit, which is a fourth method. Do not borrow Huxley's "conditions" move (a critic who does not see it is told he lacks the conditions).

Draft points several files raise. Line 53 builds its skeleton on Huxley's definition, verified only secondhand, and calls Schuon "Swiss philosopher" (his school says metaphysician; say "born in Basel"). Smith appears in the sources but nowhere in the body. Line 55 overstates Isis Unveiled. Add Guenon, Coomaraswamy and Perry or Smith by job: the argument, the checked parallels, the map, the passages.
