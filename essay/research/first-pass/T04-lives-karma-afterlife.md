## 1. The picture in brief

Blavatsky and her circle built the fullest nineteenth century system on the question Alex calls "who carries over". A person has seven principles. The lower four (body, life force, astral double, desire body) dissolve. The upper three (Atma, Buddhi, Higher Manas) continue. What continues is the individuality, also called the Ego. What does not is the personality, the mask of one life, with its name and memories. After death the desire body is shed in kama-loka. The Ego rests in devachan, a dreamlike state in which the best of the last life is fulfilled, and then returns under karma. We do not remember because memory belongs to the brain of the personality. The Ego keeps the harvest of each life, and a life that gave nothing spiritual leaves little to keep. Karma is no judge. It is the Law of Harmony, also called Karma-Nemesis, which restores a balance that action has broken. She rejects return into animals and rejects the Spiritist return of the personality.

To show this is one teaching, she and her followers set witnesses side by side: Pythagoras, Plato, Origen, the Gnostics, Kabbalah, Egyptians, Druids, Hindus, Buddhists, Gospel verses, Sufis. The later anthologists (Walker 1888, Head and Cranston 1961 and 1977) make a catalogue of it. Their method is to line texts up, equate terms (devachan with sukhavati, karma with Nemesis), read conflicts as popular distortion or later suppression, and fall back on teachers nobody can check. Scholars grant the influence and fault the equations. Her own doctrine also changed between 1877 and 1888.

For Alex the useful fact is that her answer to "what is saved" is close to his "account, not session". The caution is that her Ego is a lasting individual, while his axiom lets the whole be the one that carries.

2. The seven principles and the immortal triad.
Claim: Man is sevenfold: sthula sharira (body), prana (life), linga sharira (astral double), kama (desire), manas (mind, dual), buddhi (spiritual soul), atma (spirit). The upper three are the lasting part. Atma is no personal possession; it is the one universal Spirit, with Buddhi its vehicle.
Who: The Key; Sinnett, Esoteric Buddhism (1883), with English names; articles in The Theosophist (1881 to 1882) [U].
Cited parallels: Vedantic five sheaths, Taraka Raja Yoga, and in Theosophical writing nephesh, ruach and neshamah.
Tier: [M] for the list and Atma as universal; [U] for any Kabbalah column in the Key.
Scholars: the comparer equates what specialists keep apart. No sevenfold scheme exists in Hindu texts, and her buddhi and manas differ from the Sanskrit senses.
Use: "Atma is the common root, the lower layers differentiate" is near the axiom. Do not print the seven as Vedanta.

3. Personality and individuality: what continues and what does not.
Claim: The personality (from persona, a mask [U that the Key says so]) is one life's body, memories, desires and habits. The individuality is the reincarnating Ego (Higher Manas with Buddhi). The personality does not return. The Ego assimilates the spiritual essence of each life. A life with no spiritual aspiration leaves almost nothing to assimilate. The Ego sends a ray (lower manas) into each new personality.
Who: The Key, sections on principles, post-mortem states and the thinking principle; Secret Doctrine (1888), Proem.
Cited parallels: Plato's immortal soul, Vedantic jivatman, the skandhas (finding 9).
Tier: [M] for the split and the harvest mechanism; [U] for persona.
Use: this is the Theosophical form of the saved state: the account survives, the session closes. Terminology trap: her "Ego" is the lasting self, and Alex's "ego" is her personality.

4. After death, in order.
Claim: The body dissolves; prana returns to the common store; the astral double fades. The desire body goes to kama-loka, a semi-material plane, and disintegrates (the "second death"). The remnants are "shells", which she says mediums meet. Suicides and the violently killed linger. A rare wholly material life may sever the lower mind from the Ego; the individuality survives but that personality is lost.
Who: The Key, "On the Various Post-Mortem States" and "On the Kama-Loka and Devachan"; Sinnett (1883).
Cited parallels: Catholic purgatory [U that she says so], the Tibetan bardo, Spiritualist séances (rejected).
Tier: [M] for the sequence; [U] for suicides and the severed personality.
Scholars: not a Hindu or Buddhist doctrine; assembled from Sinnett's letters from the Masters and Western occult sources.
Use: the failure case answers Alex's worry that the whole rigs the run: only a life fails, not the individuality.

5. Devachan, and the word sukhavati.
Claim: Devachan is the long rest between lives, a subjective state in which the Ego lives the fulfilment of the best of its last life, with those it loved present in the dream. It compensates for unmerited suffering, and its length follows the spiritual energy of the life. I recall an average of ten to fifteen centuries [U].
Who: The Key; Glossary (1892) "Devachan"; Sinnett, Esoteric Buddhism.
Cited parallels: the brief gives the etymology, Tibetan bde-ba-can, the Tibetan rendering of Sanskrit sukhavati. Also the Pure Land of Amitabha; Hindu svarga [U]. Mine: Gita 9.21, where those who enjoyed the wide heaven return to the world of mortals when merit is spent [M].
Tier: [M] for dream-heaven and etymology; [U] for the interval.
Scholars: bde ba can is the Pure Land of Amitabha, a place of practice reached by the Buddha's vow, from which one does not fall back. It is no rest before the next birth. Devachan resembles the Hindu svarga, a heaven of merit with a return. The comparer reused a Buddhist name for a different idea.
Use: Gita 9.21 and the Hindu two paths (Chandogya 5.10, Brihadaranyaka 6.2) are better witnesses for "rest, then return". Use "devachan" only as Theosophy's name.

6. The return.
Claim: When devachan runs out, the Ego's karmic tendencies (the skandhas) ripen into a new personality fitted to them. The number of Egos in this cycle is fixed, so population growth means shorter intervals [U]. The undeveloped return sooner. The third proposition of the Secret Doctrine's Proem gives the pilgrimage in short: every soul is identical with the Over-Soul and passes through the cycle of incarnation under cyclic and karmic law (reported also in T02 and P16).
Who: The Key; Secret Doctrine, Proem.
Cited parallels: Hindu karma-driven rebirth; the Buddhist stream; Plato's choice of lives.
Tier: [M] for the pilgrimage; [U] for the fixed number.
Use: "the run is longer than one life" is her third proposition. She gave it as taught; Alex derives it.

7. Why we do not remember.
Claim: Memory belongs to the brain of the personality, and the new brain is new. The Ego keeps the harvest, not the autobiography. Full recall comes with high development. In ordinary life the Ego's voice shows as conscience, intuition, talent and character.
Who: The Key, the section on rebirth.
Cited parallels: [U] that she cites Lethe. Mine: Plato's Myth of Er and the Orphic water of Memory [M]; Aeneid 6.748 to 751 (Lethe after a thousand years); Yoga Sutra 4.9 (memory and latent impression alike across birth, place and time) [M].
Tier: [M] for the answer; [U] for Lethe and Virgil in her text.
Use: the answer in computer words is "the account is kept, the session closes", and the Yoga Sutra gives it a Hindu text.

8. No return into animals, and the life-atoms.
Claim: A human Ego does not return in an animal body. Pythagorean and Platonic metempsychosis is read as the soul's long passage through states, or allegory. Hindu and Buddhist animal-rebirth stories are read as popular distortion, in part the transmigration of life-atoms, the matter of a body passing into other forms.
Who: The Key; Secret Doctrine (the monad passes through mineral, plant and animal stages in earlier rounds); an 1886 article in The Theosophist [U].
Cited parallels: Plato (Timaeus, Phaedrus); Porphyry and Iamblichus, who also rejected animal rebirth (P16).
Tier: [M] for the position; [U] for the article.
Scholars: the Pali canon lists the animal realm among the destinies, and Chandogya 5.10.7 gives the womb of a dog or a pig for foul conduct, so mainstream scholars say the texts mean it. Kardec's Spiritism also rejected animal rebirth. The rejection is a nineteenth century progressive taste.
Use: if animals come up, the texts speak plainly, and Theosophy, Spiritism and Guenon each reject the popular reading for different reasons.

9. The skandhas and Buddhist no-self.
Claim: The Key and Glossary present the five skandhas (form, feeling, perception, formations, consciousness) as the attributes of a personality that become the karmic germs of the next life. She reads the Buddha's no-self as a denial of the personal self only, with a higher individuality behind it.
Who: The Key; Glossary "Skandhas"; Sinnett (1883); Olcott, A Buddhist Catechism (1881).
Cited parallels: Buddhist skandhas and nirvana; the Hindu atman.
Tier: [M]; locators [U].
Scholars: Max Muller said there is no esoteric Buddhism, citing the Buddha's "no closed fist" (Digha Nikaya 16) (reported by sibling P01 as [V]; DN 16 [M]). Most Buddhologists read the khandhas as an analysis of a person and the Buddha as denying any lasting self. The Milindapanha compares the continuity to a flame passed from lamp to lamp. The "higher Ego bearing the skandhas" is hers.
Use: say "she reads", never "Buddhism teaches". It is the clearest harmonizing in this slice.

10. Karma in the Key: balance without a judge.
Claim: Karma adjusts effect to cause on the physical, mental and moral planes. It is no person and does not reward or punish. Disturbed harmony tends to return, as a bent bough springs back. Man plans and makes the causes, and the law adjusts the effects. We weave our destiny thread by thread like a spider's web.
Who: The Key, the karma section. Find-handles (unverified): "the ultimate Law of the Universe, the source, origin and fount of all other laws" (the brief also uses "ultimate Law of the universe"); "neither rewards nor punishes"; "like a bough which, bent down too forcibly, rebounds".
Cited parallels: Hindu and Buddhist karma; Nemesis; Galatians 6:7 (brief).
Tier: [M] for gist and bough image; [U] for section and wording.
Use: the bough fits Alex's swing and shrinking swing. "No courtroom" fits his reading. Public domain: quote in full once checked.

14. The Greek and Roman witnesses.
Claim: Pythagoras and the beaten dog whose voice a friend knew (Xenophanes, DK 21 B7), and his recall of earlier lives as Euphorbus (Diogenes Laertius 8.4 to 5 [U]). Empedocles, "I have been a boy and a girl, a bush, a bird" (DK 31 B117, paraphrase). Plato: Meno 81; Phaedo (the argument from cycles of opposites; 81 to 82 on souls into animals); Phaedrus 246 to 249 (a thousand year cycle); Republic 10 (Myth of Er: choice of lives, Lethe, responsibility on the chooser); Timaeus 41 to 42; Laws 903 to 905 (the whole caring for the whole, each part serving it). Plotinus, Enneads 3.2 (souls as actors). Virgil, Aeneid 6 (mine).
Tier: [M] for texts and main locators; [U] for Diogenes and the Plotinus section.
Scholars: Burkert (Lore and Science in Ancient Pythagoreanism, 1972) is the standard reference. Whether Greek rebirth came from India is disputed; many say it arose independently. Obeyesekere (Imagining Karma, 2002) sets Greek and Indian rebirth side by side as ethical systems. The Myth of Er is myth; the comparers read it as doctrine.
Use: Republic 617e (blame is the chooser's, God is blameless) is the best Western line for responsibility over punishment. Laws 903 is the best for "parts serve the whole".

15. Egyptian and Druid witnesses.
Claim: Herodotus 2.123 says the Egyptians first taught that the soul passes through every creature over three thousand years, and that some Greeks borrowed it. Theosophists cite it as the Egyptian cycle of necessity. Caesar, Gallic War 6.14, says the Druids taught that souls pass to other bodies, which makes men brave. Lucan 1.454 to 458 says death is the midpoint of a long life. Diodorus 5.28 ties the Gauls to the Pythagorean doctrine.
Who: as listed; Walker and Head and Cranston repeat them.
Tier: [M] for texts and locators.
Scholars: Egyptologists (Hornung, Assmann [M]) find no Egyptian text with this transmigration cycle; the Book of the Dead has transformation spells and solar rebirth. The Druid reports are outsiders' who may have read Pythagoras into what they saw.
Use: cite Caesar and Lucan as "Greek and Roman writers report". Skip Egypt or flag Herodotus.

16. Jewish witnesses.
Claim: Josephus, Jewish War 2.163: only the good pass into another body. War 3.374 to 375: he tells his men that souls of the lawful dead are sent again into pure bodies after the revolution of the ages, and suicides go to darker Hades. Antiquities 18.14: the good have power to live again. Philo, De Gigantibus 12 to 15 [U]: souls descend into bodies and some ascend. Wisdom of Solomon 8:19 to 20 implies pre-existence. Kabbalah: gilgul in the Bahir (12th century), the Zohar, Isaac Luria and Hayyim Vital (Sha'ar ha-Gilgulim): souls return to repair, are rooted in the limbs of Adam, and may appear as ibbur or dibbuk. Saadia Gaon (933) rejected transmigration.

Tier: [M] for Josephus, Kabbalah and Saadia; [U] for Philo's locators.

Scholem (Kabbalah, 1974, "Gilgul") treats gilgul as a medieval development with disputed origins, possibly entering via the Bahir. Philo is Platonic: pre-existence, no repeated return.

## 4. How they compare: the method

1. A single-source story. The Wisdom-Religion is the trunk, and likeness proves descent. Pythagoras and the Druids are taken to share the Eastern teaching. This is a historical claim and the weakest part.

2. Term equation. Examples: devachan with sukhavati, karma with Nemesis, skandhas with germs, atma with the Over-Soul. The equations pass through Sanskrit and Tibetan terms read in nineteenth century translations (Wilson, Eitel, Beal, Muller's series).

3. The catalogue. Short quotes sorted by tradition, from Walker to Head and Cranston. It shows that the idea turns up everywhere and hides the context of each text.

4. Esoteric against exoteric. When a tradition disagrees (Buddhist no-self, a single Christian life, animal rebirth) the outer form is called corrupt or allegory, and the inner teaching is said to agree. Example: the skandhas read through a higher Ego. The method cannot lose.

5. Suppression. Where the West lacks the doctrine, a church is said to have removed it (Justinian, the Council of 553). Historians reject this.

6. Argument from reason. Justice, unequal starts, prodigies, one life too short. These do not depend on any text.

7. Authority beyond check. The Masters, and later Leadbeater's clairvoyance, supply what the texts do not.

How they explain differences: popular distortion, literal reading of allegory, priestcraft, suppression, teaching adapted to the age. They do not concede that a tradition may disagree.

Alex can keep the catalogue and the reason arguments and drop descent, suppression and the Masters.

## 5. What scholars say, short

- Julie Chajes (Recycled Lives, OUP, 2019): Blavatsky's teaching on rebirth changed between 1877 and 1888, from rare exceptions to the rule (reported by siblings as [V]; any further detail [U]).
- Campbell (1980), Godwin (1994), Hanegraaff (1996), Hammer (2001): Theosophy was a major carrier of Eastern ideas to the West, with European esoteric roots, and used the language of science and comparative religion to claim authority [M].
- Max Muller: no esoteric Buddhism (reported by sibling P01 as [V]).
- Guenon (1921, 1923): reincarnation as the West understands it is a nineteenth century French idea, and Theosophy gave karma a moral-retribution sense it lacked (reported by sibling P02 as [V]). He shares her idea of a hidden central tradition.
- Obeyesekere (Imagining Karma, 2002) and O'Flaherty (ed., Karma and Rebirth in Classical Indian Traditions, 1980) are the comparative and Indological standards. Paul Edwards (Reincarnation: A Critical Examination, 1996) is the main critic [M].
- Lopez (1998, 2011): the Tibetan Masters and the Western framing of the Bardo belong partly to Western imagination [M].

My own judgement: a skilled synthesizer with a weak method for settling disputes between sources. Her system is a fair statement of what Theosophists believed.

11. One sentence on method: Theosophists equated these terms; specialists keep several apart; the likeness is a hint, not a proof.
