## 1. The picture in brief

Three kinds of source matter. First, the lists of marks. Each tradition wrote a portrait of the person further along, and the portraits agree more than the doctrines do. The figure is steady under praise and blame, free of fear, harmless, gentle, glad without a cause, working without clutching at the result, not putting the self in front, and doing the same good to everyone. The Gita's sthitaprajna and gunatita, the Dhammapada arahant, the Sufi "soul at peace", Paul's fruit of the Spirit, Teresa's works, the Hasidic hishtavut, the Daoist true person and the Sikh brahmgiani draw one figure. This is Alex's strongest cross-tradition table because it compares conduct and not metaphysics, as William James did.

Second, the company of the realised. Nearly every tradition says the path is not walked alone and that the presence of someone further along is itself a means: sat-sanga, darshan, suhba, baraka, kalyana-mitta (the whole of the holy life), tzaddik, staretz, sangat. Several say in their own words that a place changes when such a person is in it. That is Alex's "a room settles". Krishnamurti, and in part the Buddha ("be islands"), push the other way.

Third, modern teachers. Meher Baba's God Speaks is the nearest single statement of the whole axiom. Ramana and Nisargadatta give clause one, the veil and letting go, and deny lives and stages at the last level. Yogananda gives the chain with a speed claim. Gurdjieff gives the chain without a free soul. The bridge teachers compare from inside one life.

Cautions: marks are idealised portraits; early progress can feel like loss; bright experiences are not marks.

## 2. Findings

### Block A: the marks

1. Gita 2.54 to 2.72, the steady-wisdom person (sthitaprajna).
Claim: Arjuna asks how to know such a person. The answer: gives up desires, content in the Self (2.55); unshaken in sorrow, free of attachment, fear and anger (2.56); neither exults nor recoils at good or bad (2.57); like an ocean that rivers enter without overflowing (2.70); lives without "I" and "mine" and finds peace (2.71). Nearby, 2.50 calls yoga skill in action.
Tier [M]: verse numbers confident, wording mine.
Use: released and still acting; 2.50 holds BE and DO in one line.

2. Gita 12.13 to 20 and 14.22 to 25 (the devotee dear to God; the one beyond the gunas, gunatita).
Claim: 12.13 to 14: no hatred of any being, friendly and compassionate, free of "mine", the same in pain and pleasure, forgiving. 12.15: the world is not agitated by this person, nor the person by the world. 12.16 to 19: impartial, skilful, the same in praise and blame. 14.22 to 25: unmoved by the qualities of nature, the same toward clod and gold, friend and foe, honour and dishonour. Related: 5.18 (sees the same in all), 3.20 to 25 (the realised still acts for the world's sake).
Tier [M].
Use: the gunatita alone reads like Part two's "cold observer". The Gita corrects itself in 12.13 (friendly, compassionate) and 3.20 to 25 (acts), so the answer to "isn't this becoming a vegetable" can come from the same book. Verse 12.15 is a mark of the room.

3. Dhammapada chapter 7 (verses 90 to 99), verse 81, and the stream-enterer.
Claim: the arahant has no fever and no sorrow (90); like the earth he does not resent, like a pillar or clear lake he is unmoved (95); the wise are not moved by praise or blame, like rock in wind (81); wherever arahants live, village or forest, that place is delightful (98). The stream-enterer has dropped three fetters (the view of a fixed self, doubt, clinging to rites), has unshakeable confidence in Buddha, teaching and community, and keeps the virtue dear to the noble ones (SN 55; single sutta unknown).
Tier [M]; verses 81, 95, 98 confident.
Use: verse 98 is the best Buddhist witness to "a room settles". The first fetter, the view of a fixed self, is the veil.

4. Sufi stations and states; Ghazali.
Claim: Qushayri's Risala (about 1045) separates maqamat, stations, which one earns and holds (repentance, renunciation, trust, patience, contentment), from ahwal, states, which are given and pass. A saying credited to Junayd compares states to lightning, and one that stays has become something else [attribution U]. Quran 89:27 names the soul at peace; 10:62 says the friends of God have no fear. Ghazali's Ihya' (about 1100): Book 32 on patience and gratitude; Book 35 on unity (tawhid) and trust, with four levels of tawhid, the last seeing only the One agent [book numbers M; levels U].
Tier [M], flags as marked.
Use: hal against maqam is a tradition's exact word for "everybody visits, nobody stays" (flow and the zone are states; a station is where you can stay). Ghazali's top level is clause one inside Islam.

5. Galatians 5:22 to 23, the fruit of the Spirit.
Claim: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control, against the works of the flesh (5:19 to 21: strife, jealousy, fits of anger). "Fruit" is singular: one growth, many marks. It follows the command to love the neighbour as yourself (5:14). Related: 1 Corinthians 13:4 to 7; Philippians 4:11 to 13 (content in any state); 1 John 4:18 and 4:20 to 21 (love casts out fear; love of the seen brother tests love of God).
Tier [M].
Use: "fruit" is the Christian word for what appears without being forced, which is Alex's point about letting go. Matthew 7:16 to 20 (known by fruits) is the test he proposes.

6. John of the Cross and Teresa on signs of progress.
Claim: John, Dark Night I.9 (also Ascent II.13), gives three signs that dryness is God's work and not slackness: no pleasure in God or creatures, a worried care for God, and loss of the power to meditate with imagination. Progress looks like loss. Teresa, Interior Castle (1577) V.3: the surest sign of keeping the two commandments is how one keeps the one about the neighbour. VII.4: the purpose of the spiritual marriage is the birth of good works.
Tier [M], to chapter.
Use: Teresa's neighbour test is the form of Alex's second check (gentler with the person in front of me). John's signs warn Part three that early progress may feel like less fun.

7. Hasidism: hishtavut, simcha, beinoni and tzaddik; Avot 4:1.
Claim: hishtavut (equalness) means praise and blame feel the same, built on Psalm 16:8 by the Baal Shem Tov tradition. Nachman of Breslov counts being always glad a great mitzvah (Likutey Moharan II, 24) and says the whole world is a very narrow bridge and the main thing is not to be afraid at all (II, 48) [locators moderately sure]. The Tanya (1796, chapters 1 to 14) calls the striver the beinoni, who never lets the pull to evil act, and the tzaddik the one in whom it is transformed. Avot 4:1: the rich is the one glad of his portion.
Tier [M].
Use: Nachman's bridge is BE (braced to released) in one line. The Tanya sets a reachable mark beside the rare one, which fits a chart whose corner nobody holds.

8. The Daoist sage and true person.
Claim: Daodejing 7: the sage puts himself last and is found first. 49: no fixed mind of his own; takes the people's mind as his, good to the good and also to the not good. 70: coarse cloth outside, jade inside. Zhuangzi 7 (end): the perfect person uses the mind as a mirror, welcoming nothing, storing nothing, responding. Zhuangzi 5: Wang Tai, a man with one foot cut off, has as many followers as Confucius, and people come empty and leave full though he gives no talk; Ai Tai Tuo is an ugly man everyone wants near.
Tier [M], chapter level.
Use: Zhuangzi 5 is a vivid "a room settles" with no lesson given. DDJ 20 and 70 say the person may look like a drifter, which is why the Drifting room needs the test of acting. The mirror answers the "cold observer".

9. The Sikh gurmukh, brahmgiani and jivan mukt.
Claim: the Guru Granth Sahib sets the gurmukh (turned toward the Guru, the Word) against the manmukh (turned toward his own mind). Guru Arjan's Sukhmani (8th ashtapadi) describes the brahmgiani: the same toward all, unmoved by pleasure and pain, like a lotus in water. Jivan mukt is freed while alive; the sadh sangat is the company where this is absorbed. Tradition sums Nanak's way as earn by honest work (kirat karo), remember the Name (naam japo), share (vand chhako).
Tier [M]; page numbers unknown; the threefold phrase is later tradition.
Use for Part three: stillness, work and giving in one line, a precedent for Alex's method.

10. Stoic marks and William James.
Claim: Epictetus, Enchiridion 48, gives signs of progress: he blames, praises and accuses no one, says nothing about himself as if he were someone, expects harm and benefit from himself alone. James (Varieties, 1902), Lectures 11 to 13: the saintly character has a sense of a wider life, elation and freedom as the self's outline softens, and a shift toward loving affections; the fruits are asceticism, strength of soul, purity, charity. Lecture 1: judge by fruits, not roots.
Tier [M]; James is public domain, so a verified quotation is possible later.
Use: Epictetus 48 is the earliest Western list of progress marks, and Marcus is already in the essay. James is the best prior argument that marks, not doctrine, show agreement.

11. Tests and false marks.
Claim: Matthew 7:16 to 20 (fruits). Didache 11 to 12: a travelling teacher who asks for money is false. MN 47: investigate even the Buddha, over a long time, for greed, hate and delusion. AN 3.65 (Kalama Sutta): test teachings by their results. Visuddhimagga XX: ten "corruptions of insight" (light, rapture, tranquillity, bliss and others) mistaken for attainment. A saying in Indian tradition, repeated around Ramana, holds that only a knower recognises a knower [source U].
Tier [M], flags as marked.
Use: "what you can check" sits in this old genre. Say the tests that work are slow (conduct over time) and a vivid experience is not one.

### Block A, continued: the company of the realised

12. Satsang and darshan (Hindu).
Claim: Bhaja Govindam 9, traditionally by Shankara, gives a chain: good company, then non-attachment, then freedom from delusion, then steadiness, then liberation in life (jivanmukti). Gita 4.34: learn by humble approach, questions and service. Mundaka 1.2.12: go with fuel in hand to a teacher established in Brahman. Darshan is seeing and being seen by a realised person as itself a transmission; Ramana's silence follows the Dakshinamurti picture of a silent young teacher who settles old pupils' doubts. In the West "satsang" often means a talk in the line of Poonja, a Ramana devotee.
Tier [M]; the Shankara attribution is traditional and doubted by some scholars.
Use: verse 9 is the best Indian text that nearness to someone further along carries a person toward freedom.

13. Baraka and suhba (Islam, Sufi).
Claim: baraka is the blessing carried by a holy person; suhba is companionship with the teacher, which the Naqshbandi put above method. Quran 9:119 (be with the truthful), 18:28 (keep yourself with those who call on their Lord at morning and evening). The hadith of the perfume seller and the blacksmith: sit with the first and you carry away a scent even without buying (Bukhari 2101, Muslim 2628).
Tier [M]; hadith numbers moderately sure.
Use: the perfumer is the cleanest old picture of a room settling without a lesson.

14. Kalyana-mitta, admirable friendship (Buddhism).
Claim: in SN 45.2 (Upaddha Sutta) Ananda calls admirable friendship half the holy life, and the Buddha says it is the whole, since with such a friend one can expect to develop the eightfold path. SN 45.49 [number U] compares it to dawn, forerunner of sunrise. Ud 4.1 and AN 9.3 (Meghiya) put it first among things that ripen liberation. Against that, DN 16 has the Buddha tell the community to be islands to themselves and to treat the teaching as teacher after his death. Tibetan manuals list the marks of a qualified teacher (ten in a list Tsongkhapa follows) [list U].
Tier [M] for SN 45.2, DN 16, Ud 4.1, AN 9.3.
Use: SN 45.2 backs "people further along" in the Buddha's own mouth; DN 16 shows self-reliance held beside it.

15. Tzaddik and rebbe (Judaism).
Claim: Avot 1:6, make yourself a teacher and acquire a friend; 1:16, make yourself a teacher and be free of doubt; 6:6 lists service of the sages and close companionship among the forty-eight ways to acquire Torah. Hasidism calls the tzaddik the foundation of the world (Proverbs 10:25). Buber's Tales of the Hasidim retells a disciple who went to the Maggid not to learn Torah but to see how he tied his shoes [Rabbi Leib; wording and source U].
Tier [M], story [U].
Use: Avot 1:6 is a short checkable line for "find someone further along, and a friend".

16. Staretz and spiritual father (Orthodox, with the West).
Claim: the Desert Fathers' sayings; John Climacus, Ladder, step 4 (obedience to a guide); the Russian elders (Zosima in Dostoevsky); and the saying credited to Seraphim of Sarov that if you acquire a peaceful spirit thousands around you will be saved [source and wording U]. The elder is chosen for discernment, not learning.
Tier [M]; Seraphim [U].
Use: Seraphim's line, if it verifies, is the Christian form of "a room settles", stated as a cause.

### The schools and the guide

17. The Traditionalists.
Claim: Guenon (Aperçus sur l'initiation, 1946) says real spiritual work needs qualification, transmission through a regular chain of initiation (silsila in Sufi terms), and inner work; initiation outside a living tradition is void. He wrote against Theosophy (Le Theosophisme, 1921) and spiritism (L'Erreur spirite, 1923) and lived in Cairo as a Muslim. Schuon founded the Maryamiyya (1965) from an Alawi Sufi line. The master is a channel of baraka. They ask the seeker to join one orthodox form, not to compare freely.
Scholars: Sedgwick, Against the Modern World (2004), includes controversies about Schuon's community [details U]. See P02 to P06.
Tier [M].
Use: Alex borrows the comparisons, not the rule. They would reject a seeker with no tradition and condemned Theosophy, which he also leans on. Say so in the essay.

18. Huxley and Heard.
Claim: Huxley's foreword to The Perennial Philosophy says knowledge is a function of being, and that direct knowledge of the Ground comes to those who have made themselves loving, pure in heart and poor in spirit [gist M]. Chapter 2 treats the saint as proof. Huxley and Gerald Heard (1889 to 1971) went to California together and studied with Swami Prabhavananda. Huxley wrote the foreword to Krishnamurti's The First and Last Freedom (1954). Heard's The Ascent of Humanity (1929) and The Five Ages of Man (1963) describe consciousness evolving in stages [details U].
Tier [M].
Use: "function of being" says marks matter more than beliefs. Heard is a rarely cited precedent for an arrow of consciousness.

19. Krishnamurti against gurus.
Claim: raised by the Theosophists (Besant, Leadbeater) as the coming World Teacher, he dissolved the Order of the Star in the East at Ommen on 3 August 1929, saying truth is a pathless land that no organisation or teacher can lead anyone to. He taught for sixty more years, denied being a guru, and held that no method or authority reaches freedom. Critics note the paradox of a teacher who disclaims teaching.
Tier [M].
Use: the best counterweight to findings 12 to 17, and he came out of one of Alex's two movements. Part three should admit that following a guide can fail.

20. Wilber: translation and transformation, states and stages.
Claim: religion has two jobs. Translation gives the self at its present level meaning; transformation moves it to a new level (A Sociable God, 1983; Integral Spirituality, 2006, with the Wilber-Combs lattice of states against stages). A peak state does not make someone further along; development does. In his integral practice the Three Jewels map onto I, it and we [locator U]. Ferrer (2002) rejects the common core behind it.
Tier [M].
Use: marks should be conduct, not visions. It fits the Drifting room and Welwood's bypassing.

21. Theosophy: Masters, chela, the Golden Stairs.
Claim: Blavatsky's circle taught elder brothers (Mahatmas), a probation for the chela, and a list of qualities called the Golden Stairs (1889): clean life, open mind, pure heart, brotherhood for all, readiness to give and take instruction, courage under injustice, defence of the unjustly attacked [count and wording U]. The Society's first object is universal brotherhood.
Tier [M], list [U].
Use: a Theosophical mark-list whose brotherhood object is the axiom's ethic. Scholars treat the Masters as contested (Campbell, Godwin; T01 to T09).

### Block B: modern teachers

For each: which clauses and links of the chain it states, and what it adds or contradicts.

22. Ramana Maharshi (1879 to 1950).
States: clause one (the Self alone is); the veil as the I-thought, the root thought all others follow, to be traced to its source in the Heart; letting go by two roads to one place, self-enquiry ("Who am I?", 1902 Q and A) and surrender to God.
Lives, karma, stages: he answered by the questioner's level. For those who think they are bodies, rebirth holds; from the Self there is neither birth nor liberation, so no stages [gist M; Talks number and wording U].
Adds: the veil as one thought. Contradicts the chain at both ends: if the Self was never bound, the whole does not experience separation, it only seems to.
Tier [M]. Sources: Talks with Sri Ramana Maharshi (1955); Who Am I?
Use: for letting go, point to the root, the I-thought. "Neo-Advaita" built "nothing to do" on him, which his own record does not fully support.

23. Nisargadatta Maharaj (1897 to 1981).
States: clause one (the Absolute); the veil as identifying with the body and the sense "I am", the first arising; letting go as staying with "I am", then going past it. I Am That (1973, talks translated by Maurice Frydman); Inchegeri Navnath line. A widely quoted saying: wisdom says I am nothing, love says I am everything, and between them life flows [gist M, place U].
Lives, karma: the person does not reincarnate; cause and effect belong to appearance [U]. Stages: none.
Adds: a plain bridge between "nothing" and "everything". Contradicts the arrow: no ladder. The English was shaped by editors [U].
Tier [M], some [U].

24. Meher Baba (1894 to 1969), God Speaks (1955).
States, in gist: God in the Beyond-Beyond state is infinite and unaware of itself; a Whim (lahar) arises, "Who am I?", and creation unfolds so the One can know itself. Consciousness evolves through forms (stone to human); the soul reincarnates in human forms, then passes inward through six planes to the seventh, God-realisation. Impressions (sanskaras) bind and must be worn away. Perfect Masters help; the Avatar returns when needed [outline M, plane details U].
Maps: clause one; clause two (the Whim, the dream of separation); clause three (God-realisation); also veil, lives, karma as sanskara, stages, return.
Adds: a reason for separation and a purpose for lives. Differs: evolution through animal forms, and the Avatar claim.
Tier [M].
Use: the nearest one-book statement of the axiom. Cite as a modern witness only, since it is revealed teaching and not argument.

25. Sri Yukteswar (1855 to 1936) and Yogananda (1893 to 1952).
States: clause one (Spirit as being, consciousness, bliss); the veil as a cosmic dream or film in which individuals are dreamed; reincarnation and karma until freed; seven realms (lokas) read as states of consciousness; return as cosmic consciousness. The Holy Science (1894) sets out to show Revelation and the Gita in harmony; Autobiography of a Yogi is from 1946.
Adds: speed. Yogananda calls Kriya yoga an airplane route against the bullock cart of natural evolution (ch. 26) and puts figures on it [gist M, figures U]. Also a saying that environment is stronger than will [gist M, source U], the "room" idea.
Contradicts: Yukteswar's yuga chronology is not accepted by Indologists.
Tier [M].
Use: bullock cart and airplane parallels run, bike, car, fly.

26. Gurdjieff (about 1866 to 1949), Ouspensky (1878 to 1947), Needleman (1934 to 2022).
States: Ray of Creation from the Absolute through All Worlds, All Suns (the Milky Way), Sun, planets, Earth, Moon (Ouspensky, In Search of the Miraculous, 1949) [order partly U]. Clause two as sleep: people are machines with many small "I"s, identified with things. Letting go as non-identification and self-remembering.
Being against knowing: knowledge alone gives little; understanding comes from knowledge and being together. Huxley's "function of being" says the same.
Soul: not given, built by Work (effort, attention, school). Ordinary people die without it, and recurrence replaces reincarnation for those who do not change. So the Work contradicts a soul that continues automatically.
Stages: Man numbers 1 to 7 (see P18). Needleman bridges to Christianity (Lost Christianity, 1980). Gurdjieff's claims are disputed.
Tier [M].
Use: the clearest school of "stillness and work together" (Part three); "All Suns" is the galaxy rung of Alex's arrow.

27. Bede Griffiths (1906 to 1993).
States: the Trinity read as being, consciousness, bliss (sat-cit-ananda): Father, Word, Spirit. The Fall as loss of unity, the rational dual mind against the intuitive; return as unitive knowledge (Return to the Centre, 1976; The Marriage of East and West, 1982). On rebirth he thought it possibly compatible with purgatory [U].
Tier [M], [U] as marked.
Use: comparison by structure (G1-6); Panikkar warns against equating.

28. Abhishiktananda (Henri Le Saux, 1910 to 1973).
States: clause one as experience, an "awakening" in which the "I" falls away (Saccidananda, 1974; Guru and Disciple, 1970). A heart attack on 14 July 1973 deepened it. Late letters say Christian forms cannot hold what he met (du Boulay, 1998).
Adds: honesty; contradicts easy reconciliation.
Tier [M].
Use: a witness against over-tidy harmony.

29. Thomas Merton (1915 to 1968).
States: true self against false (Zen and the Birds of Appetite, 1968). On 18 March 1958 at a Louisville street corner he saw others as shining and was released from the illusion of being a separate special being (Conjectures of a Guilty Bystander, 1966). In Calcutta, October 1968, he said in gist that we are already one, imagine we are not, and must recover our original unity (Asian Journal, 1973) [wording and page U].
Adds: the Calcutta sentence has the three clauses almost word for word.
Tier [M]; the Calcutta wording [U], check first.
Use: if verified, one Merton sentence can sit beside the axiom as a Christian statement of it.

## 4. How they compare

Marks first. James and the lists compare by conduct, so a Buddhist with no self, a Hindu with a Self and a Christian with a God can still be matched on fruit. This is Alex's strongest method because it needs no agreement on metaphysics.

The anthology. Huxley sets passages by theme (see P07, P08). It argues by selection, and he explains differences by temperament and by whether the writer met the conditions in the foreword.

Outer and inner. Guenon and Schuon say forms differ outwardly and converge in the inner core, treating the difference as one truth adapted to a people.

The teacher as translator. Ramana answers Christians with the burning bush's "I am" and with "be still" (Psalm 46:10) [locator U]. Yukteswar lays the Bible over Sankhya. Baba maps his chart onto Vedantic and Sufi names. Griffiths reads the Trinity as sat-cit-ananda. They compare from experience inside one vocabulary; Abhishiktananda shows the strain.

Equivalent, not identical. Panikkar speaks of equivalents and refuses a common essence (G1-6). It is the method for "same things in different words" that keeps specialists on side.

Differences are explained by language, outer against inner layer, the questioner's level (so Ramana's and Baba's answers seem to conflict), temperament, and rarely real disagreement.

## 5. What scholars say

Lists of marks are idealised portraits written inside communities, so they describe a norm, not a measured person. Katz (1978) argued that mystical experience is shaped by tradition, so marks reflect each tradition's values: equanimity and duty in the Gita, no clinging in Buddhism, love in Christianity, spontaneity in Daoism. Forman defends a shared pure-consciousness event; Ferrer rejects the perennial core. The Traditionalists are studied as a school with an exclusive rule (Sedgwick). Theosophy is treated as an influential esoteric movement with a contested reputation (Campbell, Godwin). Each modern teacher has devoted and critical biographers; the Gurdjieff record and the Poonja satsang scene are the most disputed. Indologists reject the Holy Science's dates. All from memory.
