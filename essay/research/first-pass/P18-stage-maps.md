## 1. The picture in brief

Almost every tradition that describes a path describes it in steps, and the steps carry names that sound like distance: mansions, valleys, heavens, spheres, grounds, gates, rungs, worlds, planes. The comparers use these maps in four ways. Underhill (1911) lays Christian maps side by side and claims one human pattern. The perennialist and Traditionalist writers (Schuon, Nasr, Smith, Coomaraswamy, Guenon) treat the ladder of the soul and the ladder of the cosmos as one ladder seen from two sides. Wilber and Brown test it on meditation stages. The Theosophists build one numbered map from many sources.

Three things recur. The number of rungs varies (four, five, seven, nine, ten, fourteen, thirty, one hundred), and seven is frequent, which in Mediterranean and Near Eastern cases probably comes from the seven classical planets and not from a shared inner fact. Most maps allow falls. And the end of the ladder is handled in four ways: rest (Jain siddha, Theravada parinibbana), no end (Gregory of Nyssa, Ibn Arabi), return into action (bodhisattva, Teresa's seventh mansion, the tenth ox-herding picture, baqa, Theosophy's Nirmanakaya), and denial that there was ever a ladder (Gaudapada, sudden Zen, Wilber's "one taste").

Alex's arrow, with a stage called birth after plus four, fits the return family and the second-birth family. He can say honestly that the traditions disagree about what comes after the last rung.

## 2. Findings

### 2.1 Underhill's five stages
Claim: the mystic way has five stages: awakening of the self, purification, illumination, the dark night of the soul (which she also treats as surrender), union. Behind it is the older threefold way (purgative, illuminative, unitive) from Pseudo-Dionysius.
Who: Evelyn Underhill, Mysticism (1911), Part Two "The Mystic Way"; chapter numbers locator unknown.
Words (paraphrase): the stages overlap and the soul can swing back. The unitive life is active, with Catherine of Siena, Teresa and Francis as models.
Cites: mostly Christian mystics, some Plotinus, Sufi and Hindu material.
Tier: [M] names and order; [U] her reading of the stages as the life of Christ re-enacted in the soul.
Use: the one comparer with a short numbered ladder, a low point before the top, and an ending that is work in the world. A short witness that the top is not an exit. Overlaps P11.

### 2.2 Teresa's seven mansions and John of the Cross
Claim: Teresa of Avila, The Interior Castle (1577): a castle of one diamond or clear crystal, with seven groups of mansions. One, self-knowledge. Two, perseverance. Three, an orderly life that can go dry and proud. Four, the prayer of quiet, where she says the supernatural begins. Five, the prayer of union (the silkworm becomes a butterfly). Six, the betrothal, with raptures and trials. Seven, the spiritual marriage. She says the purpose of the marriage is the birth of good works, and that Martha and Mary go together.
John of the Cross (1570s and 1580s): two nights, of the senses and of the spirit, each with an active and a passive side; beginners, proficients, the perfect; and in Dark Night book 2, around chapters 18 to 20 [U], ten steps of the ladder of love taken from Bernard and Aquinas.
Tier: [M] seven mansions and themes, ten steps; [U] chapter numbers.
Use: the seventh mansion is a Christian text where arrival produces action and, in her wording, birth. It supports Alex's point that "birth" is a beginning in John and a state in Eckhart.

### 2.3 The ladder family
Claim: the ladder is the oldest cross-tradition image of staged ascent, and each version counts differently. Counts, all from memory:
Genesis 28:12 and 17, angels ascending and descending, the gate of heaven [M]. Plato, Symposium 210a to 212a, Diotima's ladder from one beautiful body to Beauty itself [M]. John Climacus, Ladder of Divine Ascent (about 600): thirty steps, the last four on stillness, prayer, dispassion, and faith, hope and love; the icon shows monks dragged off the ladder by demons [M]. Rule of Benedict ch. 7: twelve degrees of humility, introduced with Jacob's ladder [M]. Bonaventure, Itinerarium mentis in Deum (1259): six stages after the six wings of the seraph, then a seventh of rest [M]. Origen, Contra Celsum VI.22, reporting Celsus on the Mithraic mysteries: a ladder with seven gates and an eighth above, with seven grades (Raven, Bridegroom, Soldier, Lion, Persian, Sun-runner, Father) [M]. Corpus Hermeticum I (Poimandres): the soul climbs through the seven planetary spheres, giving back one vice at each, reaches the eighth, and becomes divine [M]. Paul's "third heaven", 2 Corinthians 12:2 [M]. Hagigah 12b names seven heavens: Vilon, Raqia, Shehaqim, Zevul, Maon, Makhon, Aravot [M].
Tier: [M]; the order of the vices [U].
Use: one paragraph can call these a family. Climacus's falls match the Jain scheme (2.10).

### 2.4 Dante, the Mi'raj, and the scale of the heavens
Claim: Dante, Divina Commedia (about 1308 to 1320). Paradiso has ten heavens: Moon, Mercury, Venus, Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, the fixed stars, the Primum Mobile, the Empyrean. Purgatorio has seven terraces (pride, envy, wrath, sloth, avarice, gluttony, lust), Inferno nine circles. The Empyrean is outside space and time and has no "where" except the divine mind (around Par. XXVII, XXX) [M, lines U].
Words (paraphrase): in Paradiso I Beatrice says his rising should surprise him no more than a stream running downhill, since a soul freed of hindrance rises as a flame does [M, lines U]. In XXI, at Saturn, he sees a golden ladder going up past sight [M]. In XXVIII he sees a point of light ringed by nine circles of angels, and the circles nearest the point turn fastest, so speed rises with nearness to God [M]. In the last canto he sees the scattered leaves of the universe bound by love in one volume [M]. The nine angelic orders are Pseudo-Dionysius's, in three triads: Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones; Dominions, Virtues, Powers; Principalities, Archangels, Angels [M].
Cites: the Islamic Mi'raj is the other great graded journey: Muhammad ascends through seven heavens (Qur'an 17:1; 53:1 to 18), in the hadith one prophet each, Adam first and Abraham seventh, to the Lote tree of the farthest boundary, beyond which Gabriel does not go [M]. Asin Palacios (1919) argued that Dante drew on the Mi'raj and Ibn Arabi; Cerulli (1949) added the Latin and French "Book of the Ladder" [M]. Most scholars call a direct source possible and unproven [M].
Tier: [M].
Use: the best cultural precedent for Alex's picture. The old maps were already scale maps with the planets as steps; his solar system, galaxy and universe are the modern version. Dante's natural rising and speed rising with nearness fit his speed and gravity picture. Check cantos before use.

### 2.5 Sufi stations and states, Ansari, Attar
Claim: Sufi writers separate stations (maqamat), won by effort and stable, from states (ahwal, singular hal), which are gifts and pass. Sarraj, Kitab al-Luma (about 988): seven stations (repentance, scrupulousness, renunciation, poverty, patience, trust in God, contentment) [M] and ten states (watchfulness, nearness, love, fear, hope, longing, intimacy, tranquillity, contemplation, certainty) [U on the list and order]. Ansari of Herat (d. 1089), Manazil al-Sa'irin: one hundred stations in ten sections of ten [M], section names [U], ending in three grades of tawhid (of the common, the elect, the elect of the elect) [M].
Attar, The Conference of the Birds (about 1177): seven valleys, Quest, Love, Insight, Detachment, Unity, Bewilderment, Poverty and Annihilation [M]. Thirty birds arrive; "thirty birds" is the meaning of the king's name, the Simurgh, and they find that they are the one they sought [M]. Subsistence (baqa) follows annihilation.
Cites: Islamic only; survey: Schimmel, Mystical Dimensions of Islam (1975) [M].
Use: Attar carries Alex's axiom in a story: many, a journey, a mirror, one. Valleys are a distance word. Thousands set out and thirty arrive.

### 2.6 Ibn Arabi, the four journeys, baqa
Claim: Ibn Arabi (1165 to 1240), al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya, 560 chapters in six parts (knowledge, practices, states, abodes, meetings, stations) [M, parts U]. The highest rank is the station of no station (maqam la maqam), a person not fixed in any one station [M; Chittick, The Sufi Path of Knowledge, 1989]. He also holds that the Real never shows itself twice in the same form, so no disclosure is last [M].
Mulla Sadra (d. 1640), The Four Journeys: from creation to the Real; in the Real with the Real; from the Real to creation with the Real; in creation with the Real [M]. He taught that the soul keeps changing in substance after death [M; see part-2-notes].
Fana (passing away) and baqa (subsisting): baqa is usually read as return to life from the other side [M; P11].
Use: the third and fourth journeys are the cleanest short formula for return as action. For "no last rung", pair Ibn Arabi with Nyssa (2.15).

### 2.7 Kabbalah
Claim: four worlds: Atzilut (emanation), Beriah (creation), Yetzirah (formation), Asiyah (action), the lower three tied to Isaiah 43:7 [M]. Ten sefirot: Keter, Chokhmah, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, Malkhut, with Da'at sometimes counted for Keter [M]. Beyond them is Ein Sof, the Without End [M]. In Safed Kabbalah and Chabad the morning prayer is a climb through the four worlds [M]. Luria's scheme: withdrawal (tzimtzum), breaking of the vessels, repair (tikkun), in which scattered sparks are lifted back [M]. The Pardes story (Hagigah 14b): four entered the orchard, one died, one was harmed, one lost his faith, and Akiva entered and left in peace [M]. Survey: Scholem, Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism (1941), who notes Neoplatonic and Gnostic influence [M].
Tier: [M]; [U] when the four worlds became a fixed scheme.
Use: the Lurianic sparks are the closest Jewish match to one, separation, return. Pardes warns that ascent can hurt. Overlaps P14, P15.

### 2.8 Buddhist four stages, jhanas, 31 planes
Claim: the early path has four stages, each with a path moment and a fruit, so eight kinds of noble person: stream-enterer (sotapanna), once-returner (sakadagami), non-returner (anagami), arahant [M]. They are defined by the ten fetters. Stream entry cuts the first three (self-view, doubt, clinging to rites) and carries at most seven more lives; once-returner weakens desire and ill will; non-returner cuts them and is reborn in the Pure Abodes (five, the highest Akanittha) to finish there; arahantship cuts desire for form, desire for the formless, conceit, restlessness and ignorance [M].
Jhanas: four form jhanas, four formless attainments (infinite space, infinite consciousness, nothingness, neither perception nor non-perception), and the cessation of perception and feeling, nine abidings in all [M; Anupada Sutta, MN 111, locator U]. Cosmology has 31 planes: 11 sense-sphere, 16 form, 4 formless [M]. Sutras speak of thousandfold, thousand-thousandfold and thousand-thousand-thousandfold world systems [M, locator U].
Use: the first formless rung is literally called infinite space, so a Buddhist stage name already is "space". The 1,000 by 1,000 world systems echo his zoom; present it as an echo, not a source.

### 2.9 The ten bhumis, and the bodhisattva who returns
Claim: ten grounds: Pramudita (Joyful), Vimala (Stainless), Prabhakari (Luminous), Arcismati (Radiant), Sudurjaya (Hard to Conquer), Abhimukhi (Facing), Duramgama (Far-going), Acala (Immovable), Sadhumati (Good Intelligence), Dharmamegha (Cloud of Dharma) [M]. Source: the Dasabhumika, part of the Avatamsaka Sutra; Chandrakirti's Madhyamakavatara (7th century) is built on it [M]. They sit inside five paths (accumulation, preparation, seeing, meditation, no more learning), the first bhumi beginning at seeing [M]. Chinese schemes run to fifty-two stages, Tibetan lamrim (Atisha, Tsongkhapa) to three scopes of person [M].
Beyond the last: the vow to stay until all are free; non-abiding nirvana, where wisdom keeps one out of samsara and compassion keeps one out of nirvana [M]; the Heart Sutra mantra, gone, gone, gone beyond, gone altogether beyond, awakening [M]. The ten ox-herding pictures (Kuoan, 12th century): seeking, tracks, first glimpse, catching, taming, riding home, ox forgotten, both forgotten (an empty circle), return to the source, entering the marketplace with open hands [M]. Earlier sets stopped at the empty circle, and the last pictures are the return [M].
Use: the tenth picture and non-abiding nirvana are the sharpest Buddhist answers to "what is after the last rung". Seventh bhumi is "far-going", a distance word. Shantideva's vow, in Alex's draft already, is Bodhicaryavatara ch. 3 [U].

### 2.10 Jain fourteen stages and where the freed soul goes
Claim: fourteen gunasthanas: 1 mithyatva (deluded view); 2 sasvadana (a taste of right view while falling); 3 misra (mixed); 4 avirata samyagdrishti (right view, no vows); 5 desavirata (partial vows, the householder); 6 pramatta-samyata (ascetic, lapsing); 7 apramatta-samyata (ascetic, vigilant); 8 apurvakarana (unprecedented purity); 9 anivrtti-karana; 10 suksma-samparaya (only subtle passions); 11 upasanta-kasaya (passions suppressed); 12 ksina-kasaya (passions destroyed); 13 sayogi-kevalin (omniscient, embodied, active); 14 ayogi-kevalin (activity stilled), then siddha [M; the English glosses are mine]. A soul at stage 11 falls back because the passions were quieted and not destroyed; from 12 there is no return [M]. Sources: Shatkhandagama, Gommatasara, the Karmagranthas; survey: Jaini, The Jaina Path of Purification (1979) [M].
Where the freed soul goes: Tattvartha Sutra ch. 10, around sutras 5 to 8 [U]: it rises straight to the end of the universe by its own nature, like a flame or a gourd freed of its clay, and stops because beyond is aloka, empty space with no medium of motion [M]. The cosmos is a person fourteen rajju tall [M]; a rajju is defined by a god flying at a stated speed for six months [M-, figure U].
Use: the clearest ancient match to gravity and escape velocity: karma as weight, release as natural ascent, a hard edge of the cosmos. It also shows that the Jain ladder ends in stillness at the boundary, not return. Jain souls stay many, so this is a witness to staged growth and not to the one whole (part-2-notes).

### 2.11 Yoga Vasistha, Patanjali, and the jivanmukta
Claim: Yoga Vasistha (Kashmir, built up over centuries, core perhaps 10th century [M-]): seven stages of knowledge: subheccha (good wish), vicarana (inquiry), tanumanasa (thinning of mind), sattvapatti (attaining clarity), asamsakti (non-attachment), padarthabhavana (non-perception of objects), turyaga (going to the fourth) [M]. The first three are described as still waking, the last four as the liberated; how they map to waking, dream, deep sleep and turiya varies by expositor [U]. It also lists seven stages of ignorance, so fourteen in two ladders [M; around sargas 117 and 118, U].
Patanjali: eight limbs (II.29: yama, niyama, asana, pranayama, pratyahara, dharana, dhyana, samadhi); samadhi with seed (vitarka, vichara, ananda, asmita) and without (I.17 to 18, 41 to 51); a sevenfold last wisdom (II.27); the highest samadhi called dharma-megha (IV.29); kaivalya as the return of the gunas to their source (IV.34) [M].
The jivanmukta who stays: Advaita defends it (Vidyaranya, Jivanmuktiviveka, 14th century); Shankara allows that begun karma runs on until the body falls (locator U) [M]. The Gita says the realized person acts to hold the world together (III.20 to 25, Janaka as example) [M].
Gaudapada, Mandukya Karika II.32: from the highest standpoint there is no dissolution, no origination, no one bound, no seeker, no one freed [M, paraphrase].
Use: Gaudapada fits Alex's "treat the labels as labels". Patanjali's Yoga is dualist (purusha apart from prakriti), so equating kaivalya with Advaita liberation is the comparers' move, not Patanjali's.

### 2.12 Plotinus and Plato
Claim: Plotinus (Enneads, six groups of nine, edited by Porphyry about 301): three hypostases (the One, Intellect, Soul), matter as the lowest limit, procession out, conversion back [M]. The way up runs through the grades of virtue (civic, purificatory, then contemplative and exemplary in Porphyry, Sentences 32) and ends in the flight of the alone to the alone (VI.9.11, wording varies) [M]. Porphyry says Plotinus reached union four times in his presence and Porphyry once, at sixty-eight (Life 23) [M]. Plato: divided line, four grades (Republic VI, 509d to 511e); the cave, with the compelled return of the philosopher (VII, 514a to 520e); the place above the heavens (Phaedrus 247c) [M].
Use: Republic VII is the oldest "must go back down" text, a good partner to the bodhisattva. The Traditionalists equate the One with Brahman, Ein Sof and the Godhead. Scholars debate Indian influence on Plotinus [M].

### 2.13 Gurdjieff
Claim: seven categories of man. One, two and three are men of the moving-instinctive, emotional and intellectual centres, living by habit; four is balanced through work; five has a unified permanent I; six has objective consciousness, not yet permanent; seven has all a person can have [M; Ouspensky, In Search of the Miraculous, 1949, chapter U]. The ray of creation runs from the Absolute through all worlds, all suns, the Sun, all planets and the Earth to the Moon, with a number of laws at each step, 1, 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 96 [M, numbers U]. The law of octaves says a seven-step process stalls at two intervals unless a shock is added [M].
Use: distance from the Absolute counts the steps, and a soul is made and not given. That differs from Alex's soul that continues; flag it.

### 2.14 Theosophy: portals, two paths, initiations, planes
Claim: Blavatsky, The Voice of the Silence (1889), three fragments: the Voice of the Silence, the Two Paths, the Seven Portals [M]. The portals are dana, shila, kshanti, viraga, virya, dhyana, prajna: the six Mahayana perfections with viraga (indifference) inserted [M]. The Two Paths set personal liberation against renunciation, whose master is a Nirmanakaya who stays to help [M]. The Secret Doctrine (1888) states as its third proposition the identity of all souls with the Over-Soul and the obligatory pilgrimage of every soul through the cycle of incarnation [M, paraphrase]. Seven principles of the human being (sthula sharira, prana, linga sharira, kama, manas, buddhi, atma), seven planes, seven rounds [M]. Besant and Leadbeater, then Alice Bailey, add initiations tied to the Christian life (Birth, Baptism, Transfiguration, Crucifixion or Renunciation, Resurrection) and, in Leadbeater, to the four Buddhist stages plus the Master [M first five, U rest]. Bailey and Leadbeater set the human steps inside a stack of planetary and solar Logoi [M, detail U].
Tier: [M] portals, principles, two paths; [U] later initiations and the Logoi.
Use: the only existing map that puts human steps inside a cosmic hierarchy reaching the solar system and beyond, which is Alex's plus two to plus four. It is also where a "first birth" initiation appears. Caution: Buddhist scholars say the Theosophical Nirmanakaya and Pratyekabuddha are not the Buddhist ones; in the trikaya, nirmanakaya is the visible emanation body. Part one bans school vocabulary.

### 2.15 Birth, and what lies after it
Claim: Eckhart preaches the birth of the Son in the ground of the soul (main text the sermon on Wisdom 18:14, "while all things were in quiet silence", German Works no. 101 [U]), then a breakthrough (Durchbruch) into the Godhead beyond God, so the birth is not last (Sermon 52, "Blessed are the poor in spirit", where he prays God to rid him of God) [M]. John 3:3 and 3:7 use the Greek anothen, again or from above [M]. A dvija, twice-born, is one who has received the upanayana initiation [M]. Teresa's seventh mansion ends in the birth of works (2.2). Gregory of Nyssa, Life of Moses (390s), builds on Philippians 3:13 the idea of epektasis, a never-ending stretch toward God, so union does not stop the ascent [M]. Theosis (2 Peter 1:4) is the Orthodox word for the end [M].
Use: Alex's paragraph already says the traditions use the image differently. Add that Eckhart himself puts something after the birth. Epektasis is the best Christian authority for "even plus four may be a stage".

### 2.16 Aurobindo, Teilhard, Bucke
Claim: Aurobindo, The Life Divine (serialised 1914 to 1919, book 1939 to 1940): matter, life, mind, then above mind Higher Mind, Illumined Mind, Intuition, Overmind, Supermind [M]. Overmind still divides the aspects of the One into separate powers and Supermind sees them as one [M-]. The point is a descent that transforms life, not escape; the Mother announced on 29 February 1956 that the supramental had manifested [M]. Teilhard, The Phenomenon of Man (1955): matter, life, mind, Omega, and union differentiates, so the end keeps persons [M]. Bucke, Cosmic Consciousness (1901): simple, self and cosmic consciousness,, expecting it to become common, with Buddha, Jesus, Paul and Whitman among his examples [M, list U].
Use: the nearest precedents for a "new stage of evolution". Teilhard answers the worry that return means loss of self. Traditionalists reject all three (2.17).

### 2.17 The perennialist and Traditionalist comparers on levels
Claim: Huston Smith, Forgotten Truth (1976): four levels of reality (terrestrial, intermediate, celestial, infinite) matched to four of selfhood (body, mind, soul, spirit), the higher including the lower; he credits Schuon [M]. Schuon (The Transcendent Unity of Religions, French 1948; Esoterism as Principle and as Way, 1981): degrees of Reality (Beyond-Being, Being, Existence), and for the Muslim path sharia, tariqa, haqiqa, matched with exoterism, initiation, gnosis [M, pages U]. Nasr, An Introduction to Islamic Cosmological Doctrines (1964) and Knowledge and the Sacred (1981): the cosmic hierarchy and the five Divine Presences (Hahut, Lahut, Jabarut, Malakut, Nasut) [M, naming M-]. Guenon, The Symbolism of the Cross (1931) and The Multiple States of the Being (1932): a vertical axis of degrees and a horizontal axis of range, crossing at the Universal Man [M]. Coomaraswamy: the sun as the single exit of the cosmos, in "Svayamatrnna: Janua Coeli" (1938) and "The Sun-Kiss" (1940), with Mundaka Upanishad I.2.11 [M idea, U verse]. Perry's Treasury of Traditional Wisdom (1971) is a themed anthology; stage chapters unknown [U]. Huxley, The Perennial Philosophy (1945): no stage map of his own that I recall [U].
Method: cosmos and person are one ladder, so distance words stand for degrees of reality, not kilometres [M].
Use: mapping stages onto space is a classical move, and Traditionalist readers will say "up" is symbolic. Guenon's cross, two axes meeting at a centre, is a coincidence with Alex's drawing, not a source. These writers reject evolution and read history as decline, so "new stage of evolution" runs against them.

### 2.18 Wilber and Brown
Claim: Wilber, The Spectrum of Consciousness (1977): levels of identity from the whole universe down to persona and shadow, produced by drawing boundaries between self and not-self [M]. Later: matter, body, mind, soul, spirit; states of waking, dream, deep sleep, turiya and non-dual; the pre/trans fallacy, which warns against reading a return to unity as regression to infancy [M]. The non-dual is the wood the ladder is made of, not its top rung [M-]. Daniel P. Brown, "The Stages of Meditation in Cross-Cultural Perspective" (in Wilber, Engler and Brown, Transformations of Consciousness, 1986), compares Theravada, Mahamudra and Patanjali and argues for one deep structure under different surfaces [M].
Use: Wilber's boundary picture is Alex's axiom, one whole drawing lines inside itself. The pre/trans fallacy is a good guard sentence.

### 2.19 Daoism
Claim: inner alchemy is often given in four steps: essence into qi, qi into spirit, spirit into emptiness, emptiness into union with the Dao [M]. Zhuangzi ch. 1: the bird Peng rises ninety thousand li on a whirlwind while a cicada and a dove laugh, since the small cannot reach the great [M].
Use: the Peng is an old speed picture, useful beside run, bike, car, fly, orbit.

### 2.20 Beyond the last rung: four answers
Rest: Jain siddha; Theravada parinibbana; Yoga's kaivalya [M]. No end: Nyssa's epektasis; Ibn Arabi's no repetition; Mulla Sadra [M]. Return: bodhisattva; tenth ox picture; jivanmukta and the Gita; Teresa's seventh mansion; baqa; Plato's cave; Theosophy's Nirmanakaya; Campbell's monomyth, seventeen stages in departure, initiation and return, with the refusal of the return as a stage [M, count M-]. No ladder: Gaudapada; sudden Zen (the Shenxiu and Huineng verses, Platform Sutra); Wilber's one taste [M].
Use: all four exist, and the teacher's "birth" belongs with return and new beginning.

## 4. How they compare: the method

Side by side by count and function. Underhill sets Teresa's seven, John's two nights and the old threefold way together and asks what each stage does to the self. The Theosophists map the four Buddhist stages onto initiations by position in the sequence. This strains for the Jain fourteen or the Sufi hundred.

By a shared term. Dharma-megha, at the end of both the tenth bhumi and Patanjali's last samadhi, is the best example. The Traditionalists do the same with Beyond-Being, the Godhead, Ein Sof, Brahman and the Tao.

By a shared structure. Campbell and the Neoplatonists use out, down, up and back. Wilber uses boundaries drawn in layers. Alex's axiom has the same shape, so the structural comparison is the one he wants.

By identifying the person's ladder with the cosmos's (Smith, Nasr), so the distance words are not measurements. By experience (James, Underhill, Brown), bracketing doctrine.

Differences are explained as temperament and vocabulary (Underhill, James), exoteric and esoteric layers (Schuon), or different surfaces over one deep structure (Brown). The critic Katz says they are real.

## 5. What scholars say

Katz (1978) argues that tradition shapes the experience from the start, so cross-tradition stage maps may match the writers' vocabulary and not events. Forman (1990) answers with contentless events. Zaehner says there are at least three kinds. Ferrer, Revisioning Transpersonal Theory (2002), says Wilber-style hierarchies assume one summit and argues for plural ends [M].

Repeated sevens are weak evidence of a shared inner structure, since planets and calendar explain them [M, my observation].

Theosophy: Hanegraaff and Godwin read it as a 19th-century synthesis that reshapes Buddhist and Hindu terms; Lopez, Prisoners of Shangri-La (1998), and Johnson, The Masters Revealed (1994), are critical [M]. Traditionalism: Sedgwick, Against the Modern World (2004), places it historically [M]. Dante and the Mi'raj: possible, unproven (2.4).

1. The old maps are scale maps. Dante's spheres and the Mi'raj used the solar system as steps; his plus two to plus four is that idea in modern units. Check Paradiso I, XXI, XXVII, XXVIII.
2. Dante's natural rising and the Jain gourd and flame are the closest old pictures of "gravity is the pull of the one you started with" and "escape velocity at the corner". Check Tattvartha Sutra X.5 to 8.
3. The Heart Sutra's "gone beyond" is the most quotable line about leaving the last rung. Confirm Sanskrit and a translation.
4. Dharma-megha, in Buddhist and Yoga sources, is the cleanest verifiable term equation. Confirm Yoga Sutra IV.29.
5. Attar's thirty birds carry the axiom in a story. Confirm the pun.
6. The tenth ox-herding picture and Gita III.20 to 25 are two short sources for return as action.
7. Gaudapada II.32, with Huineng, supports "treat the labels as labels".
8. Epektasis and Ibn Arabi's no repetition support "plus four may be a stage".
9. Wilber's pre/trans fallacy is a guard sentence; Teilhard's differentiation answers "return means loss".
10. Say that stage maps run from four to a hundred steps and that no other scale I know is nine steps centred on a door.
11. "New stage of evolution" will meet Traditionalist objection; Bucke, Aurobindo, Teilhard are the defence.
