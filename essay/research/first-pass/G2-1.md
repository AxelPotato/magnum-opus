## 1. The picture in brief

The BE half of Part two is well covered. The DO half is where the traditions are most insistent and the draft is thinnest. The same position appears in every layer: release is the ground of action, not a substitute for it. The Gita says it in the god's own voice (3.22), Rabbi Tarfon as an instruction (Avot 2:16), Teresa as the purpose of the highest prayer, Zen in a story about a fox, Wang Yangming as the unity of knowing and acting, the Stoics as a named discipline.

Each tradition also names the failure of the person who uses stillness to avoid acting: the Gita's tamasic abandonment (18.7), the Church's quietism (1687, 1699), the fox of Mumonkan 2, Mark's withered hand, the Stoic idle argument, Cassian's acedia, Ghazali on trust without means. So the vegetable and bypassing worries (essay line 149) have answers inside the traditions.

Stillness and work also sit on a timetable (Benedict ch. 48, Zen samu, Seneca on alternating solitude and company, the Sabbath, the Naqshbandi solitude in the crowd), which is the material for the hand-off to Part three (line 161). The held body appears mostly as hard heart, stiff neck and knot, an ethical condition in body words; Daoist and Buddhist texts are the most body-literal. None lists Gennady's three words; the match is thematic.

Honest limit: no tradition equates release with the BE axis exactly, and the standard Advaita reading ranks knowledge above action.

## 2. Findings

A. The Gita and its readers

1. Gita 2.47 to 2.50. Claim: a right to the act and none to its fruit, with no attachment to inaction (2.47). Established in yoga, act; yoga is evenness in success and failure (2.48) and skill in action (2.50). Handles: "ma te sango 'stv akarmani", "yogasthah kuru karmani", "yogah karmasu kausalam" (Zaehner and Miller render the last as skill in action [U on exact English]). Layer: scripture. [M]. Use: the one-line answer to the vegetable worry, and a definition of high agency (evenness is BE, skill is DO).

2. Gita 3.4 to 3.9. Claim: abstaining from works does not bring freedom (3.4); no one stands without acting for an instant (3.5); one who restrains the organs of action but sits brooding on sense objects is a deluded hypocrite, "mithyacara" (3.6); do the required work, since even the body's upkeep fails without it (3.8). Layer: scripture. [M]. Use: 3.6 is the Gita's own diagnosis of the outwardly quiet, inwardly gripping person, near Part one's cold observer.

3. Gita 3.19 to 3.25. Claim: Krishna has nothing to gain in the three worlds, yet acts untiringly (a-tandrita), because if he stopped the worlds would fail (3.22 to 3.24). Janaka reached perfection by action (3.20). The wise should act unattached as the ignorant act attached, wanting lokasamgraha (3.25), the holding together of the world (translators: welfare, maintenance, cohesion). Shankara's and Aurobindo's glosses [U]. Layer: scripture. [M] verses. Use: the strongest text for a reason bigger than yourself in the DO half: the one who needs nothing still acts.

4. Gita 4.18, 5.2, 6.1, 6.16, 18.5 to 18.11, 18.23 to 18.28. Claim: the wise sees inaction in action and action in inaction (4.18). The yoga of action beats renunciation of action (5.2). The true renouncer does the work that must be done without leaning on its fruit, not the one who merely stopped (6.1). Yoga is not for those who eat or sleep too much or too little (6.16). Dropping required work from delusion is tamasic (18.7); dropping it because it hurts or from fear is rajasic and earns nothing (18.8); no embodied being can drop action entirely (18.11). The sattvic doer has steadiness and energy (dhriti, utsaha) (18.26); the tamasic doer is stiff (stabdha), lazy (alasa), despondent, a procrastinator (18.28). Layer: scripture. [M] content, [U] exact verse splits. Use: the direct answer to what the text tells someone who uses release to avoid acting: it calls that delusion. 6.1 is the verse for Drifting, 6.16 for Driven.

5. Gita 18.45 to 18.48. Claim: each finds perfection through their own work (18.45 to 18.46). One's own dharma, imperfectly done, beats another's done well (18.47; also 3.35). Do not abandon innate work for its flaws, since every undertaking is covered by fault as fire by smoke (18.48). Layer: scripture. [M]. Caution: svadharma is tied to varna (18.41 to 18.44); Ambedkar read it as caste defence; Gandhi, Vivekananda and Aurobindo re-read it as aptitude [M]. Use: "do not drop the work because it is imperfect" answers Drifting; name the caste context.

6. Isha Upanishad 2, 9 to 11. Claim: doing works here, one should wish to live a hundred years (2). Those who pursue ignorance (avidya) enter blind darkness, those absorbed in knowledge (vidya) a greater darkness (9). Whoever knows both crosses death by one and reaches immortality by the other (11). Shankara reads avidya as ritual works [M, gist]. Layer: Upanishad. [M]. Use: the shortest scriptural statement that neither axis alone will do. The essay already uses Isha 6 to 7.

7. The readers. Shankara (c. 700): both paths are taught, but liberation comes by knowledge with renunciation; he rejects combining knowledge and action. Ramanuja (c. 1100): action as worship is a stage toward devotion. Tilak (Gita Rahasya, prison 1910 to 1911, English by Sukthankar 1935): the Gita teaches karma-yoga against the renunciation reading. Gandhi (Anasakti Yoga, Gujarati; English by Desai, 1946): the core is non-attachment, and the war is inner. Aurobindo (Essays on the Gita, 1916 to 1920): works as sacrifice, joined to knowledge and devotion. Vivekananda (Karma-Yoga, New York 1895 to 1896): work as worship, detachment as mastery, the butcher who teaches a renouncer [M, gist]. The Ramakrishna Mission motto: for one's own liberation and the welfare of the world [M]. Layer: commentary and modern teaching. [M]; Gandhi's dates [U]. Use: honesty; Shankara is a prominent ally of release over action. Vivekananda also made the four yogas the Western template (Huston Smith: four temperaments) [M]. The Gita itself refuses the split (5.4 to 5.5).

B. The Christian line

8. Eckhart, Sermon 86 (Quint's numbering). Opening words as I recall them: "Intravit Jesus in quoddam castellum" (Vulgate, Luke 10:38). Quint's Sermon 2 opens with the same verse and is about the spark in the soul, so check which is cited. Claim: against the medieval reading (Mary's contemplation ranks higher), Eckhart makes Martha the mature one. Mary sits in delight and has yet to learn to live; Martha stands among things unhindered. Her "tell her to help me" is care for Mary's growth; "careworn" means with things, not in them. Words: paraphrase. Talks of Instruction ch. 10 [U]: a person in rapture who knew a sick man needed soup should leave the rapture and serve. Layer: mystic. [M]; fine points ~75%. Scholars: McGinn 2001; Mieth 1969. Use: the mystic puts work above rapture, the reverse of the cold observer.

9. Teresa of Avila. Interior Castle (1577), Seventh Mansions ch. 4: the purpose of the spiritual marriage is works, "obras, obras" (handle) [M ~85%; VII.4.6 is a guess, U]. Martha and Mary must work together in lodging the Lord. Foundations ch. 5: the Lord walks among the pots and pans [M]. Layer: mystic. [M]. Use: the top of the Carmelite map is a return to the kitchen, which fits "birth" as a start.

10. Benedict, Cassian, Evagrius. Rule ch. 48 (daily manual labour) calls idleness the enemy of the soul and fixes hours for work and sacred reading, adjusted for the weak [M]. There is no "ora et labora" in the Rule; it is a later slogan [M ~85%]. Evagrius (Praktikos 12) and Cassian (Institutes 10) describe acedia, the noonday demon of restlessness in the cell, cured by steady manual work [M]; Cassian's Abba Paul burned his woven mats yearly to keep working [M ~75%]. Layer: monastic rule, desert tradition. [M]. Use: the oldest alternation timetable; acedia is the monks' name for Drifting.

11. Brother Lawrence and de Caussade. Lawrence (Nicolas Herman, c. 1614 to 1691, Carmelite lay brother in Paris): the time of business is no different from the time of prayer; he held presence in the kitchen clamour as in church. De Caussade (1675 to 1751, Jesuit; Abandonment to Divine Providence, ed. Ramière 1861): the duty of the present moment is God's will, and abandonment includes doing it. The 1861 text's history is disputed [M ~75%]. Layer: mystic, popular. [M]. Use: stillness inside ordinary work, written in the shadow of item 12.

12. The quietist condemnations. Molinos, Guia espiritual (1675); Innocent XI, bull Coelestis Pastor, 20 November 1687, condemned 68 propositions. As I recall: to will to act offends God, who wants to be the sole agent, so one abandons oneself and stays like a lifeless body (Latin "tamquam corpus exanime" [U]); temptations need not be resisted in the passive state [U]. Fenelon, Explication des maximes des saints (1697); Innocent XII, brief Cum alias, 12 March 1699, censured 23 propositions; he submitted. Cognet and Knox: "quietism" is largely the opponents' label, and politics shaped the process. Layer: institutional teaching. [M] dates; [U] proposition texts and Denzinger numbers (DS about 2201 ff). Use: the tradition's own name and date for the vegetable error; check the "lifeless body" proposition first.

C. Buddhist, Daoist, Confucian

13. Mumonkan 2 (1229), Hyakujo's fox. An old man, once an abbot, became a fox for 500 lives for saying that a person of great practice does not fall into cause and effect. Baizhang's answer, that such a person does not obscure cause and effect, frees him, and the fox gets a monk's funeral. Dogen revisits it in the Shobogenzo [U on detail]; Heine 1999 is the study [M]. Layer: koan collection. [M]. Use: the Zen text against "the awakened are beyond consequences", the Drifting error in one story; it pairs with the karma section (line 101).

14. Ox-herding and Baizhang. In the ten pictures (Kuoan Shiyuan, 12th century) the eighth, the empty circle, is not the end; the ninth returns to the source; the tenth has the master enter the market with open hands [M]. Compare Mazu, "ordinary mind is the Way" [M]. "A day without work, a day without food" and the story of the old master refusing to eat when his tools were hidden are attached to Baizhang Huaihai (720 to 814); I cannot name the earliest source. Foulk, Poceski (2007) and Yifa (2002) treat his monastic code as lost, rebuilt from Song texts (Chanyuan qinggui 1103) [M picture, U earliest source]. Layer: Zen teaching, monastic tradition. Use: the last stage is work among people. Write "the Baizhang tradition says". "Before enlightenment chop wood, carry water; after, chop wood, carry water" has no classical source I know [U].

15. Dogen, Tenzo Kyokun (1237). The head cook's work as practice: the three minds (joyful, parental, magnanimous); handle a leaf of greens as you would your own eyes; the old cook at Ayuwang drying mushrooms in noon heat who says others are not him and asks what time he should wait for [M, Dogen's Ningbo account, 1223]. Work practice (samu) is part of the Zen monastic day [M]. Layer: monastic teacher text. [M]. Use: work as practice, no handing it off.

16. Buddhism's own DO. Vinaya Mahavagga 1.11: the Buddha sends the first sixty arahants out for the good of the many [M]. Digha Nikaya 16: strive on with heedfulness [M]. Dhammapada 276: you must make the effort, the Buddhas only point [M]. Mahayana four vows (beings numberless, I vow to free them); Shantideva, Bodhicaryavatara 10.55, to remain while space remains [M ~75%; locator U]. Engaged Buddhism: Thich Nhat Hanh in 1960s Vietnam, Sarvodaya Shramadana (1958) [M]; scholars debate whether "engaged" names something old or a modern label. Layer: scripture, vow, movement. [M]. Use: the bodhisattva vow is the Buddhist reason bigger than yourself.

17. Zhuangzi 3 and Laozi on acting. Cook Ding carves an ox so the knife passes through the gaps and stays sharp nineteen years; he cares about the Way beyond skill, follows the grain, and slows at hard spots [M]. Laozi: do by not-doing (63), the Way does nothing and nothing is left undone (37), withdraw when the work is done (9) [M]. Layer: philosophical scripture. [M]. Use: skill in action with no held body, a Chinese match to Gita 2.50, and a reminder that wu wei is not doing nothing.

18. Wang Yangming (1472 to 1529). Unity of knowing and acting, first put forward in 1509: knowing begins action and action completes knowing; knowing that is not acted on is not yet knowing. Four-sentence teaching (1527): the mind's substance is beyond good and evil; knowing good and evil is liangzhi; doing good and removing evil is investigating things. He moved from teaching quiet sitting to polishing oneself in affairs after students came to love quiet and dislike activity, and he put down a rebellion in 1519 [M]. Layer: philosopher-teacher. [M]; locators U. Use: a still mind-substance that does not rest in stillness: the structure of the Part three hand-off.

D. Jewish, Muslim, Sikh

19. Mishnah Avot 2:16 (2:15 in some editions), Tarfon. The day is short, the work great, the workers lazy, the master pressing: it is not yours to finish the work, and you are not free to desist [M, paraphrase]. Related: Avot 2:2: Torah study is good with a worldly occupation, and study without work ends in sin; Avot 1:17: the deed is the main thing; Berakhot 35b: Shimon bar Yochai asks what becomes of Torah if all plough, Ishmael says combine them [M ~80%]. Layer: rabbinic scripture. [M]. The Sabbath (Exod 20:9 to 11) builds rest into the working week [M]. Use: Tarfon is Gita 2.47 in Hebrew; Avot 2:2 is Part three's thesis from about 200 CE.

20. The Baal Shem Tov and avodah be-gashmiyut. Hasidism teaches service through material and bodily acts (eating, trade, speech), raising their sparks. The Besht is reported to have opposed severe asceticism and made joy a duty. Sources come through disciples (Toldot Yaakov Yosef, Keter Shem Tov, Tzava'at ha-Rivash) [M]. Etkes 2005 doubts how much of the historical Besht is recoverable; Schatz-Uffenheimer saw a quietist strand in early Hasidism [M ~75%]. Layer: mystic, via disciples. [M]; single sayings [U]. Use: same care as the essay already takes ("reported, through a disciple").

21. Naqshbandi. Ghijduwani (d. c. 1179) formulated rules including awareness in the breath (hosh dar dam), watch the step (nazar bar qadam) and solitude in the crowd (khalwat dar anjuman); Baha al-Din Naqshband (d. 1389) added three pauses [M]. "Dil ba yar, dast ba kar" (heart with the Friend, hand at work) is widely ascribed to Baha al-Din; I cannot recall a source, so [U]. Layer: mystic order. [M] rules. Use: the Muslim form of stillness and work together.

22. Tawakkul and the tied camel. Qur'an 3:159: when you have resolved (azamta), then trust; resolve comes first [M]. Camel hadith (Tirmidhi; Ibn Hibban): tie it and trust; grading varies by route [U on grading and numbers]. Palm-shoot hadith (Musnad Ahmad): plant the shoot even if the Hour is coming [M]. Ghazali, Ihya Book 35 (Burrell 2001): tawakkul is a state of the heart and does not require giving up means [M]. Layer: scripture, hadith, scholarship. [M] existence, [U] grading. Use: the Muslim answer to "just trust" is in the verse; check grading before citing the camel.

23. Guru Nanak and the Sikh triad. Nam japna, kirat karni (earn by honest work), vand chhakna (share): a tradition summary, not one verse. Nanak is said to have farmed at Kartarpur [M]. Ang 1245, as I recall: one who earns by labour and gives some away knows the way [M ~80%]. The Siddh Gosht (Ang 938 on) has yogis questioning his householder life [M ~75%]. Layer: scripture plus summary. Use: say "tradition summary"; give no verse for the triad.

E. The Stoics

Idle argument (Cicero, De Fato 28 to 30): "if you are fated to recover you will with or without the doctor", answered by Chrysippus with things co-fated with their means [M]. Layer: Stoic philosophy. [M]. Use: where the Stoics block misuse themselves: Ench. 46 (talk is not practice), De Fato (fate is no excuse), the reserve clause (full effort, outcome not owned).

## 4. How they compare: the method

Vivekananda sorts by temperament: jnana, bhakti, karma and raja yoga are four doors for four kinds of people (Karma-Yoga, 1896). Cost: the Gita itself refuses the split. The Traditionalists sort by rank and function: Guenon reads the Gita through the priest and warrior order and ranks knowledge above action; Coomaraswamy matches craft guilds, Hindu vocation and medieval Christian craft as one social form in which work carries meaning. Cost: a hierarchy and a politics the sources do not all share. The Theosophists sort by an ethical test: the Voice of the Silence judges the two paths by whether they leave the world to suffer. Cost: the Pratyekabuddha counter-type is polemic.

Scholars compare inside one tradition, by word: Slingerland follows wu wei, Hadot the Stoic disciplines, Idel corporeality, Heine the fox. That suits Alex: pick the word that carries the point (kausalam, horme, obras, wu wei, zhi xing he yi, avodah be-gashmiyut, kirat karni, tawakkul) and say what it does in its own argument. Comparers explain differences as temperament (Vivekananda), function (Guenon) or outer dress over an inner core (Schuon); specialists call them real differences of doctrine.

## 5. What scholars say, short

Gita: Davis (2015) and Malinar (2007) show a long fight over action against renunciation, and that each modern reader read it to suit a programme. Quietism: Cognet and Knox call the label the opponents' and the process political. Baizhang: Foulk and Poceski treat the work rule as partly a Song-period image. Zhuangzi: Slingerland argues for skill; Graham and Hansen read the book as perspectival. Traditionalists: Sedgwick (2004) shows the Guenon and Evola split and an anti-modern politics. Theosophy: Campbell (1980) and Godwin (1994) call it influential and mixed in reliability; the Voice has no known source text.
