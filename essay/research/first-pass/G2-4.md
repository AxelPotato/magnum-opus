## 1. The picture in brief

The draft's motive step offers Eastern, Neoplatonic, Kabbalistic and Sufi witnesses and then says a whole that wants to know itself needs a gap. That sentence is close to the working position of a German line (Boehme, Schiller, Fichte, Schelling, Hegel) and of a Russian line that learned from it (Solovyov, Florensky, Bulgakov, Berdyaev, Frank). They state the three clauses as philosophy: one whole, it comes to know itself by passing through otherness, and the otherness is overcome in a richer unity.

Four points matter for the essay.

First, the Western answer to "why" is mostly need or longing or the logic of self-consciousness. The Eastern answer is mostly play (lila) or overflow. A minority of the Russians answer with free sacrificial love. The essay should say which it is borrowing.

Second, these authors keep a difference. Hegel mocks a One that swallows all distinctions. Teilhard says union differentiates. Whitehead says the One is increased. Lossky allows union with God in his energies and never in his essence. This is the Christian participation against identity, and it answers the fear that return means loss.

Third, they are not perennialists. Hegel and Solovyov rank religions with Christianity on top. Schelling compares by process. Guenon and Schuon would reject a Principle that needs anything. The lineage witnesses for the logic, not for "the same thing in different words".

Fourth, two corrections: the Blavatsky critic named Solovyov is the philosopher's brother, and the Eckhart eye line has no known place on the Cologne list.

## 2. Findings

A. Boehme and the pre-Kantian background

1. Boehme, the Ungrund as a will that finds itself. Claim: before nature there is an abyss without ground, nothing and all, a will with no object. A will with no counterpart cannot see itself, so it draws itself in by desire, makes a ground, and through Yes and No becomes manifest to itself. Who: Boehme (1575 to 1624), Von sechs theosophischen Punkten (1620), Mysterium Pansophicum (1620), Mysterium Magnum (1623). English: Earle's Six Theosophic Points (1919; reissued 1958 with an introduction by Berdyaev [M, 85 percent]); Sparrow's Mysterium Magnum (1654). Words: paraphrase. Handles: Ungrund, Grund, Sucht, Lust, Gegenwurf, Ja und Nein. Traditions: Genesis, the Trinity, Paracelsian vocabulary. Tier: [M]; chapters and exact sentences [U]. Use: the oldest clear statement of "knowing needs a gap", same picture as the Sufi hidden treasure.

2. Boehme, Wisdom as the mirror. Claim: Wisdom (Weisheit, Sophia) is the eye or mirror in which the Ungrund beholds itself, and God is not manifest to himself without it. Who: Aurora (1612) and the works above. Tier: [M] gist, locators [U]. Use: gives the essay's "point of view" a Christian name and links to Schiller (5), Hegel (13) and Solovyov (20).

3. Boehme, letting go. Claim: the way back is Gelassenheit, yielding or resignation, the stilling of self-will so that the eternal hearing, seeing and speaking works through the person. Who: Vom uebersinnlichen Leben (1622; English The Supersensual Life, in Law's edition) and Der Weg zu Christo (1622). Traditions: Eckhart, Theologia Germanica (Luther edited it in 1516 and 1518). Tier: [M]; titles of the sub-treatises [U]. Use: BE in a Western voice, in the word Eckhart used.

4. Who set Boehme beside the East, and what he keeps. Franz Hartmann, a Theosophist, wrote The Life and Doctrines of Jacob Boehme (1891) [M]. Schopenhauer set Eckhart, Silesius and the Christian ascetics beside Indian renunciation in World as Will and Representation, vol. 2, chapter 48 [M, chapter U]. Nishitani read Eckhart with sunyata (33). Hegel's History of Philosophy lectures treat Boehme at length as the first German philosopher [M]. Boehme keeps the Trinity, Christ, and a fall by the creature's will. Tier [M].

5. Schiller. Claim: in Theosophie des Julius (Philosophical Letters, 1786) and the poem Die Freundschaft, the world-master was friendless, felt a lack, and made spirits as mirrors of his blessedness. Love is the pull of beings toward the whole. Tier: [M] gist, 90 percent; poem date [U]. East: none. Use: the motive in a few lines, and Hegel closes his Phenomenology with the poem's last lines (13). The Sufi hidden treasure and Schiller say the same thing.

B. Fichte

6. Fichte, the check. Claim: the absolute I can become conscious of itself only against an obstacle (Anstoss) that it posits and works to overcome in endless striving. Who: Grundlage der gesamten Wissenschaftslehre (1794 to 1795), Part III. Tier [M]. Use: the earliest German form of "needs a gap", but a moral striving, closer to DO than to BE.

7. Fichte, The Way Towards the Blessed Life (Anweisung zum seligen Leben, 1806; English by William Smith, 1849, public domain). Claim: there is one Being, one divine Life; the many conscious "I"s are its appearances; the sense of a separate self is the root of unblessedness; blessedness is love as being at one. He reads John's prologue as the real teaching and the Jewish and Pauline layers as lower. Tier [M]; the John emphasis 85 percent. Keeps: Christian vocabulary and, as he tells it, a living God. Use: nearest to Alex's tone, and an early inner-versus-outer reading of one religion. Quotable once verified.

C. Schelling

8. Schelling, the ground in God (Philosophische Untersuchungen ueber das Wesen der menschlichen Freiheit, 1809; English: Gutmann 1936; Hayden-Roy 1987; Love and Schmidt 2006). Claim: God has in himself a ground of his existence that is not God considered absolutely, nature in God. It precedes him logically, not in time. The longing the eternal One feels to give birth to itself is its first stirring, a will with no understanding yet that becomes understanding. All birth is from darkness into light. Words: I recall the longing sentence closely [M, 85 percent], but treat as paraphrase. Traditions: Boehme (named), Spinoza, Kant. East: Scholem saw a likeness to Lurianic contraction, with no proved path (Boehme, Oetinger, Molitor [U]). Keeps: a personal God, a real difference between God and creature. Tier [M]. Use: clause two almost literally.

9. Schelling, the separation as will. Claim: evil is the particular will exalting itself over the universal will, when the creature wants to be apart what it is only in unity. Selfhood is the dark ground in a person; raised to spirit and joined to love it is the highest thing. Hence a veil of melancholy over all nature. The end is God all in all (echo of 1 Corinthians 15:28 [U]). Tier [M]. Use: a Western definition of the "I" that holds itself apart. It fits the fist and the wall, and it says selfhood is transformed, not deleted.

10. Schelling, Ages of the World (Die Weltalter, drafts 1811, 1813, 1815; unpublished in his life; English by Bolman 1942, Norman 1997, Wirth 2000, each of a different draft [U which]). Claim: the divine begins with a contraction (a No) against its own expansion (a Yes). God's past is the ground. Time arises as the sequence past, present, future. The highest freedom is a will that wills nothing. Tier [M]; a Gelassenheit link [U]. Use: self-knowledge as a history, a base for "the run is longer than one life". He does not teach reincarnation; some read his timeless choice of character (1809) as pre-existence [U].

11. Schelling, Philosophy of Mythology and Philosophy of Revelation (Berlin lectures from the 1840s, published 1856 to 1857). Method: myth is not allegory or priestly invention but the necessary process by which one human consciousness passes through stages (his three potencies), each people standing at a stage. He calls the reading tautegorical: the gods mean themselves. Eleusis is the high point of myth, and Christianity the revelation beyond it. He assumes an original unity of humankind scattered by a crisis [M]. East: Indian religion is an early stage. Max Mueller attended Schelling's Berlin lectures in 1844 [M], though Mueller went the way of language. Keeps: Christianity as fulfilment. Tier [M]. Use: a way to explain agreement without borrowing or one revelation: same process, same consciousness.

D. Hegel

12. Hegel, Phenomenology of Spirit, Preface (1807; Miller's numbering). Paraphrase: the true is the whole (section 20), and the whole is essence completing itself through its development; the Absolute is a result that is what it is only at the end; substance must be grasped as subject (17). The life of God may be called a play of love with itself, but that sinks into edification without the seriousness, pain, patience and labour of the negative (19). Spirit wins its truth only by finding itself in utter dismemberment, and is strong only by tarrying with the negative (32). Tier [M]; section numbers 85 percent. Keeps: Christian only. Use: the Western reply to lila; section 32 is nearest to "the whole finds itself in dismemberment". Tarrying with pain without bracing is my reading as an image of letting go, not Hegel's.

13. Hegel, the last paragraph of the Phenomenology (section 808 [M, 80 percent]). Spirit's history is externalisation (Entaeusserung) and recollection (Erinnerung), the Golgotha of absolute Spirit. The closing two lines are Schiller's poem (5), slightly altered: from the cup of this realm of spirits, infinity foams up to him. Tier [M], 90 percent. Use: ties the hidden treasure to a canonical text. Check the German.

14. Hegel, Logic and Encyclopaedia. Claim: the Science of Logic ends with the Idea freely releasing itself (sich entlassen) as nature (Encyclopaedia section 244). Nature is the Idea in the form of otherness; Spirit is its return. Section 564 and its remark: God is God only as self-knowing, and his self-knowledge is his self-consciousness in man, so man's knowledge of God is God's knowledge of himself. Wallace (1892) translates "discharges", Miller (1969) "release" [M]. Tier [M]; 564 at 80 percent. Use: the closest single Western sentence to clauses one to three. That entlassen sounds like lassen and Gelassenheit is my observation only.

15. Hegel, Lectures on the Philosophy of Religion (Berlin, 1821, 1824, 1827, 1831; Hodgson edition 1984 to 1987; the 1827 course alone, 1988). Method: religion and philosophy share one content, Spirit, but religion gives it in picture-thinking (Vorstellung) and philosophy in the concept. Religions are ranked by how fully they show Spirit's freedom. In the 1827 course: nature religions (magic; Chinese measure; Hindu phantasy; Buddhism as being-within-self), a transition (Persian, Syrian, Egyptian), religions of spiritual individuality (Jewish sublimity, Greek beauty, Roman purpose), then Christianity read through the Trinity as God's self-differentiation and return [M, details U]. East: he reviewed Humboldt's Gita lecture in 1827 and judged the Gita below the highest [M]. Halbfass, India and Europe (1988), shows how his sources fixed the verdict. Use: Hegel is a witness to the logic, and he ranks. He would not say all traditions say the same.

16. Hegel and Eckhart's eye saying. What I recall: Hegel quotes Eckhart on the eye with which God sees me being the eye with which I see him, one eye and one seeing, in the Berlin lectures on God's self-knowledge in man, perhaps the 1827 course or the 1829 lectures on the proofs [U, 65 percent]. If so: the line was in print by 1832 (Marheineke's edition), a generation before Pfeiffer's Eckhart (1857), via manuscripts, Baader or old prints [U]. It does not test "Cologne 1326". G1-6 places the line in Sermon 12 (Quint), and I know of no source putting it on the 1326 list or among the bull's 28 propositions [U]. Use: cut "raised against him in Cologne"; if Hegel is verified, say Hegel used it. Check Hodgson's index, Lasson, Pfeiffer.

17. Hegel's check on a swallowing One. In the Preface (section 16) he mocks the Absolute as the night in which all cows are black, aimed at Schelling's identity philosophy [M, strong]. Love is described in the Encyclopaedia (section 158 addition) as distinguishing two who are not distinguished for each other [M, 75 percent]. The result is richer than the start. Use: the sentence for the fear of loss: return is not absorption.

E. Krause

18. Krause, panentheism. Karl Christian Friedrich Krause (1781 to 1832) coined Panentheismus in 1828 (Vorlesungen ueber das System der Philosophie) [M, 85 percent]: all things are in God, who is more than all. Acts 17:28 is the old scriptural echo [M]. Hartshorne and Reese, Philosophers Speak of God (1953), made it a cross-cultural class that includes Ramanuja and Whitehead [M]. Keeps: God is not the world. Tier [M]. Use: one name for the essay's whole-and-parts, and a clean answer to a pantheism charge.

F. The Russian line

19. The channel. Schelling reached Russia through the Moscow Lovers of Wisdom of the 1820s (Odoevsky, Venevitinov, Kireevsky), through Tyutchev, who knew him, and through Chaadaev's contact with him [M, details U]. Boehme and Baader came with Masonic circles [U]. So the Russian statement is downstream of Boehme and Schelling, joined to Orthodox sources (Dionysius, Maximus, Palamas). Tier [M]. Use: explains why the lines read alike.

20. Solovyov (1853 to 1900), all-unity. Claim: the Absolute is the all-one (vseedinstvo), the unity of everything that does not abolish the many. The Logos expresses it. Sophia is the ideal unity of creation, the world soul. The world soul, asserting itself, fell from unity, and the world process, ending in Godmanhood, is the re-uniting. Who: Philosophical Principles of Integral Knowledge (1877), Critique of Abstract Principles (1880), Lectures on Godmanhood (1878 to 1881; Zouboff 1944, as Godmanhood; Jakim 1995, as Divine Humanity). Sources: Neoplatonism, Boehme, Schelling, Baader, Kabbalah, Gnostic Sophia. He reviews religions in order (Eastern, Greek, Christian) with Godmanhood as measure [M, lecture numbers U]. Keeps: free creation, God above the world, pantheism rejected. Tier [M]. Use: the Russian-language source for clauses one to three, with humanity as the agent.

21. Solovyov, The Meaning of Love (1892 to 1894). Claim: erotic love is a rare glimpse of the true relation of beings, where another is felt as of absolute worth. Its meaning is the justification and salvation of individuality through the sacrifice of egoism [M, strong]. Traditions: Plato's Symposium, Christian. His Sophia visions (1862, London 1875, Egypt 1875; poem Three Meetings, 1898) are told there too [M]. Use: Alex said in the talk that being in love is life's hint; this gives him a philosopher who built a system on it.

22. Name caution. A Modern Priestess of Isis (1892; English by Walter Leaf, 1895) is by Vsevolod Sergeyevich Solovyov (1849 to 1903), a novelist and Vladimir's elder brother [M, 90 percent]. Project notes use "Solovyov" for the Blavatsky critic. The philosopher is another man. Whether Vladimir wrote on Theosophy [U].

23. Florensky (1882 to 1937). The Pillar and Ground of the Truth (1914; Jakim 1997). Claim: the law of identity (A is A) is not enough. Truth is a unity that contains difference, which Christianity states as the Trinity. A self leaves its closed identity only through another, in love and friendship. Sophia is the great root of creation. The Letter on Gehenna suggests the closed self is its own hell and leans toward restoration [U]. He was a mathematician (he used Cantor's sets), and was shot in 1937 [M]. Keeps: Orthodox, Trinitarian. East: not a comparer. Letter numbers [U]. Use: how a many can be one without ceasing to be many.

24. Bulgakov (1871 to 1944). Claim: Sophia is the divine ground of the world and also its created counterpart. Creation is God's free self-limitation, kenosis, an act of sacrificial love. The world is in God, not God: he calls this panentheism. The end is full restoration (apokatastasis), argued with caution in The Bride of the Lamb (1945; Jakim 2002). Works: Philosophy of Economy (1912; Evtuhov 2000), Unfading Light (1917; Smith 2012), The Lamb of God (1933), Sophia, the Wisdom of God (1937). Condemned in 1935 by the Moscow Patriarchate (Metropolitan Sergius) and the Karlovac Synod [M]. Sources: Schelling, Solovyov, Boehme, Gregory of Nyssa [M]. Keeps: God is free and needs nothing. Tier [M]. Use: the second Western answer, love and not need, which sits next to lila.

25. Berdyaev (1874 to 1948). Claim: there is a freedom older than being, rooted in the Ungrund, which God did not make. It is why creation can go wrong and why it can be creative. The fall into an objectified world of separate things is the estrangement; the creative act is the way back; God needs the human and the divine-human works both ways. Works: Philosophy of Freedom (1911), The Meaning of the Creative Act (1916), The Destiny of Man (1931), Slavery and Freedom (1939), The Beginning and the End (1947). On Boehme: studies in Put' (1930) and the 1958 introduction (1) [M, details U]. He faults Hegel's Absolute for swallowing freedom [M, gist]. Keeps: personality; the person does not dissolve. Tier [M]. Use: the best witness for "separation is the price of freedom and love".

26. Frank (1877 to 1950). The Unknowable (1939), Reality and Man (1956). Claim: reality is an all-unity never given as an object, known only from inside; the One and the many are related antinomically (monodualism); the primary form of being is We, not I. Sources: Cusanus, Plotinus, Dionysius. Keeps: a personal God. Tier [M]. Use: one whole with many centres, calmly put.

27. Lossky (1903 to 1958). The difference kept. Works: Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church (1944), the Controversy of Sophia (1936, against Bulgakov), and the posthumous Theologie negative et connaissance de Dieu chez Maitre Eckhart (1960) [M]. Claim: union with God (theosis) is real but happens in God's energies, never in his essence, so the creature never becomes God by nature. He reads Eckhart inside the Dionysian apophatic line. Tier [M]. Use: the exact Orthodox sentence for return without identity, and a Russian-tradition scholar of Eckhart.

28. Karsavin (1882 to 1952), On the Beginnings (1925): God creates by diminishing himself; the world returns by dying to selfhood [U]. Check first.

29. Fyodorov and Tsiolkovsky. Fyodorov (1829 to 1903), Philosophy of the Common Task (posthumous, 1906 and 1913): the task of humankind is to master nature and raise the dead; founder of Russian cosmism. Tsiolkovsky (1857 to 1935) met him in Moscow in the 1870s and used his library [M]. His own philosophy (Monism of the Universe, 1925; Will of the Universe, 1928): matter lives, atoms pass through all beings, reason spreads. The cradle line: from a 1911 letter to B. N. Vorobyov, the Earth (or the planet) is the cradle of reason (some say humanity), and one cannot live in a cradle forever [M, 80 percent; wording and noun U]. Caution: historians report that his ethics proposed removing "imperfect" life [M, 75 percent]. Russian schools teach first, second and third cosmic velocity (orbit, escape, leaving the solar system), so the reader already owns the ladder [M]. Use: the orbit-versus-escape picture in a Russian reader's own history.

30. Russian Theosophy. Blavatsky was born in Yekaterinoslav in 1831, of the Hahn and Fadeyev families, with Princess Dolgorukova as grandmother and Sergei Witte as cousin. Her first Russian publication was the travel series From the Caves and Jungles of Hindostan in Katkov's Russkii Vestnik (from 1879, signed Radda-Bai) [M]. A Russian Theosophical Society appeared in 1908 [U]. Ouspensky's Tertium Organum (1912) belongs here, as do Scriabin and Kandinsky [M]. The Roerichs (Nicholas 1874 to 1947, Helena 1879 to 1955) wrote Agni Yoga or Living Ethics (1920 to 1937), received from a Teacher whom Theosophy knows as Master M., and Helena worked on a Russian Secret Doctrine [M, publication U]. Scholars call it a Theosophical offshoot. Roerich is disputed in Russia. Tier [M].

## 4. How they compare

They rarely say "same words, different traditions". Four methods occur.

Boehme and Fichte compare nothing across religions. They read one religion at two depths, outer and inner (Fichte: John against Paul), the perennial structure inside one tradition.

Schelling compares by process. Myths are stages of one consciousness, found in each people at its place. Resemblance is explained by one process, not borrowing, and the order has a top.

Hegel compares by form. One content, Spirit, appears in pictures and in concepts. The ranking is by freedom and adequacy. His Indian verdicts rested on 1820s sources, and his own idea of his task was to show Christianity as the concept's religion. Solovyov copies the move with Godmanhood as the measure.

Later comparers drop the ranking. Hartshorne sorts by logical type, Teilhard and Whitehead by function, the Kyoto School by dialogue (Eckhart with sunyata). The Traditionalists reject the process view and keep the doctrine.

For the essay: Alex's own method, "different words, same principles", is closest to Fichte (inner against outer) and to Whitehead's tone. Saying "Hegel agrees" would be wrong. He agrees on the logic and disagrees on the equality of religions.

## 5. What scholars say

Hegel scholarship is split. Metaphysical readers (Taylor, 1975; Magee, Hegel and the Hermetic Tradition, 2001, who stresses Boehme and Hermeticism) take the Absolute's self-knowledge seriously. Cyril O'Regan, The Heterodox Hegel (1994), reads him as a modern Gnostic. Non-metaphysical readers (Pippin, Pinkard) take Spirit as a social-normative achievement and drop the divine subject [M]. Voegelin called Hegel and Schelling modern Gnostics, Jonas drew a gnostic-modern analogy, and Blumenberg disputed the thesis [M]. Benz is the standard on Schelling and Boehme [M]. Halbfass shows how limited Hegel's Indian sources were. For the Russians: the sophiology was censured in 1935, and Florovsky and Lossky answered it. Tsiolkovsky's cosmism has a respected side and a grim one.
