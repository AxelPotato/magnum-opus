## 1. The picture in brief

These people are not a school. Phrase historians (Steuco 1540, Leibniz 1714, Lessing 1779 and 1780) show the idea changing: a deposit of revelation, then a sifting task, then a claim that cannot be proved and must be lived. Western readers of the East (Emerson, Schopenhauer) supply the English and German words for one whole, the veil and the fingers, and Schopenhauer got his Upanishads by a documented chain from Dara Shikoh. Experience writers (James, Underhill, Bucke, Stace) compare reports, not doctrines, and give Alex a modest stance, a filter image, a stage ladder and a two-type map of unity experience. Indian insiders (Ramakrishna, Vivekananda, Dara, Radhakrishnan, Bhagavan Das) compare from inside one tradition, and each puts his own on top. The anthologists (Huxley, Perry, Wilson, Needleman) sort passages under headings, with different frames.

Corrections, all from memory and unverified: Huxley's word is "substantial to", and his ethic clause is about knowing the Ground, not reciprocity (essay line 107 leans on it). James's screen is "filmiest", the unanimity passage is probably Lecture XVI, and self-surrender is Lecture IX. "Art of union with Reality" is Underhill's Practical Mysticism of 1914. Lessing's essay ends on rebirth. Vivekananda's two best sentences come from two days.

## 4. How they compare: the method

Six moves recur.

1. History of the phrase (Steuco, Leibniz). Agreement is first one source, then a sifting task. Only Steuco claims a first revelation.

2. Heading and rows (Huxley, Perry, Wilson, Bhagavan Das). The editor picks the heading and sets passages in a row. The equation is made by heading and translation before the reader sees it. Wilson alone leaves room for disagreement.

3. Report and type (James, Bucke, Stace, Underhill). Treat mystics as witnesses and ask whether reports agree. James gives four marks, Bucke marks and cases, Stace seven characteristics, Underhill an order. Differences go to over-beliefs (James), interpretation (Stace), temperament (Underhill).

4. Term pairing (Dara, Schopenhauer, Radhakrishnan's notes). Put a term from A beside a term from B. Dara paired angels with gods, and his Vedanta came out Sufi. The pairing is the comparer's, not the tradition's.

5. Image (Ramakrishna's lake, Rumi's grapes, Vivekananda's streams, James's screen, Lessing's rings). The picture carries the proof and stays vague on purpose.

6. Insider speech and conditions (Vivekananda, Radhakrishnan, Huxley). Each speaks from one tradition and puts it on top: Advaita (Vivekananda, Radhakrishnan), Sufism (Dara), Christianity (Steuco, Underhill). Huxley adds that knowing needs a changed knower, the strongest and least testable move.

A worked row for Alex's karma step: Emerson's thief who steals from himself, Schopenhauer's doer and sufferer who are one, Lessing's coming back to learn, under "what I do to another I do to myself". The row hides that Emerson gives no metaphysics, that Schopenhauer's ends in denying the will, and that Lessing speaks of the individual's progress.

## 5. What scholars say

Short, from memory, [M] unless marked.

Phrase: Schmitt (1966) showed the phrase is Steuco's and Leibniz changed its sense [C]. Amberger (2019) reads Leibniz's as gradual progress [C].

Experience: Steven Katz ("Language, Epistemology, and Mysticism", 1978) holds that no experience is unmediated. Robert Forman (The Problem of Pure Consciousness, 1990) replies that some are contentless. R. C. Zaehner (Mysticism Sacred and Profane, 1957) separates nature, monistic and theistic mysticism and attacks Huxley's reading of mescaline. Stace is called circular, since cases that do not fit are called not mystical. Bucke's sample is hand picked.

Indian universalism: Paul Hacker called Hindu tolerance inclusivism, absorbing and ranking the other. Halbfass (India and Europe, 1988) and Richard King (Orientalism and Religion, 1999) call the Ramakrishna and Vivekananda form modern, made in dialogue with the West. Supriya Gandhi (The Emperor Who Never Was, 2020) reads Dara's choices as pushing a Sufi-Vedanta fit [C].

Anthologies: textual scholars say they cut passages from their argument, and Eckhart read alone sounds like Advaita. Fair summary: the agreement is partly found and partly made, and nearly every comparer had a home team.
