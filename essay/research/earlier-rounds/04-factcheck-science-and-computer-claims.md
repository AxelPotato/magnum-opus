FACT CHECK, Part two. Searches only; no files in the repo were touched. Marks: VERIFIED (a search result naming a source supports it), PLAUSIBLE (standard knowledge, not confirmed here), WRONG (corrected). Where I computed a number myself I say so.

======== A. PHILOSOPHY OF MIND AND PHYSICS ========

A1. Chalmers, "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness", Journal of Consciousness Studies 2(3): 200-219, 1995. VERIFIED (consc.net, Wikipedia "Hard problem of consciousness").
- Fair statement: the easy problems are explaining functions (discriminating, reporting, attending). The hard problem is why and how any of that processing is accompanied by experience, what it is like. VERIFIED.
- Mainstream attitude: VERIFIED in part. The 2025 COGITATE adversarial test of two leading theories (Nature, PubMed 40307561) challenged key tenets of both, and sources say there is still no agreement on the neural correlates. The 2020 PhilPapers survey has 62.4% of surveyed philosophers saying the hard problem is genuine and 29.7% saying it does not exist (consc.net/ppsurvey).
- Physicalism share: my sources conflict (51.9%, 57.4%, 56.5%). 56.5% is the 2009 survey; the 2020 figure depends on which population is counted. PLAUSIBLE: "a bare majority of professional philosophers lean physicalist". Do not print a percentage without opening the survey table.
- FLAG: the essay's version (1) says consciousness is "a product of my brain and mind". "Produced by" is itself a theory (rivals: identity, filter or transmission). The defensible wording is "depends on". Also "materialism, and often determinism" is not a necessary pair: standard quantum mechanics is probabilistic, and many materialists are compatibilists.

A2. Schrödinger on the oneness of mind.
- Exact wording: VERIFIED in secondary sources (Christian Science Monitor 1981 page; rawsemantics; several philarchive papers). "Mind is by its very nature a singulare tantum. I should say: the over-all number of minds is just one. I venture to call it indestructible since it has a peculiar time-table, namely mind is always now." Hyphenation ("over-all" vs "overall") may differ by edition, so check the CUP text before quoting.
- Source: VERIFIED. Mind and Matter, chapter 4, "The Arithmetical Paradox: The Oneness of Mind". These were the Tarner Lectures, Trinity College, Cambridge, October 1956, published by Cambridge University Press in 1958. They are reprinted in "What Is Life? with Mind and Matter and Autobiographical Sketches" (CUP).
- Earlier statement, VERIFIED: the Epilogue "On Determinism and Free Will" of What Is Life? (1944): "There is obviously only one alternative, namely the unification of minds or consciousnesses. Their multiplicity is only apparent, in truth there is only one mind. This is the doctrine of the Upanishads." (Cambridge Core listing and others.)
- Meine Weltansicht (1961, "My View of the World") is a third text. Scholarship (PMC article "Schrödinger's Doctrine of Identity") shows his lifelong Advaita Vedanta reading. I did not confirm any exact wording from it, so do not quote it.
- WRONG or unsafe: the widely circulated "the total number of minds in the universe is one" is a paraphrase. Do not put it in quotation marks.
- FLAG: he said himself that at this point he was "taking religion, not science" (same passage; CSM 1981 page). Do not say physics supports oneness.

A3. Spinoza and Einstein.
- Ethics Part I, Proposition 14: "There can be, or be conceived, no other substance but God" (Gutenberg, SEP). VERIFIED. Ethics was published posthumously in 1677 (PLAUSIBLE).
- Einstein: April 1929 telegram reply to Rabbi Herbert S. Goldstein of New York, reported in the New York Times on 25 April 1929. VERIFIED (Moment, Scientific American blog, Skeptic, Snopes). Reply: "I believe in Spinoza's God, Who reveals Himself in the lawful harmony of the world, not in a God Who concerns Himself with the fate and the doings of mankind." Translation wording varies, so quote only "I believe in Spinoza's God".
- WRONG if placed in Part I: the phrase "Deus sive Natura" (also "Deus seu Natura") belongs to Ethics Part IV, Preface (and IV, Prop. 4). The search did not pin it down, so this is PLAUSIBLE. Say "God or Nature" and cite Part I only for the one substance.
- FLAG: Spinoza's God is the lawful order of nature, with finite things as modes of it. It is not "a whole that experiences separation", and it has no purpose and no personal God. Einstein used the line to deny a personal God. Neither backs "all is one consciousness".

A4. Hoffman.
- Citation VERIFIED: Hoffman, Singh & Prakash (2015), "The Interface Theory of Perception", Psychonomic Bulletin & Review 22(6): 1480-1506, DOI 10.3758/s13423-015-0890-8. The journal ran a set of commentaries and a reply in the same year. Book VERIFIED: The Case Against Reality (Norton, 2019).
- Desktop analogy VERIFIED (Psychonomic Society featured content, "species-specific desktop"): perceptions are useful "icons" for fitness-relevant information, not a picture of the truth.
- Evolutionary-game argument VERIFIED: Mark, Marion & Hoffman 2010, J. Theoretical Biology 266: 504-515 ("interface games"); the Fitness-Beats-Truth theorem (Prakash, Stephens, Hoffman, Singh, Fields, Acta Biotheoretica, online 2020; volume and pages not confirmed). The claim is that organisms tuned to fitness payoffs outcompete those tuned to the truth.
- Criticisms VERIFIED:
  - The theorem holds for specific payoff structures, and the simulations are said to be rigged toward the interface strategy.
  - Some mapping to reality remains even when fitness is maximised.
  - Debunking-style arguments can be self-defeating (Synthese 2022, "Debunking interface theory: why Hoffman's skepticism (really) is self-defeating").
  - The jump from "perception is tuned for fitness" to "space and time are not fundamental, and conscious agents are" is the weakest step.
  - Publishers Weekly called it "intriguing but overreaching".
- Fair verdict (PLAUSIBLE): taken seriously as a provocative modelling and philosophical argument. The mild form (perception is shaped by usefulness) is accepted. The strong form (perception resembles nothing real) is a minority view, and his metaphysics is not mainstream physics.
- FLAG: write "Hoffman argues", never "science has shown space and time are an illusion".

A5. Contemporary defenders.
- Goff, Galileo's Error: Foundations for a New Science of Consciousness (Pantheon, 2019). VERIFIED. Panpsychism: consciousness is a basic feature of the physical world. Standard objection: the combination problem (how many tiny experiences add up to one mind). Goff admits it is unsolved. VERIFIED.
- Kastrup, The Idea of the World: A Multi-Disciplinary Argument for the Mental Nature of Reality (Iff Books, 2019). VERIFIED. Analytic idealism: reality is one universal consciousness, and individual minds are dissociated "alters", like dissociative identity disorder. Standard objection: real dissociation has a brain as its substrate, and the account does not explain why or how a universal mind fragments. VERIFIED.
- Kastrup is the nearest to the essay's axiom (2). He holds a PhD in computer engineering, worked at CERN and Philips Research, and founded Silicon Hive, so he is a natural ally for the author's computing voice. VERIFIED (Wikipedia, philpeople).
- FLAG: panpsychism is not "all is one" (many basic micro subjects). Keep Goff and Kastrup separate.

A6. Determinism and physics.
- Laplace, Essai philosophique sur les probabilités, 1814: VERIFIED. An "intellect" that knew all forces and positions would see the future like the past. The word "demon" is later; Laplace wrote "une intelligence". Paraphrase, do not quote the English.
- Quantum mechanics: PLAUSIBLE and supported (search: Bohmian mechanics and many worlds deterministic, Copenhagen probabilistic). Careful version: the Schrödinger equation is deterministic for the wavefunction, and measurement outcomes are probabilistic in standard (Copenhagen-style) and collapse interpretations. Bohmian mechanics and many worlds keep the whole description deterministic, with probabilities appearing from limited knowledge or from the observer's place inside the branching. Many worlds still lacks agreement on deriving the Born rule.
- Quantum fields: VERIFIED. In quantum field theory and the Standard Model, an electron is an excitation of the electron field, a photon of the electromagnetic field.
- FLAG: "particles as physics describes" should be "fields, whose excitations show up as particles". Gravity is not included in this description.

======== B. COMPUTING METAPHOR ========

B1. Virtual memory and process isolation: VERIFIED (Wikipedia "Process isolation", CS course slides). Each process has its own virtual address space and page table, mapped by the OS and MMU onto shared physical RAM.
- FLAG: "by default" is essential. The kernel and root can read anything. Same-user debugging calls can read another process, subject to permissions. Meltdown and Spectre (January 2018) leaked data across process and kernel boundaries through speculative execution (VERIFIED, multiple sources). Isolation is an arrangement, not a law.

B2. Virtualization: VERIFIED. A hypervisor runs many VMs on one host. Each VM has virtual CPU, RAM, disks and network interfaces, and behaves as if it had dedicated hardware.

B3. Shared-memory IPC: VERIFIED (Linux shm_overview(7), POSIX). Processes call shm_open and mmap to map the same object into their own address spaces. Access normally needs synchronisation (semaphores).

B4. Sandbox or container escape: VERIFIED. Exploiting a vulnerability to break out of an isolated environment and reach the host or other workloads. Cause: a flaw in the runtime, the kernel, or the configuration. Also VM escape, the same idea for hypervisors.

B5. Screen distance versus the machine: PLAUSIBLE (standard graphics knowledge). GPU textures are stored tiled or swizzled (Morton or Z order), not row by row (VERIFIED in search). Windows are normally drawn to separate buffers and then composited (not confirmed here). So pixels that are neighbours on screen need not be neighbours in memory, and side-by-side windows can belong to unrelated processes.

B6. Is the analogy used by serious thinkers? I found no canonical "many selves, one machine" source, so present it as the author's own.
- Nearest relatives: Hoffman's desktop interface (VERIFIED); Bostrom's simulation argument (VERIFIED); Wheeler's "it from bit" (1989, published 1990 in "Information, physics, quantum: the search for links", VERIFIED); Kastrup's dissociation, which is not a computing analogy.
- Bostrom, "Are You Living in a Computer Simulation?", Philosophical Quarterly 53(211): 243-255, 2003. VERIFIED. It argues that at least one of three propositions is true: humanity goes extinct before posthumanity; posthumans almost never run ancestor simulations; or we almost certainly live in a simulation. It is a trilemma, not a claim that we are simulated. The essay should say it uses a computer as a picture and does not endorse the simulation hypothesis.

======== C. SPACE FLIGHT ========

C1. Speeds. Escape speed is exactly sqrt(2) times circular orbital speed at the same distance from Earth's centre: VERIFIED (energy argument; search results).
- WRONG as a mixed ratio: 11.2 km/s is the surface escape speed. 7.8 km/s is a rounded low-orbit figure. 11.2/7.8 = 1.44, not 1.41. Like for like, by my calculation with standard constants:
  - At the surface, circular speed is 7.91 km/s (hypothetical) and escape speed is 11.19 km/s.
  - At ISS height (408 km), circular speed is 7.67 km/s and escape speed is 10.84 km/s.
- Safe: "about 7.7 to 7.8 km/s for low orbit and about 11.2 km/s to escape from the surface", and "escape speed is about 41 percent higher than orbit speed at the same height".
- NASA-style "17,500 mph" is slightly high. Real ISS speed is about 27,600 km/h.
- Extra for the staged metaphor (my calculation):
  - Leaving the Sun's pull from Earth's orbit needs about 42 km/s relative to the Sun. Launched from Earth's surface (the "third cosmic velocity") that is about 16.7 km/s, since Earth already moves at 29.8 km/s.
  - Leaving the Milky Way from our position needs about 500 to 550 km/s (VERIFIED in search; Gaia-based estimates 497 to ~550).
  - So every stage has its own escape speed.

C2. ISS: VERIFIED (NASA-derived pages). Altitude about 400 km (250 miles), varying with reboosts; my range is 400 to 420 km. Period about 90 minutes (my calculation: 92 to 93 minutes; 16 sunrises a day).
- Gravity at that height: my calculation gives 88 to 89 percent of surface gravity (88.5% at 400 km, 88.0% at 420 km). Sources say "roughly 90%", so "close to 90 percent" is fine and "about 88 percent" is exact.
- Weightlessness: VERIFIED (continuous free fall, "microgravity" is the better word).

C3. Voyager 1 crossed the heliopause on 25 August 2012 at about 122 AU. VERIFIED (Science News, NASA, Centauri Dreams). The date was established afterwards and reported in Science on 12 September 2013.
- Now about 166 AU, about 17 km/s (about 61,000 km/h), one light-day from Earth expected in November 2026 (VERIFIED, news sources).
- FLAG: "interstellar space" here means outside the Sun's bubble of particles and field. Voyager is still deep inside the Sun's gravitational domain. By my calculation, at 165 AU the local solar escape speed is only about 3.3 km/s, so it is unbound and will never return. Safe wording: "it has left the Sun's bubble, though not the Sun's gravitational reach."

C4. Milky Way about 100,000 light years across (NASA Goddard). VERIFIED. Observable universe diameter about 93 billion light years. VERIFIED. FLAG: the second is a comoving distance across the observable part only. Say "about 90 billion light years across, as far as we can see". The whole universe may be larger, possibly infinite.

C5. Airliner: Airbus A320 about 828 km/h (Mach 0.78), Boeing 737 about 830 to 870 km/h (VERIFIED, aviation sources). About 900 km/h is right for large long haul jets (about Mach 0.85; PLAUSIBLE, not confirmed here).
- Equator about 40,075 km (PLAUSIBLE): about 44.5 hours of flying at 900 km/h. The passenger-aircraft circumnavigation record is 31 h 27 min 49 s, Air France Concorde, 15-16 August 1995, with stops (VERIFIED, Guinness).
- Safe: "any place on Earth within about a day of flying, plus connections" (PLAUSIBLE: antipodal 20,000 km is about 22 hours at 900 km/h).

C6. Kerbal Space Program. VERIFIED. Developed by Squad (Mexico), first public version 24 June 2011, Steam early access March 2013, full release 27 April 2015, Take-Two bought it in 2017. Reaching a stable orbit around Kerbin is one of the first big milestones (KSP wiki, Wikipedia).

C7. Metaphor flags.
- "Escape velocity leaves Earth's gravity" is loose. Gravity never reaches zero. It means moving fast enough that Earth's pull can never bring you back.
- "Beyond the universe, the multiverse" is a speculative hypothesis (eternal inflation, string landscape, many worlds), and critics say it is hard to test. "Hint" is fine. State that it is not established. "Universe" in its strict sense already means everything.

======== D. SEVERAL LIVES LOGIC ========

D1. Strongest honest objection. The chain is valid only if you grant "the aim of development is to reach unity", which is unargued. The next step, "most people do not reach it in one life", counts something nobody can observe from outside. The conclusion, a continuing soul, then has no mechanism and can explain any outcome, including no memory at all, so it is unfalsifiable. Also, if there is only one mind, the question "who reincarnates?" needs an answer.
- Mainstream science has no accepted evidence for survival after death. Ian Stevenson's roughly 2,500 to 3,000 child cases did not convince the scientific community, because critics point to cultural influence, parental suggestion and retrospective testimony (VERIFIED, summaries).
- Standard reply from within the traditions: they do not argue from a purpose. They claim to report what practitioners observe and say it is to be tested by practice (Buddhist "come and see", ehipassiko; PLAUSIBLE). Advaita answers the "who" question by saying individuality belongs to the level of appearance, not the ultimate level. Karma and rebirth are taught as part of the same ordered cosmos in which development is real (PLAUSIBLE).
- FLAG: "so something continues: the soul" overreaches. Buddhism teaches rebirth but denies a permanent soul (anatta). What continues is a causal series, like a flame passed from candle to candle (Milindapanha; VERIFIED in search). Hindu traditions speak of atman or jiva; Bhagavad Gita 2.22 uses the change-of-garments image (VERIFIED). Abrahamic traditions mostly teach one life. Say "many traditions" and "something continues", and keep "soul" for the traditions that use it.

D2. The IT Crowd: VERIFIED. British sitcom on Channel 4, created, written and directed by Graham Linehan. It premiered 3 February 2006, ran four series of six episodes, and ended with a special on 27 September 2013. Roy Trenneman is played by Chris O'Dowd. The phone greeting is "Hello, IT. Have you tried turning it off and on again?" Roy and Moss (Richard Ayoade) both say it. It is "off and on", not "on and off". The earlier "Have you tried forcing an unexpected reboot?" is a different joke.

======== SAFE SENTENCES FOR PRINT ========

1. In 1995 the philosopher David Chalmers named the hard problem of consciousness: even when we have explained everything the brain does, how it reacts, remembers and reports, we still have to explain why any of it is accompanied by experience.
2. Most neuroscientists work on the assumption that conscious experience depends on activity in the brain, and the evidence for that dependence is strong. What nobody has yet is an accepted account of why that activity feels like something from the inside.
3. In 1944 the physicist Erwin Schrödinger ended What Is Life? by arguing that the plurality of minds is only apparent, and he called this the teaching of the Upanishads. In his 1956 lectures he wrote that the overall number of minds is just one, and he said himself that here he was speaking as a philosopher and not as a physicist.
4. Baruch Spinoza argued in his Ethics that there is only one substance, which he called God or Nature, and that everything else is a mode of it. When a rabbi cabled Einstein in 1929 to ask whether he believed in God, Einstein answered that he believed in Spinoza's God, who reveals himself in the lawful harmony of the world.
5. The cognitive scientist Donald Hoffman argues that evolution shaped perception to keep us alive and not to show us the world as it is, so that space, time and objects work like icons on a computer desktop. Most scientists accept that perception is tuned for usefulness, and many reject his stronger conclusion.
6. Philip Goff defends panpsychism, the view that consciousness is a basic feature of the physical world, and critics ask how many small experiences could add up to one mind. Bernardo Kastrup defends idealism, in which reality is one mind and individual lives are like dissociated parts of it, and critics ask why and how a single mind would split.
7. In 1814 Laplace imagined an intellect that knew every force and position in the universe and could therefore compute the whole future, which became the classic picture of determinism. Quantum theory in its standard form predicts only probabilities for measurement results, though some interpretations, such as Bohmian mechanics and many worlds, keep the description deterministic.
8. Modern physics describes the world with quantum fields, and what we call particles are understood as excitations of those fields.
9. On a modern operating system every process gets its own private address space, which the operating system and the memory management unit map onto shared physical memory, so by default one process cannot read another's memory.
10. A hypervisor lets many virtual machines run on one physical host, and each sees its own virtual hardware.
11. Processes that need to cooperate can map the same piece of memory into both their address spaces, which is called shared memory.
12. A sandbox escape is when code exploits a flaw to break out of its isolated environment and reach the host or other workloads.
13. Distance on a screen belongs to the picture: two windows side by side may belong to unrelated programs, and neighbouring pixels need not sit next to each other in memory.
14. Isolation is an arrangement and not a law: in 2018 the Meltdown and Spectre flaws showed that information could leak across the walls between processes.
15. Nick Bostrom's 2003 paper argues that at least one of three statements is true, one being that we almost certainly live in a simulation. This essay uses the computer as a picture and does not claim we live in one.
16. A low orbit needs about 7.7 kilometres per second, and escaping Earth from the surface needs about 11.2; at any height, escape speed is about 41 percent higher than orbit speed there.
17. The International Space Station circles Earth about 400 kilometres up, roughly once every 90 minutes. Gravity there is still close to 90 percent of its pull at the surface, and the astronauts float because they are in continuous free fall.
18. In August 2012 Voyager 1 passed the edge of the Sun's bubble of particles and entered interstellar space, though it is still within the Sun's gravitational reach.
19. Leaving the Sun's gravity needs about 16.7 kilometres per second from Earth's surface, and leaving the Milky Way from where we are needs something like 500 kilometres per second or more.
20. The Milky Way is about 100,000 light years across, and the part of the universe we can see is about 90 billion light years across.
21. A long haul airliner cruises at about 900 kilometres per hour, which puts nearly any point on Earth within a day or so of flying.
22. The video game Kerbal Space Program, made by Squad and first released in 2011, teaches orbital mechanics by trial and error, and reaching orbit is the first big milestone.
23. Some physicists speculate that our universe is one of many, but this idea has not been tested.
24. Many traditions teach that development continues across lives, and the argument runs from a premise, not from proof: if the aim is unity and one life is rarely enough, something must carry on.
25. In the sitcom The IT Crowd, Roy answers every call with "Have you tried turning it off and on again?"
