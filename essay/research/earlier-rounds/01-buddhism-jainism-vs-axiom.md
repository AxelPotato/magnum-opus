BOTTOM LINE
1. The author is right about the shape of the path, in both traditions. A constructed sense of a separate self is the root problem. It can end. Something continues across lives, and growth is staged. Ethics follows from taking others to be like oneself. Letting go is the method.
2. He is wrong, or a specialist will say he overreaches, on the step that the separation is the experience of one whole.
   - Jainism says the opposite in its own canon. The Sutrakritanga names and rejects the doctrine of a single soul. Liberated souls stay plural. Some souls (abhavya) are said never to be liberated.
   - Theravada has no whole, no ground and no creator. The Pali Buddha treats "all is a unity" as an extreme (SN 12.48, see below).
   - Madhyamaka and Yogacara are not monist.
   - Only some Mahayana strands come close to "one whole": Huayan, tathagatagarbha, Dzogchen's ground, and the Tibetan Jonang school's "other emptiness" (shentong). Other Buddhists have accused these strands of smuggling in a Hindu Self.
3. So the first fact-check ("fits badly") is fair for Jainism and for Theravada on the One/Whole clause. It is not fair on the other clauses.
4. The honest wording is "same structure of the problem and the way out, differing on whether a single whole lies behind it". The wording "they say the same thing in different words" is not defensible.

BUDDHISM
=====================================================

POINT 1. THE ONE WHOLE, OR NON-SEPARATION

THERAVADA
- 1a. Dependent origination. VERIFIED [Idappaccayata; MN 38 in Ñāṇamoli and Bodhi]: "When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises." It occurs at MN 38, 79 and 115 and in SN 12 (for example 12.21).
  - It states conditionality, chiefly of suffering (the twelve links). Nothing in it says there is one substrate.
- 1b. Rohitassa Sutta, AN 4.45 (parallel SN 2.26). VERIFIED [Thanissaro]: the cosmos, its origin, its ending and the path to its ending are declared to be within "this fathom-long body with its perception and intellect". It also says the end of the world cannot be reached by travelling.
  - Sue Hamilton reads early Buddhism as about experience, not ontology. VERIFIED that she argues this [Routledge blurb]. On that reading, "the world" here means the world as experienced. It supports "the world is made in experience". It does not support "the world is one".
- 1c. Udana 8.3 and AN 1.49 to 52.
  - Udana 8.3. VERIFIED wording [Thanissaro]: "There is, monks, an unborn, unbecome, unmade, unfabricated... precisely because there is an unborn... escape from the born... is discerned."
    - Bodhi writes (Buddhist Inquiry, "Reflections on Nibbana") that he knows of nothing in the discourses making the experience of nibbana itself unconditioned. Nibbana is not described as consciousness. VERIFIED via the search summary.
    - There is a Bodhi and Thanissaro debate on whether the unconditioned is an awareness. The SuttaCentral threads exist (VERIFIED), and the question is open.
  - AN 1.49 to 52. VERIFIED [Thanissaro]: "Luminous, monks, is the mind. And it is defiled by incoming defilements."
    - The commentary glosses it as the bhavanga citta. This is per Bodhi's note in the Numerical Discourses (VERIFIED via a summary). The bhavanga does not appear in the suttas, so critics call that gloss problematic.
    - Thanissaro's footnote reads luminosity as the quality the meditator develops, not as an intrinsic nature (reported in the same summary; check).

SN 12.48 (Lokāyatika) and SN 12.15 (Kaccānagotta). PLAUSIBLE-HIGH, not retrieved. The sutta reference surfaced in one search title but I did not see its text.
- Recollection: the Buddha lists "all exists", "all does not exist", "all is a unity" and "all is a plurality" as extremes, and teaches dependent origination as the middle.
- If this holds, it is the strongest single Pali text against the axiom and the author should read it before print.

HOW SCHOLARS DISTINGUISH INTERDEPENDENCE FROM A ONE OR A WHOLE
- Interdependence says things exist in dependence on causes, parts and designation. A One or Whole says one reality underlies the many.
- Abhidharma reductionism treats wholes as conventional constructs (the chariot). Madhyamaka rejects ultimate parts and wholes alike. VERIFIED [Siderits, Buddhism as Philosophy; Garfield].
- On Advaita, the One is ultimately real and the many are illusory. In Madhyamaka, nothing has intrinsic reality, including any "One". That is why Dzogchen and Madhyamaka thinkers reject substantialist non-duality.
- "Non-separation" is a loose English term. It is safe for "no separate self" and unsafe for "one cosmic Self".
- The closest Buddhist claims to a Whole are Huayan, tathagatagarbha, Dzogchen and shentong. All have been contested inside Buddhism.

POINT 2. SEPARATION AS VEIL, IGNORANCE OR ILLUSION
- 2a. Ignorance (avijja) is the first link of dependent origination. The belief in a personal self (sakkaya ditthi) is the first fetter dropped at stream entry. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH.
- 2b. The "conceit I am" (asmimana). Khemaka Sutta, SN 22.89. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: even non-returners retain a residue of "I am", like the lingering smell of a washed cloth, and it is removed last.
  - This suits the author: the sense of separation is a layered construction, undone in stages.
- 2c. What scholars say about no-self.
  - Thanissaro: not-self is a strategy for dis-identification, not a metaphysical claim, and the Buddha never drew the conclusion "there is no self". VERIFIED [The Not-Self Strategy; Selves and Not-self].
  - Bodhi: the Buddha did not say "no self" because annihilationists said it, not because a transcendent self exists. VERIFIED via a SuttaCentral report of Bodhi's endnote on SN 44.10.
  - Hamilton: early Buddhism is about experience and personal continuity, not what one is or is not. VERIFIED.
  - Gethin: PLAUSIBLE. He is the standard reader of the aggregates as a process. I did not retrieve a quote.
- 2d. Yogacara. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: the "imagined nature" (parikalpita) is the false grid of subject and object laid over experience.
  - The Pali similes of foam, bubble and mirage (SN 22.95) say the aggregates are insubstantial. They do not say the world is unreal.
- Fit: partial. The self is a construction. The Buddhist texts rarely say that the world is an illusion made by a whole.

POINT 3. THE RETURN OR END OF SEPARATION
- 3a. Nibbana. Udana 8.1 to 8.4 (8.3 VERIFIED above). PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: the ending of greed, hatred and delusion, and of "I-making, my-making and the underlying tendency to conceit" (SN 22.89 and similar).
  - It is described by negations (unborn, unmade). Bodhi insists it is not a consciousness or ground (VERIFIED).
- 3b. Mahayana. The bodhisattva's "non-abiding nirvana" refuses a private end. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH.
  - The tenth ox-herding picture, "entering the marketplace with open hands", is by Kuoan (12th century). PLAUSIBLE-HIGH.
  - Shantideva's wish to remain "as long as space remains" is BCA 10.55. PLAUSIBLE, not confirmed.
- Judgment: "end of separation" fits. "Return to knowing itself as one" fits only Huayan, tathagatagarbha and Dzogchen language. Theravada nibbana is not a return to anything.

POINT 4. SOMETHING CONTINUES, AND DEVELOPMENT CONTINUES ACROSS LIVES
- 4a. SN 15.14 to 19. VERIFIED [Thanissaro]: "From an inconceivable beginning comes the wandering-on. A beginning point is not discernible"; a being who has not been your mother (or father, brother, sister, son, daughter) is "not easy to find".
  - The conclusion drawn is disenchantment and release. Tibetans use the same idea to cultivate love (PLAUSIBLE-HIGH, lojong and lamrim).
  - Tension for the axiom: the canon refuses a first beginning. The axiom needs separation to begin, or at least a ground.
- 4b. What continues. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: MN 38 rebukes the monk Sati for claiming the same consciousness travels. Milindapanha says "neither the same nor another" and uses a flame lit from a flame. It is a continuity of causal process, not a transmigrating entity.
- 4c. The bardo debate. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: the intermediate state (antarabhava) is accepted by Sarvastivada and Tibetan schools and rejected by Theravada (Kathavatthu).
- 4d. Staging. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: stream enterer, once-returner, non-returner and arahant, with at most seven more lives for a stream enterer. It is a real staged scale across lives.

POINT 5. WHAT YOU DO TO ANOTHER YOU DO TO YOURSELF; ETHICS FROM UNITY
- 5a. Dhammapada 129 to 130. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH, paraphrase only: all tremble at violence, life is dear to all, putting oneself in the place of another, do not kill or cause killing. The Pali is a comparison ("having made oneself the example"), not an identity. Also SN 3.8 (Mallika): none is dearer to oneself than oneself, so do not harm another (PLAUSIBLE).
  - Metta Sutta, Sn 1.8. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: a mother's protectiveness extended to all beings.
- 5b. Shantideva. 8.91 VERIFIED (above). 8.101 to 102 VERIFIED [Tsadra]: a "continuum" or "group" (rosary, army) is not a findable whole, and since there is no owner of suffering, whose is it as "mine"? 8.120 VERIFIED: whoever seeks to rescue himself and others quickly should practise the supreme secret, exchanging self and other. Terms: parātmasamatā (equality of self and other) and parātmaparivartana (exchange).
  - Key finding: Shantideva reaches compassion by saying there is no owner of suffering, not by saying we are one.
  - Paul Williams, "Altruism and Reality" (1998), argues this does not work. VERIFIED that he does. Goodman and others defend it (PLAUSIBLE).
- 5c. Kamma as intention, AN 6.63. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: "Intention, monks, is kamma I say."
- Judgment: ethics from "I am like you" and "no one owns suffering". Not from "we are one body", with the Padmakara 8.91 and the commentary's "single I" as the nearest.

POINT 6. A SECOND BIRTH OR NEW STAGE
- 6a. Stream entry and the Dhamma eye. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH (SN 56.11): "whatever is subject to origination is subject to cessation". The noble one (ariya) vs the worldling (puthujjana) is a real threshold. The commentarial "change of lineage" (gotrabhu) moment makes it explicit.
  - Pali also calls noble disciples "born of Dhamma" (DN 27). PLAUSIBLE, check. That repurposes the Brahmin "twice-born" idea.
- 6b. Shantideva BCA 3.25. VERIFIED across translations [Lotsawa House and others]: "Today my birth has become fruitful... Today I am born into the Buddha's family; I am now a child of the Buddha." Wording varies by translator.
- 6c. Zen. The "great death": Dogen's "casting off body and mind" is VERIFIED [Terebess; other summaries]. He heard Ju-ching's rebuke and awakened. Hakuin and Bunan's "die while alive" are PLAUSIBLE.
- Is it a real "second birth"? In Mahayana and Zen, yes, explicitly. In Theravada, it is a threshold of insight with new-life imagery, not a rebirth.

POINT 7. LETTING GO AS THE METHOD
- 7a. Anapanasati, MN 118, step 16. VERIFIED [Thanissaro]: "I will breathe in focusing on relinquishing." Pali: patinissagga and vossagga. Cagga (renunciation, generosity) PLAUSIBLE-HIGH.
  - Upadana (clinging) as the cause of becoming and suffering. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH.
  - "Calming the bodily formation" is an earlier step. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH. A "body scan" as a technique is modern, so do not say the canon prescribes it.
- 7b. Sallatha Sutta, SN 36.6. VERIFIED [Thanissaro]: the uninstructed person feels two pains, bodily and mental; the instructed disciple feels one.
- 7c. Dogen. VERIFIED. Genjokan: studying the self is forgetting the self, and being verified by the myriad things is the dropping off of the body and mind of self and others. Dzogchen: relaxing is central (Longchenpa's "Trilogy of Rest", PLAUSIBLE; Tilopa's Mahamudra "don't control, rest" is Mahamudra, PLAUSIBLE-HIGH).

JAINISM
=====================================================

POINT 1. THE ONE WHOLE, OR NON-SEPARATION
- 1a. Equality of souls. VERIFIED: every soul (jiva) has by nature infinite knowledge, perception, bliss and energy (ananta catushtaya), hidden by karma. These are properties of each soul. They are not properties of one soul.
- 1b. Pluralism is explicit. VERIFIED [Sutrakritanga on ekatmavada; herenow4u; Wikipedia]: the Sutrakritanga states and rejects the doctrine of one soul reflected in beings like the moon in water. Its argument: if the soul were one, the death or suffering of one would be the death or suffering of all.
  - Jaini, Dundas and Matilal are the usual scholarly references for Jain pluralism against Advaita monism (VERIFIED as referenced).
- 1c. Tattvartha Sutra 5.21. VERIFIED [Wikipedia]: "Parasparopagraho jivanam", variously "Souls render service to one another" or "reciprocal dependence of living beings". It is the motto of Jainism today. It means mutual support among distinct souls.
- 1d. Anekantavada. VERIFIED as a doctrine of many-sidedness [Matilal; Dundas]. It means each view captures part of reality. It does not say every view is equally true or that reality is one. Jain thinkers used it to rank other schools, including Advaita, as one-sided.
- 1e. Acharanga: see corrections above. The point is equality of experience, not identity.
- Judgment: Jainism supports "all beings are alike and linked", and contradicts "one whole".

POINT 2. SEPARATION AS VEIL
- 2a. Karma as matter that veils the soul. VERIFIED [Pluralism Project, Harvard]: karma is a material substance that "begins to cover and discolor the luminous soul". It obscures the soul's natural powers of knowledge, vision and bliss.
  - Jainqq texts (VERIFIED via summary): knowledge-obscuring karma "does not reduce the soul's capability but restricts its exercise", like clouds over the sun. Even thick clouds do not fully hide the sun's light.
  - This is a strong structural parallel to a veil over a nature already complete. It is the veil of an individual soul, though, not of a whole.
- 2b. Mithyatva (wrong belief). VERIFIED [Jain World, gunasthanas]: the first stage is "perversity of attitude towards truth". It is the very opposite of right perception. In Jainism this means wrong belief about the soul and the world, in Jain terms, not a sense of separation.

POINT 3. THE RETURN OR END OF SEPARATION
- 3a. Moksha and omniscience. VERIFIED [Wikipedia on kevala jnana]: kevala jnana is an intrinsic quality of every soul, masked by karma, covering all substances in all their aspects.
- 3b. Siddhas. VERIFIED [Jainpedia; Pluralism Project]: a liberated soul rises to the top of the universe (siddhashila). Siddhas keep their individuality. One siddha shares space with others like lights from many lamps in a room.
  - Jain liberation is not absorption into a greater being.
  - PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: some souls (abhavya) can never be liberated. This breaks "the separation ends for all".
- Judgment: "the knowing ends the separation" fits. "Returns to being one" does not.

POINT 4. SOMETHING CONTINUES, AND DEVELOPMENT CONTINUES ACROSS LIVES
- 4a. Four gatis. VERIFIED [UPSC and Jain sources]: heavenly, human, animal and plant, and hell beings. A soul cycles under the weight of karma until liberated. Only human birth leads to liberation. Samsara is beginningless (PLAUSIBLE-HIGH).
- 4b. Gunasthana. VERIFIED [Jain World; Wikipedia]: fourteen stages from total bondage in ignorance to freedom, a process that takes many lifetimes. Stage 1 is mithyatva. Stage 4 is avirata samyagdrishti (right belief without vows). PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: 2 falling back, 3 mixed, 5 partial vows, 6 and 7 monk stages, 8 to 10 ascent, 11 and 12 delusion suppressed or destroyed, 13 and 14 omniscient with or without activity.
  - Highly relevant to a staged scale. Unlike a pure arrow, the soul can fall back.
- Judgment: fits strongly.

POINT 5. WHAT YOU DO TO ANOTHER YOU DO TO YOURSELF
- 5a. Ahimsa grounded in the equality of souls. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: all beings want to live and dislike pain (Acharanga 1.2.3 in Jacobi's sense). That is the same ground as Dhammapada 129 and Shantideva's "all one in wanting happiness".
- 5b. The forgiveness verse. VERIFIED [Jaina.org, Kshamavani]: "Khamemi savve jive, savve jiva khamantu me, mitti me savva bhuesu, veram majjha na kenai." I forgive all beings, may all beings forgive me, I am friendly with all, I hold enmity toward none. It is recited at Samvatsari (Paryushana). "Micchami dukkadam" is the shorter apology, roughly "may my bad deeds be fruitless" (PLAUSIBLE).
- 5c. Tattvartha 5.21 (above).
- Judgment: ethics from equality and kinship of souls. Not unity.

POINT 6. A SECOND BIRTH OR NEW STAGE
- 6a. Samyak darshana, right perception. VERIFIED: the fourth gunasthana (avirata samyagdrishti) is the first stage of right belief. PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: reaching it follows the "cutting of the knot" (granthi bheda). Jain texts call it the root of the path.
- No "second birth" language was found. It is a threshold of insight, and the soul can fall back from the second stage. Say "threshold", not "second birth".

POINT 7. LETTING GO AS THE METHOD
- 7a. Samayika. VERIFIED [Wikipedia; jainqq]: equanimity practice for at least 48 minutes (one muhurta) a day to remove attraction and aversion (raga and dvesa).
- 7b. Kayotsarga. VERIFIED: "giving up the body", a standing meditation where the practitioner stays motionless, unaffected by surroundings and emotions.
- 7c. Aparigraha (non-possession) and vairagya (detachment). PLAUSIBLE-HIGH: core Jain vows and attitudes.
- Judgment: strong fit with the author's Part one.

=====================================================
LAYERS: PUBLIC VS INNER TEACHING
=====================================================
(a) Public view and the deeper view, for each camp.
1. "Buddhism teaches there is no self."
   - Public: no soul, no self.
   - Theravada scholars: Bodhi (VERIFIED) says the Buddha avoided "no self" to dodge annihilationism, without affirming a hidden self. Thanissaro (VERIFIED) calls not-self a strategy, not a doctrine. SN 44.10 is the standard text where the Buddha is silent when asked "is there a self?" and "is there no self?". VERIFIED [Thanissaro]: yes would support eternalism, no would support annihilationism and would bewilder Vacchagotta.
   - Mahayana inner teaching: the buddha nature texts explicitly call it a "true self" (Mahaparinirvana Sutra, VERIFIED). Critical Buddhism says that is not Buddhism (VERIFIED).
   - So there is an inner layer, but it is Mahayana only, and it is disputed by other Buddhists.
2. "Nirvana is nothing."
   - Public: extinction.
   - Pali: the unborn and unconditioned. Bodhi: an unconditioned reality that is not consciousness. Some forest teachers: an unconditioned awareness. Both camps are Theravada.
3. "Jainism is dualist and atheist, a religion of strict rules."
   - Inner: the soul's intrinsic perfection, with karma as the veil. Digambara teachers such as Kundakunda stress the "pure soul" (suddhatman) viewpoint. PLAUSIBLE, dates debated.
   - Still plural: countless souls, no creator, no merging.
4. Jain pluralism vs Advaita monism. Jains reject a single soul; Advaita rejects plurality as ultimate. The Sutrakritanga and later Jain polemicists attack Advaita (VERIFIED); Advaitins attack Jain anekantavada (PLAUSIBLE).

OBJECTIONS A SPECIALIST WOULD MAKE
=====================================================
(b) The strongest objection to "Buddhism and Jainism say the same as Advaita Vedanta in different words".
1. Different ontology. Advaita says one real Self (Brahman) and the many are appearance. Buddhism denies any such self or ground. Jainism affirms countless real souls. Madhyamaka rejects Advaita's "substantialist non-duality" as eternalism (VERIFIED, blog level).
2. Texts. If SN 12.48 reads as I recall, the Pali Buddha lists "all is one" as an extreme. A Jain text refutes the "one soul" view (VERIFIED).
3. Ethics. Shantideva's argument (no owner of suffering) and the Jain argument (each soul equal and wanting life) do not use identity.
4. Soteriology. Siddhas stay many. Buddhist nirvana is not a return. For Advaita, liberation is recognising that one was always Brahman.
5. Beginning. The Pali canon says the beginning of samsara is not discernible, and it sets aside questions about a first cause. A "whole that begins to experience separation" is not canonical.
6. Scholarly figures and what they are known for (I could confirm only the starred ones this session):
   - Bhikkhu Bodhi*: nibbana is not a consciousness or ground; the Buddha did not deny self out of fear of annihilationism.
   - Thanissaro Bhikkhu*: not-self is a strategy; no metaphysical claim.
   - Sue Hamilton*: early Buddhism is about experience, not ontology.
   - Paul Williams*: argued Shantideva's no-self ethics fails; has written on the tathagatagarbha (details PLAUSIBLE).
   - Rupert Gethin: standard reader of dependent origination as structure (PLAUSIBLE; exact claims not retrieved).
   - Garfield* and Siderits*: dependence as causal, part-whole and designation; wholes are conventional; no ultimate ground.
   - Hakamaya and Matsumoto*: buddha nature is dhatu-vada, akin to Upanishadic monism.
   - Padmanabh Jaini, John Cort, Paul Dundas: Jain pluralism, the karma-as-matter doctrine, the stable metaphysics (general support seen; Cort's own words not retrieved).
   - Richard King (PLAUSIBLE-HIGH): "Early Advaita Vedanta and Buddhism" argues Gaudapada drew on Mahayana. So there was historical borrowing. That is a good point for the author, but it implies mutual influence and later polemic, not identity.
   - Donald Lopez* and Robert Sharf (PLAUSIBLE): Buddhist modernism reads Buddhism as consistent with science and mysticism. Steven Katz (PLAUSIBLE): "no pure experiences" is the standard objection to perennialism.
7. What they think of "interdependence as non-separation". Garfield and Siderits: it is a claim about conditionality and lack of intrinsic nature, not about a unified whole (VERIFIED in part). The ecological reading (Macy, Thich Nhat Hanh) is a modern interpretation.

(c) Best published statements supporting him, and whether they claim identity.
- Thich Nhat Hanh, interbeing: VERIFIED. He claims interdependence is what the Heart Sutra says. He claims identity of the teaching with "no separate self". He does not claim identity with Advaita.

- Joanna Macy, Mutual Causality in Buddhism and General Systems Theory: VERIFIED [SUNY Press]. "Remarkable convergences" between Buddhism and systems theory. Resonance, not identity.
- The Dalai Lama's dialogues: PLAUSIBLE. He stresses interdependence and I believe he rejects a creator and a universal consciousness. The Tibetan clear light mind is individual (PLAUSIBLE-HIGH).
- Alan Watts: PLAUSIBLE. A popular Vedanta-Buddhism-Taoism synthesiser. He is not a specialist source.

- Hubbard and Swanson (ed.), Pruning the Bodhi Tree: VERIFIED as a collection about the tathagatagarbha dispute. It documents the argument, not a consensus.
