CHRISTIANITY: WHAT THE SOURCES SUPPORT FOR THE AXIOM "ONE WHOLE, EXPERIENCING SEPARATION, RETURNING"

Method and limits. WebSearch only (WebFetch and Crossref blocked). The shared search budget ran out before my last four queries, so four items rest on standard knowledge and are marked PLAUSIBLE: Julian on "all man is one man", Eckhart "rid me of God" (Sermon 52), the Wenck pantheism charge against Cusa, and Eckhart's "God and I are one" (Sermon 6). Many search hits were popular sites. I say so where it matters. Tags: VERIFIED = a search result naming a source supports it; PLAUSIBLE = standard knowledge, not confirmed here; WRONG = correct it.

BOTTOM LINE
- He is right that Christianity has an old inner layer that says things close to his axiom. Three clauses fare differently:
  - "The separation ends and all return": strong match (Origen, Gregory of Nyssa, Maximus, Eriugena, 1 Cor 15:28).
  - "We are one body, so harming another harms oneself or Christ": strong match (Paul, Augustine, Anthony, Chrysostom, Silouan).
  - "One whole that experiences separation as its own self-experience": the weakest match. Only Eriugena, Eckhart in places, and later German idealism come near it, and parts of Eriugena and Eckhart were condemned.
- The whole inner layer keeps one line: union by participation, never identity of creature and Creator. The mystics themselves state it (John of the Cross, below). A specialist will press this hard.
- He should say "deep resonance, in the language of communion". Say "the same thing" only for ethics, surrender and return.

1. THE ONE WHOLE

Item 1a. Scripture, check by check.
- John 17:20-23 [VERIFIED as contested]. Scholars split. An "institutional" reading (unity among believers) gained ground after Bultmann's commentary. An "ontological" reading takes it as a real union of believers with the Father and Son. Sources: a Marquette dissertation, "'That they may all be one': John 17:21-23 and a Plotinian application of unity", and a Perichoresis journal summary. "Perichoresis" (mutual indwelling) is used almost exclusively of the Trinity. Extending it to creatures is contested because it can blur Creator and creature.
- John 10:30 [PLAUSIBLE]. "I and the Father are one" is about Father and Son, not about all humans. The usual bridge is John 10:34 quoting Ps 82:6 "you are gods". Hedge it.
- Acts 17:28 [VERIFIED]. "In him we live and move and have our being" is traditionally ascribed (Clement of Alexandria, Chrysostom) to the poet Epimenides. "We are his offspring" is from Aratus's Phaenomena, possibly Cleanthes. Witherington, cited in the results, says Paul uses the Greek line to separate the Creator from creation. One source notes the original was pantheistic in its pagan setting.
- Rom 11:36, Eph 4:4-6 ("over all and through all and in all"), Col 1:17 ("in him all things hold together"), Gal 3:28 [PLAUSIBLE]. Standard, not verified here. In context "all" in Ephesians is usually read as believers.
- 1 Cor 12:12-27 [PLAUSIBLE for text; VERIFIED that it is read as corporate "mystical body" theology]. 12:26: "if one member suffers, all suffer together". Paul's scope is the church.
- 1 Cor 15:28 [VERIFIED]. Greek "hina ē ho theos panta en pasin". Origen applied the Son's subjection to his body, which is the whole of humanity, and used the verse as a central scriptural argument for universal restoration. Gregory of Nyssa inherited this. His treatise is In illud: Tunc et ipse Filius, written against Arians.

Item 1b. Gospel of Thomas 77 [VERIFIED].
- Text in the version found: "I am the light that is over all things. I am all: from me all came forth, and to me all attained. Split a piece of wood; I am there. Lift up the stone, and you will find me there." Wording varies by translation.
- Found at Nag Hammadi in 1945.
- Read as holism or divine immanence.
- Caveats. It is non-canonical. Its date (late first to mid second century) is debated [PLAUSIBLE]. Part of the wood and stone line has a Greek parallel in Oxyrhynchus Papyrus 1 [PLAUSIBLE].

Item 1c. Fathers, Orthodoxy, medieval mystics.
- Augustine, totus Christus [VERIFIED]. In the Enarrationes in Psalmos he calls Christ and the Church "the whole Christ, head and body". Christ prays, loves and suffers in his members. The scope is ecclesial and sacramental, not all beings.
- Essence and energies [VERIFIED]. Palamas holds a real distinction. Creatures are contingent participants in God's energies, never in his essence. Scholars dispute whether "panentheism" fits. A journal article titled "'Orthodox panentheism' is neither orthodox nor coherent" exists (PhilArchive ROOOPI). Others say panentheism rests on Palamas. I recall Kallistos Ware using the word approvingly [PLAUSIBLE]. State the objection that this is not pantheism.
- Eckhart's eye [VERIFIED for the text and the trial]. Wording: "The eye with which I see God is the same eye with which God sees me: my eye and God's eye are one eye, one seeing, one knowing and one love." It is from the German sermon Qui audit me, on Sirach 24:30, often cited as Pr. 12 (Quint). Sermon numbering differs across editions (one source puts it at Walshe's Sermon 57), and the translator of the popular wording is unconfirmed. Cite by title and edition. It was among the objections raised at Cologne in 1326 but did NOT make the final list of the 1329 bull. A 2023 Taylor and Francis article is titled "The Eye with Which I See God is the Same Eye with Which God Sees Me: Meister Eckhart on Divine Awareness".
- Nicholas of Cusa [VERIFIED]. In De docta ignorantia God is the coincidence of opposites, the absolute maximum that is also the minimum. Things are the maximum "contracted". Creation is "enfolded" in God and "unfolded" in the world. The pantheism charge from Wenck is PLAUSIBLE only.
- Eriugena, Periphyseon [VERIFIED]. Nature includes God and creation, in four divisions. He says God is also "created" in that he is the essence of all created things. Procession and return correspond to creation and redemption. It was condemned by Honorius III in 1225, with David of Dinant and Amaury of Bene, for promoting identity of God and creation.
- Teresa of Avila, Interior Castle, seventh mansions [VERIFIED]. Spiritual marriage is likened to rain falling into a river, or light entering by two windows. Teresa stresses permanence.
- John of the Cross [VERIFIED as quoted in a Living Flame piece]. The soul's substance is not God's substance, but it "has become God through participation". I did not confirm the exact location of this passage.
- Julian of Norwich [VERIFIED in part]. The hazelnut vision ("it is all that is made"). Her term "oned". A search summary offered "we are all one in love". I could not trace it to her text, so do not print it. Her Lord and servant chapter reads the servant as Adam and everyone [PLAUSIBLE, unchecked].
- Angelus Silesius [PLAUSIBLE]. His couplets (God cannot live without me, God is a nothing) are poetry, and he wrote as a Catholic priest. Check the exact German or translation before quoting. Search snippets were unreliable.

Verdict on point 1. Scripture and the Fathers support "all things are in God, held in Christ, and humanity is one body". They do not support "all is one Being".

2. SEPARATION AS A VEIL OR FORGETTING

Item 2a. Scripture [PLAUSIBLE].
- 2 Cor 3:14-18: the veil, "unveiled faces", "being transformed into the same image". In context it is Moses's veil and the reading of the old covenant.
- 1 Cor 13:12: Greek "in a mirror, in a riddle", not "glass darkly".
- Eph 4:18: "alienated from the life of God because of the ignorance (agnoia) that is in them". This is the best single verse for forgetting and separation. The cause named is hardness of heart.

Item 2b. Genesis 3 and division, patristic [VERIFIED].
- Maximus the Confessor, Ambiguum 41. Five divisions: uncreated and created, intelligible and sensible, heaven and earth, paradise and inhabited world, male and female. Humanity is the "bond" meant to unite them. Christ accomplishes the five unions. Hedge: the standard reading is that division becomes distinction without opposition, not that creatures dissolve [PLAUSIBLE].
- Gregory of Nyssa, On the Making of Man. Humanity is created as one whole, "our whole nature from the first to the last" being one image. The division into male and female follows from foreseen fall. Restoration waits until the full number is complete.
- Augustine, Confessions 8. "I was at strife with myself, and rent asunder by myself." He rejects the Manichaean "two minds" and calls the division the punishment of sin, "of my own mind".

Item 2c. Gnostic myths [VERIFIED].
- Gospel of Truth. Valentinian, found at Nag Hammadi, probably Greek, 140 to 180, attributed by many to Valentinus. Ignorance of the Father produces anguish, terror and Error. Knowledge dissolves forgetfulness. It is more a homily than a gospel narrative.
- Hymn of the Pearl. It sits in the Syriac Acts of Thomas (the Acts are early third century). A prince is sent to Egypt, forgets his origin, sleeps, and a letter from home wakes him. Authorship by Bardaisan is a belief, not proven.
- Valentinian myth itself has a flawed fall and a defective world [PLAUSIBLE]. The Church rejected it. It fits his "forgetting" clause best and the "one whole experiences separation" clause worst.

Verdict. Christian sources have separation and ignorance language. The cause is sin or fall, and the remedy is Christ's grace. The "forgetting of an original identity" structure is Gnostic, not mainstream.

3. THE RETURN

Item 3a. Apokatastasis, condemnations, current positions.
- 543 [VERIFIED, partly]. Justinian's edict against Origen (dated 543 or 544) and a local synod at Constantinople. Targets: pre-existence of souls and restoration of all rational creatures, including demons.
- 553 [VERIFIED]. At the Fifth Council, Origen's name stands in conciliar canon 11 among condemned heretics. The "Fifteen Anathemas" are the text that says "fabulous pre-existence of souls and the monstrous restoration which follows from it". Their conciliar status is disputed. The acts do not cite them or record a discussion. Treat "553 condemned apokatastasis" as hedged.
- Not condemned [VERIFIED]. Gregory of Nyssa remains a canonised saint. A journal article asks "Why was St Gregory of Nyssa never condemned for his doctrine of apokatastasis?" (Studia UBB, Philosophia). Reincarnation as such is not named in the anathema text I saw [PLAUSIBLE].
- Maximus [VERIFIED as disputed]. A minority holds he taught universal salvation. Most deny it. Ambiguum 15 reads toward it, Ambiguum 65 against.
- Balthasar [VERIFIED]. "Dare We Hope That All Men Be Saved?" argues for hope, not certainty. The Church has never declared a named person damned. CCC 1035 and 1037 (hell exists, God predestines no one to hell) [PLAUSIBLE].
- Orthodox [VERIFIED]. Kallistos Ware, "Dare We Hope for the Salvation of All?" (Theology Digest, 1998): "Our faith in God's love makes us dare to hope that all will be saved." He separates hoping and praying from assuming.

Item 3b. Procession and return, theosis.
- Eriugena [VERIFIED]. Procession and return are the structure of the Periphyseon.
- Maximus on return [PLAUSIBLE]. The Neoplatonic triad of remaining, procession and return shapes the Church Fathers broadly. Do not give a Maximus quote.
- Athanasius [VERIFIED]. On the Incarnation 54: "autos gar enēnthrōpēsen hina hēmeis theopoiēthōmen", "he became man that we might be made god". The verb means "be made divine", with participation, not equality.
- CCC 460 [VERIFIED]. "The Word became flesh to make us 'partakers of the divine nature'" (2 Pet 1:4), quoting Irenaeus, Athanasius and Aquinas.

Item 3c. "Every knee" and "all in all."
- Phil 2:10-11, Isa 45:23, Col 1:20, Acts 3:21 "restoration of all things", 1 Tim 2:4 [PLAUSIBLE]. Opposing texts: Matt 25:46 "eternal punishment" [PLAUSIBLE].

Verdict. The return is his best match. As a doctrine of certain universal return it is not Church teaching in any branch. As a hope it is allowed, with Origen's system condemned in some form.

4. SOMETHING CONTINUES, DEVELOPMENT CONTINUES

Item 4a. Purgatory and the intermediate state [VERIFIED].
- Catholic purgatory: purification after death of those who die in grace but imperfectly purified (CCC 1030). Formalised at Lyons II (1274) and Florence (1439). Biblical anchors: 2 Macc 12:43-45 (deuterocanonical, not in the Protestant canon), 1 Cor 3:15, Matt 12:32.
- WRONG in one search summary: "Trent 1548". Trent's decree on purgatory was Session 25, December 1563 [PLAUSIBLE, from standard knowledge].
- Orthodox accept an intermediate state and pray for the dead but reject purgatory as satisfaction or fire. "Toll houses" are disputed (doctrine, opinion or heresy) [VERIFIED].
- Dante's Purgatorio, early fourteenth century [PLAUSIBLE].
- Catholic teaching rules out return to earthly life. CCC 1013: "There is no 'reincarnation' after death" [PLAUSIBLE, not confirmed here]. Heb 9:27 is the standard text.

Item 4b. Development beyond death, in one life [VERIFIED].
- Gregory of Nyssa, epektasis (Phil 3:13, "straining forward"). The soul's progress in God never ends, because God is infinite. Moses in Life of Moses is "still unsatisfied" in desire. Jean Daniélou's anthology (From Glory to Glory) is the standard introduction.
- Origen [VERIFIED/PLAUSIBLE]. He rejected metensomatosis (transmigration into other bodies) in Contra Celsum. He speculated about souls existing before birth and a schooling across ages. One scholar argues the pre-existence attribution is a myth, so hedge. A source says Contra Celsum "preserves the language" of metensomatosis. Do not call Origen a reincarnation teacher. WRONG if stated that way.

Item 4c. Reincarnation in early Christian and Gnostic circles, honest status.
- Basilides [VERIFIED as evidence exists]. Evidence is in Clement, Stromata 4.81-83, and Origen, Commentary on Romans 5.1 and on Matthew. The reports come from opponents, and scholars read them differently.
- Valentinus [PLAUSIBLE, thin]. One search summary asserts it. I could not confirm it. Do not print.
- Manichaeism [VERIFIED]. Doctrine of transmigration: ordinary hearers pass into other bodies until perfect. The elect pass through the sun and moon to the Kingdom of Light. It is a separate religion, not a Christian school.
- Cathars [VERIFIED, weak sources]. Some believed in transmigration. Inquisition records mention seven lives, but the sources are polemical or popular. Hedge: some believers, not established as universal doctrine.
- Josephus [VERIFIED as quoted, hedge]. War 2.163: the Pharisees held that the souls of the good "pass into other bodies". Many scholars read this as a Hellenised wording for resurrection. The main Jewish view then was resurrection.
- John the Baptist as Elijah [VERIFIED]. Matt 11:14, 17:12-13, Luke 1:17 ("in the spirit and power of Elijah"), and John 1:21 where John says "I am not". Mainstream scholars read this as typological or role fulfilment of Mal 4:5, not reincarnation.
- John 9:2 [PLAUSIBLE]. The question presupposes sin before birth or parental sin. Jesus rejects both answers (9:3). It is a hint about how people then thought, not a doctrine. Wis 8:19-20 shows Hellenistic Jewish belief in pre-existing souls [PLAUSIBLE].

Item 4d. The claim that Justinian removed reincarnation from the Bible [WRONG as stated].
- The canon was not edited at the Council of 553. The Bible has no reincarnation passage to remove. The council and the anathemas concerned Origenist teaching on pre-existence and restoration, and the anathemas' standing is disputed.
- One quoted scholar called the "ban on reincarnation" "an historical error" with no ecclesiastical authority. I could not identify the name, so do not attribute it.

5. WHAT YOU DO TO ANOTHER YOU DO TO YOURSELF

Item 5a. Scripture.
- Acts 9:4 [VERIFIED]. "Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?" Christ identifies with persecuted disciples, read as the corporate Christ. Augustine reads it as the head speaking for the members.
- Matt 25:40 [VERIFIED]. Craig Keener says the "least of these" are disciples, "the majority view in church history and among contemporary NT scholars". Blomberg and Carson agree. The universal reading (any needy person) is also long standing. Hedge.
- 1 John 4:20, Luke 10:25-37, Matt 18:21-35, Matt 7:12, Gal 6:7 [PLAUSIBLE]. Their logic is reciprocity and one family of believers, not identity of persons. Gal 6:7 ("whatever one sows, that will he also reap") sits in a context of conduct and judgment.

Item 5b. Fathers.
- Abba Anthony [VERIFIED]. "Our life and our death is with our neighbour. If we gain our brother, we have gained God, but if we scandalize our brother, we have sinned against Christ."
- Chrysostom [VERIFIED, paraphrase safer]. Homilies on Matthew. If you honour Christ's body at the altar, do not neglect him in the poor. A form widely quoted: "You have tasted the Blood of the Lord, yet you do not recognise your brother." The exact English varies by translation.
- Augustine's totus Christus: Christ acts and suffers in his members [VERIFIED].

Item 5c. Silouan and Sophrony [VERIFIED as presented by Sophrony]. Christ took up the "total Adam". Each person is to be seen as an eternal brother. Love of enemies follows from shared life in Adam. The saying "our brother is our life" [PLAUSIBLE, check wording].

Verdict. This is the strongest ethical match. Christianity grounds it in Christ in the neighbour and one body, not in "the neighbour is literally you". Silouan comes closest.

6. A SECOND BIRTH

Item 6a. Scripture [VERIFIED].
- John 3:3-7. "anōthen" means both "again" and "from above". The ambiguity is intended. Nicodemus hears only "again". Jesus points to "water and Spirit" (3:5).
- Titus 3:5 "washing of rebirth" uses palingenesia, found in the New Testament only here and Matt 19:28 (eschatological "renewal"). 1 Pet 1:3, 1:23 use anagennaō [PLAUSIBLE]. The Greek palingenesia was also used by the Stoics for cosmic rebirth [PLAUSIBLE].
- Plain reading: baptismal and conversion rebirth into a new life in Christ, one event that starts a process.

Item 6b. Eckhart [VERIFIED].
- German Sermon 101, on Wis 18:14 "Dum medium silentium", is part of the God-birth cycle (Sermons 101 to 104). Walshe translation, revised by McGinn, in The Complete Mystical Works (2009). The eternal birth the Father bears in eternity is born in time in human nature. God the Father gives birth to his Son in the ground of the soul. It happens in silence and in letting go of images.
- The birth of the Word in the believer has patristic roots, Origen and Augustine among them, via Hugo Rahner's study [PLAUSIBLE, unchecked].

Item 6c. Hermetic, Gnostic, Eastern [VERIFIED/PLAUSIBLE].
- Corpus Hermeticum XIII (the secret sermon on the mountain). Hermes and Tat. A method of rebirth into an immortal knower, a new noetic body of ten powers, union with the All. Graeco-Egyptian, first centuries CE. William Grese wrote "Corpus Hermeticum XIII and Early Christian Literature" [VERIFIED as a title].
- Gregory of Nyssa, Life of Moses II: we are "in a way our own parents", giving birth to ourselves by choice [PLAUSIBLE; check translation]. Maximus on three births (natural, baptismal, resurrection) [PLAUSIBLE, not verified here; do not print].

Verdict. The Christian "second birth" is baptism and a new life, not a later stage after the soul's ladder ends. Eckhart's birth of God in the soul is the bridge. Do not equate it with Gennady's "birth" stage.

7. LETTING GO AND SURRENDER

Item 7a. Scripture [PLAUSIBLE except where marked].
- Phil 2:5-8 [VERIFIED]. "ekenōsen", "he emptied himself". Commentators debate what he emptied. The "less controversial" reading is self-giving obedience. Bourgeault makes kenosis a general principle of letting go, which is a modern spiritual reading.
- Luke 22:42 ("not my will, but yours"). Maximus's two-wills teaching and the Third Council of Constantinople (680 to 681) follow from it [PLAUSIBLE].
- Matt 16:25 ("whoever loses his life for my sake will find it"). The original context is discipleship and martyrdom.
- Matt 6:25-34 and Luke 10:41. Greek merimnaō ("be anxious"). Its link to merizō ("divide") is a popular etymology, so hedge. The remedy is "seek first his kingdom".

Item 7b. Mystics [VERIFIED].
- Eckhart. His German coinages abegescheidenheit (detachment) and gelazenheit (letting go); a source calls them neologisms, so hedge. "The poor in spirit are those who want nothing, know nothing, have nothing." The Sermon 52 attribution is PLAUSIBLE. John Paul II reportedly said in 1985: "Did not Eckhart teach his disciples: 'All that God asks you most pressingly is to go out of yourself and let God be God in you'?" The source is secondary.
- John of the Cross. The Ascent of Mount Carmel and Dark Night describe an active and a passive purification of desire and attachment. A summary gave 1578 to 1579 for the Ascent, which I doubt, so give no date. "Nada, nada, nada" and "to come to be all, desire to be nothing" [PLAUSIBLE].
- Cloud of Unknowing. An anonymous Middle English text, later fourteenth century. Thoughts go under a "cloud of forgetting", and God is pierced by a "dart of longing love". The link to modern centering prayer is PLAUSIBLE only.

Item 7c. Stillness and the desert.
- Hesychasm [VERIFIED]. Palamas's Triads (1338 to 1341) defend the Jesus Prayer joined to the breath. A synod in 1351 vindicated Palamite teaching.
- Evagrius [VERIFIED]. A fourth century desert monk who adapted Stoic apatheia to Christian prayer and wrote of eight logismoi, the thoughts and passions. Evagrius himself was condemned in the 553 anathemas, though his ascetic teaching survives. Apatheia in Evagrius leads to love, "apatheia's offspring is agape" [PLAUSIBLE, Praktikos]. It is not numbness.
- Quakers [PLAUSIBLE, unverified]. Silent waiting and "centering down", and George Fox's "that of God in every one". Check before use.
- Augustine, Confessions 8 and Maximus on self love (philautia) as root of the passions [PLAUSIBLE] link a divided, self centred will to inner torment.

Verdict. This is the best supported of the seven and is openly mainstream across all branches, including Evagrius's mechanism-style analysis of thoughts and passions. It is surrender to a personal God by grace, not a self technique.

LAYERS: OFFICIAL OR POPULAR VERSUS INNER

- Orthodoxy [VERIFIED]. Lossky's The Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church (1944) argues the Eastern tradition has never made a sharp distinction between mysticism and theology, and that this integration is stronger than in Catholic and Reformed traditions. Hesychasm is dogmatically endorsed (1351). Theosis is central [PLAUSIBLE for "central", standard].
- Catholicism [VERIFIED]. CCC 460 teaches partaking of the divine nature, quoting Athanasius, Irenaeus and Aquinas. The Western word is "divinization", and it is rarely preached [PLAUSIBLE].
- Protestantism [VERIFIED as contested]. The Finnish school (Mannermaa) reads Luther's "Christ present in faith" as close to theosis, arising from Lutheran and Russian Orthodox dialogue in the 1970s. Critics within Lutheran scholarship dispute it [PLAUSIBLE]. Wesley's Christian perfection [PLAUSIBLE].
- Eckhart's treatment [VERIFIED]. Inquiry started 1326 under Archbishop Henry II of Virneburg of Cologne. In February 1327 he read a public protestation of innocence. He appealed to the pope, and the process moved to Avignon. John XXII's bull In agro dominico (27 March 1329, after his death) condemned 28 propositions: 17 heretical "as far as the words sound" and 11 "very rash" and suspect. He was not condemned by name. The Dominicans asked in 1980 for rehabilitation. The reply, relayed by Timothy Radcliffe, was that there is no need because he was never condemned by name. Secondary sources date that reply 1992 and 2010 inconsistently, so print no date.
- Boundary policing [VERIFIED]. Eriugena's Periphyseon was condemned in 1225. Marguerite Porete was burned in Paris on 1 June 1310. The Council of Vienne (1311 to 1312) condemned the "Free Spirit" and the Beguines (Ad nostrum). The Church treated the strongest language of union as dangerous, which is the specialist's point.
- Do churchgoers hear this? I found no survey. Treat as an observation, not a fact. Rohr's book subtitle claims a "forgotten reality". Turner argues medieval mysticism was ordinary, not elite (below).

SCHOLARLY OBJECTIONS

1. Creator and creature. Creation from nothing, a personal Trinity, and Chalcedon (451: two natures "without confusion, without change, without division, without separation") model union without fusion [PLAUSIBLE, standard]. John of the Cross says the soul does not take on God's substance. Palamas keeps creatures out of God's essence. Pantheism is rejected.
2. Zaehner [VERIFIED]. Mysticism Sacred and Profane (1957) distinguishes nature, monistic and theistic mysticism and says Christian union with a personal God is different from monistic union. A Catholic writing partly against Huxley, so critics call it apologetic [PLAUSIBLE].
3. McGinn [VERIFIED]. His Presence of God (volume 1, 1991) stresses that union was rarely understood as "mingling". He says mysticism is part of concrete historical religions and is not the inner common denominator of all religions. This is a direct blow to perennialism, from his strongest scholarly ally on Eckhart. His book The Mystical Thought of Meister Eckhart (2001) is the standard reading.
4. Turner [VERIFIED]. The Darkness of God argues medieval mysticism rejected "mystical experience" as the goal, and that "experientialism" is modern. Reading Eckhart as a report of an Advaita style state is anachronistic. Eckhart is a theologian preaching on scripture [PLAUSIBLE as application].
5. Kevin Hart. I could not verify any Hart claim on this question. He is a Duke Divinity scholar who writes on Christian contemplation. The titles "Dark Gnosis" and "Eckhart and Advaita" did not surface as his. Do not cite him unchecked.
6. Otto [VERIFIED]. Mysticism East and West (1926; English 1932) says Eckhart, like Shankara, aims at identity, not equality, with God. He also finds Eckhart distinct in dynamism, conscience, love and care for the world. Evidence for both sides.
7. His clause 2 is the least Christian. In Christianity separation is the creatures' fall, not God's means of self knowing. The nearest ancestors are Eriugena (condemned), parts of Eckhart, and German idealism (Hegel, Schelling, Boehme) [PLAUSIBLE].

BEST PUBLISHED SUPPORT, WITH WHAT EACH ACTUALLY CLAIMS

- McGinn: deep presence of union language in Christian mystics. Explicitly NOT a common core. [VERIFIED]
- Huston Smith, Forgotten Truth: a "primordial tradition", with esoteric traditions stressing realisation and exoteric traditions stressing belief [VERIFIED]. Claims common vision, so identity. Perennialist, and disputed by McGinn and Turner.
- Bede Griffiths: Benedictine at Shantivanam (Saccidananda Ashram) from 1968. Return to the Centre says the centre is "the place of meeting where the different religious traditions of the world have their source" [VERIFIED]. Resonance and meeting, grounded in Christ for the Christian.
- Thomas Merton, Zen and the Birds of Appetite: dialogue with D. T. Suzuki. He locates the meeting at the level of contemplative experience, not doctrine, and stays Christian [VERIFIED]. Resonance with named differences.
- Richard Rohr, The Universal Christ: perennial tradition, "Christ in all things" [VERIFIED]. Evangelical critics call it pantheism or panentheism. A popular writer, not a specialist.
- Cynthia Bourgeault, The Wisdom Jesus: reads Jesus as teaching nonduality and kenosis [VERIFIED]. Claims identity at the core of Jesus's teaching. Popular and contested by NT scholars [PLAUSIBLE].
- Rowan Williams: I verified nothing. Do not cite.
