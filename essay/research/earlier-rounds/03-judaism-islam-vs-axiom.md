BOTTOM LINE

1. Judaism: you are right more than the first fact-check allowed. Lurianic Kabbalah and Habad Hasidism contain all three clauses, and the Lurianic myth in particular has a whole that breaks into individual sparks and is then repaired. Three limits hold up under scrutiny. The unity is mostly about the Jewish soul. Separation is real and purposeful in the texts, not an illusion. Unio mystica is disputed even among specialists.
2. Islam: right for the Sufi and Ibn Arabi layer, and the Quran has real raw material (7:172, 2:115, 57:3, 28:88, 2:156). But the mainstream kalam position is a strict Creator and creation distinction, and the "inner layer" has been contested since Ibn Taymiyya. The Quran, at 23:99 to 100, flatly refuses a return to this world, which cuts against lifetimes.
3. No specialist I found supports "the same idea in different words" without qualification. The qualifiers are the creator and creature distinction (Scholem, Junayd, Ibn Arabi's "He/not He"), constructivism (Katz, PLAUSIBLE), and that each text sits inside a law and covenant tradition.

======== JUDAISM ========

1. THE ONE WHOLE
- Chabad acosmism. VERIFIED [Encyclopedia Judaica "Chabad"; MyJewishLearning "Theology of Chabad"]. Shneur Zalman of Liadi (1745 to 1813), Tanya (Likkutei Amarim, 1796). Scholarly label: acosmism. Shneur Zalman's line, as reported (paraphrase, not checked against the Hebrew): as God was alone and one before the six days of creation, so He is now, because all things are as nothing in relation to His being. "Ein od milvado" is among the most quoted phrases in Chabad, and the contemplative exercise is that no place is devoid of Him. The Gate of Unity and Faith (Shaar HaYichud VeHaEmunah) is Book 2 of the Tanya [outorah.org, Sefaria]. HEDGE: Chabad holds a two perspectives view. From God's point of view there is no world, and from ours the world is real [MyJewishLearning "Panentheism and Judaism"]. "Acosmism" is the scholars' label, and Chabad does not say the world is simply unreal. Deuteronomy 4:35 as the verse: PLAUSIBLE (the search did not confirm the verse number; "ein od" also appears at 4:39).
- Zohar: Torah, the Holy One and Israel are one. VERIFIED as cited "Zohar III 73a" [Chabad.org and Kabbalaonline, "Rashbi Basic Kabbalah Tenets"; JTSA]. HEDGE: the line is about the Torah, God and the Jewish people, not about all beings. The Tikkunei Zohar line "no place is devoid of Him" (Tikkun 57): NOT confirmed here, PLAUSIBLE.
- Cordovero and the Baal Shem Tov. VERIFIED: Idel ascribes panentheism (God in all, all in God, but not all is God) to Cordovero (1522 to 1570) [search summaries naming Idel; exact book citation not confirmed]. The Baal Shem Tov (1700 to 1760): whoever separates God from things in his thoughts has a mistaken idea of God [MyJewishLearning "Hasidic Mysticism" summary; paraphrase]. HEDGE: the claim that the Baal Shem Tov's immanence was "unknown before in Kabbalah" is contestable. The Jewish Review of Books essay "No Empty Place" says pantheist variants were openly held by many kabbalists and by Ibn Gabirol and Ibn Ezra, and that the Mitnagdim did not object to Hasidic panentheism as such. MyJewishLearning says the opposite, that it was a main charge. Specialists disagree, so avoid "the main objection of the opponents".
- Arizal and Adam's soul. VERIFIED [Chabad.org and Kabbalaonline "Root and Spark Dynamics", "Root Understanding"; New World Encyclopedia]: one soul, Adam, includes all; it divides into the patriarchs, the 12 tribes, 70 souls, then 600,000 sparks. HEDGE: the 600,000 are the souls of Israel (root: the Torah), and the sources are outreach sites, not academic.

2. SEPARATION AS VEIL, FORGETTING, ILLUSION
- Tzimtzum, shevirah, tikkun. VERIFIED [Scholem's three symbols of the Lurianic myth; Sefaria; Encyclopedia.com "Luria"]: God's self limitation, the breaking of the vessels, the repair; sparks imprisoned in the material world; the myth explains exile. HEDGE: in the myth the separation is a real catastrophe or a purposeful descent, not an illusion or a forgetting.
- Hester panim. VERIFIED Deuteronomy 31:17 (God "will hide My face"). The Baal Shem Tov, via R. Yaakov Yosef of Polnoye (d. 1783), is reported to teach that there is no real hiding, only an apparent one, a test of how we relate; and "anokhi haster astir" is read as a double concealment, so that we stop perceiving God at all [Yeshivat Maharat PDF; Kabbalaonline "Hide and Go Seek"; Sefaria sheets]. HEDGE: secondhand; no primary source was checked.
- Yesh and ayin. VERIFIED [Wikipedia "Ayin and Yesh"; Chabad.org]: in Chabad ayin means "incomprehensible", not non-being. The Maggid Dov Ber of Mezritch (c. 1704 to 1772) is the first systematic exponent of the Baal Shem Tov's thought [satyori.com bio; thin source].
- Verdict: weak to moderate. Hasidism says the world's independence is only apparent. Lurianic myth says separation is real and must be repaired. A "forgetting" idea per se is not found.

3. THE RETURN
- Zechariah 14:9, "the Lord shall be one and His name one", recited at the end of the Aleinu. VERIFIED [torah.org; outorah.org; dailyzohar.com]. HEDGE: the plain sense is God's universal recognised rule. The Zohar reads it as the unification of two aspects of God (Zeir Anpin and Malkhut, "one in one"), not as the merging of all beings.
- Tikkun and the return of sparks. VERIFIED [Encyclopedia.com "Luria"; MyJewishLearning "The Ari"]: tikkun as the "mending" or "restitution of the life of God", done through contemplative devotion and mitzvot that raise the sparks. HEDGE: a necessary human act, not a waking up.
- Devekut. VERIFIED [Encyclopedia Judaica "Devekut" via Jewish Virtual Library and Encyclopedia.com]: in Hasidism it is both the supreme achievement and the starting point, a constant state even during daily business. The scholarly dispute is VERIFIED [search summaries naming Scholem and Idel]. Scholem held that devekut is adhesion, not union, and that the mystic keeps a sense of distance. Idel, working from manuscripts, argued that many texts, above all Abraham Abulafia's ecstatic Kabbalah, describe unio mystica. Teshuvah as "return": PLAUSIBLE (not searched).

4. SOMETHING CONTINUES, DEVELOPMENT BEYOND ONE LIFE
- Gilgul. VERIFIED [Wikipedia "Gilgul"; MyJewishLearning; Jewish Encyclopedia "Transmigration of Souls"]. Early statement in the Sefer HaBahir (13th century); the Zohar; Luria's explanations and Hayyim Vital's Shaar HaGilgulim, with souls returning to mend specific flaws. Rejected, with the reasons stated: Saadia Gaon (Emunot ve-Deot), David Kimhi, Hasdai Crescas, Jedaiah Bedersi, Joseph Albo, Abraham ibn Daud, Leon of Modena. Accepted: Nahmanides and Bahya ben Asher. Maimonides never discusses it, and his psychology (Yesodei HaTorah ch. 4) is read as implying rejection [mesora.org, a partisan source]. One source says Crescas and Albo grumbled but did not reject it because it was an accepted tradition. HEDGE: the Jewish Encyclopedia (1906) says it was "foreign to Judaism" until about the 8th century and spread through Karaites, "under Mohammedan mystic influence". That is dated language and a dated thesis. Hasidic acceptance: PLAUSIBLE, not confirmed. Rashba's view: not found.
- Gehinnom, twelve months. VERIFIED [Mishnah Eduyot 2:10, via outorah.org]: Akiva says 12 months (Isaiah 66:23), Yohanan ben Nuri says from Passover to Shavuot. HEDGE: the Mishnah says "judgment of the wicked". The reading as purification is later tradition.
- Ibbur. VERIFIED [Wikipedia "Ibbur"; MyJewishLearning]: temporary joining of a righteous soul to a living person; distinct from gilgul and from the dybbuk.
- Neshamah as the continuing soul: PLAUSIBLE (not searched).

5. WHAT YOU DO TO ANOTHER YOU DO TO YOURSELF; ETHICS FROM UNITY
- Leviticus 19:18. VERIFIED [JTSA; Sefaria; Yeshivat Har Etzion]. The Akiva and Ben Azzai exchange is in Sifra Kedoshim and Jerusalem Talmud Nedarim 9:4. Akiva calls "love your fellow as yourself" the great principle; Ben Azzai says "This is the book of the generations of Adam" is greater. Genesis Rabbah 24:7 has a related version, and there R. Tanhuma says that if you shame your neighbour, know whom you shame, because "in the likeness of God He made him". WRONG in the brief: neither text reads "as yourself" as "he is yourself". The Talmudic grounding is the divine image. HEDGE: the two summaries I got disagreed on which sage holds which position in Genesis Rabbah, so name them only for Sifra and the Jerusalem Talmud.
- Tanya ch. 32. VERIFIED [Chabad.org]: love of a fellow Jew rests on all Jewish souls sharing one root in God, and only the bodies differ. This is your closest "he is yourself". HEDGE (strong): Tanya ch. 1 holds that the souls of the nations come from the unclean kelipot, and that contains no good [etzion.org.il and critic sites quoting it; the Chabad outlook is nuanced, and Schneerson showed concern for non-Jews]. The unity ethic in the Tanya's plain text is Jewish only.
- Shevuot 39a. VERIFIED [MyJewishLearning; Yeshivat Har Etzion]: "all Israel are guarantors for one another", derived from Leviticus 26:37, "each stumbles over his brother" read as "because of his brother's sin". Hillel (Shabbat 31a): "what is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow". Avot 4:2 VERIFIED [Ben Azzai]: a mitzvah draws another, a sin draws another, the reward of a mitzvah is a mitzvah. Middah keneged middah VERIFIED [Mishnah Sotah 1:7 and Sotah 8b; Sanhedrin 90a]. HEDGE: Avot 4:2 and measure for measure are about moral momentum and divine justice, not the identity of selves.
- Sanhedrin 4:5 "whoever destroys one life destroys a world". VERIFIED textual variant [Accordance forum, wist.info, other summaries]: many editions, and some manuscripts, add "of Israel"; the Jerusalem Talmud omits it. Quran 5:32 parallels it (Aaron Koller, "Three Polemical Qur'anic Citations of the Mishnah", JSQ 2025; Rutgers Bildner Center abstract). HEDGE: the Kaufmann manuscript reading was not confirmed here (PLAUSIBLE that the best manuscripts omit it). Say "many texts add 'of Israel'".

6. A SECOND BIRTH OR NEW STAGE
- "A convert is like a newborn child." VERIFIED [Yevamot 48b; also in the Sanhedrin 58 discussions, per dafyomi.co.il and yutorah]: ger she-nitgayer ke-katan she-nolad dami. Fair judgement: a real saying, but legal. It cuts a convert's prior kinship and liability. It is not a teaching about a stage after awakening.
- Ibbur is real but is soul joining, not a new stage. I found no real rabbinic or Kabbalistic parallel to "birth after plus four". Say so plainly. A loose Hasidic or Lurianic analogue is the "ascent after descent" idea (PLAUSIBLE, not checked).

7. LETTING GO, SURRENDER AS METHOD
- Bittul. VERIFIED [Chabad.org]: bittul is a fundamental tenet of Chabad, surrendering ego; bittul bimetziut is complete nullification where the sensation of self ceases. This is your best match for "tension tied to a separate self": yesh (self existence) is what bittul loosens.
- Hishtavut. VERIFIED [Chabad.org "Equanimity"; Sefaria Tzava'at HaRivash; Kabbalaonline]: the Baal Shem Tov read "shiviti" (Psalm 16:8) from shaveh, "equal": praise and insult feel the same. A very good match for "released, not relaxed".
- HEDGE: bittul is nullification before God's will, and it sits inside mitzvah observance. Hasidism does not teach dropping action.

======== ISLAM ========

1. THE ONE WHOLE
- Quran texts. VERIFIED [quran.com and quran.is translations; tafsirs on quranworld.net and quranpedia]. 2:115: "to God belong the east and the west, so wherever you turn you are facing God" (Saheeh Intl; older translations say "there is the Face of God"). 50:16: nearer than the jugular vein. 57:3: the First and the Last, az-Zahir and al-Batin. 57:4: "He is with you wherever you are". 28:88: "everything will be destroyed except His Face". HEDGE: the classical exegetes (Ibn Kathir, Qurtubi, and others) read 50:16 as nearness of knowledge and power, 57:3 as priority and knowledge, and 28:88 as an end-time statement. The Sufi present tense reading ("everything is perishing now") is PLAUSIBLE but not confirmed here. The traditional occasion for 2:115 is the direction of prayer (PLAUSIBLE).
- Ibn Arabi. VERIFIED [Chittick, Sufi Path of Knowledge (SUNY 1989) and Self-Disclosure of God (SUNY 1998); Wikipedia "Sufi metaphysics"]: wujud belongs to God alone, and things "have never smelled a whiff of wujud" (tanzih); yet all things are God's self disclosure (tajalli, tashbih). So everything is "He/not He". Chittick and other specialists note that Ibn Arabi himself never used the phrase wahdat al-wujud, and that Qunawi and later followers made wujud central [academic summaries on Qunawi]. One source says Ibn Taymiyya was the first to use the phrase, as an attack (single source; hedge). Ibn Arabi died in 1240.
- Sufi "la mawjud illa Allah". VERIFIED as a Sufi formula ("nothing exists but God") and contested: a 1903 Malabar controversy saw one scholar declare reciters infidels. Ghazali's Mishkat al-Anwar: those at the summit see that nothing exists but God [ghazali.org; Wensinck 1941 on the Mishkat]. Ghazali is a major classical Sunni authority, so this one counts as an insider voice.
- Hallaj. VERIFIED: executed in Baghdad in 922 for heresy; "Ana al-Haqq" ("I am the Truth/Real") is the traditional attribution. HEDGE: Carl Ernst (Words of Ecstasy in Sufism, SUNY 1985) is reported to call parts of the Hallaj legend unfounded, and whether he said the words is not secure [lesser source summaries]. Massignon: "a death for love". Ghazali: ecstasy, not blasphemy. Say "attributed to".

2. SEPARATION AS VEIL, FORGETTING, ILLUSION
- Quran 7:172 (alast). VERIFIED [Wikishia; Encyclopaedia Iranica "Dhikr"]: God asks the descendants of Adam "Am I not your Lord?"; humans forget; Sufis take it as the first dhikr and the origin to which they wish to return. This is your best Islamic match for "forget, then return". HEDGE: mainstream exegesis debates whether it is a real pre-existence of souls or a metaphor for innate disposition (fitra), PLAUSIBLE.
- Hijab, ghafla, nafs: Quran 42:51 (God speaks "from behind a veil"), 50:22 ("you were heedless... We removed your covering"), the nafs al-ammara (12:53), lawwama (75:2) and mutma'inna (89:27). All PLAUSIBLE, not searched.
- "Hidden treasure". VERIFIED NOT AUTHENTIC [islamqa.org and hadithanswers.com]: Ibn Taymiyya says it is not the Prophet's word and has no chain; al-Zarkashi, al-Sakhawi and Ibn Hajar agree; Ibn Arabi himself called it sound by unveiling but not established by transmission. Do not call it a hadith. Similarly "whoever knows himself knows his Lord" is not established (Nawawi), and one transmitter tradition ascribes it to Yahya b. Mu'adh al-Razi; Ibn Taymiyya and Suyuti accepted the meaning as implied by the Quran [Wikishia and other summaries].

3. THE RETURN
- Quran 2:156 ("we belong to God and to Him we return"), 28:88 ("to Him you will be returned"), 3:83 ("unto Him they will be returned"), 2:28. VERIFIED [quran.is, alquran.cloud]. HEDGE: the standard exegesis reads the return as resurrection and judgment (tafsir on 2:28).
- Fana and baqa. VERIFIED [Britannica; Wikipedia "Fana (Sufism)"]: al-Hujwiri (Kashf al-Mahjub) defines fana as the passing away of the remembrance of anything other than God; Junayd stressed subsistence after annihilation, the sober return. HEDGE: readings of fana as a merging of the human with God are condemned as heretical by many scholars.
- Hadith of the nawafil, Bukhari 6502. VERIFIED [thehouseofislam.com; Wikishia]: God says that when a servant draws near by voluntary works, "I become his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand, his leg". Ibn Arabi cites it for the unity of being; Sayyid Haydar Amuli for annihilation in God. HEDGE: the orthodox reading, that God guides and helps, is PLAUSIBLE but unverified here.
- Rumi, reed flute (Nicholson 1926). VERIFIED: "Every one who is left far from his source wishes back the time when he was united with it."

4. SOMETHING CONTINUES; DEVELOPMENT BEYOND ONE LIFE
- Ruh. VERIFIED 15:29 and 32:9 ("breathed into him of My spirit"): most commentators read it figuratively (honouring Adam, the gift of life), some literally. Ibn Arabi: a universal Spirit, many partial spirits [al-Qantara article]. Quran 17:85: PLAUSIBLE.
- Barzakh, 23:99 to 100. VERIFIED [quran.com; Wikishia "Realm of Barzakh"]: the dying person asks to be sent back and is refused, "No! It is only a word he says. Behind them is a barzakh until the day they are resurrected". HEDGE and judgement: the plain sense is against a return to this world, so barzakh supports an afterlife continuation but works against gilgul.
- Mulla Sadra. VERIFIED [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy "Mulla Sadra", Sajjad Rizvi; Journal of Comparative Philosophy, kalami.nashriyat.ir and magiran.com summaries]: two principles, substantial motion (al-harakah al-jawhariyyah) and gradation of being (tashkik al-wujud). The soul is corporeal in origin and spiritual in subsistence, and its perfecting motion continues after death. He REJECTED metempsychosis with five arguments, but accepted a "spiritual transmigration", the embodiment of deeds in the afterlife realm. Interpreters disagree on whether his later view is graded unity or personal unity of being [Persian journal articles]. So Sadra supports "development continues after this life" and not "lifetimes returning".
- Ibn Sina. VERIFIED [J. Islamic Sciences, "Ibn Sina and Abu al-Barakat al-Baghdadi on the Origination of the Soul and the Invalidation of its Transmigration"]: the soul is immaterial and immortal, originated with the body, and he refuted tanasukh.
- Tanasukh. VERIFIED [Wikipedia; NPR 2025 report on the Druze]: not accepted by mainstream Sunni and Shia Islam. Accepted by the Druze (called taqammus; they reject animal rebirth), the Nusayri (Alawite; humans and sometimes animals), and early Sabaʾi and Jarudi groups, whom the mainstream considers heterodox. A claim that "some Sufis accept it" appeared only on a Theosophical site, so I do not rely on it.
- Rumi, "I died as mineral and became a plant". VERIFIED as Mathnawi III, around lines 3900 ff in Nicholson's numbering (one source says 3501 to 3506; check). Judgement: PLAUSIBLE that it is an ascent through kinds of being, not rebirth in repeated lives.

5. WHAT YOU DO TO ANOTHER YOU DO TO YOURSELF; ETHICS FROM UNITY
- Quran 17:7. VERIFIED text: "If you do good, you do good for yourselves, and if you do evil, [you do it] to yourselves." Also 99:7 to 8 (an atom's weight; Ibn Abbas: the head of a red ant), 41:46 and 45:15. HEDGE: traditional meaning is that consequences return to the doer, not that the other is you.
- Quran 5:32. VERIFIED as parallel to Sanhedrin 4:5 (Koller 2025). One whole humanity wording. HEDGE: the verse is framed as a decree upon the Children of Israel (PLAUSIBLE, not checked here) and uses a legal source. Do not say "the Quran teaches that all humanity is one".
- Hadith, believers as one body. VERIFIED [Bukhari 6011, Muslim 2586, Nu'man ibn Bashir; islamway.net; hadeethenc.com]: "if one limb complains, the rest of the body responds with sleeplessness and fever" (one translation has "drowsiness"). The scope is believers, and the point is mercy and compassion.
- Love for your brother what you love for yourself. VERIFIED [Bukhari 13, Muslim 45, Nawawi's Forty Hadith no. 13]: Muslim has a variant "or his neighbour". Nawawi's commentary generalises "brother" to include non Muslims [as reported by sunnah.com commentary summary]. Candidate to check: the hadith qudsi "I was sick and you did not visit Me" (Muslim 2569), PLAUSIBLE, not verified.

- Fana of the will, nawafil hadith, Junayd: see 3.
- Tawakkul: PLAUSIBLE, not searched. Tension tied to the self: no source found. ======== LAYERS: public versus inner (a) ========

JUDAISM
- Maimonides (1138 to 1204): God can be described only by negation, with no positive attributes and no composition [Guide of the Perplexed; Britannica and others]. Cordovero agreed that no positive attribute applies but said the sefirot fill the gap between the Ein Sof and the world, making God both transcendent and immanent [Encyclopedia/universalis summaries]. Rashba (1235 to 1310) defended Maimonides yet opposed philosophic rationalism, and was a kabbalist [Wikipedia]. Maimonides' own Guide has a negative theology close to mystical silence [Innsbruck project summary; one source].
- Hasidism and its opponents. VERIFIED: the Gaon of Vilna led the Mitnagdim; the Vilna ban (1772 per JRB; a 1777 excommunication per MyJewishLearning and Wikipedia) condemned among other things bizarre prayer and disrespect to scholars. Disagreement whether the theology (panentheism, acosmism) was a main charge, as above. Nefesh HaChaim's tzimtzum debate is described in a Ktav book as no fundamental philosophical dispute (partisan). I could not verify the strongest version of the Mitnagdic objection to acosmism, that it voids the point of Torah study and law (PLAUSIBLE).
- How central. Kabbalah and Hasidism are central to Haredi Hasidic life and to the Zohar liturgy, and marginal to much of the Lithuanian, Modern Orthodox and non Orthodox world, where Maimonidean rationalism has the authority; Kabbalah study was traditionally restricted (Rema, Yoreh De'ah 246:4, PLAUSIBLE). Say "an inner layer with a large following", not "the Jewish view".

ISLAM
- VERIFIED. Ibn Taymiyya criticised Ibn Arabi and the wahdat al-wujud ontology [Edinburgh PhD thesis, "Ibn Taymiyya's attitude towards Sufism and his critique of Ibn al-'Arabi's mystical philosophy"]. Taftazani (Sharh al-Maqasid) and al-Biqa'i (Tanbih al-Ghabi ila Takfir Ibn 'Arabi) attacked him; Suyuti (Tanbih al-Ghabi bi-Tabri'at Ibn 'Arabi) and al-Sha'rani defended him [Wikipedia; Ottoman era sources]. The predominant view among scholars, per fatwa sites, has been to respect Ibn Arabi but to warn that his books are for the trained. Salafi and Wahhabi currents reject wahdat al-wujud as pantheism and incompatible with pure tawhid [Wikipedia].
- PLAUSIBLE: Sufi orders were a mainstream part of pre modern Muslim societies, and the modern reformist, Salafi and Wahhabi movements made Sufism contested. Not confirmed here. State it as "widespread historically, contested today".

======== OBJECTIONS (b) ========

1. The creator and creature line (strongest). Scholem: devekut is adhesion, not union, and distance is kept (VERIFIED). Junayd and Hujwiri on fana (VERIFIED). Ibn Arabi himself says "He/not He", keeping both tanzih and tashbih (VERIFIED). Chabad's acosmism is only God's point of view (VERIFIED). Counter: Idel finds unio mystica in Abulafia (VERIFIED), so a careful statement is "some mystics, not the traditions". Advaita says the self is Brahman, which no Abrahamic school says without qualification (PLAUSIBLE).
2. Separation has a different status. Advaita: ignorance (avidya). Judaism: a purposeful descent and a real catastrophe to repair; Islam: creatureliness (PLAUSIBLE). The "veil" language has Quranic footing, but the Quran does not say the separation is illusory.
3. Reincarnation. Islam's mainstream (23:99 to 100, Ibn Sina, Sadra) rejects it (VERIFIED). Judaism is split (VERIFIED). So the "lifetimes" clause has the weakest support in both.
4. Ethical unity is mostly intra community (Tanya ch. 1 and 32; Shevuot 39a; Sanhedrin 4:5 "of Israel"; the one body hadith for believers; all VERIFIED). Counters: Nawawi's reading, Ben Azzai's Adam verse, 5:32.
5. Law and covenant frame. Both traditions locate even mystical practice inside halakha and sharia (PLAUSIBLE; confirmed only indirectly by the bittul and nawafil texts).
6. Method objection: Steven Katz ("Language, Epistemology and Mysticism", 1978) argues mystical experiences are shaped by tradition, so "the same experience in different words" is unproven. PLAUSIBLE, not searched. The traditionalist school (Schuon, Nasr) holds the opposite and is partisan, PLAUSIBLE.

======== BEST PUBLISHED STATEMENTS (c) ========

- Idel, Kabbalah: New Perspectives (Yale, 1990) VERIFIED: stresses a comparative, phenomenological approach, with ecstatic (anthropocentric) and theosophical (theocentric) Kabbalah. Hasidism: Between Ecstasy and Magic (SUNY 1995) VERIFIED. He supports "union exists" (against Scholem) and qualifies "same": he stresses plurality of models.
- Scholem, Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism (1941, lectures 1938) VERIFIED. Supports the Lurianic myth as the central story; qualifies unity.
- Elior, The Paradoxical Ascent to God: The Kabbalistic Theosophy of Habad Hasidism (SUNY, 1993) VERIFIED as the standard study of Habad's doctrine of divinity. Check her exact wording before citing for acosmism.

======== CORRECTIONS TO THE BRIEF ========
- "As yourself read as he is yourself": not found in the Talmudic sources; the sources ground it in the divine image (Tanhuma) and, in Chabad, in a shared soul root (Tanya 32).
- "Sanhedrin 4:5": add the "of Israel" variant.
- "Baal Shem Tov invented divine immanence": overstated.
- "Zohar: Torah, God and Israel": it is about Israel, not all beings.
- "Hidden treasure" and "die before you die": not authentic hadith.
- "Hallaj said ana al-Haqq": traditional attribution, uncertain.
- "Barzakh": supports an afterlife, against rebirth.
