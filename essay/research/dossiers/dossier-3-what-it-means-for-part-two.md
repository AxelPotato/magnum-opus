## 1. Status, tiers and how to read this

What this is. One integrated document that answers one question: what does the research on the perennial philosophy movement and on Theosophy mean for the text of Part two? Its main purpose is to restate Alex's argument in the vocabulary of those two movements, because he expects that lens to make his meaning clearer. It also says where the draft is wrong or loose, and what to add.

Alex's correction, relayed on 2026-10-07: the perennial philosophy movement is much bigger than Huxley, and Huxley is not even the best of them. This dossier treats Huxley as one anthologist among many. Section 3 maps the shelf by job. The files support the facts that bear on the point (he was not first, not the strictest, not the largest, not the author of the argument, not the best on sources, not the best readable map) and they say plainly that no file can rank the books, so "best" is Alex's judgement. Wherever the essay needs a word for Huxley's place, this dossier suggests "best known", and it marks "not the best" as Alex's opinion.

Evidence status. No item in these files is [V]. In every verification pass the search tool was out of budget and every fetch was blocked, so nothing here was confirmed against a page or a book in this session. The strongest tier that exists is [C]. Where this dossier says "verified" it can only mean "the best supported pointer in the set". Everything below has to be opened at its source before it is printed.

Tier tags, as the files use them:

- [V] shown by a search result in the pass that reports it. None exists in this set.
- [C] carried. A first-pass colleague reported a search result in a session where search still worked, and nobody re-saw it. First-pass [V] tags in the earliest files (P01 to P03) are carried here as [C], as S1 to S5 carry them. [C-repo] marks the few items the repo notes record from an earlier session with working search.
- [S] a result supports the gist, the wording is from memory. A few survive in the earliest files, for example the locator of Coomaraswamy's Shankara line.
- [M] memory held with confidence, no search.
- [U] memory held with doubt. Not for the essay until checked. This dossier flags such items and drops the ones that carry no use.

A claim built from several parts takes its weakest tier. Two memories that agree are not evidence, so no tier was raised because a second file agrees.

Quotation rule. The task was to keep exact quotations only where a file marks them [V]. None does. So this dossier prints no verbatim quotation as a verified quotation. A short phrase in quotation marks is a title, a technical term or a find-handle, meaning a few words someone can use to locate the passage. It is never cleared for printing. Sanskrit, Pali, Greek, Latin, Hebrew and Arabic terms are the safer handles.

Sample sentences. Where this dossier drafts a sentence for the essay, it is mine, written to be put into Alex's voice, and it follows the essay's rules: no dashes, no "not X but Y", no bold, no rhetorical triads. The tiers sit on the line below, not inside the sentence. Nothing in a sample is a quotation.

What the files do not contain. No sentence by Schuon, Huston Smith or Whitall Perry, only paraphrase (S1). Any such sentence has to be copied from the book, with edition and page. S2 and G1-1 advise keeping any Schuon or Guenon sentence under about forty words, copied from the book with edition and page, and paraphrasing otherwise (several of these authors are in copyright).

### 1.1 The best supported facts in the set (all [C] or [C-repo])

These are the anchors. Everything else is weaker.

- Agostino Steuco, De perenni philosophia (Lyon, 1540) is where the phrase becomes a book title [C]. He was Vatican librarian from 1538 [M].
- Leibniz used the phrase in a letter to Nicolas-Francois Remond dated 26 August 1714, in Latin as "perennis quaedam Philosophia" [C, several results agree on date and addressee; the English is Rutherford's, unread]. Huxley's Foreword wrongly credits Leibniz with coining it [M, plus the repo notes].
- Charles B. Schmitt, "Perennial Philosophy: From Agostino Steuco to Leibniz", Journal of the History of Ideas 27 (1966), pp. 505 to 532, is the standard history of the term [C].
- Guenon: Introduction generale a l'etude des doctrines hindoues (1921), Le Theosophisme, histoire d'une pseudo-religion (1921) and L'Erreur spirite (1923) [C]. He lived in Cairo from 1930 to 1951 [C]. Smoley (Quest, 2010) reports that he attacked Theosophy while sharing its idea of one central tradition behind the religions [C].
- Coomaraswamy lived 1877 to 1947 [C]. His paper "On the One and Only Transmigrant" is Journal of the American Oriental Society, Supplement 3 (1944), 43 pages, printed with "Recollection, Indian and Platonic" [C]. It opens on a line of Shankara, with Brahma Sutra Bhashya 1.1.5 as locator [S]. He joined the Theosophical Society about 1907 and left it [C, year U]. The Dance of Siva is 1918 [C].
- Dara Shikoh wrote Majma al-Bahrayn (about 1655) and Sirr-i Akbar (1657, about fifty Upanishads in Persian, fifty-two by one count). Anquetil-Duperron put the Persian into Latin as the Oupnek'hat in 1801 to 1802, and Schopenhauer praised it [C].
- Max Muller: Introduction to the Science of Religion (1873); the 1893 article "Esoteric Buddhism" in The Nineteenth Century, which criticised the Theosophical claim [C]; and the Gifford lectures of 1892, published as Theosophy or Psychological Religion (1893), where "theosophy" is Muller's own term for an inner religion and not the Society's teaching [M, with sibling record C for the 1893 article].
- Emerson, The Over-Soul (1841) is on Standard Ebooks, with the punctuation of one sentence still open [C].
- Lessing, Die Erziehung des Menschengeschlechts (1780), argues for repeated lives in its closing paragraphs, about 93 to 100 [C for the range, U for the inner numbers].
- Vivekananda spoke in Chicago on 11 and 27 September 1893, and his two best-known sentences come from two different days [C].
- The Theosophical Society was founded in New York on 17 November 1875 with one aim. The comparative wording of its objects is current since 1896. The Benares motto was adopted about 1880. Harrison's paper is Journal of the SPR 53 (1986), pp. 286 to 310. Kardec's Spiritism dates from 1857 [C-repo].
- Julie Chajes, Recycled Lives (Oxford, 2019), shows that Blavatsky's teaching on rebirth changed between 1877 and 1888 [C]. Isis Unveiled (1877) rejected the Spiritist return and allowed rebirth only in exceptional cases [C].

## 2. What Alex means, in the vocabulary of the perennial philosophy and Theosophy

### 2.1 In his words, compressed

There is one whole and everything is in it. The whole looks at itself from a very large number of positions, and each position is a life with a point of view. From inside one position the others feel like separate things. That feeling is an accurate report of a local fact (my memory is mine, yours is yours) read as if it were a fact about the whole. The looking from many positions is a phase, and it ends when the whole knows itself as one. Because most people do not finish in one life, the run is longer than one life, and something continues. What I do to another position I do to the whole, so consequence comes back (karma as the fingers of one hand) and the golden rule is the same logic read forwards. A person can become a very good separate program (the small square) or stop assuming the separation (the big square). The stages of that move form a scale, the arrow, minus four to plus four. Letting go is the method, because it lowers the wall. He offers all this as an assumption, tests it for coherence and not for truth, and says the examples fit without proving anything. [S2 section 1, paraphrased]

### 2.2 In the vocabulary of the Traditionalist perennial philosophy

The nearest formulation is Schuon's, with Guenon's logic underneath [M for the content of both; Schuon's own wording unchecked].

- Reality is the Absolute, which is also the Infinite. Nothing can lie outside the Infinite, so it includes the possibility of something that looks like not the Absolute. That possibility, realised, is Maya: real in its own order and illusory only when taken as separate, neither being nor non-being. The Sufi formula "He and not He" is tied to it. [M]
- Schuon's ladder runs Absolute and Infinite, then Beyond-Being, Being and Existence. Atma is the Absolute seen from inside a person, and the ego is a ray that takes itself for a source. [M; the detail of the Absolute and Infinite scheme U]
- Guenon's version: the Principle is Universal Possibility, and the human state is one of indefinitely many states of the total being. The true Self was never individualised, so liberation, even in this life (jivan-mukti), realises an identity that was always so (L'homme et son devenir selon le Vedanta, 1925; Les etats multiples de l'etre, 1932) [titles C, gist M; the liberation paraphrase is C from a publisher's description].
- The fall is forgetting and deliverance is recognition. For Guenon and Schuon nothing returns because the Self never left. [M]
- Coomaraswamy puts it as two selves: the one Self, the only knower in all beings, and the contingent self, pictured by the two birds on one tree (Mundaka 3.1.1). He sets forgetting (avidya) against recollection (anamnesis in Plato, smrti in Sanskrit). The only transmigrant is the one Self, which is Shankara's line at the head of his 1944 paper. [two birds C, rest M; the Shankara line C with locator S]
- Huston Smith made the same grid readable as four levels of reality and four of selfhood, with the Infinite including the finite. [M]
- The ethic follows: the neighbour is the Self, the golden rule is the corollary of "that thou art", and humility keeps knowledge from turning into pride. [M gist, citations U]
- The method is discernment, concentration (above all on a Name: japa, dhikr, the Jesus Prayer, the nembutsu) and virtue, humility first. [M; the exact triad U]
- Staged maps in this school are degrees of Reality, not growth in time. The strict founders read history as decline and reject evolution as a world process. [M]
- Why religions differ: the outer forms differ on purpose and may contradict, because an outer form has to present itself as the one truth, and the inner centre agrees. [M]

### 2.3 In the vocabulary of Theosophy

The nearest statement is the Proem of The Secret Doctrine (1888), which has three fundamental propositions [M for the structure, U for the wording, which is one memory repeated in several files].

- One omnipresent, eternal, boundless and immutable Principle beyond thought. This matches Alex's first clause.
- Universes that appear and vanish in cycles. Nothing in Alex's axiom matches this and it should not be imported. In Theosophy the whole cycle repeats, so the ending of separation is a pause.
- The fundamental identity of all souls with the Universal Over-Soul, itself an aspect of the Unknown Root, and an obligatory pilgrimage for every soul through the cycle of incarnation under cyclic and karmic law. This is the nearest older statement of clauses one to three. It is one step softer than Alex's first clause, because her souls are identical with an Over-Soul that is only an aspect of the Principle. "Over-Soul" is Emerson's word (1841) [M].
- Her answer to why: the spark wins individuality by its own effort, passing through every form, and the pivotal doctrine admits no privileges. Unconscious divine purity becomes conscious divinity through experience. [gist M, wording U; the "unconscious divine purity" phrase is unlocated and should not be quoted]
- In the Rounds scheme (Sinnett, Esoteric Buddhism, 1883) the life-wave descends into denser matter, bottoms out on the fourth globe and climbs back carrying the self-consciousness it gained. This is the V shape of Alex's chain. [M]
- Her chain words: personality is the mask (persona) and individuality the lasting Ego; the Ego keeps the harvest of each life; kama-loka and devachan lie between lives; memory belongs to the personality's brain, which is why we do not remember. Karma is a law of harmony with no judge, so that we reward or punish ourselves, and brotherhood is a fact in nature. [M]
- Maya is illusion, and only what is changeless is real, so whatever changes is Maya (Theosophical Glossary, 1892, posthumous, edited by Mead). That sits nearer the Advaita word mithya, "dependent", than "unreal". [M]
- The gate is a reason bigger than yourself: in The Voice of the Silence the Two Paths set private liberation against the Great Renunciation, where the adept who could enter nirvana stays to help. [M]
- Why religions differ: descent from one archaic Wisdom-Religion kept by initiates. Agreement is inheritance. Difference is corruption, literal reading, lost keys, priestcraft and deliberate "blinds". The authority is the Masters. [M]

### 2.5 What changes when the argument is put in these words

Five things.

First, a motive. Guenon and Schuon give a logic with no motive: manifestation follows from Infinity. Blavatsky gives a motive: consciousness is earned by passing through separation. Alex's second clause says a whole that wants to know itself needs a position to know from. That is the motive kind, and he should say so and name it as one of two kinds. The oldest motive text in the set is Brihadaranyaka 1.4.1 to 4. [M; check in Olivelle]

Second, an assumption in place of a received teaching or a principle. Blavatsky gave her third proposition as something she had been taught. Guenon and Schuon give theirs as metaphysics known by intellectual intuition inside one regular tradition. Alex offers his as an assumption and tests it for coherence. That is a fourth way of explaining why the ideas recur, beside revelation, transmission and common experience. [M; S3 section 6, S5 section 1]

Third, derivation in place of subtraction. Huxley's Foreword reaches a common factor by subtraction. Schuon rejects that arithmetic, because the unity lies above the forms and cannot be found by averaging. Alex starts from one principle and sees what follows, which is closer to Schuon and Guenon, except that he calls his starting point an assumption. [M]

Fourth, the third clause. Advaita, Schuon and Guenon say the Self never left, and deliverance is recognition. "Returns" fits Plotinus, Proclus and Theosophy. "Ends" fits Schuon. Strict Advaita adds that at the highest level nothing began (Gaudapada, Karika 2.32). Alex can say "ends, in experience", or let both stand. [M]

Fifth, lives. No Traditionalist derives lives the way Alex does (if most do not finish in one life, either the design is bad or the run is longer). Guenon rejects the Western return of the same individual. Schuon followed in outline. Nasr, Lings and Burckhardt hold the Islamic one earthly life with barzakh and resurrection. Coomaraswamy keeps transmigration and changes the subject to the one Self. Theosophy teaches return of the Ego and argues for it by reason. The academic ancestors of Alex's dilemma are Lessing and Hick. So on lives he sits with Theosophy, Lessing and Hick, and he should not cite "the perennial philosophy" for it. [C for Guenon's position, M for the rest; Schuon's own words on rebirth are U]

A tension inside the draft follows. The dilemma treats the individual as the unit that has to finish. The axiom says the individual is a position of the whole, so the one running every life is the whole. That is why Coomaraswamy fits the axiom best and the Theosophical Ego fits the saved-state picture best. The draft holds both pictures. It can keep both if it labels them as answers at two levels. [S2 section 3]

Stages. Staged maps in the traditions are ladders of practice (Underhill, Teresa, the Voice), worlds (Dante, the Mi'raj) or degrees of reality (Schuon, Smith). Growth of a person through time is the shape of Theosophy, Aurobindo, Wilber, Bucke, Teilhard and Underhill. The strict Traditionalists reject evolutionary pictures. So Alex's natural comparers for the arrow are the first group, and the Traditionalists should be cited for "different forms, same inner teaching" and not for stages. [M]

### 2.6 What he can safely claim, and what he cannot

Safe, in the files' own judgement [S2 section 3, with tiers]:

1. The nearest older statement of the axiom is the Proem of The Secret Doctrine, once the wording is checked. She gave it as received teaching. He gives it as an assumption, with no lost original and no Masters. [wording U]
2. Schuon and Guenon give a logic for why the many follows from the One. He gives a motive. Both kinds of answer exist. [M]
3. The traditions disagree about lives, and the two movements he leans on split on it. The axiom does not settle it. [C, M]
4. On who carries over there are two answers: Theosophy's lasting individuality and Coomaraswamy's one Self. [C, M]
5. Karma is taken as given in most traditions. Deriving it from unity has precedents in Schopenhauer and Emerson. [M]
6. Stage maps run from four steps to a hundred. The files know of none numbered symmetrically about zero. [M, not searched]

Unsafe, to be avoided: "all traditions say"; "the perennialists teach reincarnation"; "Theosophy calls it birth"; "Jainism or Theravada say one whole"; "Huxley is the best" as a fact; "Traditionalists and Theosophists are allies"; "the Gita says all religions are equal".

## 3. The shelf beyond Huxley, by job

### 3.1 What the files can carry

Alex's point is right on the facts that matter. Huxley's The Perennial Philosophy (1945) is an anthology of mystics and saints under themes, with his commentary. It is one voice in a long line. The files cannot rank the books, and several say so explicitly: "best" depends on the job, and a ranking would need a criterion Alex states, such as closeness to the primary texts, openness about sources, or survival under specialist criticism. So the table below says best for what, and ranks nobody. If the essay says Huxley is not the best, it should read as Alex's opinion.

### 3.4 How to word Huxley in the essay

- Say "best known" for the English reader's door. Say it is one book in a family. Name at least one Traditionalist and one comparer outside the school each time the movement is invoked.

The files recall the metaphysic as a divine Reality "substantial to" the world of things, lives and minds, which has a scholastic ring: Reality is what the world is made of, and not only behind it. The draft's "underlying" is a fair paraphrase and a poor quotation. The ethic clause places the final end in knowing the immanent and transcendent Ground. [M, wording U]

### 3.5 Quotation and attribution traps that touch Huxley

- "Huxley coined the phrase" is false. "Leibniz coined it" is Huxley's own error in the Foreword, so do not repeat it even though it is in the book. [C/M]
- "The doors of perception" is Blake's phrase, from The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, through Huxley's title. A line about things known and unknown and the doors between circulates under Huxley's name and is a Jim Morrison line. [M]
- The reducing valve is a Broad and Bergson idea (Bergson, Matter and Memory, 1896; Broad, 1952 and 1953; Myers 1903), taken into Huxley's prose. [M]
- Huxley's Doors of Perception (1954) and Heaven and Hell (1956) are two books, often cited as one. Underhill's Mysticism (1911), The Mystic Way (1913) and Practical Mysticism (1914) are three. Smith's Forgotten Truth and Huxley's Perennial Philosophy are different books. [M]
- "Primordial tradition" is Guenon's phrase, which Smith took as the subtitle of Forgotten Truth in 1976. [M]

### 5.1 The one whole (the axiom)

Perennial authors. Guenon states it as logic: the Principle is the Infinite and Universal Possibility, nothing can lie outside it, so manifestation is included, and the true Self was never individualised [M for the books, C for the paraphrase]. Schuon gives the clearest ladder, with Atma as the Absolute seen from inside a person [M, detail U]. Coomaraswamy puts it as two selves [C for the birds, M for the rest]. Smith made it readable [M]. Burckhardt (Sufi), Schaya (Kabbalah) and Shah-Kazemi (Shankara, Ibn Arabi, Eckhart) run the same grid inside one tradition each [M; what each equates U]. Huxley's chapters "That Art Thou" and "The Nature of the Ground" and the first clause of his Foreword carry the same idea [M].

Theosophists. The Proem's three propositions, with the third nearest [M content, U wording]. Subba Row gives the Indian form: Parabrahman, Mulaprakriti as its veil, the Logos as first manifest [M]. Her Absolute has two aspects, Spirit and Matter, so it is not plain Advaita [M]. The Letters have the Masters deny a personal God and call Parabrahm absolute immutable law [M gist, letter number U]. Letter 10 is a long K.H. letter printed in the Sinnett volume and probably addressed to A. O. Hume (1880), and "Parabrahm" is an Advaita term, so do not write that Buddhism teaches "Parabrahm is law" [M/U].

Added by the topic files.
- Upanishads and Gita: Chandogya 6.2.1 to 3 and 6.3.2 (Being thought to become many, then enters its products as the living self); 6.8.7 (Advaita reads identity, Ramanuja and Madhva read it differently); Brihadaranyaka 1.4.10 (the polemic against worshipping another deity thinking "he is one, I another"; knowing is what "becomes all"); Mundaka 2.1.1 and 3.2.8 to 9 and Taittiriya 3.1, which put the three clauses in one formula; Gita 15.7, an eternal fragment of me becomes the individual self [M].
- Kashmir Shaivism (the Pratyabhijna, or Recognition, school), the closest technical match found: one consciousness (citi), free by nature (svatantrya), shows the world on its own surface (svabhitti), contracts into limited knowers (sankoca), and recovers itself by recognising what it never stopped being. The world is real throughout. Only sutras 1 and 2 of the Pratyabhijna-hrdayam are firm in memory [M]. The school sits mostly outside what the perennialist authors cite; Coomaraswamy's Nataraja is the one Shaiva picture they use, and it comes from Tamil Saiva Siddhanta [M/U].
- Other Indian systems that keep world and souls real inside one whole: Ramanuja (souls and world as God's body, individuality kept in release), the bhedabheda schools, Chaitanya (acintya-bhedabheda), the Yoga Vasistha (one consciousness living many lives and worlds with the memory switched off) [M]. They answer clause three weakly, since individuality stays.
- Chinese: Zhuangzi 2 (heaven and earth born with me, the ten thousand things one with me, then the doubt that "one" can be said); Wang Yangming's one body, hidden by selfish desire and restored by removal [M]. Graham and Hansen read Zhuangzi 2 as perspectival scepticism and not an ontological One [M].
- Western: Spinoza (Ethics I P14, P15; II P11 Corollary); Hegel (the true is the whole; Encyclopaedia 564, God is God only as self-knowing); Krause's panentheism (1828); Schaffer's priority monism (2010); Shani, Nagasawa and Wager, Goff on cosmopsychism; Kastrup (one mind, individual lives as dissociated parts); Albahari (identification, an epistemic limit) [M]. Schrodinger's 1944 epilogue states the same plurality-as-appearance. Einstein's 1950 letter is nearer the axiom than his 1929 reply [M, wording U].

Terms side by side [all comparers' equations, M]. The absolute without qualities: Nirguna Brahman and Atma (Theosophy: Parabrahm) in Hinduism; Eckhart's Gottheit, God beyond God, in Christianity; Dhat and "He and not He" in Islam; Ein Sof in Judaism (Schuon's and Schaya's pairing, U as theirs); the nameless Tao in Taoism; Dharmakaya and emptiness in Buddhism (the comparers' equation, which specialists keep apart); the One and the Good beyond being (Republic 509b) in Greece. "You are that": tat tvam asi (Chandogya 6.8.7; Brihadaranyaka 1.4.10). Soul of the whole: Emerson's Over-Soul.

Where they split. Both start from one Principle beyond words and say the human core is not other than it. They split on authority. Blavatsky gives it as received teaching from hidden Masters and one archaic Wisdom-Religion. Guenon and Schuon give it as metaphysics known by intellectual intuition inside one regular tradition, and Guenon wrote one book against Theosophy as a counterfeit (Le Theosophisme, 1921) and one against Spiritism and reincarnationism (L'Erreur spirite, 1923) while holding that a central tradition underlies all religions [C]. Specialists object to both: "advaita" is "not two", which is not "one" (Loy); Buddhist emptiness is no One; Scholem and Idel do not read Ein Sof as Nirguna Brahman [M].

For Alex. His stance, an assumption tested for coherence, is the honest one. Say once that Blavatsky's first and third propositions state the same shape, offered as received, and that Guenon and Schuon argue it as logic. Do not import her second proposition. Keep the softer step in mind if he quotes her. Never call Theosophy and the Traditionalists one family. [S1]

### 5.2 The veil and the illusion of separateness

Perennial authors. Schuon is the best source for the illusion that feels real: Maya is real in its order and unreal if taken as separate, neither being nor non-being, with a veiling side and a revealing side, tied to "He and not He"; illusion is taking the part for the whole [M; his Buddhist and Eckhart pairings U]. Guenon blames the age: individualism, which he defines as the denial of any principle higher than the individual, and the Kali Yuga, whose length of more than six thousand years is his own figure and not the Puranic 432,000 [C for the line, its lead-in U]. Coomaraswamy reads maya as the One measured out into forms (his own etymology from ma) and sets forgetting (avidya) against recollection (anamnesis, smrti) [M]. Smith: the ego is identification with the lower levels [M]. James's filmiest of screens and Huxley's brain as a reducing valve (The Doors of Perception, 1954, from Broad and Bergson) are the English pictures nearest to Alex's walls [M]. Wilber: a boundary that is drawn can be undrawn [M].

Theosophists. The Glossary (1892, posthumous, edited by Mead): only what is changeless is real, so whatever changes is Maya [M, near-verbatim]. The personality is the mask, the individuality what lasts [M; the persona etymology in the Key U]. The Voice (1889): mind is the slayer of the real [M]. Besant (The Self and Its Sheaths, 1895; A Study in Consciousness, 1904): separateness comes from the sheath [titles M, content U]. Bailey: three veils (maya, glamour, illusion) [U]. Subba Row: a veil made of the One itself [M]. The phrase "heresy of separateness" is credited to Blavatsky online and nobody has placed it [U]. Do not credit it.

Added by the topic files.
- Technical theories of illusion as a misreading of something real (G2-3). Shankara's adhyasa, superimposition, in the introduction to the Brahma Sutra commentary: the traits of the not-self laid on the Self and the reverse, natural and beginningless, a cause of suffering that knowledge of the Self removes; the examples are shell-silver, the double moon and blue space [M]. Advaita's three orders of reality (pratibhasika, vyavaharika, paramarthika) give "real inside the system" its source: the wall between programs is vyavaharika, the shared working world. The clean three-tier scheme is post-Shankara, so scholars warn against reading it back into him [M]. Yogacara's three natures (imagined, dependent, perfected) are the best structural match: what appears is dependent, how it appears is imagined, and the perfected is the constant absence of the second in the first. Freedom is a subtraction and not an addition [M; verse numbers U]. Candrakirti's two kinds of conventional, the sound (what a healthy faculty perceives, which the world can act on) and the false (a cataract's hairs, a mirage), give "real inside the system" a Buddhist name; and Madhyamaka has no substrate, so never use it to say Buddhism teaches oneness [M]. Indian philosophers put one test case to every school (khyativada): Nyaya's anyathakhyati, a real thing wrongly placed, suits the wall better than Advaita's indescribable silver [M]. Ibn Arabi's "He and not He", imagination (khayal) and barzakh keep both statements true [M]. Plato's Sophist 240a to b: a likeness is not the real thing, yet really is a likeness; Kant separates Erscheinung (appearance) from Schein (illusion), which defends the essay's care with the word [M]. Bradley's closed circle of finite centres is a public-domain image of the memory wall [M; wording U].
- Why it feels solid (G2-3). Advaita's three parts: the superimposition is natural and beginningless, so no one has an earlier clear moment to compare; an error needs a partly known substrate; and appearances are ranked by whether a later knowing cancels them (sublation) [M]. Four traditions separate a belief that argument removes from a habit that only practice wears down: the Khemaka Sutta (SN 22.89: the elder sees no self in the five aggregates yet a residual "I am" conceit remains, like the smell left in a cloth washed with lye until it is put in a scented chest; the commentary, not the sutta, calls him a non-returner); Yogacara's co-born against discriminated self-clinging (Cheng Weishi, Xuanzang 659); Tsongkhapa's innate against acquired self-grasping (Lamrim Chenmo, 1402); Advaita's badhitanuvritti, the continuing of a cancelled appearance, and the potter's wheel that keeps turning [M; some details U]. This is the clean answer to the rubber hand: the feeling of a wall does not respond to being told it is a model, as the lines in the Muller-Lyer illusion stay unequal after measuring. State one difference: the traditions claim practice can wear the habit down, and the cognitive science does not show it.
- Three texts with one "as if": Brihadaranyaka 2.4.14 (where there is duality, as it were, dvaitam iva), Katha 2.1.10 (naneva) and Gita 13.16 (vibhaktam iva), with Yoga Sutra 2.6. The best single line for "a wrong reading of something real", since the texts themselves say "as it were" [M].
- Two causes of the veil: error of knowing (rope and snake, Shankara) and clutch of will (the knot of the heart, Mundaka 2.2.8; the tangle, SN 1.23). ### 5.3 Why the whole separates itself

Perennial authors. Two kinds of answer appear and should not be merged. The logic kind is Guenon's and Schuon's: the Infinite cannot exclude any possibility, so apparent distance from itself is included. Maya is then play, neither error nor fall, and the Good tends to give itself [M]. Smith repeats it: an Infinite that could not include limits would be limited by that very fact [M, wording U]. The motive kind is older: Brihadaranyaka 1.4.1 to 4 [M]; Ibn Arabi's Real wishing to see the Names in a comprehensive being, a mirror (Fusus, Adam chapter) [M]; lila (Brahma Sutra 2.1.33, the reply to the objection in 2.1.32), which Coomaraswamy sets beside Eckhart's "without why" and Heraclitus fragment 52 [M]. Wilber (the Atman project), Grof and Watts give modern versions of the motive kind [M]. Huxley does not argue the question.

Theosophists. Blavatsky answers with a motive: the spark must pass through every form and win individuality by its own effort, and the pivotal doctrine admits no privileges [M; wording U]. The Key restates it as unconscious divine purity becoming conscious divinity through experience [M; that it is in the Key U]. Sinnett and the Letters give the Rounds as a V [M]. ### 5.4 Lives, and the soul between lives

Perennial authors. This is where the movement splits. Guenon rejects reincarnation in the Western sense as a nineteenth-century notion from Kardec and the Theosophists. The doctrine is transmigration, which is change of state, and no state is run through twice [C]. Coomaraswamy keeps transmigration and changes the subject: the one Self is the only transmigrant [C]. In 1916 he wrote of "the transmigration of character", an older formula of the English Pali school (tied to Rhys Davids), so check for quotation marks [C, U on whose]. Schuon followed Guenon on Western reincarnationism and reads rebirth as change of state or a psychic residue [U, the item most worth checking in the books]. Nasr, Lings and Burckhardt hold the Islamic line of one earthly life, barzakh and resurrection [M, what each says in print U]. Pallis, a Buddhist practitioner, keeps rebirth without a soul and the bardo [U]. Huxley's chapter "Immortality and Survival" separates survival from immortality [M, chapter number U]. Aurobindo and Wilber give staged rebirth. Jung separates five senses of "rebirth" [M].

Theosophists. By the 1880s the individuality (the Ego) returns and the personality does not. After death come kama-loka and the long dream of devachan, and we forget because memory belongs to the personality's brain [M]. Isis Unveiled (1877) rejected the Spiritist return and allowed rebirth only in exceptional cases, which Chajes (2019) documents as a change by 1888 [C]. The shift is usually placed around 1881 to 1882 [M]. Besant, Judge and Sinnett carried it to readers. Theosophists reject return into animals [M]. Chajes also argues the rule was formed from mainly Western sources (Spiritist progressive reincarnationism, Kingsford and Maitland's The Perfect Way, Sinnett, Arnold), and was not an import from India [M for the thesis, U for details]. So do not say Theosophists brought it from the East, and do not say Isis Unveiled was a main route for it. Devachan is her reading of the Tibetan bde-ba-can (which renders Sukhavati), and a Pure Land is a place of practice and not a rest between lives [M]. In Brihadaranyaka 3.7 the thread (sutra) is the wind that strings beings together at one time, so the Theosophical "thread of lives" is a Theosophical image with a Vedanta word. Gita 7.7 (gems on a thread) is the nearer image [M].

### 5.8 Stages of growth, and what lies beyond the last stage

Perennial authors. There is no single perennial stage map. The strict school describes degrees of Reality and cyclic decline, and rejects evolution as a world process [M]. Smith's four levels are worlds and not stages. Nasr and Schuon give the five divine presences and sharia, tariqa, haqiqa [M, which list Schuon uses U]. Coomaraswamy: the sun door out of the cosmos (Janua Coeli) [M]. Lings: the three certainties [M]. Underhill's five stages (awakening, purification, illumination, the dark night, union; chapter titles U) and Bucke's three levels (simple, self, cosmic consciousness; his coinage, not an Eastern term) are nearer a growth picture [M]. Huxley has no stage map [U]. Beyond the last rung the files find four answers, each an interpretive grouping: rest (the Jain siddha), no end (Gregory's epektasis; Ibn Arabi on one reading), return into action (the bodhisattva, the tenth ox picture, Teresa's seventh mansion), and no ladder at all (Gaudapada 2.32, sudden Zen) [M, readings flagged]. Birth images: John 3:3, the dvija, Eckhart's birth followed by the breakthrough (Sermon 52) [M].

Theosophists. The Voice's Three Halls and Seven Portals [M]. Besant and Leadbeater: probation, discipleship, then five initiations, with Buddhist, Hindu and Christian labels, an edit of the four Buddhist stages [M, Hindu names U]. Bailey: nine initiations and a choice of seven Paths [M, labels five to nine U]. In Bailey and in Besant and Leadbeater, "Birth" is the first initiation [M]. Blavatsky's own pilgrimage ends at the Dhyani level, and what lies above is called unknowable [M].

Added by the topic files. Corpus Hermeticum I (seven shed faults, then the eighth, then the Father) and XI.20 (to know God, become equal to God, grow immeasurably, leap out of every body and become eternity: the nearest ancient text to the zoom-out) [M, numbers U]; Excerpta ex Theodoto 78.2; the Sikh five khands (Japji pauris 34 to 37, ending in a count of solar systems and universes that echoes the space-travel mapping [M, line wording U]); the Jain fourteen gunasthanas, where a soul at stage 11 can fall back, and Tattvartha Sutra 10.5 to 8, where the freed soul rises like a flame or a gourd freed of clay to the edge of the cosmos and stops for want of a medium of motion [M, numbering U]; Dante's Paradiso (the flame that would be a surprise only if it sat still; speed rises toward God) [M, line numbers U]; Teresa's silkworm that dies and becomes a butterfly, her second-birth image [M]; the Yoga Vasistha's seven stages of knowledge [U]; Kant 1755, Swedenborg 1758 and Kardec's grades of worlds as precedents for a graded cosmos [M]; Fechner's three stages; Gerald Heard [M, details U]; Gurdjieff's Man numbers 1 to 7 and his Ray of Creation, with "All Suns" as the galaxy rung [M]; Yogananda's Kriya yoga as an airplane against the bullock cart of natural evolution, which parallels the speed picture, to be cited only if exact [M gist, figures U]; Meher Baba's six planes to the seventh [M]; Tsiolkovsky's cradle line (a 1911 letter; the planet or Earth is the cradle of reason or humanity, and one cannot live in a cradle forever [M 80 percent; the English noun U]) with the Russian schoolroom's first, second and third cosmic velocities (orbit, escape, leaving the solar system), so a Russian reader already owns the ladder [M]. Caution: historians report that Tsiolkovsky's ethics proposed removing "imperfect" life [M 75 percent]. Mention it only if the line is used.

Terms side by side [M]. Four stages: stream-enterer to arahant (ten fetters). Seven mansions: Teresa, written 1577. Fourteen stages with falls: the Jain gunasthanas. Ten grounds: the Mahayana bhumis. Seven valleys: Attar. Ten heavens: Dante; seven heavens: the Mi'raj; Hagigah 12b. Seven portals: the Voice, Fragment III.

Where they split. The strict school reads history as decline and rejects evolution, so a growth arrow is a Theosophical, Wilber and Underhill shape [M]. Theosophy cut "arhat" and "asekha" into two stages, which the Pali does not [M]. Katz says stage maps may track vocabulary [M]. Repeated sevens may come from the seven planets and not a shared inner fact, but that fits Hermes, Mithras and Dante and not Teresa, the Jain fourteen or the Buddhist ten [M]. Krishnamurti's pathless land; Ferrer: one ladder assumes one summit [M].

For Alex. The draft's "other traditions draw staged maps too" is safe. Do not write that no other scale is nine steps centred on a door: nine-step and threshold-centred scales exist, and only the zero-centred numbering is unknown to the files [M]. Add that the arrow is a personal scale and makes no claim that the world improves, which defuses the Traditionalist objection. For "birth", add that Eckhart himself puts a breakthrough after the birth, so the draft's "a lasting state" needs a clause. Never write that Theosophy "calls it birth". [S1]

### 5.9 The cosmic scale: planes, worlds, hierarchies

Perennial authors. The strict school reads the ladder of the soul and the ladder of the cosmos as one ladder: distance words stand for degrees of reality, not kilometres [M]. Smith: four nested levels. Nasr: Islamic cosmology and the five presences [M, U on Schuon's list]. Guenon: states of being and the cross, a vertical axis of degrees crossing a horizontal axis of range [M]. His cycles of the Kali Yuga describe the decline of an age and run opposite to a personal ascent [C for the cycles, the contrast is P02's reading]. Burckhardt reads Ibn Arabi's planetary heavens as stages [U year]. Wilber's Great Nest: each level transcends and includes the one below [M]. Huxley: none.

Theosophists. The Great Breath and manvantara and pralaya at every scale, with Puranic numbers (the arithmetic holds) [M]. Chains of globes, rounds and seven planes [M]. Dhyan-Chohans between the Absolute and humanity [M]. Besant and Leadbeater add Planetary and Solar Logoi; Bailey the Heavenly Man and cosmic planes, the strongest self-similar ladder, whose likeness to Alex's small square inside a 1,000 by 1,000 map is structural only [M]. Root races are what scholars find hardest to defend. Leave them out.

Terms side by side [M]. Worlds as steps: seven lokas (Hindu); five presences, Hahut to Nasut (Sufi); four worlds, Atzilut to Asiyah (Kabbalah). Worlds counted: the thousandfold systems of 1,000, 1,000,000 and 1,000,000,000 worlds (Buddhist; AN 3.80 U): a count of worlds and not of potential. A galaxy rung: Gurdjieff's All Suns as the Milky Way. A speed picture: Dante, Paradiso 28 (line numbers U). An escape picture: the freed Jain soul rising like a flame to the edge of the cosmos. A universe as one breath: the Day and Night of Brahma (Hindu and Theosophical).

Where they split. Traditionalist hierarchies are static degrees. Theosophical ones are evolutionary planes. Guenon attacked the Theosophical constitution of man. No Theosophical text maps a stage to speed, and Theosophy puts Earth's Logos above the Master where Alex's arrow leaves Earth at plus one [M].

For Alex. The old maps were already scale maps, so plus two to plus four is that idea in modern units. Dante and the Jain flame are the closest old pictures of "gravity is the pull of the one you started with". Present the Buddhist thousandfold figure as an echo and not a source. The multiverse hint has older precedents in time (the Great Breath) and in space (numberless Brahmas) [M]. If Theosophy is named as a stage-mapper, add that Blavatsky's own work differs from the layers of Besant, Leadbeater and Bailey. [S1]

### 5.10 What cuts across all nine

The two movements do not agree. Say it once, with names: Guenon (and, by inference, Coomaraswamy and Schuon [U]) called Theosophy a counterfeit and rejected its rebirth. They share only the idea of one tradition behind the religions. Huxley, by contrast, was friendly to Krishnamurti [M] and is no party to the quarrel. Keep the vocabularies apart: Masters, root races and Wisdom-Religion are Theosophical; exoteric and esoteric, primordial tradition and Beyond-Being are Traditionalist. "Primordial tradition" is Guenon's phrase, not Smith's.

How each compares. Blavatsky: genealogy, one archaic source, difference as corruption, authority from the Masters, so the method cannot lose [M]. Guenon and Schuon: begin from a principle, fix the level, compare esoteric with esoteric, explain difference as willed form [M]. Coomaraswamy: philology and symbol, line by line [M]. Huxley: subtract to a Highest Common Factor of theologies (find-handle "Highest Common Factor") [M, recalled]. Alex derives from one axiom and shows fit, which is a fourth method. Do not borrow Huxley's "conditions" move (a critic who does not see it is told he lacks the conditions). [S1]

## 6. Tradition by tradition

Base: S4, with the best additions from the G files. Section 5 holds the texts by idea; this section holds what each tradition looks like to the two movements, and the cautions that go with it. Alex's rule applies throughout: do not write that a tradition "fits badly"; show the layer (scripture, mystic, popular teaching), name it, and write a specialist's objection as what the specialist adds.

Habits that run through every tradition below [M]. The Traditionalists fix a ladder first (Absolute, Being, Existence) and compare inner layer with inner layer: Eckhart beside Shankara and Ibn Arabi, never the catechism beside the Gita. Unity lies above the forms, so outer forms may contradict. They require one regular tradition and initiation, which Alex's many witnesses do not. The Theosophists claim one archaic Wisdom-Religion. For them agreement is descent, difference is corruption, literal reading and lost keys, and equations are tabulated (the Glossary, the seven principles). Every comparer also has a top: Dara reads Vedanta in Sufi words, Vivekananda puts Advaita highest, Kukai and Zongmi their own Buddhism, the Baha'i scheme the latest revelation. The two movements disliked each other. Guenon wrote Le Theosophisme (1921) and L'Erreur spirite (1923) against Theosophy [C] while sharing its idea of one primordial teaching [C, Smoley]. Coomaraswamy joined the Society about 1907 and left [C, year U]. Whether Schuon named Theosophy himself is unchecked [U].

### 6.1 Hinduism: Upanishads, Gita, Vedanta, Yoga and the Shaiva schools

Who cites it. Everybody. Guenon treats Hindu doctrine as the oldest and most complete and states each doctrine first in Sanskrit terms, then reads the other traditions as saying it in other words [C for the books, M for the method]. Coomaraswamy builds on the Self and the self and on the one transmigrant [C]. Schuon uses Atma, Maya and the pair Absolute and Infinite (Logic and Transcendence, 1970; Survey of Metaphysics and Esoterism, 1986) [M]. Smith adds four levels of reality and of self [M]. The Theosophists use Parabrahm, Mulaprakriti, Maya, the Over-Soul (Emerson's word, taken over) and the Puranic cycles [M]. Subba Row, a Madras Advaitin, gave the Gita lectures (Krishna as Logos, Mulaprakriti as the veil of Parabrahman); the folder disagrees on their date (December 1885 printed 1886, or 1886 printed 1887, or December 1886 printed 1887), so print no year [M, year U].

Texts. Chandogya 6.8.7, 6.2.1 to 3 and 6.9 to 10; Brihadaranyaka 1.4.1 to 4, 1.4.10, 2.4.14, 3.7 and 4.4.3 to 5; Mundaka 2.1.1, 2.2.8 and 3.1.1; Katha 2.1.10 and 2.3.14 to 15; Isha 6 and 7, 9 to 11; Taittiriya 3.1; Gita 2.13, 2.22, 2.47 to 2.50, 3.4 to 3.9, 3.20 to 3.25, 4.11, 6.29 to 32, 6.37 to 45, 7.19, 12.12 to 19, 13.16, 13.28, 15.7, 15.8, 18.7 to 8, 18.20, 18.45 to 48; Brahma Sutra 2.1.32 to 36; Gaudapada's Mandukya Karika 2.32; Yoga Sutras 1.12, 2.6 and 4.3 [M all, numbers to be checked]. Rigveda 1.164.46 is about the names of gods, and "Truth is one, the wise call it by many names" is a loose English slogan, quoted by Gandhi and Vivekananda [M].

Terms they equate. One: Brahman and Atman, nirguna and saguna Brahman (Schuon's Beyond-Being and Being), Parabrahm for the Theosophists. Veil: maya, avidya, adhyasa; the Glossary defines Maya as whatever changes and ends, closer to mithya (dependent) than to "unreal". Return: moksha and jivanmukti; Guenon reads it as realising a Supreme Identity that was always so, and Schuon agrees. Lives: Guenon says transmigration means change of state and not return to the earthly state [C, locator unknown]; Coomaraswamy says the one Self is the only transmigrant; Theosophy says the reincarnating Ego returns and the personality does not. Karma: action, and "as one acts so one becomes" (Brihadaranyaka 4.4.5); Guenon's line that karma means action and nothing else overstates, since consequence is classical. Letting go: anasakti (Gita 2.47, 3.27, 18.66), and Yoga Sutras 1.12 and 4.3 [M].

Relation to the others. Guenon puts Hinduism first. Hindu inclusivism (Gita 4.11, 7.21, 9.23) lets other worship reach Krishna imperfectly (7.23 calls that fruit finite; 9.23 says worshippers of other gods worship him, but by the wrong rule), which is not "all religions are equal". Hacker's inclusivism was published as a volume in 1983, after his death in 1979, and the term is from his earlier work [M]. Dara's Majma al-Bahrayn (about 1655) set Sufi and Upanishadic terms side by side, with the Upanishads as the hidden book of the Quran [C].

Where the two movements differ. Rebirth: Theosophy teaches it as the rule, Guenon calls it a modern error, Coomaraswamy gives a third answer [C]. The seven principles of man are Theosophy's own count and no Hindu text has them. Subba Row dissented from Blavatsky and Sinnett on the number of principles and on whether Atma is a principle of a person [M that the quarrel existed, U on the points].

Best citations. Coomaraswamy's opening dictum from Shankara (Brahma Sutra Bhashya 1.1.5, in the 1944 supplement) [C; locator S]. For why the whole divides, Brihadaranyaka 1.4.1 to 4 [M]. For the axiom in Theosophy, the third proposition of the Proem [M gist, U wording]. For "a wrong reading", Brihadaranyaka 2.4.14 with Katha 2.1.10 and Gita 13.16 (the three "as if" texts) [M].

Specialist objections, as additions. Advaita is one school among several, and "advaita" means not-two, which is not "one". Gita 6.41 to 45 already shows growth across lives, so the Theosophical ladder is not purely a Western import, though its evolutionary frame is. Most Indologists read the rebirth verses in the ordinary sense, against Guenon. The caste context of svadharma (Gita 18.41 to 48): Ambedkar read it as a defence of caste, and Aurobindo re-read it as inner nature; Gandhi's reading changed over his life, so do not group him with the aptitude readers [M/U]. Shankara reads Isha 9 to 11 with vidya as worship of deities and avidya as ritual works, so he is no witness for "knowledge and action together" from those verses [M]. Shankara's date is the eighth century CE [M].

The Shaiva and Vaishnava schools (G1-7). Kashmir Shaivism (Pratyabhijna) is the closest technical match (section 5.1). It names the veil exactly: three malas (anava, the sense of being small and incomplete; mayiya, the stain of difference; karma, the sense of being a doer who keeps the receipts) and five kancukas (cloaks) that limit five attributes of Shiva [M; the mapping to Alex's wall, screen distance and karma is the checker's]. It names contraction (sankoca) and its opposite (vikasa). Its way is recognition and not escape, graded by four upayas, and grace (shaktipata) comes first in Abhinavagupta. Sanderson stresses that recognition is tied to initiation and ritual, so a purely psychological reading is a modern repackaging [M]. Dates: Abhinavagupta about the year 1000 (c. 950 to 1016 in many reference works, c. 975 to 1025 in Sanderson's) [M]. The Tamil Saiva Siddhanta has the same names for its three bonds but a different sense for the third (matter) [M]. Ramanuja (traditional dates 1017 to 1137, some historians a lifetime later [U]) makes souls and world God's body, keeps individuality in release, and puts surrender (prapatti) at the centre; the Vadakalai and Tenkalai split on grip or carried, the monkey and the cat, a ready picture for Part three [M]. The Yoga Vasistha (Moksopaya in its core, about the tenth century in Kashmir [M, 70 percent]) is the best Indian story of one consciousness living many lives and worlds, with seven stages of knowledge [U] and effort over fate [M]. Chaitanya's acintya-bhedabheda has forgetting as the cause of bondage, and does not fit "the separation ends", which Alex should say. A Theosophical Publishing House edition of Aiyer's Yoga Vasistha may exist [U]. These schools sit mostly outside what the perennialist authors cite [M/U].

### 6.2 Buddhism: Theravada, Mahayana, Tibetan, Zen

Who cites it, Traditionalists. Schuon equates nirvana with the Absolute and sunyata with Beyond-Being, reads anatta as denying only the empirical ego, and gives Buddhism its own revelation and orthodoxy, with Pure Land set beside Christian grace (In the Tracks of Buddhism, English 1968) [M]. Coomaraswamy (Hinduism and Buddhism, 1943) argues that the Buddha works by elimination as the Upanishads do and denies the aggregates and not the Self [S]. His 1916 phrase about the transmigration of character may be Rhys Davids's formula, and he later changed his view [M]. Guenon in 1921 called Buddhism a heterodox deviation [C], which I recall he read as a Kshatriya reaction against Hindu authority [M]. After Coomaraswamy, Pallis and Schuon pressed him he authorised amendments, and in 1946 he reviewed Hinduism and Buddhism as important [C]. Pallis (A Buddhist Spectrum, 1980) defends rebirth without a fixed self and the bardo [M]. Huxley equates Dharmakaya and suchness with the Ground [M].

Who cites it, Theosophists. This is Blavatsky's Buddhism, and it is a different thing. Olcott's Buddhist Catechism (1881) and the Fourteen Propositions (accepted 1891; who signed is U) are the one comparison shown to the people compared [M]. Sinnett's Esoteric Buddhism (1883) gave sevenfold man, kama-loca, devachan and the rounds [M]. The Key (1889) says the Society is not Buddhist and that no-self denies the personal soul and not the higher individuality [M]. The Voice offers a Heart Doctrine against an Eye Doctrine, a "Book of the Golden Precepts" that nobody has found, the Two Paths and the Seven Portals, which are the six paramitas with viraga added (the standard Mahayana list is six, some sources have ten, and the Pali list is ten paramis) [M]. She equates devachan with bde-ba-can, the Tibetan rendering of Sukhavati, which is a Pure Land to practise in and not a rest between lives [M]. Paranirvana reads Hindu pralaya into a Buddhist word, and her Nirmanakaya is an adept who stays, where the Buddhist nirmanakaya is a Buddha's visible emanation body [M]. The pratyekabuddha called "selfish" is a Mahayana polemical figure that she follows; the Pali paccekabuddha is a silent buddha who awakens alone [M, 80 percent].

Texts. Udana 6.4 (the blind men and the elephant, whose point is against clinging to views, with the Buddha as the one who sees) and 8.3 (the unborn, unmade, unconditioned); SN 22.59 and 22.89; SN 12.48; AN 6.63; MN 22 (the raft; calls one version of "the self is the world, after death I shall be permanent" a fool's teaching, which Gombrich reads as an answer to Upanishadic identification), MN 118 and the Sallatha Sutta (SN 36.6); DN 16; the Milindapanha; the Awakening of Faith (One Mind, ignorance as wind on water); the Heart Sutra; Shantideva 3.25, 8.90 to 91, 8.120 and 10.55 (numbering differs by translation); Dogen; the ox-herding pictures; Mumonkan 2; the Bardo Thodol [M].

Terms they equate. Every "One" in Buddhism is a comparer's equation: Dharmakaya, suchness, the Void, the Unconditioned, One Mind, Buddha-nature. Critical Buddhism calls Buddha-nature thought non-Buddhist, Nagarjuna denies any intrinsic nature including a One, and "not two" is not "one" [M]. In SN 12.48 the Buddha sets "all is a unity" among four cosmologies and teaches the middle. The sutta's own word is lokayata; "extreme" (anta) is the word in the paired SN 12.15; and "all" may mean everything that exists, so write "treats as an extreme" and not "refutes oneness" [M/U]. Veil: avidya, the wind that stirs the waves. The first fetter is sakkaya-ditthi, "identity view" or "personality view", and matching it to the veil is the essay's reading and not the sutta's wording. Return: original awakening, the bodhisattva who stays, the tenth ox picture. Lives: rebirth with no fixed soul, the flame; Theravada rejected a state between lives and the Sarvastivada accepted one [M; Kathavatthu VIII.2 U]. Karma: intention; SN 36.21 says not all suffering is karma [M]; Theosophy reads the skandhas as karmic germs. Letting go is the strongest match with Part one: SN 22.59, MN 22 and 118, Dogen, with the Zen fox as the tradition's own warning against cold release [M].

Where the two movements differ. Both read anatta as denying only the small self. The Traditionalists then say the Buddha means the Self of Vedanta, while Theosophy says a higher individuality reincarnates [M]. Guenon's revision of his early verdict is the clearest case of a comparer correcting himself. Max Muller ("Esoteric Buddhism", The Nineteenth Century, 1893 [C]) and Rhys Davids [U] criticised Blavatsky's Buddhism on its own ground, and the standard reply is DN 16, where the Buddha tells Ananda he has no closed fist of a teacher (acariyamutthi) [M]. Tantric and Mahayana traditions do have restricted teachings, so the reply holds for the Pali canon's own claim and not for every school [M]. Dharmapala, an Asian recruit, left Theosophy over its Masters and its mixing [M, date U].

Best citations. Against the secret-doctrine claim, DN 16 (numbering differs by translator; Walshe 2.25 is U; the Pali dipa means both island and lamp, so name the translation). For the unconditioned, Udana 8.3. For the Traditionalist reading, a line of Coomaraswamy quoted in a mailing-list post (lower trust, source book U, do not use) [C].

Specialist objections, as additions. Rahula says some writers read the idea of atta into the Buddha's teaching and names Coomaraswamy and Horner [C, target text U]. Collins, Gombrich and the Pali mainstream read early Buddhism as denying any abiding self. Theravada has no ground. Nattier argues the Heart Sutra Sanskrit is a back-translation from Xuanzang's Chinese of 649 [M]. Lopez and Prothero show Theosophical Buddhism as a Victorian composite [M]. Write "Schuon equates" and "she reads", never "Buddhism teaches".

Added by the topic files. The technical theories (Yogacara's three natures, Candrakirti's two conventionals, the Khemaka simile) are in section 5.2. Admirable friendship (kalyana-mitta, SN 45.2) backs "people further along" in the Buddha's own words; DN 16 shows self-reliance held beside it ("be islands") [M]. Dhammapada 98: wherever arahants live, that place is delightful, the best Buddhist witness for "a room settles" [M]. Shinran (Tannisho 3 and 13) puts letting go as dropping one's own calculation, with karma and grace together; his self-name, the foolish bald one, matches Alex joining himself into the mistake [M]. Wonhyo (617 to 686) and Zongmi (780 to 841) are the closest East Asian statement of the three clauses, and Zongmi ranks Confucian, Daoist and Buddhist accounts, putting the One Mind on top [M]. The Satipatthana pair "contracted and scattered" (sankhitta, vikkhitta): Buddhaghosa glosses contracted as sloth and torpor, so it is a dull mind and not Alex's tense one [M, 75 percent].

### 6.3 Jainism

Who cites it. The files hold no Traditionalist book and no Theosophical text that uses Jainism as a witness. That is a finding. Those who do use it are anthologists, ethic-comparers and stage-map writers: Wilson's World Scripture, the 1993 Global Ethic endnote (Sutrakritanga I.11.33) and Jaini (1979) on the gunasthanas [M]. The draft uses it for the veil, staged growth, non-harm and samayika.

Texts. Tattvartha Sutra 5.21 (parasparopagraho jivanam, souls support one another), chapter 8 (bondage by karma), 10.5 to 8 (the freed soul rises to the edge of the cosmos like a flame or a gourd freed of clay; numbering differs by recension [U]); Acharanga 1.4.1 and 1.5.5; Sutrakritanga 1.11.33 and 1.1.1; the fourteen gunasthanas, where a soul at stage 11 can fall back; anekantavada (Siddhasena, Haribhadra, Mallisena) [M]. Haribhadra's verse about no bias for Mahavira and no hatred for Kapila is Lokatattvanirnaya 1.38 in recall, and not the Shaddarshanasamuccaya [U].

Terms. Veil: karma is fine matter bound by the passions. Eight kinds of karma: knowledge-obscuring, perception-obscuring, feeling, deluding, lifespan, name, family or status, and obstructive. The four destructive (ghatiya) karmas are knowledge-obscuring, perception-obscuring, deluding and obstructive, and they damage the soul's own qualities. Only the first veils knowledge, so "karma veils the soul's knowledge" is correct for one kind and not for all [M]. One: none. Souls are many, eternal and uncreated, and Jain maya means deceit and not cosmic illusion [M]. Return: the siddha, still one of many. Letting go: samayika, kayotsarga, the vow of non-possession (possession is attachment, Tattvartha 7.17 in the Digambara numbering and 7.12 in the Svetambara), and the verse of forgiveness [M].

Relation to the others and the two movements. Anekanta gave the elephant an Indian philosophical frame [M]. Jains also hold that only their path liberates, and Shankara's commentary attacks the doctrine [M]. Neither movement uses Jainism, so nothing separates them here. The one plain statement is that Jainism agrees on the practice and objects to the One, and the Sutrakritanga refutes a single soul and says some souls are never liberated [M at best]. Jain thinkers who told the elephant story: no Jain text could be named [U]. Rest the sentence on anekantavada.

Best citations. Tattvartha Sutra 5.21 [M]. The Acharanga line about the one you intend to strike has two renderings: one modern commentary (Mahaprajna) reads the oneness of killer and killed, the older commentary reads "like you", and which English Jacobi's translation (SBE 22, 1884) gives is not known to the files [U]. Nothing in the files supports the draft's claim that Jacobi "puts it more carefully". Do not write "none other than you" until a named translation is read.

### 6.4 Judaism: Kabbalah and Hasidism

Who cites it. Schaya (L'homme et l'absolu selon la Kabbale, 1958; English 1971) equates Ein Sof with the Absolute of other traditions, Adam Kadmon with Universal Man and Purusha, and the Shekhinah with the Sufi sakina [M as the school's trope, U as Schaya's text]. Guenon calls Kabbalah genuine esoterism but wrote little on it [C]. Schuon gives Judaism little space [U]. The Theosophists equate Ain Soph with Parabrahm and the upper sephiroth with the Trimurti and the Trinity [M], through Knorr von Rosenroth, Franck and Eliphas Levi. They cite gilgul as a witness for rebirth. Blavatsky also treats Jehovah as a lower power, which breaks Alex's rule against saying a tradition fits badly [M gist].

Texts and figures. The Zohar line that the Torah, the Holy One and Israel are one (III 73a; wording U; main text or a later layer U; Tishby reads it as about the Jewish people, so keep the word "Israel"). Luria's three moves: tzimtzum, the breaking of the vessels, tikkun [M]. Adam ha-Rishon contains all souls. Gilgul in the Bahir, the Zohar and Vital; Saadia Gaon (933) against [M]. Cordovero's panentheism [U]. Shneur Zalman's Tanya (1796): Part 1 ch. 32 grounds love of a fellow Jew in one root of all souls; Part 2, the Gate of Unity and Faith, says that from the higher view (yichuda ila'ah) the world has no independent existence (ein od milvado) and from the lower (yichuda tata'ah) creatures exist [M]. The Maggid of Mezritch on ayin and bittul. Nachman, Likutei Moharan II:48: the world a very narrow bridge, and the main thing is not to be afraid at all [M strongly, paraphrase]; II:24 constant joy [U locators]. Abulafia's untying of knots [U]. Hillel at Shabbat 31a; Tamid 32a; the rabbinic argument over Leviticus 19:18 and Genesis 5:1 (Sifra Kedoshim 4:12; Genesis Rabbah 24:7) [M, locators medium]; Sanhedrin 4:5; Shevuot 39a [M].

Terms they equate. One: Ein Sof, an equation Scholem and Idel resist, since Ein Sof stands in a dynamic, even theurgic, relation to the sefirot. Veil and separation: tzimtzum and the broken vessels, which in Judaism is a real rupture to repair and not an illusion. Return: teshuvah, tikkun, devekut (Scholem held devekut is attachment, Idel holds it can be union). Lives: gilgul. Karma: measure for measure. Letting go: bittul, hishtavut, Tamid 32a [M].

The Baal Shem Tov. He wrote no book. His teaching comes through disciples (Toldot Yaakov Yosef, 1780; Maggid Devarav LeYaakov, 1781; Degel Machaneh Ephraim) and through compilations that mix early and late material (Keter Shem Tov, Tzava'at HaRibash) and the hagiography Shivhei HaBesht (1814 to 1815). Rosman (Founder of Hasidism, 1996) treats the compilations as late; Etkes (The Besht, 2005) is more generous [M; printing dates U]. Write "teachings credited to the Baal Shem Tov in the Hasidic books". The hiding of the face (Deuteronomy 31:18, haster astir, a doubled verb): the Hasidic reading hears a hiding within a hiding, so that feeling God is hidden is itself the concealment, since no place lacks Him; it is a form of presence and a call to seek, and the files could not place "a test of how we respond" or "only apparent" [M gist, locator U]. Psalm 16:8: the Hebrew shiviti ("I have set") is heard as shaveh ("equal"), so equanimity, praise and insult weighing the same, is how God stays before you [M pun and attribution, U locator].

Where the movements differ. Schuon reads Kabbalah as the inner side of the Law. The unity ethic is mostly about Jewish souls. Theosophy reads Kabbalah as part of its Wisdom-Religion and uses it for rebirth. The Traditionalists read it through the exoteric and esoteric grid, and whether Schaya treats gilgul is unknown [U].

Best citations. Luria's story, because it carries all three clauses of the axiom in one tradition's words (withdrawal for a world, shattering, repair) [M]. For the fingers, Akiva against Ben Azzai, the oldest version of the split between reciprocity and common descent, with Rabbi Tanhuma's one-line identity argument [M, medium]. Nachman's bridge [M strongly].

Specialist objections, as additions. Scholem and Idel treat Kabbalah as a historical development and do not use the grid [M]. Habad's own teaching distinguishes a lower and a higher unity and does not deny the world, so do not write "Chabad says the world does not exist". Say the Tanya is close to acosmism in one reading (Scholem's word, from the Spinoza dispute), that Elior reads a deliberate dual perspective (nullified in thought, real in practice, so the commandments bind), and add "for us it is real and the commandments bind". Habad insiders (Schochet 1988) reject "acosmism" as denial of the world; the Mitnagdim (Chaim of Volozhin) feared pantheism yet use two-level language [M].

### 6.5 Christianity: Eastern Fathers, Eckhart, the mystics

Who cites it. Coomaraswamy treats Eckhart as the clearest Western voice for the doctrine of the Self and pairs Mark 8:34 and John 12:25 with the Vedic sacrifice [C titles, M contents]. The formula Shankara : Brahman : Ishvara : Atman as Eckhart : Godhead : God : Soul is Otto's (West-oestliche Mystik, 1926; English 1932), not Coomaraswamy's [C]. Schuon makes God and Godhead the Christian form of saguna and nirguna [M, mapping U]. Shah-Kazemi sets Shankara, Ibn Arabi and Eckhart side by side [M]. Guenon held that the sacraments had sunk to the exoteric level; Schuon rejected that, and by one summary it was a central point of their split of 1948 to 1950 [C]. Huxley uses Eckhart as the Western hinge, in a line that may come through Pfeiffer's 1857 edition, which mixes authentic and doubtful sermons (the translation may be Evans 1924 or Blakney 1941 [U]) [M]. Theosophists: Besant's Esoteric Christianity (1901) reads the gospel life as five initiations and the Atonement as the Logos limiting himself, after Kingsford and Maitland's The Perfect Way [M]. Mead's Fragments of a Faith Forgotten (1900) argues the Gnostics were the thinking half of early Christianity [M]. Judge and Besant cite Origen, Matthew 11:14 with 17:10 to 13, and the Council of 553 [M].

Texts and figures. 1 Corinthians 15:28 and 12:12 to 27; Galatians 6:7 and 2:20; Ephesians 4:18; Philippians 2:5 to 8; Acts 9:4; John 17:21 and 3:3; Luke 15:17; Mark 8:34. Luke 17:21 can be "within you" or "among you", so it cannot carry the inner-Self reading alone [M]. Athanasius (De Incarnatione 54), Gregory of Nyssa's epektasis, Maximus, Evagrius on apatheia, Eriugena's Periphyseon, the Jesus Prayer [M]. Eckhart: the Godhead beyond God; Sermon 52, "Beati pauperes spiritu" (Blakney's "rid me of God"; Walshe and McGinn "free of God"); the eye saying, in "Qui audit me" (Quint 12 [M sermon, U number]); the uncreated something; On Detachment; Sermon 86 (Martha the mature one) [M]. Sermon numbers differ by Quint, Walshe, McGinn and Pfeiffer, so cite by opening words, translator and page. Theologia Germanica, Boehme, John of the Cross, Julian of Norwich (the soul's substance made of God and never apart from Him, Long Text about chs. 53 to 56 [U]), the Cloud of Unknowing. Terms they equate. One: the Godhead (Gottheit), the ground of the soul, Eriugena's nature that neither creates nor is created. Veil: the Fall as self-will; ignorance in Ephesians 4:18. Return: apokatastasis, theosis, the birth of God in the soul, and after the birth the breakthrough into the Godhead (Sermon 52). Lives: here the tradition says no. It has purgatory, Origen's pre-existence and final restoration (Origen argued against transmigration) and Gregory's never-ending epektasis. Karma: Galatians 6:7 is accountability under God with no rebirth in it. Letting go: kenosis, Gelassenheit and Abgeschiedenheit, apatheia [M].

Relation to the others. The Church policed the strongest language: Eriugena (1225), Porete (1310), Eckhart (the bull In agro dominico, 27 March 1329, after his death) [M]. Eckhart's own record: an inquiry opened at Cologne in 1326 by Archbishop Henry II of Virneburg on the complaint of two Dominicans; a written reply; a public protestation (13 February 1327); an appeal to the pope; Avignon; death before 30 April 1328, the date of a papal letter that speaks of him as dead [M]. The bull condemns 28 propositions, 17 as heretical and 11 as ill-sounding, rash and suspect of heresy; among them the world created from eternity, God's glory shining equally in every work, the bread-into-Christ's-body line (about 16, milder class), God neither good, better nor best (26), something in the soul uncreated and uncreatable (27, heretical class), and God one in every way (28, milder class) [M sayings, U numbers and class grouping]. The eye line cannot be placed on any Cologne list or among the 28 [U], so the draft's "raised against him in Cologne in 1326" has no support. Fourth Lateran canon 2 (1215) says any likeness between Creator and creature hides a greater unlikeness. Orthodoxy keeps participation and not identity (Palamas) [M].

Where the two movements differ. Guenon called Besant's version pseudo-Christianity or neo-Christianity [C]. The claim that the Council of 553 condemned reincarnation is Theosophical, and historians reject it: the anathemas concern pre-existence and the restoration of all [M]. Do not cite Jerome's Letter 130 as approval, since the anthologists seem to print a hostile passage as support [U].

Best citations. 1 Corinthians 15:28 [M]. Eckhart's eye saying is genuine [M]; the better-attested condemned proposition is the uncreated something in the soul [M]. Quote Eckhart only from a critical edition (Quint; Walshe; Colledge and McGinn, which prints the bull), because of the Pfeiffer problem. If Hegel quotes the eye saying in the Berlin lectures [U, 65 percent], the line was in print by 1832 (Marheineke's edition), a generation before Pfeiffer, and is not a creation of the anthologists.

Specialist objections, as additions. McGinn and Turner read the Godhead as the Trinity's ground and union as by grace. Flasch reads Eckhart inside Dominican and Neoplatonic theology. Schuon's claim that the Intellect is uncreated is exactly the point the 1329 bull condemned (the files do not show which Eckhart line a given Traditionalist relies on) [M/U]. Strength and weak point: this tradition is strongest on return, the one body and letting go, and weakest on a whole that experiences separation as its own self-experience; every inner voice keeps union by participation and not identity [part-2-notes, M].

Added by the topic files. Teresa's seventh mansion: the fruit is works (VII.4), and the Lord walks among the pots and pans (Foundations ch. 5 [M]). Eckhart, Sermon 86: Martha, who acts, is the mature one. The Catechism (1013, no reincarnation after death; 1030 to 1031, purgatory) [M]. Boehme, Schelling, Hegel, Solovyov, Bulgakov and the rest of the German and Russian line are in section 5.3.

### 6.6 Islam: Sufism, Ibn Arabi, Ghazali, Mulla Sadra

Who cites it. Guenon lived as a Muslim in Cairo from 1930 to 1951 and took the name Abd al-Wahid Yahya [C, initiation date U]. He finds Universal Man (al-Insan al-Kamil) under other names: Purusha, Adam Kadmon, the Chinese Wang [C]. Schuon wrote Understanding Islam (French 1961, English 1963), reads the Shahada as discernment followed by concentration, and uses sharia, tariqa and haqiqa as the template for every tradition [M]. Burckhardt (Introduction to Sufi Doctrine), Lings (The Book of Certainty, 1952), Nasr and Shah-Kazemi give the Sufi doctrine of the Essence, the Names and self-disclosure [M]. Dara Shikoh is an early systematic comparer [C]. The Theosophists use Islam little: the files show only Rumi's mineral-to-man lines in rebirth anthologies and the "shells" of kama-loka [U/M].

Texts and figures. Quran 2:115, 2:156, 7:172, 57:3 to 4, 59:19, 89:27 to 30, 5:48, 17:7, 99:7 to 8, 4:1, 23:99 to 100, 28:88, 50:16. Hadith: voluntary devotion (Bukhari 6502; single chain, one narrator questioned [U]), the believers as one body (Bukhari 6011, Muslim 2586), loving for the brother what one loves for oneself (Bukhari 13, Muslim 45), the perfume seller (Bukhari 2101, Muslim 2628 [numbers U]). Numbering differs by edition; name it. Two Sufi sayings are not accepted by hadith scholars: the hidden treasure (Ibn Arabi calls it sound by unveiling) and "he who knows himself knows his Lord"; "die before you die" is also cited as a hadith and is not accepted [M/U]. Ibn Arabi: "He and not He", the Names needing someone to be named to, the Adam chapter's mirror, creation renewed at each instant so that no self-disclosure repeats, and Tarjuman al-Ashwaq ode 11 (my heart has become capable of every form; the religion of Love), in Nicholson's public-domain translation of 1911 [M; verse numbers U]. Ghazali's Mishkat al-Anwar (three classes of the veiled; each thing has a face to itself where it is nothing and a face to its Lord where it exists; the authenticity of its last section is disputed by Watt, 1949, and defended by Landolt and Treiger [M/U]) and Ihya Book 35 (four levels of tawhid; trust includes means). Mulla Sadra: substantial motion and the Four Journeys. Qushayri: hal against maqam. Rumi, Attar, fana and baqa [M].

Terms they equate. One: al-Haqq, the Essence (Dhat). The phrase wahdat al-wujud is a later label from Ibn Arabi's school and his critics and is not clearly his own, so say "the teaching later called wahdat al-wujud"; Sirhindi's wahdat al-shuhud puts the oneness in the seer's experience (formula perhaps Simnani's [U]) and is closer to "the separation ends in experience". Veil: hijab, tajalli, ghafla (forgetting). Return: fana, baqa, "to Him we return" (89:27 to 30). Lives: mainstream theology rejects tanasukh; Ibn Arabi's non-repetition and Mulla Sadra's growth after death keep the growth and drop the return. Karma: 99:7 to 8 and 17:7, consequence with no rebirth. Letting go: islam as surrender, fana, dhikr as the Name practice beside japa, the Jesus Prayer and the nembutsu [M].

Relation to the others. Schuon makes Islam the model of the outer and inner split. Guenon reads Sufism as the heart of Islam. Quran 5:48 and 49:13 are the verses pluralist readers use. 5:48 says each community has its own law and way and is to compete in good works; classical commentators read these verses inside the Abrahamic frame [M]. Quran 5:32 is framed as a decree for the Children of Israel, so it passes on a Jewish teaching (Sanhedrin 4:5) [M].

Where the movements differ. The Traditionalists are largely Muslim and hold the mainstream Islamic position of one earthly life, barzakh and resurrection, so they reject Western reincarnation [M position, U what each says in print]. Theosophy teaches rebirth as the rule. Mulla Sadra is the Muslim case of growth without return, which fits neither.

Best citations. Ibn Arabi, Tarjuman ode 11 [M that it exists, U on verse numbers]. Quran 2:115 and the one-body hadith. The Adam chapter of the Fusus for the motive step [M gist, U wording].

Specialist objections, as additions. Classical commentators read the nearness verses (2:115, 57:4) as nearness of knowledge and power, and read wajh as direction (Tabari, Razi); Ash'ari and Salafi commentators the same for 57:4. Ibn Taymiyya and the Salafi line reject wahdat al-wujud, and Sirhindi argued for unity of witness only. Ibn Arabi held Muhammad's religion complete, so "the religion of Love" is not plain relativism (Chittick). Zaehner argued that Bistami's fana was monistic and Indian-influenced and that Junayd moved to theistic baqa, which Ernst contests [M/U]. Zamakhshari read 7:172 figuratively, as natural disposition (fitra) [U]. Quran 17:7's last clause reads "to yourselves" in Arberry, but many commentators read the preposition as "against", and Yusuf Ali adds "against yourselves" [U]. The Quran refuses a return to this world (23:99 to 100). Mulla Sadra: say "grows by its own character" and not "keeps developing after death", since whether the perfected soul still develops is [U].

### 6.7 Daoism and the Chinese one-body line

Who cites it. Guenon treats Taoism as the inner side of Chinese tradition with Confucianism outside (Insights into Islamic Esoterism and Taoism, posthumous) [C]. Schuon sets the nameless Tao beside the Absolute [U as his pairing]. Smith puts the Tao at the Infinite level, and Huxley's chapter 2 equates it with Atman and the Ground [M]. Izutsu (Sufism and Taoism, 1966 and 1967) compares structures without claiming identity [M]. No Theosophical treatment of Daoism turned up in the files.

Texts. Tao Te Ching 1, 16, 19, 25, 40, 42, 48, 55, 63, 76. Zhuangzi 1; 2 (the butterfly, heaven and earth born with me, the great awakening and the great dream); 3 (Cook Ding; fuel and fire); 4 (the fasting of the mind); 5 (Wang Tai, whom people leave full without a lesson); 6 (the four friends, the Great Clod, sitting and forgetting); 7 (the mirror; Hundun, who dies when seven openings are drilled); 22. For the Chinese one-body line: Zhang Zai's Western Inscription, Cheng Hao, and Wang Yangming's Inquiry on the Great Learning (1527), with the child at the well from Mencius 2A6 [M].

Terms. One: the Tao; Tao Te Ching 42 has the Way give birth to one, then two, and onward, so the Way is prior to the One and Dao is not "the One". Veil and separation: Hundun, differentiation as a cost. Return: return to the root (16). Letting go: wu-wei, decrease daily (48), forgetting. Slingerland (2003) names the paradox of trying to be effortless. Lives and karma: nothing in early Daoism; the Taiping jing teaches inherited burden (chengfu), the Ganying pian audited retribution and a golden rule (treat another's gain and loss as one's own), and the Lingbao scriptures took rebirth and karma from Buddhism [M, locators U]. The Daoist continuity is qi gathered and dispersed, so no individual persists. Neidan (inner alchemy) reverses the cosmogony: essence to qi, qi to spirit, spirit to emptiness, emptiness to the Way; the four-step formula is a later codification [M].

Relation to the others. The Chinese "three teachings are one" formulas were often rhetoric or joint worship (Brook) [M]. Third and fourth century Chinese Buddhists explained nirvana with Daoist words (geyi), which is Alex's method with its cost shown; Sengzhao's rewording of Zhuangzi into "same root" and "one body" is Sengzhao's and not Zhuangzi's [M; Zhao lun and case 40 of the Blue Cliff Record are pointers, U]. The Huahu theory (Laozi went west and became the Buddha) circulated by about 300 [M].

Best citations. Zhuangzi 2 and 6; Wang Yangming and the child at the well, the best non-Western parallel to two fingers of one hand, though Confucian specialists say one body means graded love, close people first [M]. Graham, Roth and Ames caution against reading the Tao as a metaphysical Ground; Graham and Hansen read Zhuangzi 2 as perspectival scepticism [M].

Trap. A line that circulates as Laozi's about letting go of what you are and becoming what you might be is not in the Daodejing in any translation known to the files. Do not print it as Laozi's [M]. Mitchell's Tao Te Ching (1988) is not made from the Chinese by his own account [M].

### 6.8 Greek and Platonic

Who cites it. Coomaraswamy sets anamnesis beside smrti ("Recollection, Indian and Platonic", JAOS Supplement 3, 1944 [C title, M content]) and the Katha chariot beside the Phaedrus chariot [M]. Schuon reads the Good beyond being (Republic 509b) as Beyond-Being, anamnesis as the Intellect remembering, and Plotinus's One as the Absolute [M]. The Renaissance comparers started here: Plethon's chain from Zoroaster to Plotinus, Ficino's prisca theologia, Pico's concordia, Steuco [C]. The Theosophists claim descent through Ammonius Saccas and his "Eclectic Theosophical School" (the Key), a picture historians call later (an eighteenth-century historians' label that Theosophists took up), and cite Plato for rebirth [M].

Texts. Republic VII (the cave, 514a to 520e, with the compelled return and the turning round of the whole soul, periagoge, 518c to d), 509b, and X (the Myth of Er, 614 to 621, esp. 617e: blame lies with the chooser). Phaedo 62b (phroura, prison or guard post), 64a, 67c to e, 70 to 77. Phaedrus 245 to 256. Meno 81 to 86. Timaeus 27d to 29c, 41e to 42d. Laws 903 to 905 (the whole caring for the whole). Sophist 240a to b. Plotinus: Enneads IV.9, IV.8.8 (our soul does not descend entire), V.1.1, I.6.9, VI.9.11. Proclus on remaining, procession and reversion. Pythagoras and the beaten dog (Xenophanes DK 21 B7); Empedocles DK 31 B117; Anaximander DK 12 B1; Marcus Aurelius 2.1, 7.13, 8.34, 11.8 (the severed limb) [M, Marcus numbers moderate].

Terms they equate. One: the One, the Good. Veil: the cave, Lethe, tolma. Return: the turning round of the soul, epistrophe. Lives: the Myth of Er and the Phaedrus; Plotinus accepted transmigration; Porphyry and Iamblichus are reported to have rejected animal rebirth, a secondhand report [M]. Karma: Republic 617e, Laws 903, Anaximander. Letting go: the sculptor, practice for dying [M].

Relation to the others and the two movements. Numenius's remark that Plato is Moses speaking Attic Greek is quoted by Clement (Stromata I.22.150.4) [C] and should not be given to Pico [U]. Plotinus has three real hypostases, so he is not a monist in Shankara's sense. Theosophy claims the Ammonius lineage. The Traditionalists read Plato as an Eastern-style metaphysician and compare Plotinus with Shankara. Guenon's many states, with no state repeated, runs against a Phaedrus-style return [M, inference].

Best citations. Republic 617e and Enneads IV.9. For VI.9.11 name the translator, because the English varies. Marcus 8.34 for the fingers with the return built in, since one can rejoin the whole. The Myth of Er order matters for the draft: the souls choose first and drink after (section 5.4) [M].

### 6.9 Sikhism

Who cites it. The files show neither movement using Sikh scripture. The comparers who do are the reconcilers and anthologists: Wilson's World Scripture and the stage-map writers (the five khands) [M]. Sikh scholars reject "Hindu-Muslim synthesis" as the origin, since the revelation came to Nanak [M]; Mandair (2009) says "one God" for Ik Onkar imports colonial categories [M].

Texts. The Guru Granth Sahib, compiled by Guru Arjan in 1604 with later additions about 1705 to 1706 (an earlier file's "1678" is unsupported) [M]. It includes Kabir, Ravidas, Namdev and Baba Farid by design. Ik Onkar, hukam, nadar. Japji pauri 1 (the wall of falsehood, kurr di pal, broken by walking in hukam), 2 (all within hukam; the one who understands does not speak in haumai), 4 (by karma the robe of the body, by grace the gate of liberation, p. 2), 34 to 37 (the five khands, ending in Sach Khand among continents, solar systems and universes without end) [M, line wording U]. Haumai as a chronic disease whose remedy lies within (about p. 466), haumai and the Name cannot dwell together (about p. 560), Guru Arjan listing Ram, Khuda, Gosain and Allah as names of one God (about p. 885) [U pages]. The gurmukh and manmukh. Tradition sums Nanak's way as earn by honest work, remember the Name, share (kirat karo, naam japo, vand chhako), a later summary and not one verse [M]. "Na koi Hindu na Musalman" is a janamsakhi saying and not scripture [M]. The pot and water and drop in the ocean images are attributed to Kabir (Bijak), and the Tagore and Underhill Songs of Kabir (1915) come from a Bengali collection and are no evidence for early Kabir [M].

Terms. One: Ik Onkar. Return: light merging into light. Lives: rebirth through a long cycle, the human birth rare. Karma and grace together are the clearest scriptural form of consequence with a way out that is not payment. Letting go: living by hukam; haumai against hukam is the Sikh name for the hidden separation. The gurmukh/manmukh pair maps onto Part two's two views [M].

Best citation. Japji pauri 4, the closing line (the draft already uses it) [M]. Check every line and page on the SGPC text.

### 6.10 Zoroastrianism and Manichaeism

Who cites it. The files show no Traditionalist treatment. The comparers are the Renaissance ones (Plethon's Zoroaster as oldest sage, Ficino, the Magian Oracles) [C] and Suhrawardi, who identified the wisdom of the Zoroastrian sages with Hermes, Pythagoras and Plato [C]. The Theosophists use it for symbol tables: Blavatsky sets seven Amshaspends beside seven Rishis and archangels, but the Amesha Spentas are six, or seven if Ahura Mazda is counted, so the seven is her count [M]. Besant's Four Great Religions (1897) matches Ahura Mazda and the holy immortals with the Hindu triple aspect [M, her count U].

Texts. Yasna 30 (two twin spirits, the wise choose); the bridge of the separator (Chinvat) and the daena; frashokereti (a saviour raises the dead, evil is undone); the Pahlavi Bundahishn says the fravashis were offered a choice to stay unembodied or enter the world, fight evil and be resurrected, and chose to enter [M, locator unknown]. One life then judgement; Parsi Khshnoom teaches reincarnation [U]. Dadistan-i-Dinik 94.5 appears in golden-rule lists [U]. No Zoroastrian witness for lives, karma or letting go appears in any file, and nothing separates the two movements here [gap].

Mani (216 to about 274) is the earliest deliberate comparer: a church that named Buddha, Zoroaster and Jesus as earlier apostles, built on a fall-and-gathering myth (Darkness swallowed part of Light; the Elect refine light in their bodies). The fall is an attack and not purposeful play, and matter is evil. He grew up in a Jewish-Christian baptist community (Cologne Mani Codex), travelled to India about 240 to 241 by later sources, and took Buddhist and Daoist vocabulary in Central Asia and China. A Manichaean and Arabic route carried the Buddha's life west as Barlaam and Josaphat [M]. Use him as a comparer and not a witness.

Best citation. The Amesha Spentas count, as a caution against copying her equations. Plethon's golden chain from Zoroaster to Plotinus is the earliest genealogy [C].

### 6.11 Egyptian, Hermetic and Gnostic

Who cites it. The Renaissance comparers (Ficino's Pimander, 1463, calls Hermes the first author of theology [C]), until Casaubon's redating in 1614 broke the genealogy [C]. The Theosophists lean on it hardest. Isis Unveiled (1877) calls the Hermetic philosophy the anciently universal Wisdom-Religion [M, wording U]. Blavatsky's first Masters were Egyptian in 1875 to 1878 before they were Himalayan [M, chronology U]. She equates the triads Osiris, Isis and Horus, the Trimurti and the upper sephiroth [M]. Mead's Thrice-Greatest Hermes (1906) belongs here, and his Fragments of a Faith Forgotten (1900) and The Gnostic John the Baptizer (1924) [M]. The Traditionalists use it for the inner face of the Christian West, with Hermeticism and the Grail in Guenon [C], and Burckhardt's Alchemy (1960) reads the Hermetic, Arabic, Chinese and Indian arts as one symbolic science [M].

Texts. Corpus Hermeticum I (Poimandres): Man leans down, loves his reflection in Nature and is joined to her; after death the soul climbs through seven zones, giving back a fault at each, enters the eighth, and rises to the Father, the good end being to be made god (the ninth is not in CH I) [M]. CH XIII: rebirth, with Tat shedding twelve torments, receiving ten powers and seeing himself everywhere; CH XI.20 [M, paragraph numbers U]. The inscription at Sais: Plutarch (On Isis and Osiris 9) says "robe", and the later "veil" comes from Proclus and Kant [M]. Herodotus 2.123: the Egyptians said the soul passes through creatures over three thousand years, and Egyptologists find no Egyptian text with this cycle; the Theosophical "cycle of necessity" is her phrase and not Herodotus's [M]. The Gnostic stream: Excerpta ex Theodoto 78.2 (freedom is knowing who we were, what we became, where we were, where we were thrown, where we hasten, from what we are redeemed, what birth is and what rebirth is); the Gospel of Truth (ignorance, terror, Error as a fog, waking from a nightmare); the Hymn of the Pearl (Acts of Thomas 108 to 113); the Mandaeans (a living witness, who hold their teaching exclusive); the "heavenly twin" cluster (the Pearl's robe, Mani's Twin, the Mandaean dmuta, the Zoroastrian daena, Suhrawardi's Perfect Nature) [M]. The Gospel of Thomas is not Valentinian. Valentinians sorted people into types, which the essay does not.

Relation to the others, and the two movements. Theosophy takes Egypt as origin and lineage. The Traditionalists take Hermeticism as one inner layer among others. Nock, Festugiere and Copenhaver read the Hermetica as Greek philosophical and religious writing of the early centuries CE, and Fowden stresses the Egyptian setting. None finds a secret ancient doctrine in the Theosophical sense [M]. Burkert shows the mystery cults were rites without systematic doctrine [M]. The Kybalion (1908) is not the Hermetica; the Emerald Tablet is Arabic; the Chaldean Oracles are not Zoroaster's [M].

Best citations. Corpus Hermeticum I, the seven spheres and the eighth, paired with CH XIII on rebirth, and Excerpta 78.2 as the one-sentence statement of origin, fall, goal and birth, credited to the Valentinian source (Theodotus) and not to Clement (editions: Casey 1934, Sagnard 1948 [U]). In Hermes the rebirth is a birth into seeing oneself everywhere, which may or may not be the school's stage after plus four. Mead's gloss is Theosophical and should be flagged as such [M].

## 7. How the comparers compare, and how they explain differences

Base: S3 (the methods) and S5 (the explanations), with the additions from the G files. Two comparing families run through all of it: the Traditionalists (Guenon from 1921, Coomaraswamy, Schuon from 1948, then Burckhardt, Lings, Pallis, Nasr, Perry, Smith) and the Theosophists (Blavatsky and after). They attacked each other, so neither is quoted as a team. Every explanation below is the comparer's claim and not the tradition's own [M; P01, P13].

### 7.1 Ten methods of comparison

For each: who uses it, one worked case, the strength, the weakness, and what Alex can borrow.

1. The thematic anthology. Huxley (1945, chapters by theme), Perry (1971, far larger, sorted by level), Stace (1960), Wilson (1991), Needleman (1974), Eliade (1967); on the Theosophical side Walker (1888) and Head and Cranston (1961, 1977) on rebirth [M]. A worked row, rebuilt from the files: the heading is "the other is oneself", with Isha 6 and 7, Gita 6.29, Leviticus 19:18, Matthew 22:39, Shantideva (Bodhicaryavatara 8) and the hadith of the believers as one body. What the row hides: Leviticus and Matthew ground love in a shared status under God, Shantideva in the lack of a fixed self and the ownerlessness of suffering, and the Isha in identity [M; Shantideva's verse numbers U]. Strength: fast and readable, and it shows wording set side by side across many traditions. Weakness: the headings belong to the anthologist, each excerpt was picked for fit, context is cut, and the translation often carries the equation already. Textual scholars add that an Eckhart line read alone sounds like Advaita and, read in its Dominican sermon, belongs to a Christian theology of the soul. Schuon rejects the arithmetic of a common factor, since the unity lies above the forms. Huxley's other move, that knowledge is a function of the knower's being, makes the claim hard to refute [M]. Borrow: Alex already does this in his table. Say the headings are his, take each passage from a named primary translation and not through an anthology, add the "what the row hides" line wherever the grounds differ, and state his selection rule (mystics, closely read scripture or different vocabulary, and say which).

2. The parallel-passage method. Coomaraswamy, Otto and Shah-Kazemi; Guenon states the doctrine first in Sanskrit terms and reads the rest as saying it in other words [M]. Worked case: Coomaraswamy's 1944 paper, which opens on Shankara (Brahma Sutra Bhashya 1.1.5) and follows one sentence or symbol through Sanskrit, Greek, Latin and German with a short comment [C for the wording, M for the locator; what else the paper cites is U]. Otto sets Eckhart beside Shankara term by term, and also records differences such as a dynamic Godhead against a static Absolute [C for the formula, U for the differences]. Strength: a reader can open the line and check it, and it matches Part two's habit of one consequence at a time. Weakness: one voice for three thousand years, tilted to Advaita; his reading of Buddhism moves between 1916 and 1943; Rahula names him; Theravada commentary reads brahma-bhuta as "become holy"; his school refuses syncretism and would not accept Alex's witness method [C/M]. Borrow: quote one line at a time from a named translation; put the comparer in the sentence ("Otto sets Eckhart's Godhead beside Shankara's nirguna Brahman"); compare inner voice with inner voice; if Coomaraswamy answers "who carries over", state the price.

3. The glossary equation of terms. Dara Shikoh (Majma al-Bahrayn, about 1655; Sirr-i Akbar, 1657) [C]; Blavatsky's Key (1889) and the Glossary (1892, edited by Mead); Purucker's Occult Glossary (1933); Schuon's grid; Subba Row's essay on the Aryan-Arhat; Faivre calls the practice concordance and a frequent but secondary mark of Western esotericism [M]. Working case: Dara sets the five elements against the Hindu panch bhut, Brahma, Vishnu and Mahesh against the angels Jibril, Mika'il and Israfil, and calls the Upanishads the hidden book (kitab al-maknun) of the Quran [C]. Strained case: Blavatsky reads bde-ba-can as deva plus chan and equates devachan with Sukhavati; alaya as Universal Soul, Anima Mundi and Over-Soul is the same move, though alaya-vijnana is an individual stream carrying seeds [M]. Strength: it shows which words do the same job, and Subba Row gave it an honest form: the count of principles differs between schemes because each cuts the same thing at different joints [M]. Weakness: the comparer picks the match, and the equation is made before the reader sees a quotation; Dara's Vedanta came out in Sufi words (Supriya Gandhi 2020 [C gist]); specialists keep apart Ein Sof and Brahman, emptiness and Brahman, buddhi and a "spiritual soul". Borrow: keep the table and label each row as a comparer's equation. Panikkar's term for the honest version is homeomorphic equivalents, terms that play a similar role in different systems without being identical; Loy's rule is to give each sense of a shared word its own name. Write "Schuon equates nirvana with the Absolute" and keep the essay's own line that emptiness means no fixed independent nature [M].

4. Symbols and myths. Blavatsky (egg, serpent, lotus, tree, cross, "keys"), Guenon (The Symbolism of the Cross, 1931), Coomaraswamy (the sun door; Figures of Speech or Figures of Thought, 1946), Campbell (1949), Jung's amplification, Eliade, Mead on the Poimandres [M]. Worked case: the veil of Isis (Plutarch's robe; the veil is later). A second: the sun door, with Mundaka 1.2.11, John 10:9 and the clashing rocks [M; verse numbers U]. Strength: a symbol carries an idea without a doctrine, and Alex's machine is of this kind. Weakness: nearly any story can be read through a key, so the method cannot be tested; J. Z. Smith says similarity is made by the comparer, and a comparison has two things and a respect; Campbell's "return" is a return to the ordinary world and not to the One. Borrow: use images as pictures and say whose each is; check wording ("robe" and not "veil" if Plutarch is used); use the sun door as a witness for Alex's door only if he says the chart's door is not the traditional exit from the cosmos [M].

5. The exoteric and esoteric split. Guenon, Introduction (1921), chapters on esoterism and exoterism [C from a listing]; Schuon, The Transcendent Unity of Religions (French 1948, English 1953), with sharia, tariqa and haqiqa as the template [M]; Blavatsky's outer form for the many and inner meaning for the few, the Eye Doctrine and the Heart Doctrine; Besant's Esoteric Christianity (1901); Smith, with The World's Religions as the public face and Forgotten Truth as the inner [M]. Worked case: Schuon on exclusivism (the outer layer must present itself as the one truth, because that makes a form work for a whole society, and the inner layer sees the outer as a symbol); his pair of salvation and deliverance. The cost: the Church condemned an Eckhart proposition about something uncreated in the soul (27 March 1329) [M]. Strength: it gives a reason why popular teaching may not carry the idea, which is Alex's own rule of 2026-10-07 in source form. Weakness: it cannot fail, since a text that disagrees is assigned to the outer layer and the esoteric is defined as whatever agrees with the metaphysics; the Theosophical version adds priestly corruption and deliberate blinds; Schuon's grid is Advaita and Sufi theory laid over other traditions, and Scholem and Idel do not use it [M]. Borrow: credit Schuon and the Traditionalists for the rule, not only Huxley, and say it is a theory some scholars reject. Use it as a reading rule with a stop: for each quote name the layer (scripture, mystic, popular teaching) and say where the popular layer says something else. Do not rescue a disagreeing text by moving it outward. Write a specialist's objection as what the specialist adds, and not as a verdict that the tradition fits badly.

6. The primordial tradition, or comparison by descent. Plethon (1438), Ficino's prisca theologia (1463), Steuco (1540) [C]; Guenon's primordial Tradition; Blavatsky's Wisdom-Religion; Smith, whose title phrase is Guenon's [M]. Worked case: Blavatsky's Introductory calls the Secret Doctrine the universally diffused religion of the ancient and prehistoric world, with proofs said to lie in secret crypts of libraries kept by the Occult Brotherhood [M, about 80 percent on the wording, not for printing]; Steuco: one principle of all things and one knowledge of it among all peoples [C, English from summaries]. Both explain agreement as inheritance and difference as loss of keys, literal reading or priestcraft. Weakness: the historical claim failed. Casaubon redated the Hermetica in 1614 [C]; no one has found the Book of Dzyan or any Senzar [M]; Coleman (printed 1895) traced much of her text to printed books [M]; most historical comparers had a home team: Ficino, Cusa and Steuco put Christianity on top and Dara read Vedanta in Sufi words [C/M]. Borrow: do not. Say in one sentence that he claims no original revelation and no lost source. His explanation of agreement is a fourth kind (common logic) beside revelation, transmission and common experience [M]. If he names the primordial claim, say that Smith's phrase is Guenon's. Add that he has no team.

7. Stage maps. Underhill, Mysticism (1911); Teresa's seven mansions; Climacus's thirty steps; the Jain fourteen gunasthanas; Attar's valleys; Kukai's ten stages; Besant and Leadbeater's initiations; Smith's four levels; Wilber [M]. Working case: dharma-megha, the tenth bhumi in Buddhism and the last samadhi in Yoga Sutra IV.29, the cleanest term equation in the files [M]. Strained case: Besant and Leadbeater map the four Buddhist stages (sotapanna, sakadagami, anagami, arhat) onto the first four of five initiations with Christian labels (Birth, Baptism, Transfiguration, Crucifixion, Resurrection) [M, Hindu labels U]; in the Pali an arahant already is an asekha, so their split of the two is an addition; their "Birth" is the first initiation and not Alex's stage after plus four. Strength: a shared shape of distance words (mansions, valleys, heavens), and the draft's line that other traditions draw staged maps is safe. Weakness: rungs run from four to a hundred; sevens may come from the planets, but that fits Hermes, Mithras and Dante and not Teresa, the Jain fourteen or the Buddhist ten; Katz would say the maps match the writers' vocabulary and Ferrer that one ladder assumes one summit; Krishnamurti dissolved the Order of the Star at Ommen on 3 August 1929; the strict Traditionalists read history as decline, and Smith's levels are worlds [M]. Borrow: say the number of rungs varies, and map by function and not by count. Claim no one-for-one match with Underhill or Teresa. Say the arrow is for a person and makes no claim that the world improves, which answers the strict school.

8. Each religion as a perspective on one Reality. Schuon (Absolute and Infinite; Beyond-Being, Being, Existence), Guenon (Universal Possibility), Smith (four levels), Hick (the Real in itself, met through personal and impersonal faces), Schmidt-Leukel [M]. Worked case: Schuon fixes the level before equating (Nirguna Brahman, Dhat and Eckhart's Gottheit at Beyond-Being; Ishvara, Allah as Lord and God the Father at Being; individual pairs U, and "Allah" is also used for the Essence). The Shahada read as discernment and then concentration is set beside neti neti and then identity with Atma. Why the forms differ: the Infinite includes every possibility, receptivity differs, and a form that stressed its own relativity would no longer save anyone [M, a reconstruction and not Schuon's numbered list]. Strength: it explains "sometimes different gods" without calling the gods the same, and it offers an argument for why a whole would separate: an infinite that excluded the finite would be limited. Weakness: the grid is circular and tilted to Advaita and Sufism; Buddhist specialists keep nirvana and the Absolute apart; Scholem and Idel, and Orthodox and Catholic theologians, keep a line between Source and many that the comparers erase; Plantinga and D'Costa say Hick's pluralism is itself a doctrine; the Traditionalists rest the claim on doctrine and the Intellect and not on experience, so Katz's attack mostly misses them [M]. Borrow: one sentence, "one comparer reads the personal God and the impersonal Absolute as two degrees of one thing, and Buddhists and many theologians would not accept the equation", and "the Infinite includes the finite" as an argument credited to the movement and labelled an argument and not a proof.

9. The claimed esoteric revelation. The Theosophical Society (1875): the Masters; the Mahatma Letters (about 1880 to 1884, published 1923 by Barker; Hao Chin 1993); the Book of Dzyan and its Stanzas; Leadbeater's clairvoyant reports; Bailey writing for "the Tibetan" [M]. The three propositions of the Proem are given as what she was taught, and not as logic. The authority rule: where a text and a Master disagree, the Master wins. Her Preface calls the book a nosegay with only the string her own, but the line is Montaigne's (Essais III.12), which she quotes [M; "string" or "thread" in her printing U]. Strength: it answers why the traditions agree, it was an early, bold act of respect for Asian texts, and Gandhi's Autobiography says the Key sent him back to his own scriptures [M gist, chapter U]. Weakness: it cannot be checked. The SPR's Hodgson report (1885) judged her phenomena fraudulent, and Harrison (Journal of the SPR 53, 1986, pp. 286 to 310) challenged it in the SPR's own journal; whether the SPR ever withdrew the report is [U]. Muller wrote against "Esoteric Buddhism" (1893). The stock reply is the Buddha's closed fist of a teacher (DN 16). Isis Unveiled (1877) did not teach reincarnation as the rule. Her root-race scheme (SD vol. II) is widely criticised as a racial hierarchy [M]. Borrow: not the method. Borrow the stance of gathering what lies scattered and saying what is one's own. Olcott's Fourteen Fundamental Buddhist Beliefs (accepted 1891; drafted earlier, 1884 [U]; signatories U) is the one Theosophical comparison shown to the people compared. The contrast sentence is ready: her reason for agreement is a lost original and hidden teachers, and his is an axiom and the reader's own check. Hammer names three strategies in this literature (appeal to tradition, to science, to experience), and Alex's is none of them [M]. Scholars treat Blavatsky as a major figure of modern Western esotericism with mixed credibility (Hanegraaff 1996; Godwin 1994; Washington 1993; Lachman 2012) and Campbell (1980) is the standard history and not an argument for authenticity. She is best used as a witness to the idea of unity in nineteenth-century English and not as an authority on any tradition (G1-2).

10. Comparison by experience. James (1902), Underhill (1911), Bucke (1901), Otto, Stace (1960); the foils Zaehner (1957), Smart (1965), Katz (1978) and Forman (1990) [M]. Worked case: Stace's two types (extrovertive and introvertive), with shared marks of objectivity, blessedness, holiness, paradoxicality and ineffability, and interpretation added afterwards explaining the differences. James gives four marks (ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, passivity), the "eternal unanimity" passage in Lectures XVI and XVII, and three conclusions: such states are authoritative for the one who has them, they give outsiders no duty to accept them, and they break the claim of the sense-bound mind to be the only kind [M]. Strength: James, Underhill and Bucke are public domain; James's second conclusion is the best authority for "not a proof"; his test by fruits supports "what you can check". Weakness: Katz says there are no unmediated experiences (p. 26 of his essay [M, recalled, keep the parenthesis]); Proudfoot and Sharf call "experience" a modern category; Zaehner says nature, monistic and theistic mysticism differ and cannot be merged; Stace's core is called circular, since cases that do not fit are called not mystical; Forman answers Katz with contentless events; the strict Traditionalists say the criterion is doctrine and intellectual intuition [M]. The lab instruments descend from Stace (Pahnke's nine categories, Hood's scale of 1975, the Mystical Experience Questionnaire of 2015), so a structure that replicates across cultures shows that people answer in parallel and not that they refer to the same thing (G1-1, G2-5). Borrow: say the claim is about texts and ideas, with "reports" and not "the same experience". A family of ideas survives Katz at the level of structure, which is all Alex claims [M]. Use James's limit on outsiders as his permission.

- Dialogue and negotiation: Cusa, De pace fidei (1453); Akbar's Ibadat Khana (1575); the Parliament of 1893. The yield was peace and policy, rarely a finding of sameness [C/M].
- Practice: Ramakrishna's lake, Gandhi's common prayer, Schuon's Name practices set side by side (japa, dhikr, the Jesus Prayer, the nembutsu). Yoga Sutra 1.12 joins practice and letting go in one line. Sameness is lived before it is argued, which suits Part three [M].
- Structure without identity: Izutsu (Sufism and Taoism, 1966 and 1967), Sells (1994), Loy (Nonduality, 1988) set shapes side by side and say where they diverge [M]. The careful method: map each thinker's key terms inside his own system first, then compare the shapes.
- Ranking: Zongmi, Kukai, Vivekananda and the Baha'i scheme put their own tradition on top; Hacker called Hindu tolerance inclusivism. Gita 4.11, 7.21 and 9.23 are inclusivist, so do not write that the Gita says all religions are equal. Every comparer has a top, and Alex's is his axiom [M].
- The shared test case (G2-3): Indian philosophers put one example, shell-silver or rope-snake, to every school and asked what each calls real in it. Alex can borrow the device and line up the traditions on one case, such as a dream, a mirage or a program's memory wall [M].
- Compare roles, not words (G1-5): find the word that carries the claim in each passage (iva, tolma, amsa, svabhava, periagoge, buren), say what role it plays in its own argument, then compare roles. Plotinus says tolma, Brihadaranyaka fear, Brahma Sutra 2.1.33 lila, Marcus discontent [M].
- Philosophers sort and do not match (G2-2): Schaffer, Nagasawa and Wager, Shani and Chalmers lay out logical possibilities and slot historical figures in, and never claim two religions say the same thing; Goff sets theism, axiarchism and cosmic purpose as rival hypotheses; Albahari takes mystical reports as data; Kastrup reads myths as allegory; Schrodinger cites the Upanishads and the mystics as precedent and not proof [M/U].
- The German line (G2-4): Boehme and Fichte compare nothing across religions and read one religion at two depths; Schelling compares by process (myths as stages of one consciousness); Hegel compares by form and ranks by freedom and adequacy; later comparers drop the ranking (Hartshorne by logical type, Teilhard and Whitehead by function, the Kyoto School by dialogue). Alex's "different words, same principles" is closest to Fichte (inner against outer) and Whitehead's tone, and saying "Hegel agrees" would be wrong [M].
- Bridges inside East Asia (G1-9): geyi (matching concepts), panjiao (ranking), hwajaeng (reconciling, Wonhyo), and Mani's founder formula [M].
- Marks first (G1-10): James and the lists of marks compare by conduct, so a Buddhist with no self, a Hindu with a Self and a Christian with a God can be matched on fruit. This is Alex's strongest method because it needs no agreement on metaphysics [M].

### 7.2 Why the religions look different if they say the same: ten families of answer

Every comparer in the files answers this, and every answer is the comparer's claim and not the tradition's own [M]. The ten families:

1. One teaching, then decay: Plethon, Steuco, Blavatsky, Guenon in part [C/M].
2. Same idea, other words: Pico, Dara Shikoh, Rumi's grapes, Subba Row [C/M].
3. One faith, many rites: Cusa, Vivekananda [C].
4. Stages of education, or a teacher adapted to an age: Lessing, Leibniz, Besant, the Baha'i scheme [C/M].
5. Outer form and inner teaching, with the diversity willed: Schuon, Guenon, Besant [C/M].
6. Levels of Reality: Schuon, Smith [M].
7. Temperament and path: Huxley, Ramakrishna, the Gita, Hodson [M].
8. Partial standpoints: the Udana, Jain many-sidedness, Rumi's elephant [M].
9. One Real through different filters: Hick [M].
10. The differences are real and should be argued and not explained away: Griffiths, Zaehner, Katz, Prothero, Ferrer [M].

Alex's answer is of a different kind to a different question. P01 lists three old explanations for the agreement itself (common revelation, common transmission, common experience) and says his is a fourth, common logic: the ideas recur because they follow from one axiom [M]. That explains why the same ideas keep turning up. It says nothing about why the words differ.

### 7.3 Each answer, and what scholars say

Schuon: the difference is willed [M gist]. The Infinite holds every possibility, so it shows in many forms and no one form uses it up. People and ages differ in what they can receive, so a message is shaped to its receiver. A form that stressed its own relativity would stop saving anyone. The contradictions between religions are real at the outer level and are no error, because the outer layer has to present itself as the one truth, which holds a whole society together, and the inner layer reads the outer as a symbol. (The image of a window that admitted all the other windows would stop working as one is the file's and not Schuon's.) He compares only within one degree and one layer: Eckhart beside Shankara and Ibn Arabi, never a catechism beside the Gita. He holds that unity is seen from inside one form, forbids mixing, and rejects a common-factor arithmetic. Scholars say the grid decides what counts as inner, so a disagreeing text is filed as outer and the thesis cannot fail; the grid is Vedanta and Sufi theory laid over the rest; history is set aside. Theravada scholars deny that nirvana is the Absolute. Whether Katz touches Schuon is [U]. Serious allegations have been reported about Schuon's circle, so cite the books and not the man.

Guenon: exoteric and esoteric, decline and orthodoxy (Introduction, 1921) [C]. Each tradition has an outer face, religious and open to all, and an inner face, initiatic and metaphysical, and only the inner carries the doctrine. Forms are adapted to a people and an age, and a tradition can lose its inner side (he thought the Christian West had). History is a decline through the Hindu ages; his six thousand years for the present Kali Yuga is his own count. Orthodoxy means regular transmission from a lineage; in 1921 that made Buddhism heterodox for him, and Coomaraswamy, Pallis and Schuon pressed him until he authorised amendments. Schuon repudiated his view that the sacraments had sunk to the exoteric level, part of their split of 1948 to 1950. So one move gave two verdicts on Christianity inside one school. Besant uses the move too (Esoteric Christianity reads the creed as the outer face of the lesser mysteries). Scholars say the rule cannot lose; Sedgwick calls Guenon's approach theological and not historical, and Legenhausen finds the comparisons tenuous and the selection prejudiced [C]. The strict school would refuse Alex's method of many witnesses with no lineage.

Huxley: temperament, idolatry, the knower, and a common factor [M]. The Foreword reaches a common factor of theologies, which is an intersection, a method of subtraction. His explanations of difference: temperament (the chapter "Religion and Temperament", the Gita's yogas of knowledge, devotion and works, and Sheldon's body types, a typology discredited as science, so cite the idea and not the authority); idolatry, meaning mistaking a symbol or doctrine for the Reality; and the condition in the knower (knowledge is a function of being, so direct knowledge needs a changed knower, loving, pure in heart and poor in spirit; do not call these "the Beatitudes"). The files' own objection is that this last move shields itself. Zaehner argued that nature, monistic and theistic mysticism differ and that Huxley folded them into a monism; Stace gave the common-core claim a better argument; Katz says no experience is unmediated. No file has a sourced Traditionalist statement about Huxley [U].

Huston Smith: levels, and two books [M]. A quarrel between a theistic and a non-theistic tradition is often a quarrel between two levels, the celestial Person-God and the impersonal Infinite. Outer forms differ and the inner teaching agrees. The two books are the split in practice: The World's Religions presents each tradition on its own terms and Forgotten Truth gives the shared map, and he meant them read together. Scholars say the levels can reassign any disagreement upward, and the grid puts both Brahman and emptiness at the Infinite while Buddhist specialists keep them apart.

Blavatsky and her circle: loss, symbol, veil, "blinds" [M]. Agreement is descent from one archaic Wisdom-Religion kept by initiates. Difference is loss, in five parts (pattern only; locators unknown): an outer form for the many and an inner teaching for the few; symbols read literally, so allegory became history; keys lost, the Mysteries closed, priestcraft kept the husk; adaptation, each age getting a teacher who restates the one teaching (Buddha, Krishna, Zoroaster, Jesus); and cycles of decline. The "blinds" (deliberate veils set by the initiates) rest on one line in one file [M, single mention]; find her own use in The Secret Doctrine before using it. Her circle adds more: Besant's Four Great Religions (1897) has each Great Teacher give the same truth in the form suited to a people and an age; Hodson ties differences in religious feeling to seven types [content U]; Subba Row argues that counts of principles differ because each scheme cuts the same thing at different joints, and he still disagreed with Blavatsky and Sinnett on the count; Olcott's fourteen propositions of 1891 are the one case where leaders of different Buddhist schools were asked to accept a short common list. Scholars say the method cannot fail, since agreement confirms it and disagreement counts as corruption; it reads Indian and Buddhist terms through a Western occult frame; Coleman traced much of her material to nineteenth-century books; Muller wrote in 1893 that there is no esoteric Buddhism, and the standard reply is DN 16. Historians reject the claim that a Church council banned rebirth. Her treatment of Jehovah as a lower power breaks Alex's rule never to say the Abrahamic religions fit badly.

Scripture, parables and saints [M]. Rigveda 1.164.46 ("ekam sat viprah bahudha vadanti") says the inspired speak of what is one in many ways, and the verse names gods (Indra, Mitra, Varuna, Agni, Yama, Matarishvan, Garutman). The slogan "Truth is one, the wise call it by many names" is a loose English form, often credited to Gandhi or Vivekananda, who quoted it. Gita 4.11, 7.21 and 9.23 are inclusivist; Hacker's thesis is that Hindu tolerance mostly absorbs the other and ranks it lower. Quran 5:48 and 2:285: each community has its law and way, and the believers make no distinction among the messengers; classical commentators read these inside the Abrahamic frame, and whether Schuon cites 5:48 and 49:13, as Muslim pluralists do, is [U]. The elephant: in the Udana (6.4) the Buddha tells it of sectarians who cling to views, with the Buddha as the one who sees, and no claim that religions are parts of one truth; Jain philosophy has many-sidedness (anekantavada), each standpoint (naya) partial and true from where it stands; Rumi's elephant in a dark house (Masnavi III) concerns the limits of sense perception more than religions; Newbigin noted the story needs a teller who sees the whole elephant. Rumi's grapes (Masnavi II, near line 3681 [U]) is the cleanest parable about words: four men share one coin and quarrel because each names a grape in his own language, angur, inab, uzum and istafil, and a man who knew all four would settle it; quote Nicholson, which is public domain. Ramakrishna's lake with many landing steps (jal, pani, water) is reported by disciples; his slogan "yato mat tato path" rests on disciples' accounts and could not be confirmed [U]; the practices of Islam and Christianity he undertook were short (about three days each, dated 1866 and 1874 [U]). Vivekananda at Chicago (two addresses) ranked Advaita highest, so he is an inclusivist. Coomaraswamy's mountain has several gateways at the foot and one summit, and the paths cannot be seen to meet until the top [C].

Hick, and the writers who say the differences are real [M]. Hick (An Interpretation of Religion, 1989): the Real an sich lies beyond every human concept, and the traditions are different human responses, filtered in a Kantian way and met as persons or as impersonal absolutes; the test is whether a tradition moves people from self-centredness to Reality-centredness. He accepts that concepts shape experience and is not a defender of a common core of experience. Scholars say: Plantinga, that pluralism is itself an exclusive claim; D'Costa, that it is not neutral and does not match what the traditions say; Buddhists, that nirvana is no manifestation of a ground; and if nothing can be said of the Real, "it is one" is out of bounds. The realists: Griffiths holds that traditions teach contradictory doctrines and the respectful course is to say so and argue; Zaehner that loving union with a personal God differs from union by identity, the objection a Christian or Muslim reader will raise against the second view; Katz that there are no pure experiences; Prothero that religions ask different questions and offer different cures; Loy that "not two" does not mean "one"; J. Z. Smith that the comparer makes the similarity and must state the respect and include difference. Alex's claim concerns texts and ideas, and Katz's concerns experience.

The "different words, same thing" move is old [C]. Pico thought Plato and Aristotle used different words for the same ideas. Dara Shikoh (Majma al-Bahrayn, about 1655) set Sufi and Vedantic terms side by side, held there was no essential difference apart from the manner of exposition, and called the Upanishads the Quran's hidden book. Cusa (1453) wrote of one religion in a variety of rites. Lessing's ring parable says the true ring cannot be shown and each son should prove his by love, which is Alex's own stance of examples without proof. Leibniz (1714) wrote of drawing gold from dross. The risk is that the comparer picks the match: Dara's Vedanta came out in Sufi vocabulary, the old comparers mostly had a home team, and translators carry equations too. The careful defenders share rules: state the respect in which you compare, use the tradition's own term, report difference as well as match, quote inner or scholarly sources and not the popular version, and do not let a similar word hide a different meaning. Panikkar's homeomorphic equivalents are the honest version. Wittgenstein's family resemblance, applied to religion by Alston and Saler, allows a cluster of themes that recur without a shared core, which fits the essay's admission that rebirth is not unanimous [M].

### 7.4 Side by side

What they share. Every answer is the comparer's. Nearly every one keeps the difference on the surface and the agreement underneath; the realists are the exception. Every one has a top: Dara reads Vedanta in Sufi words, Vivekananda ranks Advaita highest, Kukai puts Shingon highest, the Baha'i scheme puts the latest revelation last, and Schuon's grid is Vedanta and Sufi theory. Every one deals with a counterexample by reassigning it, to the outer layer, a lower level, corruption, an earlier stage or a filter.

Where they fight. Guenon attacked Theosophy (1921, 1923) while sharing the idea of a primordial teaching, and split with Schuon over Christianity [C]. Subba Row disagreed with Blavatsky on the count [U]. Zaehner argued against Huxley, and Katz against Stace [M]. On lives the movement splits: Guenon rejects Western reincarnation, Coomaraswamy has one transmigrant, and Blavatsky taught rebirth by the 1880s [C/M]. P01's fair summary of the scholarship is that the agreement is partly real, partly made, and the makers had a stake [M]. Historians of religion mostly accept that traditions changed one another and that the "perennial" frame grew in the West in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries; King, Halbfass and Hacker treat the Hindu universalist voice as partly a response to Orientalism; Sharf and McMahan treat modern Buddhist emphasis on experience as an import [M].

What each costs. Descent from one source failed as history: Casaubon redated the Hermetica in 1614, and Yates and Walker called the lineage mistaken history [C]. Outer and inner cannot lose. Levels are one school's grid. Temperament and stages carry the Sheldon and progress problems. Standpoint parables need a teller who sees the whole. Hick's filter is the cleanest philosophically and is attacked from both sides [M].

Rigour and permission trade. The strict authors (Guenon, Schuon, Coomaraswamy) demand one form and a lineage and refuse mixing. The loose ones (Huxley, Perry, Wilson) mix freely and explain less. Alex's witness method is nearer the loose camp and wants the strict camp's care [the checker's reading]. On Race's map of exclusivist, inclusivist and pluralist, an axiom on top with the traditions as witnesses sits with inclusivism, and Hick's pluralism is the one that puts no frame on top [the checker's reading]. Say so before a reader does.

How independent are the witnesses? (G1-1) The draft says the traditions "never met" and that independent traditions reached the same shape separately. Both are half true. Jaspers' independence claim is for three areas, China, India and the West (his West lumps Iran, Palestine and Greece), and it covers about 800 to 200 BCE only [M, about 85 percent]. India and Greece were joined by the Persian empire from about 520 BCE; contact is documented after Alexander (Pyrrho, Ashoka's edicts, the Milindapanha, Gandharan art) and unproven before; Plotinus went looking (the Gordian expedition of 242 to 243, Porphyry's Life 3) and what he wrote is Greek [M]. After that the witnesses are linked: Plotinus feeds Christian, Islamic and Jewish mystics (Marius Victorinus, Augustine, Pseudo-Dionysius, Eriugena, Eckhart; the Arabic Plotinus and the Liber de causis for al-Farabi, Avicenna, Suhrawardi and Ibn Arabi; Israeli and Ibn Gabirol before the Bahir and Zohar); Buddhism feeds Daoism and Neo-Confucianism (Zurcher 1959 and 1980; Bokenkamp 1997); Sufis and Vedantins have talked since the eleventh century (al-Biruni about 1030; Dara; Ernst 2003); the Buddha became a Catholic saint, Josaphat; Rumi's elephant is likely borrowed from the Indian Udana through Sana'i; Schrodinger read the Upanishads through Schopenhauer (Moore 1989) [M]. English readers met the East through nineteenth and early twentieth century translators and comparers (Anquetil-Duperron, Schopenhauer, Muller, the Theosophists, Evans-Wentz 1927). The documented chain Dara, Anquetil, Schopenhauer, Deussen (and later Schrodinger) is real; Emerson is not on it, since the Over-Soul (1841) predates it and his Indian reading came by Wilkins's Gita, Rammohan Roy's Upanishads, Colebrooke and Jones (Christy 1932) [M]. So Emerson is an independent witness who arrived by another road, which is a stronger point for Alex and not a weaker one. The checker's own count: roughly four independent hubs for the axial-era ideas the essay needs (the Indian Upanishadic and Sramana world, the Greek Platonic and Pythagorean world, the Chinese world, and the Hebrew scriptures with Persian input), with several documented bridges and not ten; Jaspers counted three [the checker's synthesis, M]. Rebirth may have arisen more than once: Obeyesekere, Imagining Karma (2002; subtitle Ethical Transformation in Amerindian, Buddhist, and Greek Rebirth), argues ethical rebirth eschatologies arose in several regions, and Bronkhorst, Greater Magadha (2007), has karma and rebirth belonging to the eastern Gangetic world and entering Brahmanical texts by borrowing, so Upanishads and early Buddhism are not independent of each other (Gombrich reads the Buddha as answering the early Upanishads) [M]. The convergence tests (Hood's scale, the Mystical Experience Questionnaire) use a list from one Western philosopher and mostly Western samples, and cognitive science of religion (Boyer, Atran, Barrett, Bloom, Bering) predicts shared ideas from shared minds, which cuts both ways: convergence fits either view and cannot alone support the axiom [M]. Galton's problem (1889) is the name for the issue: cultures cannot be counted as independent samples when they share descent or contact; Condorcet and Hume's rival-miracles argument both need independence for agreement to count [M]. For Guenon and Blavatsky "independent" means independent historically and one in source (a primordial tradition; a Wisdom-Religion), and for Schuon and Smith one metaphysical Source behind different forms, so contact is beside the point for them; Katz adds that Eckhart and Dionysius, or Rumi and Sana'i, are closer to one witness than two [M].

### 8.3 Twelve rules for wording equations that stay true to each tradition (S3)

1. Name the respect. Write "both say the sense of a separate self is built and can end", never "they teach the same thing" [M].
2. Claim texts and ideas, not feelings. Say "reports", "a family of ideas", "keep turning up". The draft's own wording is right [M].
3. Give the tradition's own term first, then the gloss, then its job. Maya means dependent, neither fully real nor simply unreal, and "illusion" translates it badly. Blavatsky's own gloss makes maya what changes and ends, close to mithya [M].
4. Attribute every equation to its maker. "Schuon sets nirvana beside the Absolute and many Buddhist scholars keep them apart." "Blavatsky equated devachan with Sukhavati, and the Tibetan name renders Sukhavati." Say "the teaching later called wahdat al-wujud", since the phrase is not clearly in Ibn Arabi's own works [M].
5. Match inner with inner at the same level, and say which layer each quote comes from. Never rescue a disagreeing text by moving it outward.
6. Say where the grounds differ under similar words. For the golden rule: reciprocity (Hillel, Analects 15.24, Matthew 7:12), analogy (Gita 6.32, Sutta Nipata 705), identity (Isha 6 and 7, Gita 13.28). Shantideva reaches compassion by the ownerlessness of suffering, and the Acharanga line reads as oneness only in one modern commentary [M].
7. Keep each tradition's own account of what continues. Buddhism: no fixed self and yet something continues (the lamp-flame in the Milindapanha). Advaita: the one Self is the only transmigrant, and the individual does not carry over. Judaism: gilgul, which Saadia rejected. Islam: growth after death without return (Mulla Sadra) and tanasukh mostly rejected. Christianity: one life, purification, then epektasis. Say "rebirth" or "transmigration", since "reincarnation" carries Spiritist baggage [M].
8. Use each tradition's own relation word. Christianity keeps participation and not identity. Chabad is close to acosmism in the Tanya's reading, which is not "the world does not exist". In Judaism separation is a rupture to repair. Jainism agrees on the practice and objects to the One, since souls are many. The Traditionalists reject Theosophical return [M/U].
9. Quote only from a named translation. Do not take lines from memory, an anthology or a quote site (section 8.5).
10. Write an objection as what a specialist adds, never as "this tradition fits badly". Never claim a tradition says what it does not. The draft's habit is the model [M].
11. Say once that every comparer had a home team and a top, and that Alex has no team and his top is his axiom [M].
12. Say once that the movement is plural (8.2, line 1) [M].

### 8.4 What not to say (S5 section 5)

- That the traditions or their texts say all religions are the same. Prothero, Griffiths and Zaehner are the standard replies, and the Rigveda, the Gita and the Udana were not written for pluralism [M].
