# Part zero: Before you start

I'm Alex. I work in cyber security, and my spiritual search has run for more than fourteen years. In many ways I'm a modern monk with a day job, living in a kind of neo-monastery.

If you've ever seen a monk, Buddhist or Christian, someone who took the serious spiritual path in one of the religions, and thought to yourself, I want that too, minus the religion: that's pretty much what I thought and felt when I chose this life.

That choice cost me a more standard life. Most of the friends I grew up with, and I'm still friends with them, have a partner, and some have kids. I don't, because I can't say that's what I want.

This book is here to convey ideas. It isn't a course, because a method is learned from people, the way a craft is. It has three parts, and each one has a different job.

Part one is the rational part, and I've kept it deliberately earthly. It uses no spiritual vocabulary and asks for no belief. It is about two things you can check in your own life, how much you make things happen and how much you let go of whatever holds you back, and why almost nobody works on both. Its two words, DO and BE, carry the rest of the book. It's also the part you can hand to a partner or a friend who rolls their eyes at the word spiritual. And if someone handed this book to you, Part one was written with you in mind.

Part two is the spiritual part: where all of this comes from and where it leads. It starts from a view of reality that I call an assumption, quotes many traditions and says plainly where the reading is mine, and it is what I actually believe. You can disagree with it and still use the rest.

Part three is the practical side: the conditions that make it realistic to walk the whole road in one life, from the ones you can start alone tonight to the ones that need other people.

You can stop after any part, and each one makes the next easier to judge.

## Who taught me

I studied many traditions on my own, and this book is a distillation of that whole journey. For the last six years of it my teacher has been Genadiy Givin. He was born in Riga and studied at Riga Technical University. In his teens he met people who had something he wanted, and what he saw in them gave him a huge wish to know what they knew. That wish led him to the man who became his teacher. He describes the people who handed this on to him, himself included, as ordinary people without titles. He adapted what they handed on to life in the 21st century. For him the knowledge is what matters.
