# Part zero: Before you start

This essay has three parts, and each one has a different job. Here is what to expect from them, so you can set your own pace. The aim of the whole essay is to convey ideas. It isn't a course, and it doesn't try to teach you a method from the page.

Part one is the rational part, and I've kept it deliberately earthly. It uses no spiritual vocabulary and asks for no belief. It is about two things you can check in your own life, how much you make things happen and how much you let go of whatever holds you back, and why almost nobody works on both. If you have practiced for years, it may feel light, with sitcom characters and everyday chores in it. That is on purpose, and there are two reasons to read it anyway. DO and BE are the words Parts two and three use, so everything after rests on them. And it is the part you can hand to someone who has never meditated: a partner, a colleague, a friend who rolls their eyes at the word spiritual. It gives you a way to say what you do and why, with nothing they'd need to take on trust.

Part two is the spiritual part, and it has its own role: it says where all of this comes from and where it leads. It starts from a view of reality that I call an assumption, it quotes many traditions as witnesses, and it says plainly where the reading is mine. You can disagree with it and still use the rest.

Part three is the practical side of the method: how it is done in the reality of the 21st century. It starts with one thing you can try alone tonight and goes on to what needs other people, a team and a guide.

You can stop after any part, and each one makes the next easier to judge.

## Who is talking, and who taught me

I'm Alex. In my teens I had a lot of questions: why things are the way they are, and why I am the way I am. Over time those questions led to a spiritual search. I have worked with computers for about ten years, eight of them in cyber security, which I still do. My spiritual journey is much longer, more than fourteen years.

Everything in this essay that goes beyond what you can check in your own life comes from one teacher, Genadiy Givin, and the method in Part three is the one he teaches.

Genadiy was born in Riga. As a young man he became a master of sport in martial arts while he studied at Riga Technical University. In his teens he met people who had woken up, in the sense Part two describes, and what he saw in them gave him a huge wish to know what they knew. That led him to the man who became his teacher. The knowledge comes to him through a long line of teachers and students, and he describes everyone in it as ordinary people without titles, himself included. He says that on his own he isn't worth much, and that the knowledge he passes on is what matters.

On a personal note, he is the wisest and most compassionate person I have ever met, and from my own experience of him that is enough. I can't prove to you that he is right. I feel it. I see the changes in me, and I see them in the hundreds of students who studied personally with him and in the tens of thousands who study with his students, following his method. For this part, that is enough.
