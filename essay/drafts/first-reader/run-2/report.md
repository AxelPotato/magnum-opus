# First read: Parts one, two and three, in order (2026-10-12)

Cast: M (Mara, product designer, sympathetic), D (Dan, software engineer, wary of gurus and paid retreats, one strike allowed), R (Ruth, 52, long-time meditator with a strong wish to wake up, what the lectures call a ready student). All three met the essay cold at Part one and read it in order, 175 passages, 21,500 words. I had read Part three earlier in the thread, so every reading step ran through fresh readers. Skim and recall ran through fresh readers as well. I did not read Parts one and two myself in this run, and I did not run the trust ledger, so nothing below is about word-level signals.

## 1. Skim gate

D, shown only the scanner view: no, he does not open it. 21,500 words, and "the outline reads like a funnel". What decided it was Part three's headings: the teacher and the method, "The courses and retreats cost money", "who checks the teacher?", and "Centres, and the settlement".

Where the three agreed on a loss:
- The seven-times and forty-nine team number (M -1, R -2, and D noted the same style of number elsewhere).
- "Unite around the direction first and put down our certainty later" and "that reaction is the lesson" (M -1 and 0, R -1 and 0): a closed loop.
- The goal list at the start of section 1 (M and R both -1: prospectus language, "the productivity pole again").
- "Inner work with no measurable outer goal is a pleasant way to spend an afternoon" (M "slightly harsh", R -1 "dismissive of sitting practice").
- Paying and "free places have a poor record" (M -1, R -1: "no sums, no sliding scale").
- The settlement: "a new era of human evolution" (M -1, R 0, both "the humour is gone").

Where they split: the cult line and "who checks the teacher?" won trust from M and R (+1 each); D never got there. The blind-spot argument was R's best moment in the intro and D's first strike.

Shared across Parts one and two (all three, mostly): the scripture blocks were skimmed; "the axiom prefers the second" and the rebirth step read as circular; Stevenson's cases without caveat cost trust (M, D); the -4 to +4 scale and the escape-velocity numbers read as numerology (D, R, M).

R only: the first fifteen passages of Part one were neutral to bored ("sitcom characters, chores and productivity framing"); the stillness instructions are accurate but basic for a seasoned meditator and the other practices are listed without how; the teacher is unnamed with no account of what he has realised; the "turn it off and on again" line flattened the death passage.

## 3. What survived (recall, from each reader's log)

All three retold the same arc: two axes and four rooms, a reason bigger than you, the oneness axiom, then a practical program that ends in a school. M: "ends up reading like a pitch for his school". R: a "path of wish, practice, team and teacher that leads toward a school and a workshop".

Phrases that stuck for two or more: a gym where nobody lifts, own a calendar, a bomb with a radio, bailed a boat with the hole still open, the rusty lever, "I'll try to say this without sounding like a cult", "Who checks the teacher? Nobody inside the method does, and I'd rather say so than dodge it", seven times nothing is still nothing, "I don't want to believe it's there. I want to see it for myself", space port.

Peak: M, the machine and sandbox-escape idea in Part two for the idea, the rusty lever for persuasion. R, the rusty lever. D, the studies stretch in Part one for trust. Center of gravity, all three: Part one and the honest, personal moments. Ending: M and R finished warm to the ideas and guarded toward the school.

One action, as each wrote it: M, ten minutes of stillness, apologise first to someone, ask a friend to be a pair. R, go to a workshop, ask about fees, keep her own practice. D, would worry about his friend and want to ask about cost and whether the friend can leave.
