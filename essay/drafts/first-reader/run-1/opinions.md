# Opinions on Part three, Draft D

These come from the two readings (M is Mara, D is Dan) and are anchored to what they logged. They are opinions: the readers say where it happened, and how to fix it is your call. I have not drafted replacement text. Your earlier decisions (wish first, payment in section 9, the settlement as flagship, the teacher trusted, your goal list wording) are treated as fixed, so nothing below asks you to undo them.

## 1. The worry and its answer are 16 passages apart

M at passage 4 ("centres where people live together makes my shoulders go up, a tiny cult alarm") and at passage 20 ("exactly the right thing to say, he joins himself into the problem") is one worry with the answer arriving late. D never got there: he left at passage 3 and the limits (you can question, a mentor can be removed, you can leave) are in section 6. In the draft the school is introduced in the intro paragraph that M reacted to, and the cult line, the limits and "who checks the teacher" are the passages that bought trust. Moving the first honest admission up to where the school is introduced would put the answer next to the worry. Which one to move, and how much, is yours.

## 2. "A thousand lives" is the strike

D quit at passage 3 on two points together: the thousand lives stated as background and the nudge test, which he read as unfalsifiable because both outcomes confirm it (same failure in a new costume, or everything fine and dissatisfied anyway). M also dropped to -1 there: "I accepted Part two as a lens, not as a syllabus, and this is asking me to hold it as fact before giving me anything to do." Your own notes list the thousand lives under "still unsupported in the lectures" (it appears once, in passing). I would at least reconsider the number, and the paragraph's position directly after the guide and team sentence. If you keep it, a version that could be wrong (something you saw, not a test both results pass) is what D wanted.

## 3. Nothing to do until section 7

M: "I wanted something for tonight", "I wish it had been section 1", then at passage 23 "this is what I came for." Her first doable thing was "ask yourself, quietly, how much you want this" (passage 9), which she did. You decided the order (wish first, and it varies by person), so I am not suggesting a reorder. The opinion is that nothing in the opening tells a reader that section 7 can be done tonight, alone. A pointer in the intro would give the skimmer and the impatient reader a handle without moving anything.

## 4. The goals list reads like a brochure

The list is your wording, so I am not touching it. The prose around it could carry your voice: M wanted "to see his actual reason" (passage 11), and a line about which of these were yours, or a pointer to Part two's "Why I want this", would turn a list of anyone's wishes into a person's.

## 5. Sections 9 and 10 change the voice, and both readers cool

M at 30: "I can see the sales funnel too, and I'm aware it's the same funnel the whole essay has been leading me through." D, at the skim, read the whole back half as "a paid-retreat funnel with a compound at the end."

Opinions, all within your decisions:
- Section 9 ends on money, so the last taste of the section is the sum and where it goes. The strongest lines in the section are "moved back above the same bar" and "Ordinary life is the training ground". The payment paragraph could sit earlier in the section.
- In section 10 the space port paragraph answers the worry that the settlement paragraph raises, but it comes second. Putting the purpose (you leave from it, and nobody builds one to stay) before the scale (proof, seed, a new era) changes what a skeptic meets first.
- The [ALEX] slot is the part of this I would most want filled: place, size, who it is for, how to join. M and D both met an abstract grand claim. A concrete place is the thing that makes a claim like this checkable.

## 7. Smaller things the readers hit

- Passage 13: after "the source" and "the slow wearing away of the ego", M: "it slid back into spiritual-speak", and she skimmed the second half. This is the one place in sections 1 to 6 where she lost the thread to vocabulary from Part two.
- Passage 15: "the real gain is bigger than the figure" read to M as upselling, the opposite of a hedge, and cost a notch of trust before the accountant line repaired it.
- Passage 22: the pilot analogy half works. M noticed that pilots are regulated by outsiders and the teacher is not. The comparison lands on trust and invites the point about oversight.
- Passage 24: stillness is stated clearly, then a list of other practices with no how: "I wanted a 5-minute recipe and got a menu of names", "'reviving a part of the body' is opaque". The list also made the third job feel missing for one passage because the stillness paragraph reads as a break.
- Passage 26: "He declines to give a timetable though, which frustrates me; I'd have liked 'start with five minutes, here is how'." This is a deliberate choice of yours (schedules vary), so I flag it only as a place a first-time solo reader looked for a starting dose.
